## By the end of this chapter you can

1. Explain why any recorded dataset is an institutional selection, not an unmediated copy of reality.
2. Show how non-random missingness ($\rho\_{R,Y} \ne 0$) produces the Big Data Paradox, where more observations raise confidence in an erroneous answer.
3. Classify the three kinds of empty cells in an archive, and trace how the mediation circuit's guard sites block institutional distortion.

## 1. The survey that only asks those present

A principal stands at the bus loop at 7:45 on a Tuesday, handing out a one-page questionnaire on whether
students feel safe commuting after dark. First-period homeroom collects the forms. Almost every
student present checks the box marked "Safe," and by noon the district office publishes a reassuring
safety score.

Who is missing from that spreadsheet? The students who never stepped off the morning bus. Some are ill,
some work an overnight shift to pay household rent, some were kept home because the walk to the bus
stop after dusk had been dangerous, some have already dropped out. The students who did not cross the
schoolhouse threshold on Tuesday are a different sample of how the commute feels than the students at
their desks.

When the school board votes to trim evening crossing-guard patrols, the information missing at decision
time is the **unrecorded denominator**: how many students were exposed to the dark walk in the **World**
but filtered out before reaching the **Archive**. An honest shift observation separates the observed
count from the missing count, so that unrecorded demand is bounded across its full support instead of
being treated as zero. A summary table that reports a large row count while hiding its intake gate lets
the **Institution** mistake a property of its own administrative filter for a property of the
neighborhood. That erodes its **Epistemic Margin** ($S\_{\mathrm{epi}}$) and sets up **Collapse
Proposition 1 (Blind Undersizing)**.

Collecting data is not the error. The discipline is asking, before any table is trusted: *who would not
be in this table, and why?*

## 2. Recorded is not the same as happened: Wald's missing armor

Every institution sees a particular slice of the world, and its own door defines the slice. A hospital
records the patients who arrive at the triage desk. It does not record the people who were ill and
stayed home, the people who died before the ambulance arrived, or the people whose suffering has no
diagnostic billing code. What is absent from the list is not therefore absent from the world. Bhaskar
named the error of confusing the two: the epistemic fallacy, in which a statement about what can be
known is treated as a statement about what is, though absence is real and can be causally efficacious
(Bhaskar 1975; Bhaskar 1993). The person who never reached the door is not a gap in our knowledge of
the hospital. He is a fact about the hospital. The vocabulary repeats the exclusion in its grammar.
Koselleck showed that political and administrative concepts are built asymmetrically, the collective
naming itself positively and the excluded appearing only as its negation (Koselleck 2006), so a table
built from such concepts can record the excluded only as an absence — and the missing row is therefore
the hardest one to argue for.

During World War II, the statistician Abraham Wald at the Statistical Research Group was asked where to add heavy armor to combat aircraft (Wald 1943). Military engineers had mapped the bullet holes on returning bombers, noting dense concentrations on the wings and fuselage (Zone A), and almost none on the engines or cockpit (Zone B). The military impulse was obvious: bolt the armor onto the wings, where the bullet holes were concentrated.

Wald saw the flaw at once. The survey included only the aircraft that *returned to the airfield*. The same logic can be written down exactly. Let $q\_z \in (0, 1)$ be the conditional lethality of a hit in zone $z \in \lbrace{}A, B\rbrace$. Only the fraction $1 - q\_z$ of planes hit in zone $z$ survives to be counted in the hangar, so each observed survivor hit in zone $z$ stands in for $\theta\_z = q\_z / (1 - q\_z)$ unseen planes at the bottom of the ocean. Count returning aircraft by zone across two combat waves whose loss rates differ, and the two waves give two equations in the two unknown $\theta\_z$:

$$S\_{1,A}\mkern3mu\theta\_A + S\_{1,B}\mkern3mu\theta\_B = M\_1, \qquad S\_{2,A}\mkern3mu\theta\_A + S\_{2,B}\mkern3mu\theta\_B = M\_2$$

where $S\_{w,z}$ is the number of returning aircraft hit in zone $z$ in wave $w$ and $M\_w$ the number missing in that wave. Subtract one wave from the other, one zone drops out and the other solves; substitute back and the first solves too. The lesson does not depend on the arithmetic. The zone with fewer visible hits stands in for far more missing aircraft per hit, so the estimated lethality of a hit is concentrated exactly where the archive is blank. The armor had to go where the Archive was blank, and the zone that looked safest was the one that killed.

## 3. More rows do not fix a filter: The Big Data Paradox

This is the part that surprises planners. Collect enough data and the sampling error washes out, surely?

It does not, and the reason is straightforward arithmetic. In 2018, Xiao-Li Meng proved the **Big Data Paradox** (Meng 2018): a sample mean departs from the population mean in proportion to the correlation between being recorded and the outcome, so that collecting more rows can move the estimate further from the truth. Meng's identity prices that displacement and nothing else. Add Kish's design-effect term for records that arrive in clusters, and the two compose into the harmonic precision law derived in [Chapter 34](34-sample-population-archive.md), in which the effective sample size obeys:

$$\frac{1}{n\_{\mathrm{eff}}} = \frac{\mathrm{DEFF}}{n} + \left(\frac{1 - f}{f}\right)\rho\_{R,Y}^2, \qquad f = \frac{n}{N}$$

For unclustered draws ($\mathrm{DEFF} = 1$) this simplifies to $n\_{\mathrm{eff}} = n / \bigl(1 + (N - n)\rho\_{R,Y}^2\bigr)$. Each denominator term prices a distinct structural loss between the **World** and the **Archive**:

1. **The clustering penalty ($\mathrm{DEFF}/n$):** Intraclass correlation $\hat{\rho}\_I$ inflates the design effect, $\mathrm{DEFF} = 1 + (\tilde{m} - 1)\hat{\rho}\_I$, so a survey whose respondents arrive in households, wards or arrival days carries fewer independent observations than rows. A household survey and a call-centre log are both of this kind: the rows are real, the independence is not, and the effective sample is the design effect away from the row count.
2. **The selection penalty ($((1 - f)/f)\rho\_{R,Y}^2$):** Even a small correlation between being recorded and the outcome destroys a large fraction of the sample once the population outgrows the sample by much. The penalty enters as $\rho\_{R,Y}^2$ and not as $\rho\_{R,Y}$, so the effective sample of a large archive falls as the reciprocal square of the correlation. The row count enters through $f$ twice over: two archives of the same nominal size and the same $\mathrm{DEFF}$ are ordered by $\bigl((1 - f)/f\bigr)\rho\_{R,Y}^2$, and the $1/f$ is what separates two archives of equal $n$ sitting in populations of different size. $\mathrm{DEFF}$ is the second axis; neither factor orders an archive on its own. This is the sense in which a very large survey can be worse than a very small one: the small one knows it is small.

Both penalty terms are read off the design, with one exception the law cannot supply. $\mathrm{DEFF}$ is a property of how the rows were assembled and can be estimated from the cluster structure. $\rho\_{R,Y}$ is defined against the population mean the archive is trying to recover, so it is a hypothesis about the recording mechanism and not a statistic. The law is a family indexed by the declared bound $\bar{\rho} \ge |\rho\_{R,Y}|$: at $\bar{\rho}$ the effective sample is at most $\bigl(\mathrm{DEFF}/n + \tfrac{1 - f}{f}\bar{\rho}^{2}\bigr)^{-1}$, and at $\bar{\rho} = 1$ the displacement itself obeys the sharp bound $|\bar{Y}\_n - \bar{Y}\_N| \le \sigma\_Y\sqrt{(1 - f)/f}$. A report that quotes one $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}$ has fixed $\bar{\rho}$ somewhere, and the honest report says where. The buffer follows the same substitution. The identification interval of [Chapter 09](09-sets-relations-functions.md) puts the upper end at $U\_t = p\_{\mathrm{obs}}\bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{obs}} + (1 - p\_{\mathrm{obs}})y\_{\max}$ when a fraction $1 - p\_{\mathrm{obs}}$ of a bounded outcome is unobserved, so $U\_t - \bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{obs}} = (1 - p\_{\mathrm{obs}})(y\_{\max} - \bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{obs}})$: capacity provisioned at $U\_t$ rather than at $\bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{obs}}$ buys exactly that gap as slack instead of discovering it as a shortfall.

Volume helps in one regime and fails in the other:

- **An independent door and unclustered draws ($\rho\_{R,Y} \to 0$ and $\hat{\rho}\_I \to 0$).** Both penalties vanish and $n\_{\mathrm{eff}} = n$. This is the condition under which the naive interval is the honest one, and it is a property of the recording mechanism, not of the sample size.
- **A complete census ($f = n/N \to 1$).** As $n \to N$, $(1 - f)/f \to 0$ eliminates the selection penalty because no unrecorded outside remains. Census-taking is expensive precisely because it buys the second term and nothing else.

Gather more rows through a biased door ($f \ll 1$, $\rho\_{R,Y} \ne 0$) and the naive standard error merely shrinks around the wrong number. The result is catastrophic institutional overconfidence.

## 4. Three ways to be empty

An empty cell in an archive is not one thing. It can be at least three:

1. **Unmeasured (Missingness):** The phenomenon occurred, but the instrument was not looking or the question was skipped. Statistical imputation cannot cure selective missingness, because it projects correlations from recorded survivors onto missing rows; only partial-identification bounds preserve coverage.
2. **Measured and Null (Negative Evidence):** The instrument observed the state and recorded zero. A patient with no fever is not a patient with a missing temperature; the zero is an informative signal.
3. **Unrepresentable (Ontological Exclusion):** The category does not exist in the institutional schema: no column, no dropdown option, no billing code, and the phenomenon is invisible.

To bound the unseen "dark figure" of unrecorded classes or individuals, the standard instrument is Anne Chao's Cauchy–Schwarz lower bound: $\hat{n}\_{00,\mathrm{Chao1}} = f\_1^2/(2 f\_2)$ for single-stream multinomial class frequencies (Chao 1984), and across $t$ incomplete capture-recapture registries without within-list replacement (Chao 1987; Chao & Jost 2012), $(t - 1)/t$ times that ratio — which for $t = 2$ registries with $f\_1 = n\_{10} + n\_{01}$ single-list captures and $f\_2 = n\_{11}$ shared captures gives

$$\hat{n}\_{00,\mathrm{Chao2}} = \frac{f\_1^2}{4 f\_2} = \frac{(n\_{10} + n\_{01})^2}{4 n\_{11}} \ge \frac{n\_{10}\mkern3mun\_{01}}{n\_{11}} = \hat{n}\_{00,\mathrm{LP}},$$

so that unequal list coverage ($n\_{10} \ne n\_{01}$) raises the bound strictly above Lincoln–Petersen by AM–GM while heterogeneous catchability across individuals leaves $\hat{n}\_{00,\mathrm{Chao2}}$ a conservative floor on the true unseen count. The gap between the two estimates is itself the useful output: it is the size of the assumption you would have to believe. Alongside it, the vintage literature (Croushore & Stark 2001) audits post-release archive revisions: what a real-time record said when the decision was taken, and what it says now.

## 5. The mediation circuit and its guard sites

Stochology models data not as a static table, but as a path traversing a **mediation circuit across three planes**: the **World** (where events happen), the **Archive** (where events are recorded), and the **Institution** (where decisions are deliberated and executed).

Seven distinct **guard sites** ($T\_1$ through $T\_7$) sit along that loop and protect the **Nuclear Triad** ($S\_{\mathrm{epi}}, S\_{\mathrm{vital}}, S\_{\mathrm{gov}}$). They frame the inquiry ($T\_1$), test archival claims with severity in the world ($T\_2$), audit world-to-archive capture and representation ($T\_3$), guard formalization ($T\_4$), preserve temporal survival order ($T\_5$), monitor performative feedback ($T\_6$), and enforce structural liability through a ledger for third parties who never consented and were never counted ($T\_7$), which prices a solvency buffer against the risk such parties are made to carry. Without them, institutional records become funhouse mirrors that reflect only their own administrative convenience.

### Effective sample, not sample

Rows are not information. A clustered file carries the information of its effective sample, not of its
row count, and the Keynesian weight built from that effective sample sits close to one only when the
effective sample dwarfs the reference scale — so ten times the rows through the same clustering buys
far less weight than a reader expects. The capture-recapture bound can put most of the universe outside
both lists at once. And the file is not even stable: across real-time vintages, revisions move the
figure that policy was decided on, and a share of quarters change sign between the first release and
the benchmark. The survey tradition that named the design effect also named the remedy (Kish 1965):
report the effective sample next to the raw one, and a reader can see how much information the table
actually carries.

### When there is no outside

The thesis stops holding when the record itself defines the population, because then there is no outside to estimate. If the universe is the archive, absence does not exist, and the right procedure is to declare the universe rather than estimate its complement; the formalisation of that case belongs to another chapter and is not repeated here. Where the universe is genuinely larger than the record, name the two lists, fit one estimator to both, and publish the estimate with the assumption that the lists are independent — and publish the bound that assumes they are not.
