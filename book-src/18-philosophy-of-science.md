> Third chapter of the philosophical block in Part II. The subject is the criterion by which a claim is
> admitted to a body of knowledge, and the reasons every such criterion is contested.

## By the end of this chapter you can

1. State the falsifiability criterion and the objection that a single hypothesis is never tested alone.
2. Distinguish a progressive from a degenerating research programme, with the observable difference.
3. Say why the demarcation question matters for institutions and not only for philosophers.

## 1. Falsificationism and the Demarcation Criterion

Consider two advisory teams presenting macro-risk frameworks to a central bank board. The first offers a narrative
elastic enough to explain a boom as "liquidity confidence" and the next morning's crash as "endogenous sentiment
reversal", so no market print can embarrass it. The second registers a numerical threshold before the quarter opens:
if interbank spread dispersion exceeds a declared bar for a declared number of consecutive days without a funding
freeze, its contagion hypothesis is dead.

Karl Popper (1934/1959) sought to solve the problem of induction by reformulating scientific rationality around that
exact asymmetry (Popper 1959). Science advances not by inductive verification but by deductive falsification. No
finite set of favourable observations can logically prove a universal generalisation, and a single genuine
counterexample can deductively refute it, so the hallmark of empirical science is **falsifiability**. A proposition
$H$ is scientific if and only if it divides the universe $\Omega$ of conceivable observational statements into two
non-empty sets:

1. The permitted set $\mathcal{C}(H) \subset \Omega$ with which $H$ is compatible;
2. The non-empty **forbidden set** $\mathcal{F}(H) = \Omega \setminus \mathcal{C}(H) \ne \emptyset$ of potential falsifiers.

Take the case where $\mathcal{F}(H) = \emptyset$. A theory forbids nothing, since it has a post-hoc rationalisation
for every conceivable outcome, and its empirical information content is identically zero. Take the opposite case,
where $\mathcal{F}(H)$ excludes almost the entire state space $\Omega$. The hypothesis is then highly informative and
immediately vulnerable to a single discordant measurement. For Popper, the epistemic strength of a hypothesis is
measured by the severity of the attempted refutations it has survived when exposed to its forbidden set
$\mathcal{F}(H)$, not by the volume of friendly illustrations collected in its favour.

## 2. The Duhem–Quine Thesis and Holistic Underdetermination

Picture a risk desk whose Value-at-Risk model fails three times in a month ($\neg O$). Management does not discard
the Gaussian copula assumption ($H$). It blames an "overnight data-feed lag" ($A\_1$), a "holiday calendar artifact"
($A\_2$) and a "temporary dealer holiday" ($A\_3$), and the core hypothesis survives on the server because the
auxiliary web absorbed the refutation.

Pierre Duhem (1906) and Willard Van Orman Quine (1951) identified the structural flaw in naive falsificationism
(Duhem 1906; Quine 1951). A theoretical hypothesis $H$ is never confronted with empirical observation in total
isolation. An empirical prediction $O$ is deduced from an extensive web of conjunctions:

$$H \land A\_1 \land A\_2 \land \cdots \land A\_k \implies O,$$

where $A\_1, \dots, A\_k$ are auxiliary hypotheses: the physical theories governing measurement instruments,
calibration constants, software pipelines, data preprocessing heuristics, and experimental execution integrity. When
the predicted observation fails ($O$ does not occur, $\neg O$), modus tollens entails only that the conjunction is
false:

$$\neg (H \land A\_1 \land \cdots \land A\_k) \iff \neg H \lor \neg A\_1 \lor \cdots \lor \neg A\_k.$$

The failure does not localise the blame. In corporate and governmental institutions the Duhem–Quine leeway becomes
an administrative shield: failing models are perpetually excused by blaming external data anomalies rather than
flawed core assumptions.

Two operational bounds constrain the holism. The first is **Invariant Causal Prediction**
(Peters, Bühlmann & Meinshausen 2016), which defeats whole-system evasion by testing which predictor subset keeps an
invariant regression slope across distinct environmental regimes
([Reactive Worlds, Performativity, and Goodhart's Law](48-reactive-worlds-performativity-goodhart.md)). A predictor
that carries the causal relation holds its slope across regimes, while a spurious proxy fitted to the same archive
changes slope from one regime to the next, so intersecting the subsets that survive every regime isolates the invariant
set instead of excusing the whole web.

The second is a **dated single-hypothesis counterexample**. Robert Millikan (1913) measured the elementary electron
charge via oil droplets, using a calibrated apparatus and a single isolated hypothesis. His published value
($-1.592 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$ against the modern $-1.602 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$, an offset of well under one
per cent) was refined by correcting the viscosity of air, not by debating an unidentifiable web of auxiliaries.

## 3. Lakatosian Research Programmes: Progressive vs. Degenerating

Imagine a forecasting unit that greets every out-of-sample miss by bolting on a new post-hoc dummy: a "weather
shock" in Q1, a "port congestion" in Q2, a "sentiment regime" in Q3. Each patch restores a pristine in-sample $R^2$ on
yesterday's archive while predicting nothing new about tomorrow. Imre Lakatos (1970) resolved the Duhem–Quine tension
by replacing isolated theories with **research programmes** (Lakatos 1970):

- A **hard core** of foundational commitments, protected against refutation by a methodological negative heuristic;
- A **protective belt** of auxiliary hypotheses that absorb anomalies and undergo continuous adjustment.

Lakatos established an objective, observable demarcation between two evolutionary regimes. A research programme is
**theoretically and empirically progressive** when its auxiliary adjustments lead to the prediction of novel,
hitherto unexpected empirical facts *before* the new observation is collected. It is **degenerating**, or
pseudoscientific, when its protective belt expands solely to accommodate known failures ex post, retrofitting ad-hoc
parameters to rescue institutional prestige. The accounting rule prices each ad-hoc protective-belt adjustment
$k \in \mathbb{N}\_0$ against a programme's terminal evidential value $E\_T > 0$:

$$R\_{\mathrm{Lakatos}}(E\_T, k) = \frac{E\_T}{k + 1}, \qquad \Delta W\_{\mathrm{dB}}(k) = -10\log\_{10}(k + 1).$$

The accounting rule has two ends. At $k = 0$, $R\_{\mathrm{Lakatos}}(E\_T, 0) = E\_T$ and
$\Delta W\_{\mathrm{dB}}(0) = 0\text{ dB}$, so the programme retains all of its evidential weight. As
$k \to \infty$, $R\_{\mathrm{Lakatos}}(E\_T, k) \to 0$ and $\Delta W\_{\mathrm{dB}}(k) \to -\infty$, which
bankrupts the programme's warrant regardless of its in-sample fit.

## 4. Deborah Mayo's Error-Statistical Severe Testing

Consider two validation audits of a storm-surge model. The first tunes many free parameters on historical tide
gauges until retrospective error falls below a chosen tolerance and calls the model "validated", a procedure
guaranteed to find a fit even if the physical core were false. The second locks every parameter before the season
opens and tests against an out-of-sample critical refutation bar $d\_{\mathrm{crit}}$ where failure is a live,
observable event.

Deborah Mayo (1996/2018) operationalized scientific warrant through the **severity principle** (Mayo 2018):

> Data $\mathbf{x}$ provide severe evidence for hypothesis $H$ if and only if:
>
> 1. $\mathbf{x}$ accords with $H$ (good fit); and
> 2. With high probability, the test procedure would have produced a result discordant with $H$, had $H$ been false.

Severity demands that a model pass a test that had a genuine capacity to break it. A pre-registered falsifier with
minimum relevant effect $\delta\_{\min}$, paired-difference dispersion $\sigma\_d$, and effective sample size
$n\_{\mathrm{eff}}$ sets the critical refutation threshold at:

$$d\_{\mathrm{crit}} = \delta\_{\min} - z\_\alpha\mkern3mu\mathrm{SE}, \qquad \mathrm{SE} = \frac{\sigma\_d}{\sqrt{n\_{\mathrm{eff}}}},$$

The claim is refuted whenever the observed effect satisfies $d\_{\mathrm{obs}} \le d\_{\mathrm{crit}}$, with
post-data severity $\mathrm{SEV} = \Phi\bigl((\delta\_{\min} - d\_{\mathrm{obs}})/\mathrm{SE}\bigr)$.

Two ends bound the test. As $d\_{\mathrm{obs}} \to 0$ with $d\_{\mathrm{crit}} > 0$, the ratio
$d\_{\mathrm{obs}}/d\_{\mathrm{crit}} \to 0$ and refutation is immediate. Where $\sigma\_d$ grows until
$\mathrm{SE} > \delta\_{\min}/z\_\alpha$, $d\_{\mathrm{crit}}$ falls below zero and no positive observation can
refute the claim. Raising the effective sample size at fixed dispersion lowers $\mathrm{SE}$, *raises*
$d\_{\mathrm{crit}}$, and sharpens the refutation ratio $d\_{\mathrm{obs}}/d\_{\mathrm{crit}}$. More statistical
power makes refutation easier, never harder.

## 5. Epistemic Injustice and Testimonial Silencing

Science and institutional decision-making are human social practices, vulnerable to systemic distortions and to
institutional selection for low-power false-positive novelty (Smaldino & McElreath 2016). Miranda Fricker (2007)
identified two modalities of **epistemic injustice**.

In **testimonial injustice**, an observer's testimony receives a credibility deficit owing to prejudice against their
social identity, status, or organisational position. On the eve of the Space Shuttle *Challenger* launch
(27 January 1986), frontline engineers accurately warned of O-ring embrittlement in freezing temperatures. Management
discounted the warning as "unscientific" and asked them to take off their engineering hat and put on their management
hat. In **hermeneutical injustice**, a community lacks the conceptual, linguistic, or statistical resources to make a
collective harm intelligible, which prevents victims from rendering their experience communicative.

An institution that silences frontline warnings commits an ethical violation and blinds its own sensors. The
frontline operator is the sensor located closest to physical friction and vital reserve depletion. A frontline
sensor's diagnostic weight is measured in decibans by $W = 10\log\_{10}(\mathrm{TPR}/\mathrm{FPR})$, and its
positive predictive value at base rate $\pi$ is
$\mathrm{PPV} = \pi\mkern3mu\mathrm{TPR} / \bigl(\pi\mkern3mu\mathrm{TPR} + (1 - \pi)\mathrm{FPR}\bigr)$.
Discounting that testimony destroys early warning capacity before executive governance can halt a trajectory toward
ruin.

### What a severe test costs

A programme proves itself progressive by surviving tests it could have failed. Four pre-registered falsifiers
([Methodological Families, Process Tracing, and Reflexivity](50-methodological-families-reflexivity.md)) are declared
before the data arrive. Three survive with severity near one, and one fails: an observed effect below its critical
threshold at a small effective sample, with a near-certain chance of detection had it been real. The threshold moves
with power. At the same dispersion, a larger effective sample lifts the critical bar, so a refutation is a property
of the triple (effect, dispersion, power) and never of the effect alone.

Underdetermination is beaten by invariance rather than by more argument. The physical predictor's slope holds
across environments while the spurious proxy swings, and intersecting the sets that survive every environment narrows
the candidates rather than ratifying the whole archive.

The price of rescue is explicit. An evidential value divided by $k+1$ ad hoc adjustments falls by the factor
$k + 1$. The cold-fusion announcement of 23 March 1989 accumulated more than a dozen such adjustments within a year,
losing most of its evidential value without a single new measurement (Fleischmann & Pons 1989; Lakatos 1970).

### When no test can be severe

The thesis fails when the effective sample is so small that no test can reach the declared power. The verdict is
then "not testable", neither acceptance nor rejection. Declare the insufficiency, name the collection that would fix
it, and postpone the verdict. The falsifier returns exactly that third state, so a small sample cannot be read as
either answer.
