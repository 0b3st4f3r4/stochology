> Bridging ethics and institutional design: what makes a collective decision legitimate, auditable,
> contestable inside human attention limits, and bound against irreversible ruin.

## By the end of this chapter you can

1. Separate procedural and constitutional legitimacy—including principal-agent splits, commons rules, *potentia* versus *potestas*, and non-export across external reception perimeters—from lucky outcomes and bare legality.
2. State the three-part conjunction of accountability with its anti-closure safeguard, and explain why non-domination requires contestability and hermeneutical openness over formal votes alone.
3. Explain how status discounting and committee delays stretch recovery latency past survival time, and state what legitimacy requires when prior contestation fails.

## 1. Legitimacy Versus Correctness

Consider two public water utilities that both get through a dry summer without rationing. The first utility kept no published reserve schedule, ignored gauge drift, and gambled on late-August rain two days before intake pipes ran dry. The second followed a pre-registered drawdown schedule, posted spring trigger thresholds publicly, and maintained an auditable emergency reserve. After one mild season ($T = 1$), both balance sheets look identical. Over repeated decades ($T \to \infty$), the first hits ruin on the first multi-year drought while the second survives forecast misses and keeps public trust.

Institutions fail systematically because decision-makers rarely bear the same costs as the public they serve. In **principal-agent relationships**, citizens and downstream users (the *principals*) delegate power to managers and regulators (the *agents*), whose pay, bonuses, or promotions often reward cutting visible costs today while hiding slow fragility. That same split drives **commons dilemmas** (Ostrom 1990): each competing unit captures immediate savings by overdrawing a shared aquifer, pasture, or reserve buffer, while everyone shares the eventual collapse—unless binding rules enforce monitoring and fair sanctions.

Lucky correctness mimics real competence only briefly. Max Weber (1922) classified three sociological grounds for obedience (*Herrschaft*): traditional, charismatic, and rational-legal. Yet formal statutory legality can still operate purely from the top down without citizen voice. Normative **procedural legitimacy** under uncertainty demands three stronger standards: rules declared publicly *before* outcomes arrive, two-way accountability between deciders and affected residents, and premises kept open to refutation.

When institutions organize collective self-power (*potentia*) through an obediential mandate rather than alienated command from above (*potestas*; drawing on Spinoza's *Theological-Political Treatise* via Negri 1981 and Dussel 2008—while rejecting closed *more geometrico* substance monism that would collapse concrete plurality into a single deductive axiom; Spinoza 2007 [1670], chs. 16–17), rules tie directly to shared survival. Authorities that hoard internal cushions while pushing risk onto outsiders decay into sovereign capture ([Aim and the Three Planes](29-aim-and-the-three-planes.md); [Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md)). Internal approval from a domestic board never justifies dumping harm onto unrepresented **external reception perimeters** whose tolerance margin defines a **reception basin** examined across [Chapter 19](19-ethics-and-the-imposition-of-risk.md) and Part III ([Chapter 40](40-institution-incentive-metric.md))—and no institution may certify its own innocence. Across those perimeters, legitimacy enforces the refusals of the [Prologue](00-preface.md) and [Chapter 27](27-what-judging-is.md): no metric surveillance over how warnings are received, no scapegoating or pact betrayal, and no covert premise-rigging.

Whether a rule actually binds depends on its constitutional rank. As [Evolution, Variation, and Selection](25-evolution-variation-selection.md) proves, whenever unbuffered rivals escape full tail penalties while prudent stewards pay reserve costs, calm competition strips out prudence before storms arrive. Any rule that competing actors can vote away stays inside the game and gets repealed during quiet booms. Only an overarching constitutional floor that charges full tail risk flips the competitive differential so reserves endure ([The Triad and the Minimum](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md)). Thus the governance coordinate is **not one reading among three**: it fixes the sign of the competitive differential on which the other margins are priced. Rules written below the units they regulate express wishes, not constraints.

Rules also hollow out without formal repeal. Gramsci's (1971) **hegemony** shows manufactured consent securing silent compliance, echoing Ibn Khaldūn's *Muqaddimah* on how fiscal extraction erodes solidarity (*ʿaṣabiyya*) and strips shared reserves long before laws fall (Rosenthal 1958). Miranda Fricker (2007) named **hermeneutical injustice**—lacking shared concepts to name one's injury—which with Paulo Freire's (1970) pedagogy critique and Silvia Rivera Cusicanqui's (2012) *ch'ixi* contestation means bearers must help co-author inquiry frames under *infidélité du milieu* (Canguilhem 1943/1966; [Chapter 29](29-aim-and-the-three-planes.md); [Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md)). Even Hobbes (1651, ch. 21) released subjects once protection fails, and Spinoza (2007 [1670], ch. 17) showed power never transfers wholly: perpetual emergency spends the institution's warrant.

## 2. The Triad of Institutional Accountability

After a flood-gate failure, an inquiry asks for the risk-model logs. Auditors discover administrators quietly changed the warning weights after the storm without leaving a trail, while firing a junior operator for "human error."

"Accountability" remains an empty word unless built as a formal relationship between an actor and an independent forum (Bovens 2007). Using binary indicators ($1$ if present, $0$ if missing), accountability combines three non-substitutable components whose product equals their minimum—never a weighted score trading one element for another:

$$\mathrm{Accountability} = \mathrm{Observability} \times \mathrm{Authority} \times \mathrm{Consequence}, \qquad \mathrm{Observability},\mkern3mu \mathrm{Authority},\mkern3mu \mathrm{Consequence} \in \lbrace 0, 1\rbrace.$$

Read aloud, accountability equals $1$ only when all three ingredients hold simultaneously, and collapses to $0$ whenever any single link is missing:

1. **Observability**: Decision logs, assumptions, data vintages, warnings, and refusals must be open to independent inspection *before* outcomes arrive—though pre-outcome archival sealing verifies timing only after the seal, never pre-seal omissions or rigged premises ([Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md)).
2. **Authority**: The oversight forum or affected community must hold real power to halt, quarantine, or overturn choices, not merely write polite memos.
3. **Consequence**: Real sanctions or restructuring must reach deciders who ignore evidence, drain buffers, or silence alarms.

If observability fails, punishment turns into scapegoating; if authority fails, hearings turn into suggestion boxes; if consequence fails, bad actors shrug off rules. Yet scoring $(1, 1, 1)$ is necessary without being enough when organizations normalize small rule waivers into disaster (Vaughan 1996; [Chapter 40](40-institution-incentive-metric.md)). Accountability therefore requires a fourth anti-closure check: independent reserve monitors, power to reopen waivers, and non-self-extending sunsets (§3).

School data confirm that supportive voice alone cannot substitute for accountability on standardized outcomes. Across 545 New York City public schools (Higgins et al. 2022) where observed English attainment fell from $86\unicode{x25}$ in 2007–08 to $69\unicode{x25}$ by 2010–11, net of felt accountability and controls (Model 2) psychological safety predicted *lower* target-attainment log-odds ($\gamma = -2.89$, $p < .01$) while felt accountability predicted *higher* log-odds ($\gamma = 3.52$, $p < .01$). Under that model, implied attainment for high-safety, low-accountability climates dropped from $73\unicode{x25}$ to $35\unicode{x25}$—a distraction spiral the authors call a `"complaining zone"`—while moderate-low safety ($2.6$ on a 1-to-4 scale) paired with high accountability stayed close to $100\unicode{x25}$ (versus $71\unicode{x25}$ high/high and $61\unicode{x25}$ low/low). Accountability must steer between short-term `"moral corruption"` that sacrifices the future (Gardiner 2023) and Black's (2008) `"accountability trilemma"`, where tangled mandates end up ignored, co-opted, or `"destroy that which it is they seek to make accountable"`.

## 3. Philip Pettit's Contestability and Non-Domination

Philip Pettit (1997) defined republican freedom as **non-domination**: structural protection against arbitrary power, rather than hoping rulers stay kind. Its everyday shield is **contestability**. An institutional policy is formally contestable **only if**:

- Affected communities receive the exact evidence and decision rules before execution;
- Bearers can appeal to an independent forum applying zero status discounting—judging claims on evidence rather than rank—while triaging alerts inside human attention limits (Black 2008);
- Valid challenges compel reversal and repair, and an independent audit verifies no harm spills onto external reception perimeters.

Formal appeal rules still fail unless grounded in hermeneutical openness when framing inquiry ([Aim and the Three Planes](29-aim-and-the-three-planes.md)) so harmed people have words to state their grievance (§1). Moreover, Christian List and Philip Pettit (2002, Theorem 1; 2011) proved the **discursive dilemma**: separate majority votes on individual premises can endorse a decision whose logical conjunction the group rejects overall. Avoiding that incoherence demands a constitutional conjunctive veto backed by corroborated standing ([Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md))—a safeguard akin in spirit to Kwasi Wiredu's (1996) Akan consensual democracy, which refuses to override harmed minorities by raw headcounts. Where statutory duties themselves clash in genuine antinomies, paraconsistent deontic logic (da Costa & Carnielli 1986; [Layer 32](82-layer-evidentiary-tort-constitutional-legal-order.md)) isolates the conflict for public contestation rather than licensing deontic explosion or unilateral decree.

Even structured deliberation hits practical ceilings. In a New Haven Deliberative Poll ($n = 132$; Farrar et al. 2010), knowledge (proportion correct on a $0$–$1$ scale) rose `"34.5 per cent"` ($0.254$ to $0.599$, $p < 0.01$; single-peakedness $0.52$ to $0.80$) on low-salience revenue sharing, versus `"15.5 per cent"` ($0.386$ to $0.542$, $p < 0.01$; $0.77$ to $0.81$) on airport expansion. Yet biased frames skew equality (Steel et al. 2020), Fishkin (2020) proves a trilemma separating equality, deliberation, and mass scale, and Mansbridge et al. (2012) show disconnected forums drift into domination. Goodin & Dryzek (2006) likewise show minipublics move slowly (160 citizens, 50 hearings, 6 weekends in British Columbia) and remain exposed to pre-emption (Australia's October 1999 poll).

Prior contestability does admit a **bounded emergency exception**. During a sudden payments freeze shorter than any hearing window, a central bank may inject liquidity under strict constitutional precommitment (Elster 1979): independent reserve monitors, automatic non-self-extending sunsets, and post-crisis audit reopening with clawbacks. Precommitment fails whenever monopoly coercion lets leaders silence monitors or extend emergency powers indefinitely. Custody and oversight therefore require federated counter-power, revocable delegates ([Prologue](00-preface.md); Proudhon 1863; Bakunin 1873; Ostrom 1990), and §1's residual refusal when protection fails.

Criticizing alienated *potestas* does not license abolishing formal rules altogether. As Jo Freeman (1972) demonstrated in *The Tyranny of Structurelessness*, stripping away explicit procedures inside movements or large organizations merely hands decision power to opaque, unaccountable informal cliques. Under bounded human attention near ten bits per second (Zheng & Meister 2025) and organizational decision premises (Simon 1997; Ostrom 1990), complex coordination cannot dispense with formal mediation; instead, Stochology **desacralizes** the formal frame—keeping every $\mathcal{F}\_t$ provisional, bitemporally sealed, revocable through attested counter-inductive reopening ($\mathcal{F}\_t \subsetneq \mathcal{F}\_{t+1}^\ast$), bounded by survival clocks ($\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}} \le \mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}}$), and falsifiable *ex post* under pre-registered tests ($F\_1$–$F\_5$).

Across mutual-aid networks, counter-power covers **community data sovereignty and non-confiscatory exit**. Record legibility never authorizes external seizure: the CARE Principles—Collective Benefit, Authority to Control, Responsibility, and Ethics (Carroll et al. 2020)—and Free, Prior, and Informed Consent (`FPIC`) bind extraction, while federated constitutionalism (Kropotkin 1902; Ostrom 1990) lets dissenters leave without losing reserves or archival access, linking incentives ([Chapter 40](40-institution-incentive-metric.md)) to polycentric commons ([Chapter 85](85-layer-polycentric-commons-urban-biospheric-order.md)).

## 4. Testimonial Injustice and the Algedonic Bypass

A plant technician spots a cooling-pump pressure drop early enough that immediate shutdown would beat core damage. Instead, layered approval committees stretch effective recovery time past remaining survival time. The warning arrives on time, and dies politely in review.

In cybernetics, Geoffrey Vickers (1965) explained institutional regulation as appreciative judgment over a whole situation—fact judgments alongside value judgments—before parts are split apart, warning that rigid bureaucratic filters arrive too late for fast physical breakdowns. Stafford Beer (1972) diagnosed why tall hierarchies suppress bad news: each layer polishes problems away. Beer built two safeguards against upward filtering: **sporadic direct audits** of frontline operations bypassing middle management, and the **algedonic bypass**—an emergency pain channel straight from the floor to policy oversight ([Chapter 40](40-institution-incentive-metric.md); [Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md)). Whenever Miranda Fricker's (2007) **testimonial injustice** discounts frontline warnings by rank, detection latency stretches until even early alerts miss the survival clock ([Chapter 47](47-irreversible-time-vital-margin.md)).

Removing status bias never eliminates cognitive triage: because appeal forums (§3), overlapping monitors (Black 2008), and plant alarms share finite attention, Part IV joins §3's rank-blind intake with pre-authorized sentinels, corroborated off-shift triage, and protected unsafe-work refusal ([Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md)).

Neither direct audits nor pain channels work when reporting ruins the reporter (Near & Miceli 1996). Permanent staff risk social isolation despite firing rules, while subcontracted, temporary, and trainee workers closest to physical spills face quiet silencing via non-renewal—turning *potentia* (§1) back into domination (*potestas*; [Chapter 40](40-institution-incentive-metric.md)). Non-domination (§3) therefore joins federated pre-termination warning records with imminent-danger work refusal, independent external channels (unions, inspectorates, ombudsmen), and bounded blameless reviews that protect observability (§2) without excusing measured negligence.

Everyday workplace norms rely on those shields. Under heavy caseloads and conflicting quotas, `"street-level bureaucrats"` normalize silence, and training washes out unless working conditions change (Lipsky 1969). Across 41 clinical-year medical students (Stephens et al. 2022), one student doubted themselves during a confident senior's wrong-patient consult, while removing summative assessments and modeling senior intellectual candor opened uncertainty talk—alongside reflection-driven rumination and regret requiring guided support. Once retaliation is bounded, three habits sustain candor: bridging specialist boundaries while protecting dissent (Edmondson & Harvey 2017; Steel et al. 2021), auditing near-misses regardless of luck (Christianson et al. 2011), and tying reflection directly to action plans (Schippers et al. 2014).

## 5. Hans Jonas's Precautionary Imperative and Irreversible Barriers

In *The Imperative of Responsibility* (1979/1984), Hans Jonas framed the ethical ceiling on technological power:

> *"Act so that the effects of your action are compatible with the permanence of genuine human life."*

Modern systems can cross **irreversible ruin**—an absorbing collapse (reserves $R\_t \le 0$) where cost–benefit averages fail. Survival depends on sizing vital buffers ([Chapter 47](47-irreversible-time-vital-margin.md)) across two regimes. In the **buffered regime**, survival time meets or exceeds effective recovery time, leaving room for contestation and repair before reserves hit zero. In the **exhausted-slack limit**, effective recovery time exceeds survival time, so ruin arrives before rescue regardless of good intentions.

### Legitimacy does not buy latency

When remaining survival time allows sentinel detection yet falls shorter than committee-delayed recovery time, democratic votes arrive after collapse. Still, tight clocks never license a self-certified emergency trap where one office squeezes buffers and waves its own exceptions through. Fast overrides remain legitimate only under §3's constitutional precommitment: independent clock monitors, non-self-extending sunsets, post-crisis audit reopening, and fiduciary clawback ([Chapter 50](50-methodological-families-reflexivity.md)).

Survival slack minus recovery time counts physical hours, while votes count authorization; on an overloaded network, negative slack forces binding upstream caps before crisis strikes. Discursive aggregation (§3; [Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md)) adds a parallel check: itemized committee minutes conceal premise-majority contradictions unless joint safety verdicts sit beside premise tallies.

### Precaution without paralysis

Critics object that precaution freezes action whenever hazards sit on both sides. Well-designed rules avoid paralysis three ways. First, van Herten and Bovenkerk (2021) pair `'precaution-as-preparedness'` with `'precaution-as-prevention'`, demanding minimal scientific backing against speculative alarms alongside consistency, non-counterproductivity, and comparative proportionality; Nordgren (2023) adds mandatory decision revision as evidence emerges under practicability, non-arbitrariness, proportionality, and justification. Second, Sunstein (2010) rejects passive wait-and-learn delay even when irreversibility sits on both sides, while Birch (2017) warns that waiting for overwhelming consensus can carry high cost. Third, pairing constitutional reserve floors ([Chapter 25](25-evolution-variation-selection.md)) with fast frontline bypasses (§4) checks present institutions' weakness at guarding future lives (Gardiner 2023) without stalling crises in committee.

### When contestation before the fact is impossible

Ex-ante contestation fails during an irreversible emergency with certain victims, when delay is fatal before hearings can sit. Irreversible loss cannot be "paid back" afterward. Legitimacy then requires a pre-declared barrier, automatic sunset, post-event audit reopening, restitution where harms can be redressed, and structural reform blocking unconsented export onto external reception perimeters. Following Hannah Arendt's (1958) account of irreversible action and promise-keeping, recording every uncompensated remainder keeps the lack of prior consent permanently visible rather than buried in silence.
