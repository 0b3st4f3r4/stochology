## 1. Coarse-Graining and Emergent Regularity

Consider a dispatcher watching thousands of households switch appliances on and off. At the scale of a single
household demand jumps erratically; across an uncorrelated city the aggregate load curve is smooth and lawlike. A
macroscopic container holds on the order of Avogadro's number ($N \sim 10^{23}$) of molecules, and tracking each
deterministic trajectory is both impossible and unnecessary: pressure, volume, temperature and entropy are emergent
properties of a statistical **coarse-graining**.

Coarse-graining partitions the continuous $6N$-dimensional phase space $\Gamma$ into finite macroscopic cells
$\Delta \Gamma\_k$ of normalised phase-space volume
$\Omega\_k = \operatorname{Vol}(\Delta \Gamma\_k) / h\_0^{3N}$. Under the **Boltzmann–Gibbs canonical ensemble**
(Boltzmann 1877), when a system exchanges heat with a thermal bath at absolute temperature $T > 0$ (in kelvins,
$\text{K}$), the equilibrium probability $p\_i \in (0, 1)$ of occupying a specific microstate $i$ of energy
$E\_i \in \mathbb{R}$ (in joules, $\text{J}$) is governed by the Boltzmann factor:

$$p\_i = \frac{1}{Z(T)} e^{-E\_i / (k\_B T)}, \qquad Z(T) = \sum\_{i} e^{-E\_i / (k\_B T)},$$

where $k\_B$ is Boltzmann's constant and $Z(T) > 0$ is the canonical **partition function**. Grouping all
$\Omega(E)$ microstates that share macroscopic energy $E$ yields the coarse-grained macrostate probability
$P(E) = Z(T)^{-1} \Omega(E) e^{-E/(k\_B T)} = Z(T)^{-1} \exp\bigl((S(E) - E/T)/k\_B\bigr)$.

The Second Law of Thermodynamics emerges because the combinatorial multiplicity $\Omega(E) = e^{S(E)/k\_B}$ of the
equilibrium macrostate overwhelmingly dominates the accessible phase space. That is a foundational scientific
principle: **lawlike regularity can emerge at an aggregate level of description without existing in any individual
part**.

## 2. The Ergodic Hypothesis and Its Systemic Breakdown

Equilibrium statistical mechanics relies on the **ergodic hypothesis**, formulated by Ludwig Boltzmann and
J. Willard Gibbs (Boltzmann 1877; Peters 2019). For a stationary system that mixes rapidly across an invariant
phase-space measure $\mu$, the infinite-horizon time average of an observable $f: \Gamma \to \mathbb{R}$ along a
single trajectory equals the spatial average across an ensemble of parallel copies:

$$\lim\_{T \to \infty} \frac{1}{T} \int\_0^T f\bigl(\mathbf{x}(t)\bigr)\mkern3mudt = \int\_\Gamma f(\mathbf{x})\mkern3mud\mu(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbb{E}\_{\mathrm{ensemble}}[f].$$

Whenever the ergodic hypothesis holds, an analyst may legitimately substitute a cross-sectional average across
hypothetical parallel worlds for the sequential experience of a single entity through time.

In institutional life, finance, and ecology, **ergodicity breaks down through three structural mechanisms**:

1. **Absorbing ruin barriers**: Gas molecules in a vessel cannot go bankrupt or die. In biological and organisational systems, crossing the ruin boundary ($R\_t \le 0$) is an absorbing state: the trajectory terminates permanently. An ensemble average across many ventures, of which almost all fail and one achieves astronomical wealth, yields a high positive expected value, yet a single institution following that policy faces an overwhelming probability of extinction. Once an unbuffered trajectory hits $R\_t \le 0$ its state freezes there for the remainder of the horizon.
2. **Multiplicative compounding and the Itô variance drain**: A reserve or endowment $R\_t > 0$ compounds sequentially via $dR\_t / R\_t = \mu\mkern3mudt + \sigma\mkern3mudW\_t$ with ensemble drift $\mu \in \mathbb{R}$ and volatility $\sigma > 0$, so the ensemble mean grows at rate $\mu$ ($\mathbb{E}[R\_T] = R\_0 e^{\mu T}$). Applying Itô's formula to $\ln R\_t$ (Itô 1951; Peters 2019) proves that the single-trajectory time-average growth rate is strictly smaller:

$$g = \lim\_{T \to \infty} \frac{1}{T}\ln\left(\frac{R\_T}{R\_0}\right) = \mu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 < \mu.$$

3. **Historical path dependency and non-stationarity**: Even for simple arithmetic time averages, expanding the observation window $T$ does not produce convergence to a fixed ensemble limit when the underlying historical regime shifts. The long daily record of an equity index is the standard witness: the arithmetic mean return computed over its earliest decade and the mean over its latest decade differ by a factor of order two, and the running cumulative mean does not settle as the window grows, wandering with the composition of the index and with the monetary regime behind it. The ensemble drift over the whole record is itself eroded to a smaller time-average growth by the Itô variance drain of the same series. Waiting for a longer window never turns a non-ergodic, regime-shifting trajectory into a stationary gas.

## 3. Scale, Correlation, and the Resurgence of Fluctuations

For $N$ independent and identically distributed microscopic parts, the relative fluctuation around the aggregate mean
shrinks with the inverse square root of system size:

$$\frac{\sigma\_N}{\mu\_N} \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}.$$

For a mole-scale gas, $N$ is Avogadro's number and the relative fluctuation is of order $1/\sqrt{N}$, small enough
that macroscopic pressure is virtually deterministic.

In infrastructural and financial networks, by contrast, the unit count $m$ is modest and components are coupled by
common shocks. Given $m \ge 1$ units with common variance $\sigma^2 > 0$ and pairwise correlation $\rho \in [0, 1]$,
the variance of the sample average $\bar{X}\_m = \frac{1}{m}\sum\_{i=1}^m X\_i$ is:

$$\mathrm{Var}(\bar{X}\_m) = \frac{\sigma^2}{m} + \frac{m - 1}{m}\rho\sigma^2 \longrightarrow \rho\sigma^2 \quad \text{as } m \to \infty.$$

Two explicit cases expose why naive diversification fails under coupling:

1. **Uncorrelated units ($\rho \to 0^+$):** The coupling term $\frac{m-1}{m}\rho\sigma^2$ vanishes identically and
$\mathrm{Var}(\bar{X}\_m) = \sigma^2 / m \to 0$ as $m \to \infty$, recovering classical $1/\sqrt{m}$
law-of-large-numbers smoothing.
2. **Lockstep units ($\rho \to 1^-$) and the large-network floor ($m \to \infty$ at fixed $\rho > 0$):** As
$\rho \to 1^-$, $\mathrm{Var}(\bar{X}\_m) = \frac{\sigma^2}{m} + \frac{m-1}{m}\sigma^2 = \sigma^2$ for every
$m \ge 1$: pooling $m$ perfectly correlated units reduces volatility by nothing at all. At any positive correlation
$\rho > 0$, letting $m \to \infty$ leaves an irreducible variance floor $\rho\sigma^2 > 0$, so a large portfolio of
correlated units converges to the volatility of one unit shrunk by the square root of the correlation coefficient ($\sqrt{\rho}\mkern2mu\sigma$) rather than to zero.

When correlation acts across time via lag-1 autocorrelation
$\rho\_1 = \exp(-\lambda\_{\mathrm{eng}}\Delta t) \in [0, 1)$, consecutive observations share memory over an
**integrated autocorrelation time** $\tau\_{\mathrm{corr}} = \frac{1 + \rho\_1}{1 - \rho\_1}$, reducing $n$ nominal
time steps to an effective sample size $n\_{\mathrm{eff}} = n / \tau\_{\mathrm{corr}}$. The engineering recovery
horizon scales as the logarithmic decay time of that memory,
$\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{recovery}} = -\frac{\kappa \Delta t}{\ln \rho\_1}$, approaching
$\tau\_{\mathrm{corr}} \to 1$ as $\rho\_1 \to 0^+$ and diverging to $+\infty$ as $\rho\_1 \to 1^-$.

Clustered tail losses behave the same way further out in the tail. A tail design effect above one divides the count
of nominal exceedances by that factor, so a plug-in rupture estimate computed on the nominal count understates the
tail probability by exactly the design effect, and its nominal interval is centred on a probability the record never
had.

## 4. C.S. Holling: Attraction Basins and Ecological Resilience

Picture a grid operator monitoring voltage stability during a multi-day heatwave. For seventy hours minor line trips
damp out smoothly, until one more transformer outage crosses an invisible threshold into a cascading blackout.
Crawford Stanley (C.S.) Holling (1973) formalised the distinction between local equilibrium damping and survival
across regime boundaries (Canguilhem 1966; Holling 1973; Scheffer et al. 2009).

**Engineering efficiency** optimises performance near a single steady-state equilibrium, stripping away spare
capacity and redundancy to maximise utilisation and fast local return ($\lambda\_{\mathrm{eng}}$). **Ecological
resilience** by contrast measures the width $D = x\_{\mathrm{high}}^\ast - x\_{\mathrm{tip}}$ and potential barrier
depth $\Delta U$ of the **basin of attraction**: the maximum shock the system can absorb before crossing a
separatrix $x\_{\mathrm{tip}}$ into an alternate, degraded regime.

The bistable resilience landscape is governed by the differential equation
$\dot{x} = f(x; h, K) = r x(1 - x/K) - h x^2 / (x^2 + s^2)$, where carrying capacity $K$ erodes under allostatic wear
and harvesting pressure $h$ drives the upper stable equilibrium $x\_{\mathrm{high}}^\ast$ toward the unstable fold
threshold $x\_{\mathrm{tip}}$. Under stochastic forcing of noise intensity $D\_{\mathrm{noise}} > 0$, **Kramers'
escape rate** across the potential barrier
$\Delta U = \int\_{x\_{\mathrm{tip}}}^{x\_{\mathrm{high}}^\ast} (-f(x))\mkern3mudx$ scales exponentially as:

$$k\_{\mathrm{escape}} \propto \exp\mkern-3mu\left(-\frac{\Delta U}{D\_{\mathrm{noise}}}\right).$$

When unbuffered utilisation shrinks the relative basin radius $D\_t / D\_0$ toward the boundary, the restoring
eigenvalue weakens with it. The worn **critical slowing down** autocorrelation rises toward one, the integrated
autocorrelation time lengthens by a comparable factor, and because the barrier-crossing hazard depends exponentially
on the barrier depth, $k\_{\mathrm{escape}}$ surges long before the basin width visibly collapses.

## 5. What Physics and Ecology Demand of Governance

Survival along a single irreversible trajectory requires governing for Holling basin resilience rather than
point-equilibrium efficiency:

1. Size physical reserves to protect the **distance to the basin boundary** ($D\_t / D\_0$), not merely the mean operating point;
2. Refuse ensemble expectations whenever absorbing ruin barriers or variance drains separate the time average from the ensemble average;
3. Monitor critical slowing down ($\rho\_1$) and ensure that autonomous survival slack exceeds recovery delay ($\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} \ge \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{recovery}}$) across extreme correlated ramps, of the kind a metered electricity record supplies when load autocorrelation stays close to one through a heatwave.

### Fluctuation, correlation, memory

Fluctuations shrink with the square root of the number of independent parts, and parts that remember each other are
not independent. For an ideal gas the particle count is Avogadro's number and the relative fluctuation is negligible.
Divide that count by a large correlation factor and the effective count falls by the same factor while the
fluctuation grows by its square root, so memory forms part of the mechanism rather than noise around it. The ensemble
average is the right object only when the system mixes on a timescale shorter than the window (Boltzmann 1877;
Peters 2019); when it does not, the average describes one trajectory.

In a metered load record the lag-one autocorrelation stays close to one through a heatwave, and the worn eigenvalue of
the assumed dynamics is greater under stress than with a buffer. The two capacity layouts, not one trajectory, then
carry basins of different width, and the worn layout's is the narrower of the two. Correlation time is what a plan
must outlive: it grows without bound as $\rho\_1 \to 1$, so a plan drawn against a mixing assumption is drawn against
the one regime the record excludes.

### When there is an invariant measure

The thesis fails when the system has an invariant measure and mixes quickly, because the ensemble average is then the
correct quantity and the time average converges to it. The condition is a mixing time short against the window. When
it is violated, report the correlation time, the basin and the distance to the barrier, and refuse a single
trajectory as evidence about the ensemble.
