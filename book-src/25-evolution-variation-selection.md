## 1. The Three Algorithmic Conditions of Selection

A hospital network ranks its clinics every year on one scalar scorecard. The top quarter receives extra budget, the bottom tenth closes, and incoming directors copy the routines of top scorers.

Natural selection (Darwin 1859)—later formalized into three canonical conditions—needs neither conscious design nor moral progress. It operates as an automatic filter whenever three conditions meet:

1. **Phenotypic variation**: Units differ in traits, routines, or structure ($z\_i \in \mathbb{R}$, non-zero spread).
2. **Differential fitness**: Variants differ in survival and reproduction rates ($w\_i \ge 0$) in their shared environment.
3. **Heritability**: Successful traits pass forward to descendants through genes, teaching, or operating manuals.

Before touching algebra, notice the simple bookkeeping rule behind evolutionary change. Across one generation, the average trait in a population can shift for only two reasons: either individuals with higher trait values leave more offspring (**selection**), or offspring fail to copy their parents identically (**transmission bias**, such as mutation or drift).

Price (1970) and Fisher (1930) turned that bookkeeping into an exact identity. Suppose a population of $n$ units carries traits $z\_i$ and fitness scores $w\_i \ge 0$ under uniform $1/n$ empirical weighting ($\mathbb{E}[f\_i] := \frac{1}{n}\sum\_{i=1}^n f\_i$, mean fitness $\bar{w} = \mathbb{E}[w\_i] > 0$), while offspring mean trait is $\bar{z}' := \mathbb{E}[w\_i z\_i']/\bar{w}$ and copying shifts satisfy $\Delta z\_i = z\_i' - z\_i$ (with convention $w\_i = 0 \implies w\_i \Delta z\_i := 0$ for any finite dummy $z\_i'$). The change in population mean $\Delta \bar{z} = \bar{z}' - \bar{z}$ satisfies **Price's covariance equation**:

$$\Delta \bar{z} = \frac{1}{\bar{w}}\mathrm{Cov}(w\_i, z\_i) + \frac{1}{\bar{w}}\mathbb{E}[w\_i \Delta z\_i].$$

Read aloud, total trait change equals the fitness–trait covariance divided by average fitness (the selection pull) plus the fitness-weighted average copying shift (transmission bias). Setting $z\_i = w\_i$ yields $\Delta \bar{w} = \mathrm{Var}(w\_i)/\bar{w} \ge 0$ whenever fitness-weighted transmission bias vanishes ($\mathbb{E}[w\_i \Delta w\_i] = 0$), which holds in particular under exact clonal copying in a fixed environment ($\Delta w\_i = 0$ everywhere, heritability $h\_w^2 = 1$ when $\mathrm{Var}(w\_i) > 0$). In Price's discrete covariance reconciliation of Fisher's (1930) **Fundamental Theorem of Natural Selection**, tracking partial change under a fixed environment and average excesses gives selection's additive contribution $V\_A(w)/\bar{w}$—obeying $V\_A(w)/\bar{w} \le \mathrm{Var}(w\_i)/\bar{w}$ under non-negative genotype–environment covariance ($\mathrm{Cov}(A, D + E) \ge 0$, typically assumed zero so environmental compensation never inflates $V\_A > V\_P$): when fitness differences are purely environmental ($h\_w^2 = 0$) with stationary mean fitness ($\bar{w}' = \bar{w}$), $\mathbb{E}[w\_i \Delta w\_i] = -\mathrm{Var}(w\_i)$ cancels the selection gain completely. Two limits bound this filter:

1. **Zero trait spread ($\mathrm{Var}(z\_i) = 0$):** By Cauchy–Schwarz, $\mathrm{Cov}(w\_i, z\_i) = 0$, halting selection no matter how intense the rewards.
2. **Fitness-neutral trait ($\mathrm{Cov}(w\_i, z\_i) = 0$):** Selection vanishes, leaving $\Delta \bar{z} = \frac{1}{\bar{w}}\mathbb{E}[w\_i \Delta z\_i]$, pure transmission drift.

Annual rankings, bonuses, and closures supply all three ingredients mechanically, reshaping workplace habits whether managers intend it or not.

## 2. Fitness Landscapes, Bet-Hedging, and Canalization

Sewall Wright (1932) pictured evolving populations climbing hills on **fitness landscapes**. Blind hill-climbing faces two built-in limits: **local-optimum entrapment** (a population cannot cross a fitness valley to reach a taller peak, trapping mediocre routines) and **pleiotropic trade-offs** (pushing one trait—milk yield or sprint speed—quietly weakens immunity, bone density, or lifespan).

In fluctuating environments, multiplicative reproduction $N\_T = N\_0 \prod\_{t=1}^T W\_t$ enforces **evolutionary bet-hedging** (Seger & Brockmann 1987). Any strain that multiplies fast in calm seasons yet hits zero in one harsh year ($\mathbb{P}(W\_t = 0) > 0$) scores a high arithmetic average yet suffers $\mathbb{E}[\ln W\_t] = -\infty$ and certain extinction. Long-run persistence maximizes logarithmic path growth $g = \mathbb{E}[\ln W\_t]$ (Seger & Brockmann 1987; Peters 2019). Strictly inside positive outcomes with finite variance and small bounded relative excursions ($|W\_t - \bar{W}| \ll \bar{W}$ around mean $\bar{W}$, preventing rare near-zero shocks from inflating the logarithmic remainder), second-order expansion gives:

$$g = \mathbb{E}[\ln W\_t] \approx \ln \bar{W} - \frac{\sigma\_W^2}{2 \bar{W}^2}.$$

Read aloud, sustainable logarithmic growth equals the logarithm of average payoff minus a variance penalty that grows with squared volatility. Desert annual plants hedge via dormant seeds. By contrast, Northwest Atlantic cod fisheries maximized short-term catches and stripped older spawning fish, eroding age-structure buffers against bad recruitment years until the 1992 fishery collapse (Hutchings & Myers 1994).

Developmental buffering links evolution back to [Chemical Rates, Equilibrium, and Buffers](24-chemical-rates-equilibrium-buffers.md). Conrad Waddington (1957) showed how stabilizing selection carves **canalization** pathways (*chreods*, originally spelled *creodes*), explicitly warning that the epigenetic landscape is a visual heuristic rather than a strict energy surface. Deep valleys cushion minor genetic or environmental noise, while shallow ridges break under heavy shock. Heat-shock proteins like $\text{Hsp90}$ mask hidden variation until extreme stress overflows the ridge and exposes latent defects at once (Rutherford & Lindquist 1998). Because natural selection updates norms slowly by culling whole organisms across generations, living individuals and institutions rely on Canguilhem's (1943/1966) within-lifetime flexibility ([Chapter 24](24-chemical-rates-equilibrium-buffers.md); [Regulation and Homeostasis](26-regulation-and-homeostasis.md)).

## 3. Motoo Kimura and Neutral Genetic Drift

Change over time does not prove improvement. Motoo Kimura's (1968) **neutral theory** showed that most molecular (nucleotide or amino-acid) substitutions are selectively neutral or nearly neutral and fix through random **genetic drift** in finite populations (Kimura 1968).

Consider a diploid population of constant census count $N \ge 1$ and effective breeding size $N\_e > 0$ (which shrinks below $N$ during bottlenecks, skewed breeding, or uneven family sizes; the census-$N$ vs. effective-$N\_e$ split generalizes Kimura's 1968 constant-size substitution identity). Random binomial sampling across generations creates allele-frequency variance $\mathrm{Var}(\Delta p) = p(1 - p) / (2N\_e)$. A fresh neutral mutation in one of $2N$ exchangeable neutral gene copies starts at $p\_0 = 1/(2N)$ and spreads to fixation purely by chance with probability $u(p\_0) = 1/(2N)$—matching $1/(2N\_e)$ only when $N\_e = N$, and canceling census size in the neutral substitution rate $k = 2N\mu \cdot \frac{1}{2N} = \mu$. Selection steers evolution only when selective advantage dwarfs drift ($|s| \gg 1/(2N\_e)$); when $|s| \ll 1/(2N\_e)$, luck governs which variant wins (Kimura 1968). Note the split: fixation probability scales with $1/(2N)$, while per-generation drift noise scales with $1/(2N\_e)$.

In small organizations, administrative drift mimics genetic drift. Routine staff turnover randomly fixes arbitrary buzzwords and rituals that future leaders later praise as battle-tested wisdom.

## 4. W. Ross Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety

W. Ross Ashby (1956) established a fundamental cybernetic limit on control in *An Introduction to Cybernetics*, the **Law of Requisite Variety**:

> *"only variety in R can force down the variety due to D; variety can destroy variety."*

Consider a regulator confronting finite non-empty sets ($1 \le |D|, |R|, |O| < \infty$) of outside disturbances $D$, response repertoire $R$, and outcomes $O$ via payoff table $\Phi : D \times R \to O$, where logarithmic variety $\mathcal{V} = \log\_2 |S\_{\mathrm{set}}|$ counts binary distinctions in a finite set $S\_{\mathrm{set}}$. Suppose every fixed response $r \in R$ keeps different disturbances separate—no passive shielding maps two disturbances to one outcome (under $k$-fold overlap, the bound relaxes by $\log\_2 k$; Ashby 1956; [Aim and the Three Planes](29-aim-and-the-three-planes.md)). Ashby's law bounds outcome variety $\mathcal{V}\_O$ against disturbance variety $\mathcal{V}\_D$, regulator variety $\mathcal{V}\_R$, and tolerable outcome spread $\mathcal{V}\_O^{\mathrm{tolerable}}$:

$$\mathcal{V}\_O \ge \mathcal{V}\_D - \mathcal{V}\_R, \qquad \mathcal{V}\_R \ge \mathcal{V}\_D - \mathcal{V}\_O^{\mathrm{tolerable}}.$$

Read aloud, outcome scatter can never drop below disturbance variety minus regulator variety; holding outcomes inside a tolerable band demands at least as much regulator variety as the gap between incoming shocks and acceptable outcomes. Script $\mathcal{V}\_O, \mathcal{V}\_D, \mathcal{V}\_R$ denote Ashby logarithmic state counts (distinct from phenotypic variance $V\_P$ and Keynesian weight $V$ in [What Judging Is, and What It Is Not](27-what-judging-is.md) and [Probability as Language](30-probability-as-language.md)). Variety alone is necessary, not sufficient: Roger Conant and Ashby (1970) proved that every good regulator $R$ of a system $S\_{\mathrm{sys}}$ must encode an internal model $h : S\_{\mathrm{sys}} \to R$ (here map $h$ is distinct from §1 and §5's heritability $h\_w^2, h^2$), failing whenever rigid central scripts erase local perception or discretion ([Aim and the Three Planes](29-aim-and-the-three-planes.md)).

Human attention tightens that limit further. Against roughly $10^9\text{ bits/s}$ of raw sensory input, conscious human decision bandwidth saturates near $10\text{ bits/s}$ (Zheng & Meister 2025). Over any decision window $\Delta t > 0$, when incoming shock rate $\mathcal{V}\_D / \Delta t$ outpaces central oversight bandwidth, centralized clearance queues jam. Ashby's (1958) variety amplification and Elinor Ostrom's (1990, 2010) **polycentric organization** ([Chapter 85](85-layer-polycentric-commons-urban-biospheric-order.md)) preserve local flexibility $\mathcal{V}\_R$ while routing routine exceptions off-shift (Ostrom 2010). Overarching constitutional floors (§5) therefore lock tail-loss rules above competing units while leaving local problem-solving discretion intact inside them.

## 5. Artificial Selection as the Biological Root of Goodhart's Law

A dairy breeder selects cows solely for single-lactation milk volume and inadvertently breeds a lame, exhausted, short-lived herd. Just like the opening hospital ranking, research funders rewarding publication counts and flashy $p$-values select for underpowered, fragile studies (Smaldino & McElreath 2016). Jay Lush (1937) laid the quantitative basis for the **breeder's equation**, later standardized in symbolic form linking mid-parent linear selection response $R = \bar{z}\_{\mathrm{offspring}} - \bar{z}\_{\mathrm{population}}$ to selection differential $S = \bar{z}\_{\mathrm{selected}} - \bar{z}\_{\mathrm{population}}$ (or dimensionless intensity $i = S / \sigma\_P$ using $\sigma\_P = \sqrt{V\_P} > 0$, giving $R = i\mkern2mu h^2 \sigma\_P$) and narrow-sense heritability $h^2 = V\_A / V\_P \in [0, 1]$ (assuming non-negative genotype–environment covariance $\mathrm{Cov}(A, E) \ge 0$):

$$R = h^2 S.$$

Read aloud, generational gain in the targeted trait equals its heritability multiplied by how aggressively top performers were picked (distinct from Ashby's $R$ and vital reserves $R\_t$; [What Judging Is, and What It Is Not](27-what-judging-is.md)). Optimizing one narrow score strips unmeasured vital capacity.

In institutions, this selective erosion appears as **Goodhart's Law** and **Campbell's Law** (Goodhart 1975; Campbell 1979; Strathern 1997; [Chapter 07](07-when-the-measure-becomes-the-goal.md)). Paying teams on a narrow proxy metric $M$ decouples score from real goal $Q$ and drains hidden safety margins (formalized as Collapse Proposition 3 in [The Triad and the Minimum](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md)). Additive variance shrinks toward zero ($h\_t^2 \to 0$) at a plateau where linear forecasts $T R\_0$ (multiplying generation horizon $T$ by baseline generation-0 selection response $R\_0$, distinct from the epidemiological reproduction number $R\_0$) overstate gains while unmeasured buffers fail early. The full Goodhart taxonomy belongs to [Institution, Incentive, Metric](40-institution-incentive-metric.md) and [Reactive Worlds: Causal Invariance, Performativity, and Goodhart's Law](48-reactive-worlds-performativity-goodhart.md).

### Selection among institutional forms

Selection acts across competing organizations, not only inside one unit. Even when holding emergency reserves protects the whole community during rare storms, any single firm or clinic that quietly strips its reserve cuts operating costs—and grows faster during quiet years—unless an overarching rule charges every player for unheld risk.

Let share $x \in (0, 1)$ of competing units hold a vital reserve at continuous calm cost rate $c > 0$ ($\pi\_R = -c$), while unbuffered rivals (temptation type, subscript $T$) hold none and pay only a calm regulatory charge $q \ge 0$ ($\pi\_T = -q$). Shocks arrive randomly as a Poisson process with mean inter-shock interval $T\_{\mathrm{shock}} > 0$ (rate $1/T\_{\mathrm{shock}}$, abbreviated $T$ in [Chapter 40](40-institution-incentive-metric.md) and [Chapter 50](50-methodological-families-reflexivity.md)), wiping out any unbuffered unit on its first shock. Between shocks, replicator competition $\dot{x} = x(1 - x)(\pi\_R - \pi\_T) = (q - c) x(1 - x)$ drives the log-odds ratio $\ln(x/(1-x))$ via net divestment advantage $\gamma\_{\mathrm{select}} := c - q$:

$$\frac{d}{dt}\ln\\!\left(\frac{x}{1-x}\right) = \frac{\dot{x}}{x(1-x)} = \pi\_R - \pi\_T = q - c = -\gamma\_{\mathrm{select}}, \qquad \gamma\_{\mathrm{select}} := c - q.$$

Read aloud, whenever reserve cost $c$ exceeds regulatory charge $q$ ($\gamma\_{\mathrm{select}} > 0$), the log-odds of prudent units holding reserves drops steadily at rate $\gamma\_{\mathrm{select}}$ during calm periods. Under limited liability ($q = 0$), $\gamma\_{\mathrm{select}} = c > 0$; under partial internalization ($0 \le q < c$), $\gamma\_{\mathrm{select}} = c - q > 0$. Prudent share decays as $x(t) = x\_0 e^{-\gamma\_{\mathrm{select}} t}/(1 - x\_0 + x\_0 e^{-\gamma\_{\mathrm{select}} t})$, taking deterministic calm time $t^\ast$ to sink from $x\_0$ down to $x\_1 \in (0, x\_0)$:

$$t^{\ast} = \frac{1}{\gamma\_{\mathrm{select}}}\ln\\!\left(\frac{x\_0(1-x\_1)}{x\_1(1-x\_0)}\right) > 0.$$

Read aloud, stronger divestment temptation $\gamma\_{\mathrm{select}}$ shrinks the time needed to hollow out prudence before a crisis arrives.

**Proposition (competitive extinction, exponential case).** Let first-shock time follow $\tau\_{\mathrm{shock}} \sim \mathrm{Exp}(1/T\_{\mathrm{shock}})$ ($T\_{\mathrm{shock}} > 0$), absorbing unbuffered units on first hit so post-shock surviving share jumps to $x(\tau\_{\mathrm{shock}}^+) = 1$ while the pre-shock left limit is $x(\tau\_{\mathrm{shock}}^-)$. For $0 < x\_1 < x\_0 < 1$ and $\gamma\_{\mathrm{select}} = c - q > 0$, the chance that reserve coverage sinks to $x\_1$ before the first shock arrives is:

$$\mathbb{P}(\tau\_{\mathrm{shock}} > t^{\ast}) = \exp\\!\left(-\frac{t^{\ast}}{T\_{\mathrm{shock}}}\right) = \left[\frac{x\_1(1-x\_0)}{x\_0(1-x\_1)}\right]^{1/(\gamma\_{\mathrm{select}} T\_{\mathrm{shock}})}.$$

Read aloud, this probability climbs monotonically toward $1$ as the calm-divestment index $\gamma\_{\mathrm{select}} T\_{\mathrm{shock}}$ grows large. A reckless rival does not need to survive storms to win the calm season; it wins if stripping finishes before the storm hits. With expected waiting time $\mathbb{E}[\tau\_{\mathrm{shock}}] = T\_{\mathrm{shock}}$, expected pre-shock log-odds satisfy $\mathbb{E}\bigl[\ln\bigl(x(\tau\_{\mathrm{shock}}^-)/(1-x(\tau\_{\mathrm{shock}}^-))\bigr)\bigr] = \ln\bigl(x\_0/(1-x\_0)\bigr) - \gamma\_{\mathrm{select}} T\_{\mathrm{shock}}$. Whenever $\gamma\_{\mathrm{select}} > 1/T\_{\mathrm{shock}}$ ($\gamma\_{\mathrm{select}} T\_{\mathrm{shock}} > 1$), expected pre-shock log-odds drop by more than $1\text{ nat}$ before the first shock:

$$\mathbb{E}\\!\left[\ln\\!\left(\frac{x(\tau\_{\mathrm{shock}}^-)}{1-x(\tau\_{\mathrm{shock}}^-)}\right)\right] < \ln\\!\left(\frac{x\_0}{1-x\_0}\right) - 1.$$

Read aloud, when reserve-stripping outruns shock arrival, prudence loses over a factor of $e$ in **geometric-mean odds** (over $1\text{ nat}$ of expected log-odds) before nature tests it once. Writing $L\_{\mathrm{shock}} > 0$ for average per-shock loss (distinct from [Chapter 16](16-what-knowledge-is.md)'s epistemic loss $L$), $L\_{\mathrm{shock}}/T\_{\mathrm{shock}} > c$ means full reserve coverage ($x = 1$) beats zero coverage ($x = 0$) socially—yet calm drift ($q - c < 0$) keeps $x = 1$ unstable. That social-optimality bound $L\_{\mathrm{shock}}/T\_{\mathrm{shock}}(1) > c$ is even conservative when falling coverage $x$ shortens endogenous time between shocks $T\_{\mathrm{shock}}(x)$, even though faster shock arrival simultaneously trims the pre-shock stripping window $\mathbb{P}(\tau\_{\mathrm{shock}} > t^\ast)$ and expected pre-shock log-odds drop. An arbitrary inter-shock survival curve $\Sigma\_{\mathrm{surv}}(t) = \mathbb{P}(\tau\_{\mathrm{shock}} > t)$ (distinct from selection differential $S$) yields the parallel pre-shock probability $\Sigma\_{\mathrm{surv}}(t^\ast)$.

Under calm replicator dynamics $\dot{x} = x(1-x)(\pi\_R - \pi\_T)$, boundary states $x \in \lbrace 0, 1\rbrace$ stay invariant, and full internalization ($q \ge c$) prevents calm stripping ($\pi\_R - \pi\_T = q - c \ge 0$). Under partial internalization ($0 \le q < c$), $\gamma\_{\mathrm{select}} = c - q > 0$ pulls trajectories toward $0$ between shocks; whether pairing mean shock interval $T\_{\mathrm{shock}}$ with explicit post-shock jumps, replenishment $r > 0$, and boundary re-entry into $(0, 1)$ yields an interior stationary balance remains an **unproved open heuristic conjecture**, unused by the minimum safeguard or any later result.

An overarching constitutional floor flips the competitive sign by enforcing $q \ge c$, setting $\pi\_R - \pi\_T = q - c = -\gamma\_{\mathrm{select}} \ge 0$ ($\gamma\_{\mathrm{select}} \le 0$). Thus the governance coordinate is **not one reading among three** ([Legitimacy, Accountability, and Institutions](20-legitimacy-and-institutions.md); [The Triad and the Minimum](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md)): it sets the sign of the competitive differential pricing the other margins.

Ostrom (1990) separated operational, collective-choice, and constitutional rules. Only the constitutional tier binds the game from above, because rules that rivals can vote away get repealed whenever $\gamma\_{\mathrm{select}} > 0$. Even a solid floor $q \ge c$ leaves three hazards open: proxy gaming of measured tail risk under Goodhart's law ([Reactive Worlds](48-reactive-worlds-performativity-goodhart.md)), abuse of emergency exceptions without sunsets ([Legitimacy, Accountability, and Institutions](20-legitimacy-and-institutions.md)), and dumping unpriced harm onto unrepresented external reception perimeters ([Institution, Incentive, Metric](40-institution-incentive-metric.md); [Chapter 85](85-layer-polycentric-commons-urban-biospheric-order.md)). External spillover is audited ex post by Part III's retrospective diagnostic ([Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md)); without uncaptured measurement and reception accounting, calm rivalry steadily erodes prudence.
