## 1. The Anatomy of a Negative Feedback Loop

A wall thermostat switches a furnace on whenever a room cools below its dial setting and switches it off once the air warms. Living organisms manage something far richer, which Claude Bernard (1878) called defending the stability of the internal environment (*milieu intérieur*): sweating, shivering, narrowing blood vessels, and shifting setpoints so fragile cells stay alive across wild weather swings.

**Negative feedback** keeps vital variables inside survivable corridors by pushing back against drift. By W. Ross Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety ($\mathcal{V}\_O \ge \mathcal{V}\_D - \mathcal{V}\_R$; Ashby 1956, 1958; [Chapter 25](25-evolution-variation-selection.md); formalized as $\mathcal{V}\_O \ge \mathcal{V}\_D - \mathcal{V}\_R$ in [Chapter 29](29-aim-and-the-three-planes.md)), keeping outcome variety $\mathcal{V}\_O$ narrow against disturbance variety $\mathcal{V}\_D$ requires matching regulator variety $\mathcal{V}\_R$ (distinct from graph vertex set $V$ below, potential $V(x)$ in §2, and variability $V\_{\mathrm{var}}$ in §3). Every minimal loop couples four working parts:

1. **Sensor**: measures the current state $y(t)$;
2. **Comparator**: computes error $e(t) = y^\ast(t) - y(t)$ against target setpoint $y^\ast$;
3. **Controller**: computes corrective action $u(t) = \mathcal{K}(e)$;
4. **Effector**: applies physical effort to steer $y(t)$ back toward $y^\ast$.

Delay breaks simple corrections. Think of adjusting a shower knob connected to a long hot-water pipe: if you twist the handle aggressively before warm water reaches your hand, you bounce endlessly between scalding and freezing. Consider a scalar state $y(t) \in \mathbb{R}$ around target $y^\ast$ with passive damping rate $a > 0$ ($\text{s}^{-1}$), external push $d\_{\mathrm{dist}}(t)$, proportional feedback gain $k\_p \ge 0$ ($\text{s}^{-1}$, dimensionless ratio $K = k\_p / a \ge 0$, distinct from §2's capacity $K\_0, K\_{\mathrm{eff}}$), and measurement-to-action lag $\tau\_d \ge 0$ ($\text{s}$; [Chapter 21](21-mechanics-determinism-chaos.md)):

$$\dot{y}(t) = -a\bigl(y(t) - y^\ast\bigr) - k\_p \bigl(y(t - \tau\_d) - y^\ast\bigr) + d\_{\mathrm{dist}}(t).$$

Read aloud, state drift equals passive self-return plus delayed feedback correction driven by how far off target the system stood $\tau\_d$ seconds ago, plus any outside disturbance. When stable (guaranteed for $k\_p \le a$), feedback shrinks a constant open-loop offset $\Delta y\_{\mathrm{open}} = d\_{\mathrm{dist}} / a$ down to $y\_{\mathrm{ss}} - y^\ast = \Delta y\_{\mathrm{open}}(1 + K)^{-1}$:

1. **No feedback ($k\_p \to 0 \implies K \to 0$):** $(1 + K)^{-1} \to 1$, leaving the full unbuffered offset $\Delta y\_{\mathrm{open}}$.
2. **Delayed oscillation ($K \to \infty$ vs. Hopf boundary):** Without delay ($\tau\_d = 0$), cranking gain up ($K \to \infty$) drives offset to zero smoothly. With real lag $\tau\_d > 0$ and high gain $k\_p > a$, crossing $\tau\_d \sqrt{k\_p^2 - a^2} > \arccos(-a / k\_p)$ ($\to k\_p \tau\_d > \pi / 2$ as $a / k\_p \to 0$) triggers hunting oscillations—which grow exponentially in the purely linear equation unless bounded into sustained limit cycles by effector saturation—even when external disturbance vanishes ($d\_{\mathrm{dist}} = 0$).

Real reserve networks do not share one fixed lag; communication delays fluctuate across links. Picture a decentralized network of reserve units coupled along edges $E \ne \emptyset$ of a **simple** (unweighted, loopless) undirected graph $\mathcal{G} = (V, E)$ with graph Laplacian $L\_{\mathcal{G}}$, largest eigenvalue $\lambda\_{\max}(L\_{\mathcal{G}}) > 0$, node degrees $d\_i, d\_j$, incidence entries $B\_{ie} \in \lbrace -1, +1 \rbrace$ on edge $e = \lbrace i, j \rbrace \in E$, and edge mismatch $z\_e := x\_i - x\_j$ evolving under symmetric edge-delayed coupling $\dot{x}\_i(t) = -\sum\_{e=\lbrace i,j\rbrace \in E} B\_{ie}\,\psi\_e\bigl(z\_e(t - \tau\_e(t))\bigr)$, where $\psi\_e$ satisfies sector inequality $\gamma\_{\mathrm{osm}} z^2 \le z\,\psi\_e(z) \le \Gamma z^2$ ($0 < \gamma\_{\mathrm{osm}} \le \Gamma$, distinct from §2's wear sensitivity $\gamma$). Let heterogeneous edge delays obey a uniform global worst-case envelope $0 \le \tau\_e(t) \le \bar{\tau}$ ($\bar{\tau} > 0$) with uniform worst-case jitter bounds $-\mu\_- \le \dot{\tau}\_e(t) \le \mu\_+ < 1$ ($0 \le \mu\_+ < 1$, $\mu\_- \ge 0$). Naive constant-delay checks fail because ignoring jitter or Doppler flux distortion ($\eta(\dot{\tau}\_e) = 1 - \dot{\tau}\_e(t) \in [1 - \mu\_+, 1 + \mu\_-]$) hides sudden instability, whereas edgewise Lyapunov–Krasovskii terms yield an explicit closed-form sufficient envelope.

Combining Seuret and Gouaisbaut's (2013) Wirtinger integral inequality with Park, Ko, and Jeong's (2011) reciprocally convex combination lemma inside positive conductance sector $[\gamma\_{\mathrm{osm}}, \Gamma]$ (raw sector ratio $\rho\_{\mathrm{sec}} := \gamma\_{\mathrm{osm}}/\Gamma \in (0, 1]$, distinct from §3's queue utilization $\rho$) yields a global ceiling on maximum delay $\bar{\tau}$, called the nonlinear delayed-coupling quadratic (`NL-Q`) ceiling $\bar{\tau}^{\mathrm{NL\text{-}Q}}\_{\max}(\mu\_+, \mu\_-, \rho\_{\mathrm{sec}}) = H\_{\mathrm{Thm3}}(\mu\_+, \mu\_-, \rho\_{\mathrm{sec}}) / \bigl(\bar{\eta}\,\Gamma\,\lambda\_{\max}(L\_{\mathcal{G}})\bigr)$:

$$\bar{\tau} < \bar{\tau}^{\mathrm{NL\text{-}Q}}\_{\max}(\mu\_+, \mu\_-, \rho\_{\mathrm{sec}}) := \frac{H\_{\mathrm{Thm3}}(\mu\_+, \mu\_-, \rho\_{\mathrm{sec}})}{\bar{\eta}\,\Gamma\,\lambda\_{\max}(L\_{\mathcal{G}})}, \qquad H\_{\mathrm{Thm3}}(\mu\_+, \mu\_-, \rho\_{\mathrm{sec}}) := \sqrt{1 - \frac{\mu\_+}{2} + \sqrt{\frac{\mu\_+^2}{4} + (1 - \mu\_+)\frac{2\rho\_{\mathrm{sec,eff}}}{1 + \rho\_{\mathrm{sec,eff}}}}},$$

where the $\sqrt{2}$-baseline numerator $H\_{\mathrm{Thm3}}(\mu\_+, \mu\_-, \rho\_{\mathrm{sec}}) > 0$ takes **raw** $\rho\_{\mathrm{sec}}$ and internally substitutes $(\bar{\eta}, \rho\_{\mathrm{sec,eff}}) = (1, \rho\_{\mathrm{sec}})$ under pure telemetry versus $\bigl(1 + \mu\_-, \frac{1 - \mu\_+}{1 + \mu\_-}\rho\_{\mathrm{sec}}\bigr)$ under Doppler distortion ([Chapter 53](53-layer-macro-coupled-topology-order.md); [Appendix A](appendix-a-mathematics.md) §A.2). Maximum safe delay shrinks whenever upper conductance $\Gamma$, coupling $\lambda\_{\max}(L\_{\mathcal{G}})$, or delay jitter grows, sitting strictly below the scalar Hopf limit $\pi/2$ (and just slightly stricter than an exact scalar Jensen LMI). Because global eigenvalues $\lambda\_{\max}(L\_{\mathcal{G}})$ cost too much during crises, Anderson and Morley's (1985) degree bound $\lambda\_{\max}(L\_{\mathcal{G}}) \le \max\_{\lbrace i, j\rbrace \in E}(d\_i + d\_j)$ reduces global testing on simple graphs to one-hop degree checks $(d\_i + d\_j)/B \le 1$ against a uniform global worst-case budget $B := \theta\_{\mathrm{safe}}\, H\_{\mathrm{Thm3}}(\mu\_+, \mu\_-, \rho\_{\mathrm{sec}}) / (\bar{\eta}\,\Gamma\,\bar{\tau})$ built from global worst-case $(\bar{\tau}, \mu\_+, \mu\_-)$ across $E$ (with safety factor $\theta\_{\mathrm{safe}} \in (0, 1)$, since merely edge-local delay bounds fail on asymmetric graphs)—a live per-step rule, not an after-the-fact Part III diagnostic. Two limits remain: under dead-band isolation ($\gamma\_{\mathrm{osm}} \to 0^+$, so $\rho\_{\mathrm{sec,eff}} \to 0^+$), $\bar{\tau}^{\mathrm{NL\text{-}Q}}\_{\max}$ remains positive at $1/\bigl(\bar{\eta}\,\Gamma\,\lambda\_{\max}(L\_{\mathcal{G}})\bigr)$ (guaranteeing bounded flux arrest rather than strict equilibrium consensus), while heavy-tailed jumps ($\alpha < 2$) leap out of small-signal stability altogether.

Break any of the loop's four pieces, and institutional failure leaves a telltale trace:

1. **Missing sensor**: blind to slow drift until sudden collapse;
2. **Defective or contested comparator**: gathers numbers without clear standards—or locks onto a politically disputed target $y^\ast$ needing open contestation ([Chapter 20](20-legitimacy-and-institutions.md));
3. **Paralyzed controller**: logs warnings while divided authority blocks action;
4. **Saturated effector**: issues urgent orders after physical capacity is already spent.

Alarms fail not from missing sensors, but when middle layers choke them or effectors stall. Stafford Beer (1972, 1979) countered upward filtering via **sporadic direct audits**—System $3^\ast$, formalized in Beer (1979)—and the **algedonic bypass channel** (Beer 1972) ([Chapter 40](40-institution-incentive-metric.md); [Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md)); pairing that bypass with corroborated triage and status-blind hearing guards against alarm floods and testimonial injustice alike (Fricker 2007; [Chapter 20](20-legitimacy-and-institutions.md) §4; [Chapter 50](50-methodological-families-reflexivity.md)).

## 2. Allostasis, Allostatic Load, and Biological Canalization

Walter Cannon (1926, 1932) coined **homeostasis** in 1926 and elaborated it in 1932 for the physiological checks that keep blood sugar, temperature, and salt steady. Because living creatures are not rigid thermostats waiting passively for errors, Peter Sterling and Joseph Eyer (1988) introduced **allostasis**: preserving viability by predicting demand ahead of time—raising heart rate, cortisol, blood pressure, and glucose before a sprint or stress arrives.

Chronic emergency mobilization pays a steep hidden price—**allostatic load**, cumulative—and, under sustained chronic overload, irreversible—wear across neural, cardiovascular, metabolic, and immune systems (McEwen 1998). Suppose regulatory effort $u(t) \ge 0$ rises above sustainable baseline maintenance $u\_{\mathrm{basal}} \ge 0$ over $[0, T]$ (evaluating cumulative overload without restorative remodeling during rest intervals $u(t) < u\_{\mathrm{basal}}$, tracked in [Chapter 47](47-irreversible-time-vital-margin.md) and [Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md)), draining starting capacity $K\_0 > 0$ into remaining effective capacity $K\_{\mathrm{eff}}(T)$ at wear sensitivity $\gamma > 0$ (distinct from $\gamma\_{\mathrm{select}}$ in [Chapter 25](25-evolution-variation-selection.md)):

$$A(T) = \int\_0^T \max\bigl(0,\mkern3mu u(t) - u\_{\mathrm{basal}}\bigr)\mkern3mudt, \qquad K\_{\mathrm{eff}}(T) = \max\bigl(0,\mkern3mu K\_0 - \gamma A(T)\bigr).$$

Read aloud, cumulative overload $A(T)$ sums every hour spent above sustainable effort, eroding effective reserve capacity linearly until capacity hits zero at $A(T) \ge K\_0 / \gamma$. That erosion shrinks Holling's stability basin $D\_t / D\_0$ toward a **tipping point** ([Chapter 23](23-statistical-physics-and-time.md)). As the basin flattens under continuous noise, engineering relaxation time $\tau\_{\mathrm{eng}}$ stretches via critical slowing down (Scheffer et al. 2009), whereas big heavy-tailed jumps ($\alpha < 2$) can vault the rim in one shock before $\tau\_{\mathrm{eng}}$ warns anyone—and local damping time $\tau\_{\mathrm{eng}}$ always stays distinct from full institutional recovery time ([Chapter 12](12-derivatives-and-convexity.md); [Chapter 47](47-irreversible-time-vital-margin.md)).

Holding a visible gauge steady spends hidden structure underneath. Under Canguilhem's (1943/1966) **vital normativity** ([Chapter 24](24-chemical-rates-equilibrium-buffers.md)), surviving environmental swings requires freedom to shift operating setpoints instead of cranking loop gain $k\_p$ onto an obsolete $y^\ast$ and burning out $A(T)$. An agency can hold a headline target flat right up to sudden collapse, or quietly shift exhaustion onto caregivers and external communities ([Chapter 29](29-aim-and-the-three-planes.md); [Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md)). In [Chapter 25](25-evolution-variation-selection.md)'s landscape metaphor, a healthy basin forms a valley $V(x)$ with positive transverse curvature $\partial^2 V / \partial x^2 > 0$ absorbing fluctuations until chronic stress pushes the system over the rim.

## 3. Queueing Theory and John Kingman's Heavy-Traffic Approximation

An ambulance dispatch centre trims idle crew hours until average utilization hugs full capacity. On quarterly accounting sheets, labor efficiency looks sparkling; on Friday nights, callers wait hours whenever a few extra emergencies arrive together.

Model a service desk as a stationary $GI/G/1$ renewal queue with mutually independent i.i.d. interarrival times (mean rate $\lambda > 0$, squared coefficient of variation $0 \le c\_a^2 < \infty$) and i.i.d. service times (mean service rate $\mu\_s > \lambda$, mean service duration $\tau\_s = 1/\mu\_s > 0$—distinct from §1's feedback lags $\tau\_d, \tau\_e(t)$—and squared coefficient of variation $0 \le c\_s^2 < \infty$), defining dimensionless **utilization** $\rho$:

$$\rho = \frac{\lambda}{\mu\_s} \in (0, 1).$$

Read aloud, utilization divides how fast jobs arrive by how fast servers clear them, measuring the busy fraction of capacity. Under these i.i.d. $GI/G/1$ hypotheses with finite variability ($0 \le c\_a^2, c\_s^2 < \infty$, requiring tail exponent $\alpha > 2$), John Kingman (1962) proved the exact general upper bound on mean queue waiting time $\mathbb{E}[W\_q] \le \bigl(\frac{c\_a^2/\rho^2 + c\_s^2}{2}\bigr)\phi(\rho)\tau\_s$ across $\rho \in (0, 1)$ (while Kingman 1961 established the $\rho \to 1^-$ heavy-traffic asymptotic approximation $\mathbb{E}[W\_q] \approx V\_{\mathrm{var}}\,\phi(\rho)\,\tau\_s$), scaling with the hyperbolic multiplier $\phi(\rho) = \rho / (1 - \rho)$, dimensionless variability $V\_{\mathrm{var}} = \frac{1}{2}(c\_a^2 + c\_s^2)$, and mean service time $\tau\_s$ ([Appendix A](appendix-a-mathematics.md) §A.2). Whenever arrivals or service times vary ($c\_a^2 + c\_s^2 > 0$), wait times blow up without bound as utilization approaches one ($\rho \to 1^-$). With infinite variance ($\alpha \le 2$) or bursty, correlated arrivals (Brown et al. 2005), i.i.d. finite-variance bounds understate wait times even further below full load ([Chapter 12](12-derivatives-and-convexity.md); [Time and Dependence](38-time-and-dependence.md); [Chapter 47](47-irreversible-time-vital-margin.md)).

## 4. Running Near the Ceiling: The Illusion of Efficiency

Treating spare capacity as pure waste springs a three-part trap:

- A facility can hum smoothly in a **vulnerable** subcritical state ($\rho < 1$) until one brief surge overflows capacity and retry storms lock effective utilization above one ($\rho\_{\mathrm{eff}} > 1$), trapping the system in **metastable failure** long after the initial spike passes (Bronson et al. 2021).
- During subcritical recovery ($\rho < 1$, finite variability), clearing an accumulated backlog scales as $\mathcal{O}\bigl(1/(1 - \rho)\bigr)$ alongside actuation lag $\tau\_{\mathrm{actuate}}$; when effective load stays saturated ($\rho\_{\mathrm{eff}} \ge 1$) without shedding demand, backlogs never clear and recovery time grows infinite.
- During a physical outage, an unbuffered system drains emergency stocks before help arrives.

Unused capacity ($1 - \rho$) is not fat; it is the shock absorber keeping recovery finite. Across disrupted links where live round-trips stall, delay-tolerant store-carry-forward custody (Fall 2003) keeps state safe during blackouts, while local $r$-robust outlier trimming (LeBlanc et al. 2013) stops faulty or adversarial peers from hijacking consensus—an asynchronous resilience parallel developed in [Chapter 83](83-layer-distributed-consensus-sre-order.md) §1.

## 5. The Survival Inequality: TTS vs. TTR

Joining queue congestion with Canguilhem's vital margins produces the fundamental survival inequality (Simchi-Levi et al. 2014; [Chapter 47](47-irreversible-time-vital-margin.md)). In short, **TTS** stands for *time to survive* (how long remaining reserves hold out under crisis drain) and **TTR** stands for *time to recover* (how long an organization takes to spot trouble, decide, and restore control).

Write $B\_t \ge 0$ for physical buffer stock, $c\_{\mathrm{imp}} \in [0, 1)$ for impairment reducing usable reserves, and $\dot{D}\_{\mathrm{crisis}} > 0$ for crisis drain rate (buffer drawdown, distinct from basin width $D\_t$). Survival time equals usable stock divided by drain rate $(1 - c\_{\mathrm{imp}}) B\_t / \dot{D}\_{\mathrm{crisis}}$ evaluated under worst-case constant drain without concurrent refill. Meanwhile, effective recovery time sums three strictly non-overlapping sequential lags over institutional records $\mathcal{I}\_t$ (subscripts distinguishing recovery phases from §1's $\tau\_d, \tau\_e$ and §3's $\tau\_s$): detection lag $\tau\_{\mathrm{detect}}$ (inflated whenever warnings suffer rank-based status discounting; Fricker 2007), deliberation lag $\tau\_{\mathrm{delib}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$ constrained near $10\text{ bits/s}$ (Zheng & Meister 2025), and physical actuation time $\tau\_{\mathrm{actuate}}$ ($\mathcal{O}(1/(1 - \rho))$ for $\rho < 1$; [Chapter 38](38-time-and-dependence.md)).

Persistence demands that **survival time meet or exceed effective recovery time** under a genuine resource budget—without secret bailouts (Kornai 1986) or dumping deficits onto external reception perimeters ([Chapter 29](29-aim-and-the-three-planes.md); [Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md)). Push capacity to the ceiling with zero slack, and survival time drops toward zero while recovery time shoots toward infinity, ensuring ruin on the first real hit. Knowing uncertainty bounds without holding buffers fails on exhaustion; both margins endure only when governance enforces zero status discounting, stops reserve pillage, and prevents external spillover ([Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md)).

### The ledger of a queue

Under stationary finite-variance arrivals ($\alpha > 2$), Kingman's multiplier $\phi(\rho) = \rho / (1 - \rho)$ has load elasticity $\frac{d\ln \phi(\rho)}{d\ln \rho} = \frac{1}{1 - \rho} > 1$ across $\rho \in (0, 1)$ ([Chapter 12](12-derivatives-and-convexity.md)), hovering near $1^+$ at light traffic and shooting upward as $\rho \to 1^-$. Smoothing variability ($V\_{\mathrm{var}}$) trims queue waits linearly at fixed load; wherever bursts cannot be scheduled away, spare capacity $(1 - \rho)$ remains mandatory—and under infinite variance ($\alpha \le 2$) or shifting regimes, sizing buffers from past record highs fails.

### When accepting the queue is better than holding slack

Holding extra idle capacity can relax only when losses are fully reversible, mean effective load stays safely subcritical ($\rho\_{\mathrm{eff}} < 1$) with guaranteed backlog clearing, and affected people consent without uncompensated wear or queues spilling onto domestic caregivers or external reception perimeters ([Chapter 24](24-chemical-rates-equilibrium-buffers.md); [Chapter 47](47-irreversible-time-vital-margin.md)). Then the governing clock is ordinary discharge wait rather than crisis survival.

Canguilhem's (1943/1966) vital normativity supplies the unifying conclusion: forcing a metric into a narrow groove with high feedback gain as conditions change turns adaptive resilience into irreversible allostatic wear (Cannon 1926, 1932; Sterling & Eyer 1988; McEwen 1998). Living regulation and waiting queues obey the same hyperbolic saturation geometry under bounded capacity—an isomorphism of limits rather than identical machinery ([Chapter 24](24-chemical-rates-equilibrium-buffers.md))—requiring real headroom and freedom to adjust norms before cumulative wear or queue delay outruns survival time.
