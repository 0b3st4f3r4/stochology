> holds itself in a survivable range, and why the cost of doing so is a permanent budget line.

## 1. The Anatomy of a Negative Feedback Loop

In 1865 Claude Bernard observed that independent life requires the constancy of the internal environment
(*milieu intérieur*). In 1932 Walter Cannon named the physiological processes that hold those steady states
**homeostasis** (Cannon 1932).

Biological and cybernetic regulation keeps vital variables inside survivable boundaries by negative feedback.
A minimal closed loop needs four parts:

1. **Sensor**: Measures the instantaneous state of the regulated physical variable $y(t)$;
2. **Comparator**: Calculates the error signal $e(t) = y^\ast(t) - y(t)$ relative to a setpoint or target range $y^\ast$;
3. **Controller**: Decides a corrective regulatory policy $u(t) = \mathcal{K}(e)$;
4. **Effector**: Executes physical work on the environment or organism to drive $y(t)$ back toward the setpoint.

For a regulated variable $y(t) \in \mathbb{R}$ with passive relaxation rate $a > 0$ ($\text{s}^{-1}$), disturbance rate $d(t)$, and active proportional gain $k\_p \ge 0$ ($\text{s}^{-1}$, dimensionless loop gain $K = k\_p / a \ge 0$) across transport delay $\tau\_d \ge 0$ ($\text{s}$), closed-loop dynamics follow:

$$\dot{y}(t) = -a\bigl(y(t) - y^\ast\bigr) - k\_p \bigl(y(t - \tau\_d) - y^\ast\bigr) + d(t),$$

which compresses the open-loop steady-state offset $\Delta y\_{\mathrm{open}} = d / a$ under constant disturbance $d$ to $y\_{\mathrm{ss}} - y^\ast = d / (a + k\_p) = \Delta y\_{\mathrm{open}}(1 + K)^{-1}$. Two regimes bound it:

1. **No feedback ($k\_p \to 0 \implies K \to 0$):** the sensitivity $(1 + K)^{-1} \to 1$, and the system absorbs the full uncompensated environmental offset $\Delta y\_{\mathrm{open}}$.
2. **Delayed instability ($K \to \infty$ vs. Hopf delay boundary):** At $\tau\_d = 0$, $(1 + K)^{-1} \to 0$ stably; at $\tau\_d > 0$ with $k\_p > a$, pushing gain or delay past $\tau\_d \sqrt{k\_p^2 - a^2} > \arccos(-a / k\_p)$ (which approaches $k\_p \tau\_d > \pi / 2$ in the weak-damping limit $a / k\_p \to 0$) drives out-of-phase hunting oscillations.

Remove or corrupt one component and the institutional failure is characteristic:

1. **Missing sensor**: The institution is blind to environmental drift, learning of failure only upon catastrophic collapse;
2. **Defective comparator**: The institution collects data but lacks standards to recognize deviation;
3. **Paralyzed controller**: Deviations are detected and documented, but decision authority is fragmented and no action is commanded;
4. **Saturated effector**: Corrective orders are issued, but physical capacity is exhausted and cannot alter the world.

A post-incident review finds that "warnings were raised but nothing was done." Almost never is the root cause a
lack of information, since the sensor functioned. It is a failure of effector authority, or a hierarchical
filter that delayed the signal past the phase boundary.

Stafford Beer (1972) solved that latency trap in viable system cybernetics with the **algedonic bypass
channel** ($S3^\ast$) (Beer 1972). It is a direct, non-hierarchical pain/distress circuit breaker, and it
bypasses the intermediate bureaucratic layers when frontline likelihood ratios cross an emergency threshold.
What it prevents is Miranda Fricker's testimonial injustice (Fricker 2007) silencing a warning early enough to
matter.

At the interpersonal scale of dialogue the same geometry appears as a normalized attraction basin between two
speakers, $\Theta\_{\mathrm{basin}} = \mathrm{clamp}\bigl((\tau\_{\mathrm{tol}} - (d + \omega s)) / \tau\_{\mathrm{tol}},\mkern3mu 0,\mkern3mu 1\bigr)$,
where $\tau\_{\mathrm{tol}} > 0$ is the tolerance bandwidth, $d \ge 0$ the drift rate, $s \ge 0$ the shock
intensity, and $\omega > 0$ the weight the loop gives a shock relative to drift. As $d \to 0$ and $s \to 0$ the
loop stabilizes inside any positive bandwidth. Once the combined drift and shock load reaches the bandwidth the
basin closes and the communicative loop ruptures irreversibly. The structure is Beer's, one level down.

## 2. Allostasis, Allostatic Load, and Biological Canalization

Walter Cannon (1932) defined **homeostasis** as the coordinated physiological processes that maintain steady
internal states (Cannon 1932). A living organism is not a static thermostat. Peter Sterling and Joseph Eyer
(1988) introduced **allostasis**: the active maintenance of viability through physiological change in
anticipation of environmental demand (Sterling & Eyer 1988).

Internal setpoints are dynamic. Against exertion or threat the brain mobilizes cortisol, raises arterial blood
pressure, accelerates heart rate, shunts glucose to skeletal muscle.

The biological cost is **allostatic load** (McEwen, 1998): cumulative, irreversible wear on arterial walls,
neural pathways and renal filters from chronic physiological mobilization (McEwen 1998). When regulatory effort $u(t)$ exceeds basal maintenance level $u\_{\mathrm{basal}}$ over horizon $[0, T]$, cumulative wear integrates as:

$$A(T) = \int\_0^T \max\bigl(0,\mkern3mu u(t) - u\_{\mathrm{basal}}\bigr)\mkern3mudt, \qquad K\_{\mathrm{eff}}(T) = K\_0 - \gamma A(T),$$

The wear erodes the effective carrying capacity $K\_{\mathrm{eff}}(T)$ of the Canguilhem–Holling survival basin
(Canguilhem 1966; Holling 1973). It weakens the local restoring rate $\lambda\_{\mathrm{eng}}$, drives the lag-1
autocorrelation $\rho\_1 = \exp(-\lambda\_{\mathrm{eng}}\Delta t)$ toward one, and inflates the recovery time
$\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{recovery}} = -\kappa \Delta t / \ln \rho\_1$.

Sustained load narrows the relative basin width $D\_t / D\_0$ by a large factor and pushes the worn
autocorrelation toward one at the same time. The buffer that keeps the output stable is being spent to keep it
stable. A living body, or a public service, can hold its primary output metric perfectly flat while internal
allostatic wear pushes the system toward sudden, catastrophic organ rupture.

In developmental biology, Conrad Waddington (1957) formulated **canalization** through the epigenetic
landscape $V(x)$ (Waddington 1957). Developmental pathways follow deeply carved valleys (*chreods*) with
positive curvature $\partial^2 V / \partial x^2 > 0$, and that curvature buffers phenotypes against genetic
mutation and thermal noise.

Molecular chaperones such as $\text{Hsp90}$ (Rutherford & Lindquist 1998) absorb mutational volatility physically
and keep development normal until environmental stress exhausts the chaperone buffer. Cryptic mutations are
then expressed at once, and a systemic phenotypic crisis follows.

## 3. Queueing Theory and John Kingman's Heavy-Traffic Approximation

A regional dispatch desk trims staffing until average utilization sits against the ceiling. Unit labour cost
looks exemplary on paper, and ambulances queue for hours on every minor arrival burst.

Under load, regulation fails through queue congestion. Take a service facility receiving work at mean arrival
rate $\lambda > 0$ with mean service rate $\mu > \lambda$. The traffic intensity, or **utilization**, is:

$$\rho = \frac{\lambda}{\mu} \in [0, 1).$$

John Kingman (1961) proved the **heavy-traffic approximation** for general single-server queues ($G/G/1$)
(Kingman 1961). As utilization approaches capacity ($\rho \to 1$) the expected waiting time in queue
$\mathbb{E}[W\_q]$ converges to:

$$\mathbb{E}[W\_q] \approx \frac{\rho}{1 - \rho} \left( \frac{c\_a^2 + c\_s^2}{2} \right) \tau,$$

where $\tau = 1/\mu$ is the mean service time, $c\_a = \sigma\_a / \mu\_a$ is the coefficient of variation of arrival inter-arrival times, and $c\_s = \sigma\_s / \mu\_s$ is the coefficient of variation of service times. Write the variability content as $V\_{\mathrm{var}} = \frac{1}{2}(c\_a^2 + c\_s^2)$, so that
$\mathbb{E}[W\_q] \approx \frac{\rho}{1 - \rho} V\_{\mathrm{var}} \tau$ (exact for $M/G/1$ where $c\_a^2 = 1$, and asymptotic as $\rho \to 1^-$ in general $G/G/1$). Two limits follow:

1. **An idle facility ($\rho \to 0$):** $\frac{\rho}{1 - \rho} \to 0$, so $\mathbb{E}[W\_q] \to 0$ and the wait is negligible beside the service time itself.
2. **Saturation ($\rho \to 1^-$):** $\frac{\rho}{1 - \rho} \to +\infty$, so the expected wait diverges and the ratio between the saturated operating point and a light one is unbounded.

Two operational lessons come out of the formula:

1. **Delay is hyperbolic in slack**: the hyperbola $\frac{\rho}{1 - \rho}$ governs it. The denominator
   $1 - \rho$ is the uncommitted recovery headroom of the system, so the factor is one less than the reciprocal
   of the slack the efficiency report counts as waste: $\frac{\rho}{1-\rho} = \frac{1}{1-\rho} - 1$, not
   $\frac{1}{1-\rho}$. Halve the slack and the factor is multiplied by $\frac{2-s}{1-s}$ in slack $s$, which
   exceeds two everywhere and approaches two only at the wall. Move utilization from four-fifths to
   nineteen-twentieths and queue delay nearly quintuples, because available recovery capacity falls from
   one-fifth to one-twentieth.
2. **Variability multiplies delay on its own**: the factor $\frac{c\_a^2 + c\_s^2}{2}$ scales delay
   independently of utilization. Arrivals that cluster in bursts (Brown et al. 2005), or fat-tailed service
   times, compound queue congestion. In a closed-schedule clinic with pre-booked slots
   ($c\_a^2 \to 0, c\_s^2 \to 0$) and guaranteed daily discharge, $\mathbb{E}[W\_q] \to 0$ even close to
   full load.

## 4. Running Near the Ceiling: The Illusion of Efficiency

Management doctrine treats unutilized capacity as waste to be eliminated. Kingman's arithmetic says otherwise:
running against capacity is a trap with three parts.

- Let the slack equal the size of an ordinary demand burst and the effective load crosses one. The queue then
  diverges without bound, backlog grows linearly with time, and retry storms trigger metastable failures
  (Bronson et al. 2021).
- The time required to clear a backlog, $\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{recovery}}$, diverges as
  $\mathcal{O}\bigl(1/(1 - \rho)\bigr)$.
- Let a crisis disrupt normal operations and an unbuffered system needs an astronomical recovery time. It burns
  through emergency reserves before recovery can take hold.

Spare capacity ($1 - \rho$) is not idle waste. It is the physical shock absorber that keeps recovery times
finite.

## 5. The Survival Inequality: TTS vs. TTR

Integrating queue congestion with Canguilhem's vital margins yields the master survival condition of
Stochology, derived in [Irreversible Time and the Vital Margin](47-irreversible-time-vital-margin.md).

An institution facing disruption survives only if its Time-To-Survival, the duration its vital reserves can
sustain operations during a crisis, exceeds its Time-To-Recovery, the time for congested service queues and
damaged infrastructure to return to viability. That is the condition
$\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} \ge \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{recovery}}$, and it is necessary and physical.
Full viability additionally requires epistemic and governance integrity across the Nuclear Triad.

Size an institution to run at the ceiling with no vital reserves and $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}}$ shrinks
toward zero while $\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{recovery}}$ inflates toward infinity. Absorption at the ruin barrier
follows at the first shock. An agent carrying only the epistemic estimate of its ignorance is ruined at the
shift that exhausts it; an agent carrying both margins traverses the same shocks.

At the interpersonal scale of dialogue, the same Canguilhem–Holling attraction-basin geometry governs whether a
communicative relationship absorbs interpretive noise without irreversible rupture.

### The ledger of a queue

Regulation fails at the ceiling. Take a metered load record with its own spread of half-hourly demand, its own
ratio of extreme percentile to peak, its own measured burstiness. Size installed capacity just above a high
quantile of that record and utilization runs into the region where the waiting time exceeds the time the
reserve can sustain operations. The system fails the survival inequality before any component physically
breaks.

Size instead against a multiple of the observed maximum and utilization sits where the wait stays a fraction of
the survival time. Survival then holds with a wide margin.

Cut the arrival variability and the wait falls at the same utilization, with the Kingman factor untouched.
Utilization rises and waiting rises with it, and the elasticity between the two is far above one while
installed capacity barely moves. Capacity is the expensive instrument; variability is the cheap one.

The ledger of a queue is not written where the efficiency report is read. The utilization multiplier in heavy traffic is
$\rho/(1-\rho)$: one less than the reciprocal of the slack, unbounded at full load, multiplying $V\_{\mathrm{var}}\tau$ under the same hyperbolic law the classical queueing literature derived for the single server (Kingman 1961).

### When accepting the queue is better than holding slack

The thesis fails when the loss is reversible and the queue can be discharged later. The move is then to accept
the queue and measure the discharge time, not to hold margin against it. The condition is a guaranteed discharge
path with a tolerant client. The procedure is to report the time to discharge rather than the time to survive,
because only one of the two is the relevant delay.

Homeostasis in the biological sense draws the same distinction. A regulated variable is held inside a band by
feedback whose gain costs something, and the cost of gain is the price paid for the band (Cannon 1932;
Sterling & Eyer 1988). The queue is the same object seen from the other side, and the two literatures never
cite each other.
