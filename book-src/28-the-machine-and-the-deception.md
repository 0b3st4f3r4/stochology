## By the end of this chapter you can

1. Say what a predictive model actually computes, and what it cannot compute, without invoking
   any mathematics.
2. Recognise the four recurring shapes of algorithmic deception: fluent wrongness, metric
   capture, authority laundering, and confident extrapolation.
3. Apply a three-question filter to any claim about a model, and reject the claim on evidence
   rather than on taste.

## 1. What a model does

Picture a regional credit officer reviewing an automated fraud-and-default risk score for a household requesting emergency working capital. The screen displays one crisp probability, distilled from a long historical archive. Denial costs the household its seasonal harvest and its place in the ledger, an irreversible rejection never recorded in the bank's training archive; approval risks a bounded write-off. Before asking whether the displayed number is "accurate", the officer must ask what physical and institutional facts were erased to produce it.

Strip away the marketing vocabulary and any predictive model performs one operation: it stores statistical regularities observed in historical pairs of inputs and outputs, and returns, for a novel input, the output most consistent with those regularities under the lossy compression it was constrained to use. Every model is therefore an archived *summary of the past*, and every compression is lossy in a direction chosen by an institutional author.

Three limitations follow immediately:

- A model cannot report a regularity absent from its training record, however blatant and urgent that regularity is to the frontline operators inhabiting the situation.
- A model cannot distinguish *the world is structured like this* from *the archive was collected like this*; the distinction between territory and map is unrepresentable within its weights.
- A model has no internal mechanism for announcing *my compression fails in this region*; its compression applies everywhere by construction, including where its assumptions are violated.

Above all, a statistical model cannot supply causal authority. A pattern of association can be fitted to near-zero training error while the intervention a decision-maker is about to execute moves the physical system the other way. The jump from correlation to operational action is a separate logical claim requiring distinct identifying assumptions, never licensed by predictive accuracy. When a policy optimizer is allowed to exploit a fitted linear surrogate beyond its training anchor inside a nonlinear physical world, greedy optimization dreams a trajectory return the world will not pay: the surrogate extrapolates smoothly, the physical system bends, and the trajectory is driven into absorbing ruin. A penalty on the discrepancy between surrogate and world restrains the optimizer inside the region the surrogate supports, which lowers the exposure to the ruin without removing it: at any finite reserve the probability of ruin stays positive (Cramér 1930; Lundberg 1903).

## 2. Premise-rigging and the four shapes of deception

In *Administrative Behavior*, Herbert Simon observed that organizational power operates less through explicit commands than through the **control of decision premises** (Simon 1997). An automated system goes beyond offering advice: it pre-determines the categories of thought, the allowable variables, and the objective loss function. The decision is rigged at the level of the premise.

A theory that explains the apparatus from inside the order it serves has taken that order as its frame; the critical account is distinguished by treating the frame's reproduction as the object (Horkheimer 1937). Carnap's criterion of cognitive meaning cuts across this in the opposite direction: a claim is meaningful only if some observable outcome distinguishes it from its negation (Carnap 1932). A premise that no observation could dislodge is not a strong premise but an empty one, which is why a rigged frame is also a brittle frame.

And when algorithmic models assign absolute dogmatic priors ($\mathbb{P}(H) = 0$ or $1$) to unprecedented events, they violate **Cromwell's rule** (formalised by Dennis Lindley (Lindley 1985)): no finite evidence can ever revise a posterior away from zero or one, creating an impervious cognitive trap. In the inquiry framing of [Chapter 43](43-inquiry-openness-multiverse.md), premise-rigging is measured by the **Premise-Rigging Index**:

$$\mathrm{PRI} = \max\bigl(c\_{\mathrm{epi}},\mkern3mu c\_{\mathrm{spill}}\bigr) = \max\left(1 - \frac{w\_{\mathrm{disp}}}{w\_{\mathrm{true}}},\mkern5mu 1 - \frac{D\_{\mathrm{disp}}}{D\_{\mathrm{true}}}\right) \in [0, 1],$$

where $c\_{\mathrm{epi}} = 1 - w\_{\mathrm{disp}} / w\_{\mathrm{true}}$ is the concealed fraction of identification width ($0 \le w\_{\mathrm{disp}} \le w\_{\mathrm{true}}$, $w\_{\mathrm{true}} > 0$) and $c\_{\mathrm{spill}} = 1 - D\_{\mathrm{disp}} / D\_{\mathrm{true}}$ is the concealed fraction of spillover damage ($0 \le D\_{\mathrm{disp}} \le D\_{\mathrm{true}}$, $D\_{\mathrm{true}} > 0$, with $c\_{\mathrm{spill}} = 0$ if $D\_{\mathrm{true}} = 0$). Cutting that index at a declared threshold separates communicative influence from strategic premise-rigging: concealing width or spillover beyond the threshold is the diagnostic, and no further machinery is needed in order to name the regime.

None of these deceptions needs a malicious engineer. Each falls out of pattern compression meeting institutional incentives:

**Fluent wrongness.** Coherence and truth are generated by distinct mechanisms. The machinery that produces syntactic fluency and persuasive explanations operates whether the underlying premises are sound or fabricated: as Emily Bender, Timnit Gebru, Angelina McMillan-Major, and Margaret Mitchell diagnosed in the **stochastic parrot** critique, a model trained strictly on form stitches together plausible sequences without communicative intent, world-grounding, or accountable reference (Bender et al. 2021). Lacking any internal marker that separates verified knowledge from confabulation, fluency is no evidence of truth.

**Metric capture.** Any published metric tied to organizational evaluation becomes an operational target. Optimizing the proxy improves the measurement while degrading the reality it was meant to reflect. As formalised in [Chapter 48](48-reactive-worlds-performativity-goodhart.md), this mapping is strictly one-way: the four shapes of algorithmic deception remain distinct, and *metric capture* alone decomposes into the four Manheim–Garrabrant mechanisms (Goodhart 1975; Manheim & Garrabrant 2018). The dominant capture mechanism is read in a strict priority order over two observed conditions and one measured tail extremeness: strategic gaming decides the classification whenever it is present, causal symptom intervention next, tail extremeness beyond a declared bar third, and regression to the mean of a noisy proxy otherwise.

$$v = \begin{cases} \text{Adversarial} & \text{if strategic gaming is present}, \\ \text{Causal} & \text{else if the symptom is intervened on}, \\ \text{Extremal} & \text{else if the proxy is pushed into its tail}, \\ \text{Regressional} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}.$$

Two boundary cases fix the partition: a quiet proxy whose tail is not being pushed classifies as regressional, which is pure selection on noisy proxy error; and strategic actors gaming the metric classify as adversarial whatever the tail looks like.

**Authority laundering.** A proposition gains unwarranted credibility as it travels upward through a hierarchy. A raw correlation becomes a dashboard entry, then a line in an executive briefing, then policy, shedding its caveats, its sample-size limits and its error margins on the way. James March and Herbert Simon termed this pathology **uncertainty absorption** (March & Simon 1958): inferences are communicated while the evidence that could qualify or contest them is discarded. Standard bureaucratic prose enables the laundering because its grammar allows bare declarative claims with no syntactical slot for provenance. Indigenous languages with obligatory **morphological evidentiality** (Tuyuca and Tariana, documented by Alexandra Aikhenvald (Aikhenvald 2004)) require every declarative verb to carry a morpheme marking the fact as witnessed, deduced, inferred or heard, so the marker cannot be omitted. In the speech-act theory of J. L. Austin and John Searle (Austin 1962; Searle 1969), the laundering conceals a silent reversal in the **direction of fit**: an assertive statement (*word-to-world*) becomes a performative directive (*world-to-word*) that compels subjects to conform to the algorithm's expectations.

**Confident extrapolation.** Outside the support of its historical data, a model does not halt and does not express humility. It computes an output on the nearest available manifold, with the same confidence it shows when interpolating dense observations. Crises and phase transitions live in the unsupported tails, which is what makes this the most dangerous of the four.

## 3. A three-question filter

Any decision-maker confronted with an algorithmic recommendation should apply a simple, non-negotiable
three-question filter before permitting the claim to influence action:

1. *What specific empirical observation would refute this model's claim?* If the architects cannot name a
   concrete outcome that would prove the model wrong, the system is performing unfalsifiable rhetoric rather
   than empirical science.
2. *Who bears the downside if this recommendation fails, and who captures the upside if it succeeds?* When the
   deployers bear zero economic or bodily consequence for false negatives or false positives, the output is
   contaminated by moral hazard, and the asymmetry of exposure is itself primary information.
3. *What physical realities does the archive fail to record, and does this model's validity depend upon them?*
   If the recommendations presuppose the stability of factors that leave no digital footprint, the confidence is
   an artifact of institutional blindness.

Formally, Question 2 requires declaring the decision threshold (the cost–loss threshold of Pauker & Kassirer 1975, and the efficiency-adjusted threshold $p^\ast = C / (\eta L)$ held inside the open unit interval, derived in [Chapter 43](43-inquiry-openness-multiverse.md)), together with the **Expected Value of Perfect Information**:

$$\mathrm{EVPI}(\pi) = \max\bigl(0,\mkern5mu \min(C,\mkern3mu \pi \eta L) - \pi \min(C,\mkern3mu \eta L)\bigr).$$

Under any declared framing the two thresholds diverge whenever the costs diverge. An institution that prices a missed event at the institutional loss $L\_{\mathrm{inst}}$ computes $p^\ast\_{\mathrm{inst}} = C/(\eta L\_{\mathrm{inst}})$, and its value of information is capped by the false-alarm cost $C$, so information is worth little to it. For the affected citizen, who bears a much larger loss $L\_{\mathrm{affected}}$, the threshold falls in proportion to the loss and the value of information rises toward the full cost $C$. Same model, same output, two verdicts. The difference lies entirely in the declared cost pair, and it is the citizen's threshold the decision actually violates — which is what makes the disagreement legitimate enough to enter serious deliberation.

## 4. Hype as an institutional product

The boom-and-bust cycle of artificial intelligence and predictive modeling — waves of extravagant claims followed by bitter "AI winters" — is not a story about public misunderstanding. It is an equilibrium outcome of the political economy of institutional funding. Sponsoring agencies and capital markets reward bold demonstrations of capability; demonstrations attract capital; and no actor in the speculative loop is tasked with auditing what has *not* been achieved, nor what structural vulnerabilities have been accumulated. The funding collapses after the 1957 perceptron hype, the 1966 ALPAC report on machine translation, and the 1973 British Lighthill Report (Lighthill 1973) all show the same exhaustion of institutional patience, and all of them ran on optimism that nothing had grounded.

A dated clinical demonstration of uncalibrated hype is the external validation of the proprietary Epic Sepsis Model by Andrew Wong and colleagues across $27{,}697$ hospitalized patients (Wong et al. 2021): despite vendor marketing of high discrimination, under real clinical prevalence the model missed $67\%$ of sepsis cases while generating an $88\%$ false-alarm rate ($1 - \mathrm{PPV}$), which makes fluent wrongness an arithmetic consequence of base-rate neglect rather than a rhetorical glitch. What prevents such a deployment is a score decomposition rather than a single headline number: the Brier score of a probabilistic forecast splits exactly into reliability, resolution, and uncertainty, and a regularized isotonic (pool-adjacent-violators) decomposition makes the split honest under a monotone recalibration (Dimitriadis, Gneiting & Jordan 2021). A model whose resolution is dwarfed by its reliability term is miscalibrated in a way that no discrimination statistic reveals.

The lesson is the same each time. A discipline survives not by arguing louder but by building artifacts whose failure conditions are published in advance, whose error bars are bounded, and whose institutional responsibilities are sealed before the trial begins.

## 5. Perceptrons, and what a local device structurally cannot see

The story that matters is not the rise and fall of a technology. It is the shape of the mathematical objection that interrupted it. The pattern recurs: a device is demonstrated, the demonstration is generalised into a claim about intelligence, the claim is inflated by funding, and a proof arrives that is narrower than the rebuttal it licenses.

The pre-history of the field had already split along two competing metaphors: Warren McCulloch and Walter Pitts's propositional calculus of threshold logic neurons (McCulloch & Pitts 1943) alongside Vannevar Bush's associative *Memex* trails (Bush 1945), versus symbolic heuristic search. The 1956 summer project at Dartmouth is the conventional opening: McCarthy, Minsky, Shannon and Rochester proposed a programme on "artificial intelligence" on the premise that every feature of learning could be described precisely enough for a machine to simulate it (McCarthy et al. 1955), where Allen Newell and Herbert Simon demonstrated the *Logic Theorist* (Newell & Simon 1956) and Oliver Selfridge soon introduced the parallel feature-demon *Pandemonium* architecture (Selfridge 1959). The institutional accelerant was the Sputnik shock of 1957 and the creation of ARPA in 1958, which funded the field on a demonstration basis rather than a replication basis, the same procurement structure that would later produce the metric-capture equilibrium of §4.

Rosenblatt's perceptron (1957–1958) was a real device: a thresholded linear combination of an input vector, trained by a convergent error-correction rule (Rosenblatt 1958). Its limitation is a fact of geometry. A single-layer threshold element realises an affine half-space, so it separates exactly the dichotomies whose classes are affinely separable. Exclusive disjunction is the smallest counterexample, because no hyperplane in the input space separates it; what the device lacks is not power but a hidden layer, a change of basis in which the dichotomy becomes separable.

Minsky and Papert's *Perceptrons* (1969) made the objection stronger than the XOR folklore suggests, and this is the part popular memory drops. They proved two families of results about **diameter-limited** perceptrons, devices whose decision depends only on a bounded neighbourhood of the input, which is exactly the regime of local feature detectors (Minsky & Papert 1969):

1. **Parity (their Theorem 3.1.1).** The predicate $\psi\_{\mathrm{PARITY}}(x) = 1$ iff an odd number of the $n$
   input bits are on cannot be computed by any diameter-limited perceptron whose diameter is smaller than $n$.
   Any local window sees an even or odd count inside the window and nothing about the rest, so the global parity
   of the whole input is not a function of any bounded collection of local views.
2. **Connectedness (their Theorem 0.8).** The predicate $\psi\_{\mathrm{CONNECTED}}$, true when the set of "on"
   cells of a retina forms one connected component, cannot be computed by any perceptron whose order (the number
   of input cells a single coefficient may read) is bounded independently of the image size. Deciding whether
   two marked cells lie in the same component is a global reachability question, and reachability has no
   constant-size certificate.

The consequence reaches far beyond 1969: **locality of the evidence is not neutral with respect to the question being asked**. A device that reads only neighbouring cells can be arbitrarily accurate on local statistics and still structurally blind to a global predicate. The same obstruction reappears in [Chapter 53](53-layer-macro-coupled-topology-order.md) for network topology: cascade reachability and load redistribution in a coupled network travel along paths rather than sitting inside single nodes, and no purely node-local specification certifies whether a removal propagates across the graph. Yet the theorem also cuts against careless auditors. When the operational target truly is an affinely separable function of local features, a diameter-limited linear device sees everything there is to see, and punishing the model for failing on a non-separable objective that the institution badly posed is a premise error by the auditor. It is also why the syntactic certificate of [Chapter 56](56-layer-formal-computable-type-order.md) has to inspect the *shape* of the command instead of waiting for the semantics of its execution: the property to be verified is not a function of any bounded observation window.

The surrounding institutional record shows how a narrow theorem becomes a broad verdict. Hubert Dreyfus's *What Computers Can't Do* (1972) argued from phenomenology rather than geometry that formal symbol manipulation cannot capture embodied know-how (Dreyfus 1972); the British Science Research Council's Lighthill Report (1973) concluded that the field had failed on its combinatorial promises (Lighthill 1973); and the Mansfield Amendment of 1973 required American defence funding to be justified by direct military relevance. None of those three interventions proved what the subsequent "AI winter" narrative claimed. What happened is the more stochological fact: a funding equilibrium collapsed because no actor was tasked with auditing the distance between the demonstration and the claim.

The technical recovery is equally precise about what had to be added. Hopfield networks (1982) reframed associative memory as the dynamics of an energy function, so that retrieval became descent rather than lookup (Hopfield 1982). Backpropagation, popularised in 1986, supplied the gradient that makes a hidden layer trainable, exactly the change of basis whose absence made XOR unlearnable (Rumelhart et al. 1986). Minsky's *Society of Mind* (1988) drew the moral the 1969 book had implied: general competence is not the property of one homogeneous element but of a structured organisation of specialised, mutually constraining agents (Minsky 1988). That thesis is the ancestor of the architecture this treatise uses, and the reason the aggregation of judgment in [Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md) is treated as a constitutional problem (how agents are composed) rather than an averaging problem.

## 6. The imitation game and the ladder from AGI to ASI

Turing's 1950 paper replaced an unanswerable question with an operational one: instead of asking whether a machine thinks, ask whether an interrogator, deprived of the physical facts, can distinguish its answers from a human's (Turing 1950). The substitution is both a genuine epistemic advance and a genuine trap.

The advance converts a metaphysical dispute into a measurable protocol with a pre-registered question, a stated population of interrogators and a defined loss. The trap is that the protocol measures **indistinguishability of the transcript**, and a transcript is exactly the artifact that fluent wrongness (§2) is built to corrupt. A system optimised against an interrogator's discrimination learns the distribution of interrogator expectations, not the structure of the world; and the interrogator, being a bounded observer, has a finite budget of questions. The imitation game is therefore a test of a *proxy*, exposed like every proxy to the four variants of [Chapter 48](48-reactive-worlds-performativity-goodhart.md).

The contemporary ladder inherits the same structure. Whether framed through Kevin Kelly's macro-historical account of the *technium* as an accumulating system of technological lineages (Kelly 2010), through narrow-task breakthroughs such as AlphaGo's combination of deep policy and value networks with Monte Carlo tree search (Silver et al. 2016), or through the DeepMind levels taxonomy that orders systems from emerging to competent, expert, virtuoso and finally artificial general intelligence, with superintelligence defined as performance beyond the most capable humans across the relevant range (Morris et al. 2023), the same structural distinction governs. As a descriptive scale the ladder is useful; as a target it repeats the error of §4 unless each rung is read through the three axes of this framework. A system can be expert on a closed board or archive tape and blind on the open-world decision tape (no epistemic margin), virtuoso at full utilisation and one shock away from absorption (no vital margin), and superhuman in capability while captured by the institution that funds it (no governance). The ladder measures the first tape; the treatise exists because the other two are not implied by it.

### Name the cost pair before the accusation

A model cannot be accused of deception until the cost pair has been named. Institutional costs put the decision threshold high; the costs borne by the person who is refused put it low; and the gap between the two is not a finding about the model. It is a finding about who was allowed to set the parameter.

That gap changes what counts as a mistake. A filter that is wrong by the institutional threshold is right by the bearer's threshold, and the accusation of blindness is then an accusation about the parameter and not about the machine. Double the institutional loss and the threshold halves, closing part of the gap: the same model, the same output, a different verdict, decided entirely by a number that nobody put in the report.

The taxonomy of metric capture is the other half of the diagnosis, and it is older than the machine-learning literature that rediscovered it (Goodhart 1975; Manheim & Garrabrant 2018). Capture is not an error in the model; it is the model being correct about a target that stopped tracking the goal.

### When the cost pair is not agreed

The thesis fails when the error's cost is asymmetric and unknown, because the threshold is then undefined and the accusation cannot be tested. The procedure is to declare both costs before judging, refuse the accusation while the pair is not agreed between the parties, and treat the disagreement about the pair as the object of the dispute rather than the model's output.

## Exercises

3. Give a case in which a machine accused of not seeing is innocent, and say which premise the accuser violated (answer: a separable target; the model saw what existed, the target was badly posed).
