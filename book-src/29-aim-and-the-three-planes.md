1. Draw the full circuit of a single act of judgment: material world, archive and model,
   institutional deliberation.
2. Explain why cutting the circuit at any one plane produces a specific, predictable failure.
3. Distinguish a good shot that missed from a bad shot that hit.

## 1. Three planes, one circuit

A rural health district: one frontline clinic counts thirty acute febrile cases in a single week. In the material world ($P\_1$), viral transmission and patient dehydration advance in continuous, irreversible time. In the archive ($P\_2$), those thirty cases arrive as delayed batch rows, filtered by clinic operating hours, diagnostic test stockouts, household clustering. In the institution ($P\_3$), the district procurement committee must decide before the last supply truck of the week leaves how many units of its emergency reserve $R\_0$ to dispatch. Break any one link (an unrecorded cluster in $P\_1 \to P\_2$; a point forecast that hides reporting lag in $P\_2 \to P\_3$; a procurement board that discounts the frontline nurse's warning in $P\_3 \to P\_1$) and the whole act of judgment fails.

Every act of consequential judgment passes through three distinct ontic planes, in an inescapable sequence:

The **world** plane ($P\_1$) is physical and social reality: raw load, material degradation, patient illness,
atmospheric turbulence, liquidity shocks. It operates under its own irreversible time and physical constraints,
and it does not consult the model.

The **archive** plane ($P\_2$) is the structured repository of what was measured, recorded, and inferred: the
telemetry, the sensor streams, the database tables, the fitted models, and the intervals they license. This
plane is inevitably thinner than the world, capturing only what an institution had the sensors to detect, the
budget to log, and the vocabulary to categorize.

The **institution** plane ($P\_3$) is deliberation, authority, and accountability: who decides, on what legal
warrant, with what vital reserves at stake, at whose physical risk, and who will answer when the consequences
unfold.

The premise this book works from is that these three planes form **one unified mediation circuit**, and that the irreducible unit of analysis is the closed loop. The indivisibility is inherited, not discovered: Vickers had already described regulation as the appreciation of a whole situation before any part of it is decided (Vickers 1965), and Beer and Simon argued it from viable systems and from bounded rationality. What this program adds is that the loop is executable and measured, and not only described.

```
                  +-----------------------------------+
                  |         INSTITUTION (P3)          |
                  |  Deliberation, Authority, Reserves|
                  +-----------------+-----------------+
                                   / \
                                  /   \
                  Decision & Act /     \ Evidentiary Intake
                     Transit T6 /       \ Transit T4
                               /         \
                              v           \
     +-------------------------+         +-------------------------+
     |        WORLD (P1)       |         |       ARCHIVE (P2)      |
     | Material Process & Loss |-------->| Registers, Fitted Models|
     +-------------------------+ Transit | & Partial Bounds        |
                                   T3    +-------------------------+
```

Across those planes run six ordered crossings of distinct planes (three direct loop transits and three reflexive locks that run back along the same edges) plus one integral closure. Each of the $3 + 3 + 1 = 7$ transits carries one guard site, named here by transit and by what it guards:

1. **Direct loop transits**:
   - **Observe** ($P\_1 \to P\_2$, T3): *Observation and Transduction*. World events become archive rows under
     selection mechanisms, filtering thresholds, and measurement error.
   - **Formalize** ($P\_2 \to P\_3$, T4): *Inference and Evidence*. Models are estimated, partial-identification
     intervals are constructed, and hypothesis tests are evaluated.
   - **Perform** ($P\_3 \to P\_1$, T6): *Intervention and Risk Imposition*. Decisions are executed, resources
     are committed, and vital reserves are deployed.
2. **Reflexive locks**: the same three edges traversed against the loop, each auditing a premise that the
   corresponding direct transit silently presumed:
   - **Frame** ($P\_3 \to P\_2$, T1): inquiry framing is fixed before the archive is queried: premise-rigging,
     the cost–loss threshold, the Cromwell reserve, and openness to the question that was never asked.
   - **Test for severity** ($P\_2 \to P\_1$, T2): the bounds and hypotheses carried out of the archive are tested
     against the world, so that analytical choices cannot be p-hacked into place and mistaken for evidence.
   - **Temporal** ($P\_1 \to P\_3$, T5): the physical vital lock, verifying that the resilience basin is intact and
     that Kingman queue congestion still leaves $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} \ge \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{recovery}}$.
3. **Integral closure**:
   - **Liability** (T7): triadic closure
     $M\_{\mathrm{Leontief}} = \min(S\_{\mathrm{epi}}, S\_{\mathrm{vital}}, S\_{\mathrm{gov}})$.

The closure at the liability site enforces

$$M\_{\mathrm{Leontief}} = \min\bigl(S\_{\mathrm{epi}},\mkern3mu S\_{\mathrm{vital}},\mkern3mu S\_{\mathrm{gov}}\bigr) \le S\_i \quad \forall i \in \lbrace\mathrm{epi}, \mathrm{vital}, \mathrm{gov}\rbrace.$$

Two boundary cases show what the minimum does. Let the governance margin go to zero with the epistemic and vital margins held high: circuit integrity collapses to zero while the compensatory arithmetic mean still reports a majority of the two surviving axes. Set all three margins equal, and the two functional forms coincide.

## 2. Cutting the circuit and missing data mechanisms

Cut the circuit at any single plane and you get a pathology you can recognize on sight:

**World-only amnesia ($P\_1$ isolated).** Action occurs without memory or institutional reflection, and every
crisis is confronted as if it had no history. The institution fails not from lack of effort but from structural
amnesia: the same error is paid for repeatedly, and no archive accumulates to prevent its recurrence.

**Archive-only irrelevance ($P\_2$ isolated).** Models are fitted with exquisite rigor, bounds are computed,
intervals are tight, and papers are published. Yet the institution never connects its decisions to the findings.
The failure is *irrelevance with high production values*: the most seductive pathology, because it mimics
scientific excellence while catastrophe proceeds unchecked.

**Institution-only autocracy ($P\_3$ isolated).** Power decides by administrative fiat. When the archive conflicts
with political convenience, the archive is silenced, re-baselined, or doctored; alternatively, it documents an
impending rupture and no official has the standing to halt the operation. The failure is deliberate blindness
ending in preventable ruin.

The three severances correspond to the three policy ablations worked through in [Chapter 42](42-sequential-loop-naive-agent.md): an agent that acts on an uncalibrated archive, an agent that computes exact bounds with no physical reserve slack, and an agent that exposes the reserve to institutional pillaging. In each case the Leontief minimum collapses toward zero and the terminal reserve reaches the ruin boundary.

At the world-to-archive transit (T3)
three classic missingness mechanisms formalized by Donald Rubin (Rubin 1976):

- *Missing Completely at Random (MCAR)*: The probability of an event being recorded is independent of both observed
  and unobserved variables. This is the only benign regime where listwise deletion leaves point estimates unbiased.
- *Missing at Random (MAR)*: Missingness depends on observed features within the database schema. While recoverable
  through conditional modeling, the assumption of MAR is strictly untestable from the data itself.
- *Missing Not at Random (MNAR)*: Missingness depends directly on the unobserved value itself: the most severely
  depleted reserves go unreported, the most distressed patients leave the triage queue, the most toxic loans are
  concealed. Under MNAR, point estimation is impossible; the honest analyst must construct partial-identification
  bounds under worst-case assumptions.

To prevent historical revisionism and hindsight bias across the archive transit, sound systems enforce **bitemporal data architecture** (Croushore & Stark 2001): every record stores both *valid time* (the physical timestamp when the event occurred in $P\_1$) and *transaction time* (the timestamp when the archive $P\_2$ recorded or amended it), so that no reading of the record can show a value that was not yet published at the moment of the decision. A real-time vintage archive read this way exposes the revision structure of a statistical release: an initial-to-latest revision of non-trivial size, a far larger maximum absolute revision in a few release rounds, and no look-ahead leaks once the transaction time is respected.

And where entire subpopulations are missing from the register, Anne Chao's dark-archive species estimator (Chao 1984) calculates the number of completely unobserved classes $\hat{n}\_{00} = f\_1^2 / (2 f\_2)$ from the count of singletons ($f\_1$) and doubletons ($f\_2$), bounding the silence of the vault across two contrasting regimes:

- **Sparse singleton-heavy archive:** when failure modes are observed once far more often than twice, the estimator returns a large unseen-class count, and the archive is mostly dark.
- **Saturated doubleton-rich archive:** when singletons and doubletons arrive in comparable numbers, the lower bound contracts toward the observed count, and the archive is nearly complete.

## 3. Good aim, bad outcome

Suppose an archer executes a technically flawless shot under optimal stance and aim, but an unforeseen squall
deflects the arrow into the sand (Cicero 45 BCE). Was the action virtuous?

In *Apt Belief* and *Judgment and Agency*, Ernest Sosa introduced the **AAA framework** of virtue epistemology (Sosa 2007; Sosa 2015), which distinguishes three
evaluative levels:

1. **Accuracy** (*skopós*): Was the outcome successful? Did the arrow hit the bullseye?
2. **Adroitness** (*télos*): Was the performance executed with genuine competence, following valid rules of craft? This is Antipater's *télos*, the archer doing everything within his power.
3. **Aptness**: Was the performance accurate *because* it was adroit?

Only when accuracy is causally produced by adroitness does the act attain *aptness*, the one performance worthy of institutional credit. Sosa's framework and Jonathan Baron and John Hershey's experimental demonstration of *outcome bias* (Baron & Hershey 1988) together form a deterministic $2 \times 2$ bitemporal table over two boolean inputs, whether the performance was adroit ex ante and whether it was accurate ex post (worked out in [Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md)):

- $(\text{true}, \text{true}) \mapsto$ **fully apt** (accurate *because* adroit);
- $(\text{true}, \text{false}) \mapsto$ **adroit, unlucky** (Antipater's master archer deflected by an exogenous shock);
- $(\text{false}, \text{true}) \mapsto$ **lucky** (an unbuffered gamble saved by moral luck, which outcome-biased audits falsely promote);
- $(\text{false}, \text{false}) \mapsto$ **inapt and inaccurate**.

Above these three sits a fourth, sovereign grade of judgment: the agent who is adroit, knows the limits of his
competence, **and knows when not to shoot**. Declining to release the arrow when the target is shrouded in fog or
has been corrupted is not cowardice but meta-cognitive virtue; an agent that cannot lower the bow will be ordered
to fire into darkness and will obediently cause catastrophe.
Historically, even when all three planes physically exist (telemetry in $P\_1$, engineering charts in $P\_2$, a formal launch review in $P\_3$), the circuit still collapses if the institutional plane discounts frontline warnings, the exact pathology Miranda Fricker termed **testimonial injustice** (Fricker 2007).

## 4. The circuit as the unit of judgment

Because judgment is a property of the full circuit, an empirical claim that addresses only one plane is
structurally incomplete:

- *"Our machine learning model predicts a low probability of system failure"*. A bare archive-plane
  ($P\_2$) statement: it says nothing about whether that probability is survivable by the physical grid ($P\_1$), nor who
  is legally authorized to disconnect load ($P\_3$).
- *"We maintain a fixed emergency inventory in the warehouse"*. A bare world-plane ($P\_1$) statement: it
  says nothing about whether supply chain replenishment latency ($P\_3$) can restore that stock before exhaustion.
- *"The safety board unanimously approved the operational waiver."* A bare institution-plane ($P\_3$)
  statement: it says nothing about whether the physical premises presented to the board were grounded in
  verified archive telemetry ($P\_2$).

A complete stochological judgment synthesizes all three: an honest, bounded estimate from the archive, a verified physical survival condition in the world, and an accountable, pre-registered decision in the institution. The three foundational ethical refusals of [Chapter 27](27-what-judging-is.md) ($R\_1, R\_2, R\_3$) do not map 1-to-1 onto a single isolated axis of the Nuclear Triad. Each refusal spans the mediation circuit across two scales:

- **Refusal $R\_1$ (Renouncing control over reception):** Spans the framing site ($S\_{\mathrm{epi}}$, Adorno's *Non-Identical* (Adorno 1966) and interpretive openness), the temporal site ($S\_{\mathrm{vital}}$, Canguilhem's dialogic margin of tolerance (Canguilhem 1943/1966)), and the performative site ($S\_{\mathrm{gov}}$, the Strathern/Goodhart mirror-surveillance penalty (Strathern 1997)).
- **Refusal $R\_2$ (Exposing medium adulteration and pact fraud):** Spans the archive site ($S\_{\mathrm{epi}}$, corrupted evidentiary signals), the temporal site ($S\_{\mathrm{vital}}$, Batesonian double-bind oscillation and its escape (Bateson 1972)), and the liability site ($S\_{\mathrm{gov}}$, the Arendtian promise lifecycle (Arendt 1958) and the Girardian distinction between scapegoat and structural liability (Girard 1986)).
- **Refusal $R\_3$ (Refusing to turn cognitive influence into manipulation):** Spans the framing site ($S\_{\mathrm{epi}}$, Simon's decision premises against premise-rigging (Simon 1997)), the performative site ($S\_{\mathrm{gov}}$, Habermas's communicative versus concealed strategic action (Habermas 1984)), and the liability site ($S\_{\mathrm{gov}}$, the Spinozist and Beerian algedonic entitlement (Spinoza 2007; Beer 1972)).

At the interpersonal scale, the dialogic reception score $S\_{\mathrm{rec}} = \omega\_{\mathrm{comm}} (1 - \lambda\_e e\_{\mathrm{norm}})(1 - \lambda\_P P\_{\mathrm{surv}}) \in [0, 1]$ takes its maximum under open dialogue and collapses toward zero under panoptic surveillance, and a two-agent dialogue instantiation carries this exact three-plane loop between two interacting agents before it scales to macro-institutional reserves.

This indivisibility grounds the Spinozist foundation of the institutional plane. In the political ontology developed in [Chapter 20](20-legitimacy-and-institutions.md), an institution is never an autonomous, transcendent entity endowed with alienated sovereignty (*potestas*). It is the organized manifestation of the constituent power (*potentia*) of the multitude (*multitudo*), coordinating collective preservation (*conatus*) under shared uncertainty (Negri 1981). When the institutional plane is isolated from the world and the archive, authority decays into command without feedback; when it is eliminated entirely under naive assembleism, vital reserves are pillaged under uncoordinated predation. An immanent institution integrates all three planes: deliberation is bounded by verified archive telemetry, authority is checked by horizontal contestability, and common vital reserves are preserved against irreversible ruin.

### Cutting the circuit

Cut the circuit at any plane and the failure is the one that plane was there to prevent. The integrity of judgment is therefore a minimum, and a minimum is what a mean cannot fake.

The divergence this argument predicts is isolated by the full $2 \times 2 \times 2$ cube whose ratio is derived in [Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md) and whose eight corners are worked through in [Chapter 50](50-methodological-families-reflexivity.md): each plane is held present or removed while the other two stay intact, so the corners compare two safeguards preserved against one removed. Within any corner the seven guard sites partition as $3 + 2 + 2 = 7$ across $(S\_{\mathrm{epi}},\ S\_{\mathrm{vital}},\ S\_{\mathrm{gov}})$, and the recognizer issues a collapse verdict as soon as any single guard margin falls below zero, however high the other two stand. The mean therefore misleads most exactly where a plane is severed, which is the case in which a dashboard is most likely to be read.

Three direct transits (observe, formalize, perform), three reflexive locks (frame, test for severity, temporal) and one integral three-plane closure (liability) give $3 + 3 + 1 = 7$, whose triadic combinations are developed in [Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md). The count is what makes the circuit checkable: a plane that is missing severs its guard sites, and the syntax of the trajectory fails with it. The vocabulary is older than the formalism: aiming as a conjectural art with its own standard of correctness is Plato's problem in the *Philebus*, and the three planes are a later name for what he had already separated. The method that performs the separation has a name in the same dialogues: dialectic, the collection of a scattered kind into one and its division again at the joints, which Plato treats as the condition of saying anything definite about a subject matter (Plato c. 360 BCE, *Sophist* 253d; *Phaedrus* 265d–266c). The seven sites are a division of that kind, and the claim that they are exhaustive is a claim the method allows to fail, which is why the count is checkable rather than stipulated.

### When the full circuit is an overcost

The circuit is mandatory when the action is irreversible and the institutional plan carries a veto, and not mandatory in a reversible, single-plan action, where demanding all three planes is a cost with no corresponding risk. The procedure is a reversibility test first: if the action is cheap to undo, closing the plane of the world is enough, and the other two are deferred rather than waived.
