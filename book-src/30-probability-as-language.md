## By the end of this chapter you can

1. Write down the axioms of probability, distinguish precise coherent odds from imprecise credal intervals, and say what each condition forbids.
2. Update a belief with Bayes' rule inside a declared frame and read the result as conditional evidence rather than an intrinsic property of the world.
3. Explain why an *anytime-valid* statement differs from a fixed-horizon statement, and where ordinal invariance and single-case probability stop.

## 1. The axioms and what they exclude

Imagine a quality-control laboratory assaying specimens every Friday to test whether a new filtration membrane reduces contaminant drift. Instead of locking a single sample horizon, the director checks a textbook $95\unicode{x25}$ confidence interval each week at nominal level $\alpha = 0.05$ and halts the moment it excludes zero. Repeated peeking with fixed-horizon intervals drives the true false-positive rate far above $\alpha$ (Howard et al. 2021; Ramdas et al. 2023). Only anytime-valid sequential bounds—nonnegative test supermartingales and, for composite nulls, $e$-processes—keep Type I error controlled under arbitrary unbounded optional stopping without a pre-fixed horizon (Ville 1939; Ramdas et al. 2023). The physical specimens ($P\_1$) were honest; the mistake sat inside the archive's temporal grammar ($P\_2$).

In [Chapter 15](15-probability-as-a-measure.md), we built probability as a measure on a triple $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$, where $\Omega$ is the space of possible worlds and $\mathcal{F}$ is the collection of askable events. Andrey Kolmogorov (1933) anchors those conditions on a pre-registered $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{F} \subseteq 2^\Omega$ (fixed at framing lock `T1` of [Chapter 29](29-aim-and-the-three-planes.md)). His three axioms act as consistency rules forbidding incoherent books of beliefs:

- **Non-negativity** ($\mathbb{P}(A) \ge 0$ for all $A \in \mathcal{F}$) forbids assigning negative plausibility below impossibility.
- **Normalization** ($\mathbb{P}(\Omega) = 1$) forbids leaving unforeseen outcomes outside an exhaustive partition.
- **Countable additivity** ($\mathbb{P}\bigl(\bigcup\_{i=1}^\infty A\_i\bigr) = \sum\_{i=1}^\infty \mathbb{P}(A\_i)$ for pairwise disjoint $A\_1, A\_2, \dots \in \mathcal{F}$) forbids mismatching a whole event with the sum of its disjoint pieces.

Different mathematical traditions derive slightly weaker or stronger rules from these premises. Assuming universal comparability and smooth $C^2$ combining laws, Richard Cox (1946) translates Boolean plausibility onto **finitely** additive probability up to monotone rescaling. As Joseph Halpern proved, however, Cox's original axioms fail on finite Boolean domains without extra structure and require an explicit density/richness axiom even in infinite ones (Halpern 1999). Likewise, two-sided fair-betting coherence (Ramsey 1926; de Finetti 1937; [Chapter 15](15-probability-as-a-measure.md) §2) and preference coherence (Savage 1954) establish **finite** additivity alone. Extending to infinite unions requires Kolmogorov's (1933) separate Continuity Axiom ($B\_m \downarrow \emptyset \implies \mathbb{P}(B\_m) \to 0$).

Probability therefore speaks a **plural** language with three legitimate meanings:

1. **Physical frequency or propensity:** tied to repeatable mechanisms or stable reference classes ([Chapter 15](15-probability-as-a-measure.md) §2).
2. **Complete subjective credence:** exact odds derived from two-sided fair bets.
3. **Imprecise credal intervals:** lower and upper bounds $[\underline{P}(A), \overline{P}(A)]$ spanning a set $\mathcal{P}$ of admissible probability laws (Walley 1991).

Whenever ambiguity blocks exact odds—as Daniel Ellsberg (1961) demonstrated against Savage and John Maynard Keynes (1921) argued for partially ordered evidential weight—one-sided betting coherence replaces single numbers with intervals. That discipline enforces conjugacy $\overline{P}(A) = 1 - \underline{P}(A^c)$ and superadditivity $\underline{P}(A \cup B) \ge \underline{P}(A) + \underline{P}(B)$ for disjoint events without exposing the agent to a sure loss (Walley 1991). Nor is full Bayesian updating always best: when datasets are tiny or shifting rapidly, simple frugal heuristics often outperform full probabilistic models (Brighton & Gigerenzer 2012). Above all, probability supplies an evidentiary syntax—separate from consequence loss matrices—not a decision oracle.

## 2. Measurement scales, invariance, and the dialectical penumbra

Numbers enter statistical archives carrying scale rules fixed at formalization guard `T4` ([Chapter 29](29-aim-and-the-three-planes.md)). Those commitments separate nominal labels, ordinal ranks, interval differences, and ratio quantities with true zeros (Stevens 1946; Krantz et al. 1971; formalized in [Chapter 33](33-measuring-and-erring.md) §2). Averaging ordinal triage grades (`severe = 3`, `moderate = 2`, `mild = 1`) invents equal spacing absent from clinical reality.

Categorization also inhabits Nicholas Georgescu-Roegen's (1971) **dialectical penumbra**, echoing Heraclitus's unity of opposites (Heraclitus *c.* 500 BCE, DK B88). Between sharp labels ("safe" versus "unsafe", "healthy" versus "diseased") lies a contested boundary zone $[c\_L, c\_U)$ holding **penumbra mass** $\mathbb{P}(c\_L \le X < c\_U)$. As Georges Canguilhem (1943/1966) argued, imposing an arbitrary cutoff $c \in [c\_L, c\_U)$ mistakes a convention for biological law. It erases physiological slack and shifts reported counts whenever framing (`T1`) or scale (`T4`) rules move. Rigid true–false bivalence over those boundary zones reflects historical abstraction rather than timeless nature—traceable from the secondary binary break against primary cosmotheism (Assmann 2010) to civic and monetary abstraction in the early Greek *pólis* (Vernant 1982; Sohn-Rethel 1978; Seaford 2004). Treating both probability and logic as situated human languages (da Costa 1980) keeps formal boundaries answerable to the material penumbra rather than mistaking categories for ontology.

For two ordinal cohorts $X$ and $Y$ ($m, n \ge 1$), rank comparisons stay intact under any shared strictly increasing recoding $f$. Rather than inventing distances between ranks, we compare pairs directly through the empirical **dominance index** $\hat{\Delta}\_{\mathrm{MW}}(X, Y) = \mathbb{P}\_{\mathrm{emp}}(X > Y) + \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{P}\_{\mathrm{emp}}(X = Y) \in [0, 1]$, estimating $\Delta\_{\mathrm{MW}} = \mathbb{P}(X > Y) + \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{P}(X = Y)$ ([Chapter 33](33-measuring-and-erring.md) §3 formalizes this Mann–Whitney index and proves why ordinal means flip signs). Because $\Delta\_{\mathrm{MW}}$ is only a pairwise rank average—weaker than full stochastic dominance and sometimes intransitive across three cohorts—analysts must report boundary penumbra mass $\mathbb{P}(c\_L \le X < c\_U)$ beside it.

## 3. Conditioning and belief revision

Within a pre-registered event space $\mathcal{F}$ (`T1`), learning an observed event $E\_{\mathrm{ev}} \in \mathcal{F}$ with $\mathbb{P}(E\_{\mathrm{ev}}) > 0$ (distinct from §4's *e*-value $E$) updates belief by normalizing probabilities over the surviving possibilities via Bayes' rule:

$$\mathbb{P}(H \mid E\_{\mathrm{ev}}) = \frac{\mathbb{P}(E\_{\mathrm{ev}} \mid H)\mkern3mu\mathbb{P}(H)}{\mathbb{P}(E\_{\mathrm{ev}} \mid H)\mkern3mu\mathbb{P}(H) + \mathbb{P}(E\_{\mathrm{ev}} \mid \neg H)\mkern3mu\mathbb{P}(\neg H)}.$$

In plain words, your posterior belief equals the prior weight on hypothesis $H$ scaled by how likely event $E\_{\mathrm{ev}}$ is under $H$, divided by the total likelihood of $E\_{\mathrm{ev}}$ across both $H$ and its opposite.

To stop dogmatic zeros from paralyzing learning, **Cromwell's rule** clamps prior probabilities inside $[\varepsilon\_{\mathrm{floor}}, 1 - \varepsilon\_{\mathrm{floor}}]$ ($0 < \varepsilon\_{\mathrm{floor}} < 1/2$ for a binary pair $(H, \neg H)$, and $p\_i \in [\varepsilon\_{\mathrm{floor}}, 1 - (m - 1)\varepsilon\_{\mathrm{floor}}]$ with $0 < \varepsilon\_{\mathrm{floor}} < 1/m$ across $m \ge 2$ disjoint hypotheses; Lindley 1985; [Chapter 15](15-probability-as-a-measure.md) §4). Yet Bayes updates only hypotheses already inside $\mathcal{F}$. Missing mechanisms stay invisible until **attested counter-inductive reopening** enlarges the frame ($\mathcal{F}\_t \subsetneq \mathcal{F}\_{t+1}^\ast$; [Chapter 15](15-probability-as-a-measure.md) §1; [Chapter 32](32-the-question-before-the-data.md)).

Notice how strongly the base rate $\pi = \mathbb{P}(H) \in (0, 1)$ governs any positive test result. Let a diagnostic test have sensitivity $\mathrm{TPR} = \mathrm{Se} = \mathbb{P}(+ \mid H) \in (0, 1]$ and specificity $\mathrm{Sp} = \mathbb{P}(- \mid \neg H) \in (0, 1)$, giving false-positive rate $\mathrm{FPR} = 1 - \mathrm{Sp} \in (0, 1)$. Before trusting a positive alarm, ask what share of positive triggers are true cases—the **positive predictive value** ($\mathrm{PPV}$):

$$\mathbb{P}(H \mid +) = \frac{\mathrm{Se}\mkern3mu\pi}{\mathrm{Se}\mkern3mu\pi + (1 - \mathrm{Sp})(1 - \pi)} = \left(1 + \frac{1 - \mathrm{Sp}}{\mathrm{Se}}\mkern3mu\frac{1 - \pi}{\pi}\right)^{-1}.$$

Read aloud, false alarms outnumber true alerts ($\mathbb{P}(H \mid +) < 1/2$) whenever **prior odds** fall below the inverse likelihood ratio: $\frac{\pi}{1 - \pi} < \frac{1 - \mathrm{Sp}}{\mathrm{Se}}$. Notice it is prior odds against that likelihood ratio—not $\pi$ alone—that decides alert reliability. Alan Turing and I. J. Good measured the test's own evidential push in **decibans**, $W = 10\log\_{10}(\mathrm{TPR}/\mathrm{FPR})$ (Good 1950, 1979). Posterior log-odds in decibans equal prior log-odds $10\log\_{10}\bigl(\pi/(1 - \pi)\bigr)$ plus $W$, so even strong test weights cannot save an ultra-rare base rate. Translating conditional rates into natural counts restores human clarity (Gigerenzer & Hoffrage 1995), while at severe testing guard `T2`, Deborah Mayo (2018) argues from severe-testing criteria and John Ioannidis (2005) models via positive predictive value that nominal $p < 0.05$ without severe design or high pre-study odds routinely yields false positives (empirically borne out in Open Science Collaboration 2015).

## 4. Evidence that accumulates: e-processes and anytime validity

In [Chapter 15](15-probability-as-a-measure.md) (§4), we met martingales and Ville's inequality as the mathematics of fair betting. Why does that matter for scientific testing? Fixed-horizon *p*-values require pre-committing sample size $n$; peeking each Friday and stopping early breaks error control (Howard et al. 2021). Think of an **$e$-value** instead as a bet placed against a null hypothesis $\mathcal{P}\_0$: if $\mathcal{P}\_0$ is true, no fair betting strategy can expect to multiply your starting dollar past $1$, no matter when you choose to cash out.

Formally, at severity lock `T2`, an *$e$-value* for composite null $\mathcal{P}\_0$ is a non-negative variable $E \ge 0$ satisfying $\mathbb{E}\_P[E] \le 1$ for every $P \in \mathcal{P}\_0$ (Ramdas et al. 2023). Across a growing archive filtration $(\mathcal{F}\_t)\_{t \ge 0}$, an adapted non-negative process $(E\_t)\_{t \ge 0}$ is an **$e$-process** for $\mathcal{P}\_0$ if $\mathbb{E}\_P[E\_\tau] \le 1$ at **every** stopping time $\tau$ and every $P \in \mathcal{P}\_0$. Every non-negative test supermartingale starting at $E\_0 \le 1$ is an *$e$*-process, though composite nulls admit *$e$*-processes formed as minima or mixtures rather than single supermartingales (Ramdas et al. 2023). For any false-alarm tolerance $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ with $E\_0 \le 1$, maximal martingale control guarantees anytime-valid Type I protection without alpha-spending schedules (Ville 1939):

$$\sup\_{P \in \mathcal{P}\_0} \mathbb{P}\_P\left(\exists t \ge 1:\ E\_t \ge \frac{1}{\alpha}\right) \le \alpha.$$

In plain words, under any law in the null family, the chance that cumulative betting wealth ever reaches $1/\alpha$ at *any* time $t$ never exceeds $\alpha$.

That anytime-valid guarantee carries an honest width cost compared to a single fixed-sample look. To see how wide the premium is, suppose observations $X\_i$ have mean $\mu$, running average $\bar{x}\_t = \frac{1}{t}\sum\_{i=1}^t X\_i$ ($t \ge 1$), and independent or martingale-difference centered increments $X\_i - \mu$ that are conditionally $\sigma$-sub-Gaussian given past history $\mathcal{F}\_{i-1}$ ($\mathbb{E}[\exp(\lambda(X\_i - \mu)) \mid \mathcal{F}\_{i-1}] \le \exp(\lambda^2\sigma^2/2)$ for all $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$) with known proxy scale $\sigma > 0$ (remembering that heavy-tailed jumps studied in [Chapter 31](31-distribution-and-moment.md) violate sub-Gaussian tails and require bounded or robust detectors). Pick a significance level $\alpha \in (0, 0.5]$ and a **dimensionless** prior variance ratio $\tau\_{\mathrm{mix}}^2 = \sigma\_0^2/\sigma^2 > 0$. Howard et al. (2021) derive the normal-mixture confidence-sequence radius $r\_t(\alpha)$ such that $|\bar{x}\_t - \mu| < r\_t(\alpha)$ holds uniformly over all $t \ge 1$ with probability at least $1 - \alpha$:

$$r\_t(\alpha) = \sigma \sqrt{\frac{2(t\tau\_{\mathrm{mix}}^2 + 1)}{t^2\tau\_{\mathrm{mix}}^2}\mkern3mu\ln\\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{t\tau\_{\mathrm{mix}}^2 + 1}}{\alpha}\right)}.$$

Read across time $t$, this radius stays $\sigma$-scale-invariant and behaves cleanly at every step:

1. **At $t = 1$:** the mixture keeps $r\_1(\alpha)$ comparable to $\sigma$, avoiding fake single-point narrowness.
2. **For every finite $t \ge 1$:** $r\_t(\alpha) > z\_{1-\alpha/2}\sigma/\sqrt{t}$ strictly; that gap is the exact price of allowing optional stopping.
3. **As $t \to \infty$:** $r\_t(\alpha) \to 0$ at rate $\mathcal{O}\bigl(\sqrt{\ln(t)/t}\bigr)$, so its **ratio** to fixed-sample $z\_{1-\alpha/2}\sigma/\sqrt{t}$ diverges slowly as $\Theta(\sqrt{\ln t})$ rather than tending to one.

An *$e$*-process can also track betting wealth directly. For conditionally $1$-sub-Gaussian standardized observations $z\_t$ with running average $\bar{z}\_T = \frac{1}{T}\sum\_{t=1}^T z\_t$ ($T \ge 1$) and a pre-selected bet $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$, cumulative wealth is $E\_T = \prod\_{t=1}^T \exp(\lambda z\_t - \frac{1}{2}\lambda^2)$ ([Chapter 15](15-probability-as-a-measure.md) §4). Taking the log-average rate gives $\frac{1}{T}\ln E\_T = \lambda(\bar{z}\_T - \frac{1}{2}\lambda)$: for fixed $\lambda$, whenever realized drift $\bar{z}\_T > 0$, wealth grows ($E\_T > 1$) across $0 < \lambda < 2\bar{z}\_T$, breaks even ($E\_T = 1$) at $\lambda \in \lbrace 0, 2\bar{z}\_T\rbrace$, reaches its retrospective parabola peak at $\lambda^\ast = \bar{z}\_T$ (whereas valid anytime-valid Kelly bets must be predictable—pre-committed against expected drift $\mu$ or updated via mixtures—never plugged in ex post as $\bar{z}\_T$), and decays toward zero as $T \to \infty$ under fixed $\bar{z}\_T > 0$ only if you over-bet past twice the drift ($\lambda > 2\bar{z}\_T$) or bet on the wrong sign ($\lambda < 0$), while any fixed $\lambda > 0$ decays when $\bar{z}\_T \le 0$.

This wealth framing also disciplines hypothesis patching. Operationalizing Imre Lakatos's qualitative demarcation (Lakatos 1970)—without attributing a formula to Lakatos—Stochology splits a pre-registered ceiling of $K\_{\max} \in \mathbb{N}\_0$ *ad hoc* scope-narrowing patches on any valid *e*-value ($E\_T \ge 0$ with $\mathbb{E}\_P[E\_T] \le 1$ for all $P \in \mathcal{P}\_0$) via $E\_T^{\mathrm{adj}} = E\_T / (K\_{\max} + 1)$ ([Chapter 32](32-the-question-before-the-data.md) §2). Pre-committing $K\_{\max}$ preserves Ville's inequality across the $K\_{\max} + 1$ branches because $\sum\_{j=0}^{K\_{\max}} (K\_{\max} + 1)^{-1} = 1$. By contrast, because $\sum\_{k=0}^\infty (k + 1)^{-1}$ diverges, un-preregistered post-hoc discounts $1/(k+1)$ destroy anytime validity. The fixed divisor penalizes opportunistic narrowing ($\mathcal{F}\_{t+1} \subset \mathcal{F}\_t$), while sparing attested counter-inductive reopening ($\mathcal{F}\_t \subsetneq \mathcal{F}\_{t+1}^\ast$; [Chapter 15](15-probability-as-a-measure.md) §1) once refuted assumptions are replaced and tested on fresh data.

## 5. Probability is a language, not an intrinsic property

Treating probability as an intrinsic physical constant mistakes a frame-relative summary for a natural object. Under a shared identifiable model and compatible priors, accumulating data pull Bayesian posteriors together. Yet two honest observers can remain apart—or diverge permanently—whenever samples are finite, parameters are only partially identified (formalized in [Chapter 35](35-inference-and-evidence.md)), recording frames $\mathcal{F}$ or intake selection mechanisms differ ([Chapter 29](29-aim-and-the-three-planes.md)), or priors sit on mutually singular supports.

John Maynard Keynes (1921) separated probability balance from **evidential weight**, and Peter Walley (1991) formalized updating over lower and upper previsions $[\underline{P}(A), \overline{P}(A)]$ when $\underline{P}(E\_{\mathrm{ev}}) > 0$ (where $E\_{\mathrm{ev}}$ denotes the conditioning event, distinct from §4's *e*-value $E$). Building on that distinction, Stochology indexes **intra-schema evidential weight** as $V\bigl(n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho}), n\_0\bigr) = n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho}) / \bigl(n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho}) + n\_0\bigr) \in [0, 1)$ (formalized across [Chapter 33](33-measuring-and-erring.md)–[Chapter 34](34-sample-population-archive.md) and [Appendix A, *Mathematical Reference*](appendix-a-mathematics.md) §A.1), comparing effective sample size $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho}) \ge 0$ against a declared ignorance scale $n\_0 > 0$. Even when $V \to 1$, weight inside a schema stays completely blind to categories excluded at framing (`T1`) before recording begins ([Chapter 33](33-measuring-and-erring.md) §4). Stochology therefore forbids silencing policy disputes with "the" objective probability: disagreements decompose cleanly into priors, loss tables, scales, and evidential weight.

### What fixed-sample peeking deletes, and the cognitive ceiling on monitoring

Using fixed-sample confidence intervals under optional stopping secretly deletes §4's $\Theta(\sqrt{\ln t})$ width premium (only strict single-look plans pay zero stopping penalty; [Chapter 15](15-probability-as-a-measure.md) §4), just as undocumented tweaks conceal the deciban penalty $10\log\_{10}(K\_{\max} + 1)$ ($10$ decibans equal one $10:1$ likelihood ratio). Following [Chapter 29](29-aim-and-the-three-planes.md) §4 and [Chapter 36](36-decision-loss-threshold.md), `T1`–`T4` framing, prior, and scale checks stay off-shift so live emergency teams handle only prioritized, natural-frequency alerts.

### When the event is single and not repeatable

Frequentist probabilities—and two-sample empirical rank dominance $\hat{\Delta}\_{\mathrm{MW}}(X, Y)$ ($m, n \ge 1$)—do not apply to a unique, unrepeatable indivisible commitment lacking any validated reference class. Unlike credal intervals $[\underline{P}, \overline{P}]$ that declare structural bounds without claiming repeatable frequency, inventing a point probability on a one-off leap exports unpriced tail risk onto affected external reception perimeters ([Chapter 29](29-aim-and-the-three-planes.md) §1, §4). Honest judgment names that refusal, states what bounds hold, and passes indivisible conflicts to decision thresholds and institutional safeguards ([Chapter 36, *Decision, Loss, Threshold*](36-decision-loss-threshold.md)).
