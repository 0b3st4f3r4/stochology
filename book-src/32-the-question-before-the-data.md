## 1. Every question arrives framed

Consider a national labor ministry that commissions an empirical evaluation of an automated youth job-placement platform. Months before the first row of data is logged, the procurement tender defines "program success" as thirty-day software module completion among enrollees who possess residential broadband, and sets the reporting horizon to a single fiscal quarter. Displaced youths without home internet never enter the denominator, and trainees whose short-term placements collapse in month four lie beyond the observation window. The favorable conclusion is not fabricated by corrupt statisticians after the fact; it is engineered into the administrative architecture of the question before observation begins.

An empirical inquiry is never an unmediated aperture opened onto nature. It arrives carrying an institutional sponsor, a budget, a deadline, and a pre-existing preference about which findings would be convenient. Hermeneutics reached the same conclusion from the side of understanding, and its version is stronger. Gadamer argued that the question has primacy in inquiry, that a question is possible only inside a horizon, and that the horizon is not an obstacle to be removed before the real question appears but the condition under which anything can appear as an answer at all (Gadamer 1960). If that is right there is no unframed data against which a frame could be measured, and the audit of a frame can only compare it with another frame that is itself declared. That is what the index below does: it compares a width that was declared with a width that was concealed, and it does not pretend to a view from nowhere.

None of that scaffolding is hidden. None of it appears in the rows and columns of the resulting database.

The observational instrument is therefore vastly wider than the dataset itself. Who funded the study decided which populations were sampled; the investigators' promotion incentives decide which hypotheses are publishable; the commissioning institution's legal liability decides which variables are actionable. A genuine audit of evidence has to interrogate those upstream commitments, because institutional framing fixes the boundaries of the answer before the first observation is logged.

In the inquiry framing, the net welfare impact of an intervention decomposes into the measured focal gain $\Delta U\_{\mathrm{focal}} \in \mathbb{R}$ minus the unrecorded spillover damage $D\_{\mathrm{spillover}} \ge 0$ inflicted outside the inquiry's frame:

$$\Delta U\_{\mathrm{global}} = \Delta U\_{\mathrm{focal}} - D\_{\mathrm{spillover}}.$$

A Type III error occurs whenever the focal program appears beneficial ($\Delta U\_{\mathrm{focal}} > 0$) while the unrecorded externalized damage makes the global balance negative ($\Delta U\_{\mathrm{global}} < 0$). A programme can report a healthy focal gain and still destroy more value downstream than it creates, and the arithmetic of the report will never show it. To detect whether the decision premises were narrowed strategically, the framing is summarized by a **Premise Rigging Index** ($\mathrm{PRI} \in [0, 1]$) built from the concealed fraction of epistemic uncertainty $c\_{\mathrm{epi}} = 1 - w\_{\mathrm{disp}}/w\_{\mathrm{true}}$ ($0 \le w\_{\mathrm{disp}} \le w\_{\mathrm{true}}$, $w\_{\mathrm{true}} > 0$) and the concealed fraction of spillover harm $c\_{\mathrm{spill}} = 1 - D\_{\mathrm{disp}}/D\_{\mathrm{true}}$ ($0 \le D\_{\mathrm{disp}} \le D\_{\mathrm{true}}$, $D\_{\mathrm{true}} > 0$, with $c\_{\mathrm{spill}} = 0$ if $D\_{\mathrm{true}} = 0$):

$$\mathrm{PRI} = \max\bigl(c\_{\mathrm{epi}},\mkern3mu c\_{\mathrm{spill}}\bigr) = \max\left(1 - \frac{w\_{\mathrm{disp}}}{w\_{\mathrm{true}}},\mkern5mu 1 - \frac{D\_{\mathrm{disp}}}{D\_{\mathrm{true}}}\right) \in [0, 1].$$

Cutting that index at a declared threshold separates communicative influence from strategic premise-rigging: an inquiry that displays its width and its spillover is communicating, and an inquiry that conceals either beyond the threshold is rigging its own premises. That is the first guard site — the framing transit — and it is checked before any data are collected.

One rule holds across all scientific and operational evaluation: **an empirical inquiry that cannot fail is not an inquiry; it is institutional theatre.** If no conceivable pattern of observations would compel the commissioning body to abandon its preferred course of action, the data collection is ornamental, and most internal program reviews are built unfalsifiable from the start.

## 2. Four narrowing moves and Lakatos problem shifts

Picture a municipal hospital auditing emergency-department overcrowding. It restricts the cohort to walk-in daytime patients (*scope*), replaces diagnostic accuracy with door-to-triage clock time (*metric*), benchmarks that time against the worst winter surge on record (*comparison*), and omits patients who left the waiting room without being seen (*silence*). Each step looks like routine data cleaning, yet together they convert a clinical crisis into an administrative triumph.

The narrowing happens long before data collection begins. It takes four recurring forms:

**Scope.** The target population is surreptitiously replaced by an accessible, compliant subpopulation.
"Does this algorithmic intervention improve student learning?" becomes "does this software improve quiz
scores among students who complete all modules on high-speed home internet?", an entirely different question
whose answer is predetermined to look favorable.

**Metric.** A complex, multidimensional state is replaced by an easily quantifiable proxy that happens to
correlate with it in benign conditions. Diagnostic acuity is replaced by patient throughput; deep comprehension
is replaced by standardized test scores; social stability is replaced by arrest tallies. Once established, the
proxy metric is reified and never questioned.

**Comparison.** The counterfactual baseline is selected post hoc to flatter the favored program. Comparing
performance against the worst historical quarter, against an obsolete technology, or against total inaction
manufactures the illusion of massive success, while comparison against an inexpensive alternative is silenced.

**Silence.** The inquiry's ontological boundary is drawn so that unrecorded harms cannot be articulated. If a
particular ecological damage, psychological toll, or community displacement lacks an assigned column in the
relational database, the resulting cost-benefit analysis is internally consistent, mathematically optimal, and
grotesquely false.

Formally, let $\theta^\ast \in \mathbb{R}$ denote the substantive welfare estimand required by the real-world decision and $\tilde{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}$ denote the proxy estimand actually answered by the narrowed study. The total framing distortion telescopes across the four intermediate estimands ($\theta\_{\mathrm{scope}}$, $\theta\_{\mathrm{metric}}$, $\theta\_{\mathrm{comp}}$):

$$\tilde{\theta} - \theta^\ast = (\theta\_{\mathrm{scope}} - \theta^\ast) + (\theta\_{\mathrm{metric}} - \theta\_{\mathrm{scope}}) + (\theta\_{\mathrm{comp}} - \theta\_{\mathrm{metric}}) + (\tilde{\theta} - \theta\_{\mathrm{comp}}).$$

Because this bias lies entirely between $\tilde{\theta}$ and $\theta^\ast$, collecting millions of additional rows narrows the sampling variance around $\tilde{\theta}$ toward zero while leaving the framing gap $|\tilde{\theta} - \theta^\ast|$ untouched.

In the philosophy of science, Imre Lakatos (1970) distinguished **progressive** from **degenerating research programmes**.
A progressive programme anticipates novel, unexpected facts, expanding its explanatory core. A degenerating
programme, by contrast, responds to anomalies by constructing an endless belt of protective ad hoc auxiliary
hypotheses, narrowing its empirical scope to explain away failures. When an organization deliberately manufactures
doubt or narrows its scope to hide known hazards, it practices **agnotology**, the culturally and institutionally
induced production of ignorance documented by Robert Proctor and Londa Schiebinger (Proctor & Schiebinger 2008).
Every un-preregistered *ad hoc* auxiliary adjustment $K \in \mathbb{N}\_0$ penalizes the raw sequential evidence $E\_t$ to $E\_t / (K + 1)$, which prevents a degenerating problemshift from masquerading as empirical confirmation: a programme that patches its way past every anomaly has to pay for each patch in the currency it is claiming.

## 3. Severe testing and the Kennedy-O'Hagan discrepancy model

Suppose a coastal engineering agency calibrates a hydrodynamic flood simulator against ten years of benign tide-gauge records by tuning a single bed-roughness parameter $\theta$, omitting subsurface levee seepage. On calm tides the simulator fits to the millimeter. When a cyclonic surge arrives outside the calibration regime, subsurface piping undermines the seawall.

To rescue empirical inquiry from degenerating framing, Deborah Mayo (2018) formulated the **Severity Principle**:
a scientific claim $H$ passes a severe test with data $x\_0$ if and only if:

1. $x\_0$ accords with the predictions of $H$, and
2. There was a very high probability that the testing procedure would have yielded a result *less* in accord
   with $H$ if $H$ were false in a specified, substantively important way.

Severe statistical power against a minimum substantively meaningful effect $\delta\_{\min} > 0$ at significance level $\alpha \in (0, 1)$, dispersion $\sigma > 0$, and effective sample size $n\_{\mathrm{eff}} > 0$ is:

$$\mathrm{Power}\_{\mathrm{severe}}(\delta\_{\min}) = 1 - \Phi\mkern-3mu\left(z\_{1-\alpha} - \frac{\delta\_{\min}\sqrt{n\_{\mathrm{eff}}}}{\sigma}\right) \in (0, 1).$$

A test with low statistical power or an analysis that permits endless post-hoc parameter adjustments cannot
subject a hypothesis to severe testing. Passing an un-severe test provides zero epistemic warrant for action.

When mathematical simulators or predictive models are calibrated against real-world observations, Marc Kennedy
and Anthony O'Hagan (2001) demonstrated that the observed reality $y(x)$ must be modeled as the sum of the
computer code $\eta(x, \theta)$, an explicit **model discrepancy** term $\delta(x)$, and observation error $\epsilon$ (Kennedy & O'Hagan 2001):

$$y(x) = \eta(x, \theta) + \delta(x) + \epsilon.$$

If the analyst ignores $\delta(x)$ and attempts to force the model into agreement with the data solely by adjusting
the calibration parameters $\theta$, the parameter estimates become hopelessly confounded: the model absorbs
structural deficiencies into warped physical parameters that fail when extrapolated outside the training regime.
The discrepancy term is a Gaussian process anchored on empirical forecast residuals, and its behaviour is the
diagnostic: the posterior discrepancy standard deviation stays small inside the regime the residuals cover and
expands back toward its prior ceiling as soon as the query point leaves that regime. The same confusion appears in
planning. An unregularized optimizer that acts inside a miscalibrated world model, with no penalty on the
discrepancy between model and world, drives the trajectory to a dream return it never realizes and hits the ruin
wall on the way; penalizing the discrepancy restrains the plan inside the region the model supports.

## 4. Pre-registration, Cromwell's rule, and the multiverse

Imagine six research teams given the same administrative dataset to evaluate a wage-subsidy policy. Each makes a defensible, textbook-standard choice about outlier trimming and covariate adjustment; five find a strong positive effect, one finds an effect just below the cutoff. An advocacy group that cherry-picks the specification with the largest test statistic while hiding the other five transforms honest sensitivity into manufactured certainty.

Pre-registration exists to constrain the analyst against their own post-hoc rationalizations. A valid one binds the sample collection protocol, the cleaning transformations, the model specification, and, decisively, what empirical finding would constitute unambiguous refutation. Done as a hollow ritual, it lists the model family and leaves the threshold of failure unstated, preserving the very flexibility that permits p-hacking.

And an honest inquiry must respect **Cromwell's rule**, formulated by Dennis Lindley (1985): never assign an
absolute prior probability of zero or one to any contingent hypothesis ($\mathbb{P}(H) \in (0, 1)$) (Lindley
1985). Setting $\mathbb{P}(H) = 1$ permanently blinds Bayes' theorem, ensuring that no empirical anomaly, no
matter how severe, can ever dislodge the belief. In practice every prior is bounded away from the absorbing
barriers by a declared floor, and that floor is a stipulation rather than a measurement. Yet Cromwell's rule also
has an operational boundary: when a contingent hypothesis has been categorically refuted by sealed data,
preserving a large artificial prior prevents learning; the proper procedure is to replace literal Cromwell
openness with a declared negligible floor and say in the same sentence that the floor is stipulated rather than
measured.

Where multiple defensible analytical choices exist (differing covariate adjustments, outlier cutoffs, or
functional forms), the proper response is **multiverse analysis** and specification curve analysis (Steegen et al.
2016; Simonsohn, Simmons, & Nelson 2020). Rather than selecting the single specification that produces a desired
p-value, the analyst runs all $K$ defensible specifications. To control the False Discovery Rate (FDR) across
arbitrary, highly dependent model specifications without making unverifiable independence assumptions, the
**$e$-Benjamini-Hochberg ($e$-BH)** procedure of Ruodu Wang and Aaditya Ramdas (2022) (Wang & Ramdas 2022) sorts
the $K$ specification $e$-values in descending order $E\_{(1)} \ge E\_{(2)} \ge \dots \ge E\_{(K)}$ and finds the
largest rank $k^\ast$ satisfying:

$$E\_{(k^\ast)} \ge \frac{K}{k^\ast \alpha}.$$

The specification multiverse reports both the effect range $[\min\_k \hat{\beta}\_k,\mkern3mu \max\_k \hat{\beta}\_k]$ and a directional-and-evidential consistency ratio: the fraction of specifications that share the median effect sign with an $e$-value large enough to count as a discovery. In the worked case, most of a handful of defensible specifications clear the $e$-BH bar and one falls short — not because it has the opposite sign, but because its evidence is insufficient. That distinction is the point of the decomposition: a specification that fails on evidence is a different object from a specification that disagrees, and a report that merges the two has hidden the disagreement it was supposed to expose.

## 5. Whose threshold?

Consider a municipal water authority and a downstream residential neighborhood reading the same toxicological report, which estimates a $\hat{p} = 0.30$ probability of aquifer contamination. Both parties accept the laboratory data and the statistical model without dispute, yet the utility announces that no filtration retrofit is warranted while residents demand immediate intervention. Their disagreement is not about the data; it is about whose loss sits in the denominator of the action threshold.

The final element fixed by an inquiry's framing is the decision threshold for action, the cost–loss threshold
whose expected-regret counterpart is derived in [Chapter 36](36-decision-loss-threshold.md) §2:

$$p^\ast\_{\mathrm{CL}} = \frac{C}{\eta L},$$

where $C > 0$ is the cost of intervention, $\eta \in (0, 1]$ is its mitigation effectiveness, and $L > 0$ is the loss incurred if the catastrophe
occurs. The ratio $C/(\eta L)$ is held inside the open unit interval and obeys two instructive boundary limits:

1. *Vanishing intervention cost ($C \to 0$ with $\eta L > 0$ fixed)*: as the precaution becomes nearly free relative to the avoidable damage, $C/(\eta L) \to 0$ and the threshold collapses toward zero, licensing preventive action at the slightest non-zero hazard probability.
2. *Vanishing net protection capacity ($\eta L \to C$ or $L \to 0$)*: as the avoidable loss $\eta L$ shrinks down to the intervention cost $C$, the ratio $C/(\eta L) \to 1$ and the threshold approaches one, meaning that even near-certainty of the event barely justifies the outlay.

Critically, as Helen Longino (1990) and Sandra Harding (1991) demonstrated in the epistemology of scientific framing (Longino 1990; Harding 1991), the loss $L$ is not a single objective number. For an administrative agency or corporation, $L\_{\mathrm{inst}}$ is its own private exposure: regulatory fines, litigation reserves, stock depreciation. For the vulnerable community or patient, $L\_{\mathrm{affected}}$ is irreparable physical, health or livelihood devastation. Because $L\_{\mathrm{inst}} \ll L\_{\mathrm{affected}}$, the two thresholds land far apart:

$$p^\ast\_{\mathrm{inst}} = \frac{C}{\eta L\_{\mathrm{inst}}} \gg \frac{C}{\eta L\_{\mathrm{affected}}} = p^\ast\_{\mathrm{affected}}.$$

In the worked case the institutional threshold sits high — well above the observed probability of harm
— while the affected community's threshold sits low, and the distance between them is the framing gap. Because the
threshold is inversely proportional to the loss, any degradation of mitigation effectiveness $\eta$ pushes both
thresholds upward and widens the gap further, so the party with the smaller loss can always buy time by
questioning the efficacy of the remedy.

This is the political anatomy of Kimball's Type III error: answering the institution's cost-minimization question
while pretending to solve the public's survival question. In the sequential reserve trajectory, a naive optimizer
operating inside a benign small world finishes with its reserve intact and zero ruin, whereas stripping the
epistemic margin in the reactive environment degrades the terminal reserve and produces a non-zero ruin rate
across draws: the same optimizer, a different world, and a failure that no amount of estimation precision repairs.

### The threshold is not in the data

The threshold is not a property of the evidence. With the costs borne by the institution it sits high; with the costs borne by the person affected, low. A finding that falls between them is simultaneously a mandate to act and a reason to wait, and both sides can then accuse each other of bad faith using the same data and the same arithmetic.

The framing moves that produce that gap are four, and each narrows the question without touching the evidence: scope, metric, comparison and silence. Nor is a specification that holds across a set of pre-registered analyses thereby informative. Consistency of $k - 1$ out of $k$ means one disagreed, and the question is whether the one that disagreed was the one the decision needed. Consistency is a property of a set of specifications, not of the world.

The distinction between answering the posed question well and answering the needed one at all is why
pre-registration is a floor rather than a ceiling (Kimball 1957). A pre-registration can be impeccable and still
test an irrelevant question with full severity, and the cost that then goes unmeasured is the error of the third
kind.

### When the framing is imposed

The thesis fails when the question is fixed by law or by contract and the analyst cannot move it: framing is then a declared constraint rather than a choice. The procedure is to say so, move the margin to the axis that remains under control, and stop charging the analyst for a decision that was made above them.

2. Express the directional-and-evidential consistency ratio symbolically for $K$ specifications, and explain why a specification that fails on evidence should not be counted together with one that disagrees in sign.
3. Describe an impeccable pre-registration that informs nothing, and name the quantity it failed to measure (answer: an irrelevant question tested with severity; the cost of the third kind of error).
4. Give the case in which Cromwell's rule should be abandoned and what replaces it (answer: a hypothesis refuted by sealed data; a negligible probability with its class declared).
5. Name the four narrowing moves of section 2 and give one example of each in a line (answer: scope, metric, comparison and silence).
