## By the end of this chapter you can

1. State, for any inquiry, who commissioned it, who pays for the error, and which answer was
   already preferred.
2. Detect the four moves that narrow a question before any data is collected: scope, metric,
   comparison, and silence.
3. Write a pre-registration that would survive contact with a disappointing result.

## 1. Every question arrives framed

A national labor ministry commissions an evaluation of an automated youth job-placement platform. Months before the first row is logged, the procurement tender defines "program success" as thirty-day software completion among trainees with residential broadband, measured over one fiscal quarter. Displaced youths lacking home internet never enter the denominator. Trainees whose short-term jobs vanish in month four sit beyond the measurement window. No statistician faked a number; the flattering outcome was built into the question before observation started.

An empirical inquiry never opens an unmediated window onto nature. Every study arrives carrying an institutional sponsor, a budget, a deadline, and a preferred answer. Philosophical hermeneutics states an even stronger limit: Hans-Georg Gadamer argued that every question asks inside an inherited horizon, which is the very condition making any answer visible at all (Gadamer 1960). Because no view sits outside all horizons, auditing a frame means comparing one declared frame against a wider declared frame, comparing concealed width against disclosed width rather than claiming a view from nowhere.

Procurement terms sit clearly on paper, yet vanish from database rows. Who pays sets which groups get sampled; promotion rules pick which findings get published; liability decides which variables become actionable.

Every cutoff, floor, and cap in [Chapter 31](31-distribution-and-moment.md) rests on choices made at **Guard Site `T1`** (*Pre-Draw Framing*), the reflexive lock from Institution ($P\_3$) back toward Archive ($P\_2$) unveiled in [Chapter 29](29-aim-and-the-three-planes.md) and [Appendix E](appendix-e-lexicon.md). Before formalizing how framing fails, write net social welfare as focal program gain $\Delta U\_{\mathrm{focal}} \in \mathbb{R}$ minus uncounted external spillover harm $D\_{\mathrm{spillover}} \ge 0$:

$$\Delta U\_{\mathrm{global}} = \Delta U\_{\mathrm{focal}} - D\_{\mathrm{spillover}}.$$

Allyn Kimball's **Type III error** (Kimball 1957), giving the right answer to the wrong question, strikes whenever narrow focal gains look positive ($\Delta U\_{\mathrm{focal}} > 0$) while unrecorded spillover turns net welfare negative ($\Delta U\_{\mathrm{global}} < 0$).

To measure how sharply a study's frame shrinks reality before rows enter the table, draw on Herbert Simon's account of organizational **decision premises** (Simon 1997), without attributing a formula to Simon, to build the **Premise Rigging Index ($\mathrm{PRI}$)**. Compare two gaps: how much uncertainty width the study hides ($c\_{\mathrm{epi}} = 1 - w\_{\mathrm{disp}}/w\_{\mathrm{true}}$, for $0 \le w\_{\mathrm{disp}} \le w\_{\mathrm{true}}$ with $w\_{\mathrm{true}} > 0$) and how much spillover harm it hides ($c\_{\mathrm{spill}} = 0$ if $D\_{\mathrm{true}} = 0$, else $1 - D\_{\mathrm{disp}}/D\_{\mathrm{true}}$ for $0 \le D\_{\mathrm{disp}} \le D\_{\mathrm{true}}$ and $D\_{\mathrm{true}} > 0$). Taking the worst concealment defines:

$$\mathrm{PRI} = \max\bigl(c\_{\mathrm{epi}},\mkern3mu c\_{\mathrm{spill}}\bigr) \in [0, 1], \qquad \text{reducing to } \max\left(1 - \frac{w\_{\mathrm{disp}}}{w\_{\mathrm{true}}},\mkern5mu 1 - \frac{D\_{\mathrm{disp}}}{D\_{\mathrm{true}}}\right) \text{ for } D\_{\mathrm{true}} > 0.$$

Read aloud, $\mathrm{PRI}$ sits at $0$ when the study discloses true width and true spillover completely, and climbs toward $1$ whenever either uncertainty or external damage is cropped out of sight. As [Chapter 28](28-the-machine-and-the-deception.md) and [Chapter 43](43-inquiry-openness-multiverse.md) establish, however, an archive cannot certify its own omissions from inside an incomplete ledger; $\mathrm{PRI}$ must compare internal claims against external registers (`T3`) and frontline alerts (`T7`). Protecting live shift attention ([Chapter 29](29-aim-and-the-three-planes.md) §4), framing checks lock before dispatch so crews act under pre-declared cutoffs and rank-blind emergency overrides.

One rule holds across all empirical work: **an inquiry that cannot fail is not an inquiry; it is institutional theatre.** Whenever no observable dataset could force a commissioning body to reject its favorite plan, data collection is pure ornament.

### Requirements engineering as pre-data world-making

In software and systems engineering, pre-data framing takes explicit shape in **requirements engineering**: eliciting goals, drawing boundaries, and specifying what an artifact must do inside its environment (Nuseibeh & Easterbrook 2000). Far from routine paperwork, specification writing is `T1` world-making. Every requirements document separates **indicative** facts that hold in the environment regardless of software from **optative** targets the software must enforce (Zave & Jackson 1997; Nuseibeh & Easterbrook 2000). Signed functional limits, error envelopes, and out-of-scope stakeholder notes act as **decision premises** (Simon 1997) locking down `T1 -> T3`: deciding which events enter logs, which harms remain unaskable (formalized in [Chapter 33](33-measuring-and-erring.md) §4), and how models get evaluated.

Checking software merely against a frozen specification mistakes internal consistency for real-world adequacy (Nuseibeh & Easterbrook 2000). Formal type checkers can prove resource rules syntactically while missing unmodeled physical users or correlated system outages. Passing every scripted unit test inside a cropped boundary is Type III error in code (Kimball 1957). Auditing specifications therefore checks $\mathrm{PRI}$ ex ante against external records (`T3`) and frontline staff alarms (`T7`). Keeping checklists lean and refutable (Nuseibeh & Easterbrook 2000), `T1` pre-declares failure envelopes while holding frames open to attested reopening ($\mathcal{F}\_t \subsetneq \mathcal{F}\_{t+1}^\ast$) under quarantine ([Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md)).

## 2. Four narrowing moves and Lakatos problem shifts

Picture a municipal hospital auditing emergency-room overcrowding. It limits the study to daytime walk-in clinics (*scope*), swaps diagnostic accuracy for door-to-triage clock speed (*metric*), benchmarks against the worst winter flu surge on file (*comparison*), and drops patients who walked out unexamined (*silence*). Every step passes as routine cleaning, yet together they disguise a clinical breakdown as success.

Four recurring moves narrow a question before data enter the database:

**Scope.** Replacing the true population with an easy subset converts "does this software improve student learning?" into "does it lift quiz scores among broadband-connected completers?", rigging the denominator in advance.

**Metric.** Swapping real purpose for an easy proxy (triage minutes for diagnostic survival, test drills for mastery, arrest counts for neighborhood safety) reifies what correlates only under calm conditions.

**Comparison.** Choosing a weak baseline after the fact (the worst historical year, obsolete tools, or doing nothing) manufactures gains while hiding cheaper alternatives.

**Silence.** Leaving ecological toll, unpaid care work, or community displacement off the database schema produces internally consistent cost–benefit numbers that are completely false.

Whenever all intermediate targets sit on one shared commensurable real scale (failing when harms are incommensurable, §5) under a declared sequential ordering of the four moves, let $\theta^\ast \in \mathbb{R}$ denote the real policy target and $\tilde{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}$ the narrowed study result. Three intermediate distortions ($\theta\_{\mathrm{scope}}$, $\theta\_{\mathrm{metric}}$, $\theta\_{\mathrm{comp}}$) decompose total framing bias across four additive steps:

$$\tilde{\theta} - \theta^\ast = (\theta\_{\mathrm{scope}} - \theta^\ast) + (\theta\_{\mathrm{metric}} - \theta\_{\mathrm{scope}}) + (\theta\_{\mathrm{comp}} - \theta\_{\mathrm{metric}}) + (\tilde{\theta} - \theta\_{\mathrm{comp}}).$$

Read aloud, collecting ten million extra rows shrinks sampling noise around $\tilde{\theta}$ toward zero while leaving the framing gap $|\tilde{\theta} - \theta^\ast|$ completely untouched.

In philosophy of science ([Chapter 18](18-philosophy-of-science.md)), Imre Lakatos (1970) split **progressive** programmes, which predict novel facts as their protective belt grows, from **degenerating** programmes that tack on *ad hoc* excuses to survive failure. When institutions manufacture doubt or narrow frames to conceal hazards, they practice **agnotology** (Proctor & Schiebinger 2008). As [Chapter 30](30-probability-as-language.md) (§4) proved for anytime-valid *e*-processes ($\mathbb{E}\_{H\_0}[E\_T] \le 1$), pre-declaring a ceiling $K\_{\max}$ of protective variants penalizes opportunistic scope-shrinking ($\mathcal{F}\_{t+1} \subset \mathcal{F}\_t$) via divisor $K\_{\max} + 1$, while sparing **attested counter-inductive reopening** ($\mathcal{F}\_t \subsetneq \mathcal{F}\_{t+1}^\ast$; Feyerabend 1970) routed into provisional quarantine ([Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md)).

## 3. Severe testing and the Kennedy-O'Hagan discrepancy model

A coastal agency calibrates a storm-surge simulator against ten years of calm tide gauges by tuning a single seabed-friction parameter $\theta$, omitting underground seawall seepage. On quiet tides the fit matches to the millimeter. When a cyclone hits, seepage undercuts the wall.

To block degenerate framing, Deborah Mayo (2018) formulated the **Severity Principle** ([Chapter 18](18-philosophy-of-science.md) §4). A claim $H$ passes a severe test on data $x\_0$ if and only if:

1. $x\_0$ agrees with the predictions of $H$, and
2. There was a very high probability the procedure would have yielded a result *worse* for $H$ if $H$ were false in a specified, important way.

Mayo (2018) separates pre-data design **power** from **post-data severity** $\mathrm{SEV}(\delta \le \delta\_{\min}; x\_0)$ once statistical model assumptions pass residual adequacy checks (Spanos 2021; formalized in [Chapter 35](35-inference-and-evidence.md) and [Chapter 43](43-inquiry-openness-multiverse.md) §2.3; [Appendix E](appendix-e-lexicon.md)). An underpowered non-rejection provides no severe evidence of safety ($\delta \le \delta\_{\min}$), though asymmetric losses ($C < \eta L$) may still compel precaution.

For computer simulations, Marc Kennedy and Anthony O'Hagan (2001) separate simulator output $\eta(x, \theta)$ from systematic **model discrepancy** $\delta(x)$ (distinct from §3's harm boundary $\delta\_{\min}$ and [Chapter 31](31-distribution-and-moment.md)'s deductible $\delta$) and measurement noise $\epsilon$ (formalized in [Chapter 37](37-model-fit-and-error.md) §4). Omitting $\delta(x)$ forces structural flaws into distorted parameter tunes $\theta$. Honest inference models $\delta(x)$ explicitly with disciplined priors so simulators cannot pretend uncalibrated extrapolation is certainty.

## 4. Pre-registration, Cromwell's rule, and the multiverse

Six research teams receive identical administrative records on a wage subsidy. Using standard textbook choices for outlier rules and control variables, five find strong benefits and one falls shy of statistical significance. An advocate publishing only the strongest specification turns honest sensitivity into fake certainty.

Pre-registration keeps exploration distinct from confirmation. Exploratory scans seed new hypotheses for explicit `T1` reopening, whereas confirmatory `T2` testing requires committing data collection, cleaning steps, models, and unambiguous failure criteria beforehand. Listing only a broad model family while leaving failure rules vague preserves full p-hacking room.

Honest priors obey **Cromwell's rule** (Lindley 1985; [Chapter 30](30-probability-as-language.md) §3): never set prior probability to $0$ or $1$ on a contingent empirical claim, enforcing an interior floor so counter-evidence can always move belief.

Across the "garden of forking paths" (Gelman & Loken 2014), **multiverse** and **specification curve** audits (Steegen et al. 2016; Simonsohn, Simmons, & Nelson 2020) pre-enumerate a closed family of $K \ge 1$ reasonable models before looking at outcomes. Suppose each model outputs an anytime-valid $e$-value $E\_1, \dots, E\_K \ge 0$ ($\mathbb{E}\_{H\_0}[E\_k] \le 1$). To control the False Discovery Rate at level $\alpha \in (0, 1)$ even when specifications correlate arbitrarily, Wang and Ramdas (2022) sort $E\_{(1)} \ge \dots \ge E\_{(K)}$ and use the **$e$-Benjamini-Hochberg ($e$-BH)** cutoff, accepting up to the largest rank $k^\ast \in \lbrace 1, \dots, K\rbrace$ satisfying:

$$E\_{(k^\ast)} \ge \frac{K}{k^\ast \alpha},$$

and rejecting zero hypotheses ($k^\ast = 0$) if no specification clears the bar. In plain words, the top result must clear $K/\alpha$, the second must clear $K/(2\alpha)$, and so on, protecting error control without independence assumptions.

Because Steegen et al. (2016) warn against collapsing a multiverse into one scalar, and because Simonsohn, Simmons, and Nelson (2020) report median effect, dominant sign share, and significance share, let $\hat{\beta}\_j$ denote specification $j$'s estimated effect ($j \in \lbrace 1, \dots, K\rbrace$, distinct from Type II error $\beta$ in [Chapter 33](33-measuring-and-erring.md) and GPD scale in [Chapter 31](31-distribution-and-moment.md)). Stochology reports both the effect range $[\min\_j \hat{\beta}\_j,\mkern3mu \max\_j \hat{\beta}\_j]$ and its **directional-and-evidential consistency ratio**: the fraction of all $K$ pre-registered specifications that match the median sign and clear $e$-BH ($k^\ast \ge 1$ with $E\_j \ge K/(k^\ast \alpha)$, falling among the top $k^\ast$ sorted $e$-values). Notice two limits: inflating $K$ with near-duplicates distorts the ratio, and leaving specifications unenumerated outside $K$ makes the effect range only a lower bound on model spread while the consistency ratio tracks only discovered branches, whereas adding specifications post hoc breaks $e$-BH false discovery rate control and can move the consistency ratio up or down ([Chapter 43](43-inquiry-openness-multiverse.md)). Failing on evidence differs from flipping effect sign, so reporting both keeps disagreement visible.

## 5. Whose threshold?

A municipal water authority and downstream residents examine the exact same toxicological report. Both agree on the estimated aquifer contamination probability. Yet the utility refuses a filtration retrofit while residents demand immediate shutdown. They disagree not over data, but over whose harm sets the decision threshold.

Recall from [Chapter 01](01-deciding-without-knowing.md) (derived fully in [Chapter 36](36-decision-loss-threshold.md)) that a cost–loss cutoff compares intervention outlay $C > 0$ against avoided harm $\eta L$ ($\eta \in (0, 1]$, $L > 0$) via $p^\ast\_{\mathrm{CL}} = \min(1, C/(\eta L))$. As Helen Longino (1990) and Sandra Harding (1991) demonstrated, background values and social location shape inquiry premises, so loss $L$ is never a single neutral figure. An institution's financial fine $L\_{\mathrm{inst}}$ routinely sits far below community health and livelihood harm $L\_{\mathrm{affected}}$. Whenever $\eta L\_{\mathrm{affected}} > \eta L\_{\mathrm{inst}} > C$ with $L\_{\mathrm{inst}} \ll L\_{\mathrm{affected}}$, cutoffs split widely: $p^\ast\_{\mathrm{inst}} = C/(\eta L\_{\mathrm{inst}}) \gg C/(\eta L\_{\mathrm{affected}}) = p^\ast\_{\mathrm{affected}}$ (and if $\eta L\_{\mathrm{inst}} \le C < \eta L\_{\mathrm{affected}}$, $p^\ast\_{\mathrm{inst}}$ hits $1$ while $p^\ast\_{\mathrm{affected}} < 1$ still calls for action).

Whenever a shared hazard estimate falls between $p^\ast\_{\mathrm{affected}}$ and $p^\ast\_{\mathrm{inst}}$, [Chapter 43](43-inquiry-openness-multiverse.md) calls that separation the **framing gap**. On the unclamped interior ($\eta > C/L\_{\mathrm{inst}}$), lowering effectiveness $\eta$ pushes both thresholds higher and widens the gap, letting low-liability agencies stall precaution by debating efficacy; once $\eta \le C/L\_{\mathrm{inst}}$, however, $p^\ast\_{\mathrm{inst}}$ remains clamped at $1$ while further reductions in $\eta$ push $p^\ast\_{\mathrm{affected}}$ upward toward $1$, narrowing the gap to zero once $\eta \le C/L\_{\mathrm{affected}}$. This is Kimball's (1957) Type III error inside governance: answering the agency's balance-sheet question while claiming to test the public's survival question.

### The threshold is not in the data

Action cutoffs sit in consequences, not observations: high for corporate liability, low for exposed households. An estimate inside that gap serves simultaneously as an alarm for residents and an excuse for delay, letting both sides quote identical data.

High multiverse consistency cannot fix a cropped frame either. Even $K - 1$ consistent specifications leave one specification outside, whether weak in evidence or opposite in sign, and that outlier may carry the danger the study missed. Consistency checks the pre-listed specification grid, not reality; an impeccably pre-registered test can still answer the wrong question (Kimball 1957).

### When the framing is imposed

Two clear limits bound these framing rules. First, legal or contractual mandates locking a narrow question confirm the framing problem rather than refuting it. Analysts must state the imposed *skopós*, hold safety margins elsewhere, and publish $\mathrm{PRI}$ plus the $p^\ast\_{\mathrm{inst}}$–$p^\ast\_{\mathrm{affected}}$ gap against the broader public problem. An imposed mandate never licenses spilling harm past affected communities' capacity to absorb it, escalating immediately to institutional safeguards ([Chapter 40](40-institution-incentive-metric.md); [Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md)).

Second, multiverse tools stop working whenever $K$ cannot be pre-enumerated, grows opportunistically after inspection, or involves harms incommensurable with money $C$, disabling scalar $\tilde{\theta} - \theta^\ast$ and $p^\ast\_{\mathrm{CL}}$ ([Chapter 36](36-decision-loss-threshold.md)).
