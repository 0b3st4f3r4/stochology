## By the end of this chapter you can

1. Distinguish precision from accuracy, and name the case where more precision makes a decision
   worse.
2. Identify the measurement scale of a variable (nominal, ordinal, interval, ratio) and say which
   operations that scale permits.
3. Refuse a summary statistic that is not invariant under the transformations the scale allows.

## 1. Precision, accuracy, units, and metrological traceability

Picture an intensive-care telemetry monitor displaying a patient's mean arterial blood pressure to fine decimal resolution every second at a reading $x\_{\mathrm{obs}}$ safely above a clinical intervention threshold $\tau\_{\mathrm{crit}}$, fed by a catheter pressure transducer whose zero-point has drifted upward by $+b > 0$ due to an uncalibrated thermal offset. The bedside staff see razor-sharp decimal stability and withhold vasopressors, while the patient's true mean arterial pressure $x\_{\mathrm{obs}} - b < \tau\_{\mathrm{crit}}$ sits well inside the hypotensive danger zone. High resolution in the display had masked a fatal offset in the instrument.

Precision is a property of a measurement apparatus: how finely it differentiates between neighboring states.
Accuracy is a property of a factual proposition: how closely it corresponds to the physical reality it claims
to describe. A measurement can be exquisitely precise while being completely inaccurate (a micrometer gauge
calibrated with an offset bias) and it can be accurate while remaining coarse, which is the honest condition of
most human knowledge.

The institutional failure that plagues modern data systems is **false precision**: presenting an estimate at
a decimal resolution the underlying procedure cannot justify. Publishing a risk probability to four significant
digits derived from seventeen historical incidents, or an index to three decimal places from a survey with an
unmeasured non-response rate, is not harmless decoration. Digits constitute an explicit claim about the resolving
power of the evidence, and decision-makers are entitled to act on that claim. A number published without an
uncertainty interval is a claim to infinite precision.

International physical metrology codifies this discipline in the **Guide to the Expression of Uncertainty in
Measurement** (`BIPM/JCGM GUM` (BIPM/JCGM 2008)). Total measurement variance decomposes into two distinct regimes. **Type A uncertainty** is evaluated by statistical analysis of series of observations, dominated by random stochastic scatter that shrinks as $1/\sqrt{n}$. **Type B uncertainty** is evaluated by scientific judgment using prior calibration certificates, instrument resolution limits, temperature drift, and the **definitional uncertainty** ($u\_{\mathrm{def}}$) inherent in specifying the measurand itself, and remains strictly $O(1)$ regardless of sample size $n$.

To see how these two error regimes act on estimation, write the measurement equation for observation $i \in \lbrace1, \dots, n\rbrace$ as $X\_{\mathrm{obs},i} = X\_i + b + u\_i$, where $X\_i \sim (\mu\_X, \sigma\_X^2)$ is the true physical state, $b \in \mathbb{R}$ is a systematic Type B calibration offset, and $u\_i \sim (0, \sigma\_u^2)$ is random Type A sensor noise uncorrelated with $X\_i$. Two distinct failures follow immediately:

1. *Systematic drift under growing volume ($b \neq 0$)*: The sample mean $\bar{X}\_{\mathrm{obs}} = \bar{X} + b + \bar{u}$ has variance $(\sigma\_X^2 + \sigma\_u^2)/n \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$, concentrating with arbitrary precision around the shifted value $\mu\_X + b$ rather than $\mu\_X$.
2. *Classical attenuation bias in regression ($\sigma\_u^2 > 0$)*: Suppose an analyst estimates the linear structural relationship $Y\_i = \alpha + \beta X\_i + \varepsilon\_i$ by ordinary least squares using the noisy proxy $X\_{\mathrm{obs},i} = X\_i + u\_i$ (with $b = 0$ and $\mathrm{Cov}(X, u) = \mathrm{Cov}(\varepsilon, u) = 0$). The probability limit of the slope estimator is attenuated toward zero by the **reliability ratio** $\lambda \in (0, 1]$:

$$\hat{\beta}\_{\mathrm{OLS}} \xrightarrow{p} \frac{\mathrm{Cov}(X + u,\mkern3mu \beta X + \varepsilon)}{\mathrm{Var}(X + u)} = \beta\mkern3mu\frac{\sigma\_X^2}{\sigma\_X^2 + \sigma\_u^2} = \beta \lambda.$$

When noise variance equals signal variance ($\sigma\_u^2 = \sigma\_X^2$), the reliability ratio is $\lambda = 0.50$, and ordinary least squares systematically understates the true causal slope $\beta$ by half regardless of how many millions of rows are collected.

And under the **unbroken chain of metrological traceability** (`VIM 2.41` (BIPM/JCGM 2012)), every link
in an empirical calibration hierarchy must inherit the expanded uncertainty of the standard upstream from it.
No downstream statistical manipulation can ever legitimately report an uncertainty narrower than the reference
standard against which its instruments were calibrated. Nor is measurement free: additional measurement is warranted only while its marginal cost $\Delta C\_{\mathrm{meas}}$ remains below the marginal reduction in expected decision loss $\Delta\mathrm{EVPI}$, and beyond that crossing every further decimal destroys net value.

## 2. Stevens's scale taxonomy and permissible transformations

Imagine two post-operative surgical wards where nurses ask recovering patients to rate their pain on a five-point ordinal card (`1 = none`, `2 = mild`, `3 = moderate`, `4 = severe`, `5 = excruciating`). A hospital quality dashboard averages the integer codes, reports a mean pain of $2.33$ in Ward B against $2.67$ in Ward A, and penalizes Ward A. The dashboard has treated arbitrary rank labels as though the distance between mild and moderate matched the distance between severe and excruciating.

Variables do not all measure the world in the same mathematical way. In 1946, S. S. Stevens established the
definitive taxonomy of measurement scales, placed on axiomatic foundations by David Krantz, R. Duncan Luce, Patrick Suppes, and Amos Tversky (1971), defined by their mathematical invariance under specific transformation
groups (Stevens 1946; Krantz et al. 1971):

| Scale | Defining Relation | Permissible Group Transformation | Invariant Mathematical Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Nominal | Equivalence ($=, \neq$) | One-to-one bijection $f(x)$ | Mode, contingency tables, entropy |
| Ordinal | Strict order ($>, <$) | Strictly increasing monotone $f'(x) > 0$ | Median, quantiles, Mann-Whitney dominance |
| Interval | Known unit, arbitrary zero | Positive affine $f(x) = a x + b\ (a > 0)$ | Arithmetic mean, variance, Pearson $r$ |
| Ratio | Natural physical zero | Positive similarity $f(x) = a x\ (a > 0)$ | Geometric mean, coefficient of variation, ratios |

Two widespread practices commit severe scale violations:

1. *Averaging ordinal codes*: Computing an arithmetic mean over ordinal Likert scales ("strongly agree = 5",
   "neutral = 3") or clinical triage categories treats qualitative rank orders as if they possessed constant
   interval units. To see the reversal explicitly, suppose three patients in Ward A report ordinal pain codes $\lbrace1, 2, 5\rbrace$ (arithmetic mean $8/3 \approx 2.67$, median $2$) and three patients in Ward B report $\lbrace2, 2, 3\rbrace$ (arithmetic mean $7/3 \approx 2.33$, median $2$), so $\bar{X}\_A > \bar{X}\_B$. Now apply the strictly increasing monotone relabeling $\psi: (1, 2, 3, 4, 5) \mapsto (1, 4, 5, 6, 7)$, which preserves every patient-by-patient order relation ($x\_i < x\_j \iff \psi(x\_i) < \psi(x\_j)$). Under $\psi$, Ward A becomes $\lbrace1, 4, 7\rbrace$ with mean $12/3 = 4.00$, whereas Ward B becomes $\lbrace4, 4, 5\rbrace$ with mean $13/3 \approx 4.33$, inverting the ranking to $\overline{\psi(X)}\_A < \overline{\psi(X)}\_B$.
2. *Ratios of interval quantities*: Stating that a temperature of $40^\circ\text{C}$ is "twice as warm" as
   $20^\circ\text{C}$ is meaningless; under the positive affine transformation to Fahrenheit ($f(x) = 1.8x + 32$, yielding $104^\circ\text{F}$ vs $68^\circ\text{F}$), the
   ratio drops from $2.00$ to $104/68 \approx 1.53$. Ratios are licensed only when the scale has a true physical zero, such as thermodynamic temperature in kelvins ($313.15\text{ K} / 293.15\text{ K} \approx 1.068$).

## 3. The invariance test and non-parametric dominance

The operational check that enforces scale discipline is the **invariance test**:
> Take any proposed summary statistic or decision rule. Apply an arbitrary mathematical transformation that the
> variable's measurement scale permits. If the substantive decision, ranking, or qualitative conclusion changes,
> then the statistic was measuring the arbitrary artifact of the coordinate system, not the physical state of the world.

When applied to ordinal observations $X = \lbrace{}x\_1, \dots, x\_m\rbrace$ and $Y = \lbrace{}y\_1, \dots, y\_n\rbrace$, the arithmetic mean fails the invariance test instantly.
By contrast, the **Mann-Whitney stochastic dominance** probability:

$$\Delta\_{\mathrm{MW}}(X, Y) = \frac{1}{m n}\sum\_{i=1}^{m}\sum\_{j=1}^{n}\left(\mathbf{1}\lbrace{}x\_i > y\_j\rbrace + \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{1}\lbrace{}x\_i = y\_j\rbrace\right) = \mathbb{P}(X > Y) + \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{P}(X = Y) \in [0, 1],$$

passes the invariance test under any strictly increasing monotonic transformation $\phi$. Because $\phi$ is strictly increasing, $x\_i > y\_j \iff \phi(x\_i) > \phi(y\_j)$ and $x\_i = y\_j \iff \phi(x\_i) = \phi(y\_j)$ for every pair $(i, j)$, leaving every term in the double sum unchanged: $\Delta\_{\mathrm{MW}}(\phi(X), \phi(Y)) = \Delta\_{\mathrm{MW}}(X, Y)$. On the surgical pain cohorts $A = \lbrace1, 2, 5\rbrace$ and $B = \lbrace2, 2, 3\rbrace$ above ($9$ pairs: three strict wins by $5$, two ties at $2$, and four losses), $\Delta\_{\mathrm{MW}}(A, B) = (3 + 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2}) / 9 = 4 / 9$ identically both before and after the recoding $\psi$. Where an experimental treatment produces systematically faster recovery than a standard therapy the claim is purely ordinal: it does not require hospital stay to be measured on an idealized ratio scale, and it survives monotonic drift in the hospital logging clocks.

The same asymmetry between rank summaries and moment summaries is what Alexander J. McNeil's extreme-value treatment of industrial fire insurance losses makes unavoidable (McNeil 1997): a severity distribution whose tail carries the mass is one where a single extreme claim moves the parametric mean and reverses a mean-based ranking under a convex monotone transformation, while an ordinal dominance comparison of the same two portfolios does not move at all. A location estimate with a bounded breakdown point keeps the centre it had when a small fraction of the archive is arbitrary, which is what the mean cannot do (Huber 1964).

In psychometrics and educational measurement, Samuel Messick's unified validity framework (Messick 1995)
formalized three structural threats that persist even when scale transformations are respected:

1. **Construct underrepresentation**: The metric omits critical, unmeasured dimensions of the underlying capacity.
2. **Construct-irrelevant variance**: The score is contaminated by extraneous noise, language barriers, or socioeconomic
   friction unrelated to the true competence.
3. **Consequential invalidity**: The social, institutional, and economic damage triggered when the metric is tied to
   high-stakes incentives and warps operational behaviour.

These three psychometric threats are answered at the framing (`T1`), archive (`T3`) and performative
(`T6`) guard sites of the grammar of aim.

## 4. Missing is not zero

Suppose a municipal disaster-relief database records residential flood damage across twelve districts. In the three poorest riverfront wards, washed-out bridges prevent assessors from entering for seventy-two hours, leaving their damage cells blank. An automated allocation script parses empty fields as zero and routes emergency repair crews to dry upland neighborhoods. The database did not record zero damage; it recorded the absence of the observer.

An empty cell in an empirical database has three mutually incompatible interpretations, and software libraries
routinely conflate them:

1. **Not observed**: The physical event occurred, but the instrument failed to record it.
2. **Observed as absent**: The instrument inspected the state and confirmed that the event did not occur.
3. **Not askable**: The category does not exist in the database schema, so the phenomenon cannot be represented.

Treating unobserved cases as absent biases damage rates downward, creating an illusion of safety precisely in the most vulnerable regions. Worse, treating unaskable categories as absent converts institutional blindness into an assertion of non-existence.

The evidential weight of an archive cannot be measured by raw byte volume. Following John Maynard Keynes's *Treatise
on Probability* (Keynes 1921), evidential weight $V(n\_{\mathrm{eff}}, n\_0)$ depends upon the completeness of the archive relative to the domain
of inquiry, governed by the design-adjusted effective sample size $n\_{\mathrm{eff}} \ge 0$ (after Kish's treatment of clustering and of unequal survey weights) and the reference ignorance scale $n\_0 := n\_{\mathrm{req}}(\alpha, \beta, p^\star) > 0$ anchored to the decision's error tolerances ($\alpha + \beta < 1$), relative resolution $\varepsilon\_{\mathrm{rel}} > 0$, and cost–loss threshold $p^\star = C/(\eta L) \in (0, 1)$:

$$V(n\_{\mathrm{eff}}, n\_0) = \frac{n\_{\mathrm{eff}}}{n\_{\mathrm{eff}} + n\_0} \in [0, 1), \qquad n\_0 := n\_{\mathrm{req}}(\alpha, \beta, p^\star) = \left(\frac{z\_{1-\alpha} + z\_{1-\beta}}{\varepsilon\_{\mathrm{rel}}}\right)^2 \frac{1 - p^\star}{p^\star}.$$

This concave rational function is strictly increasing in $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}$ ($\partial V / \partial n\_{\mathrm{eff}} = n\_0 / (n\_{\mathrm{eff}} + n\_0)^2 > 0$) and is anchored by two solved boundary regimes:

1. *Vacuous or degenerate archive ($n\_{\mathrm{eff}} \to 0$)*: When non-response or extreme intra-cluster correlation collapses the effective sample toward zero, the weight tends to zero: an archive that has lost its effective sample carries no more evidential weight than an empty one, however many rows it holds.
2. *Asymptotic evidential saturation ($n\_{\mathrm{eff}} \to \infty$)*: Dividing numerator and denominator by $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}$ yields $\lim\_{n\_{\mathrm{eff}} \to \infty} (1 + n\_0/n\_{\mathrm{eff}})^{-1} = 1$, approached from below and never attained, so that beyond the scale $n\_0$ every additional order of effective sample buys a vanishingly small increment of weight.

An archive with millions of rows collected under an unmeasured selection filter carries massive false precision, and its genuine Keynesian weight stays near zero. Discount the raw row count to its design-adjusted effective sample, bound the missingness that no weight can repair, and the weight is honest: a margin computed on the rows reports evidence the archive has not earned, while the same count discounted reports the little it has.

### Weight saturates, sample does not

Weight is not probability. Two archives can both sit so close to saturation that their weights are indistinguishable, and the one with the smaller effective sample can still carry the larger weight if its domain scale is generous. Neither weight tells you which archive to trust: what separates the two is the effective sample itself, a factor the weight hides by construction.

Saturation is the point. The weight is a ratio with a ceiling of one, so it reports how much uncertainty has been removed and stops reporting once the removal is nearly complete. A reader who compares two archives by their weights compares two numbers that have both run out of room.

The admissibility of a summary is older than the statistic: a mean is admissible on an interval or a ratio scale, whereas dividing interval temperatures such as $104^\circ\text{F}$ by $68^\circ\text{F}$ to obtain $1.53$ is invalid on a Fahrenheit interval scale and ratios are licensed only on a ratio scale with a physical zero (Stevens 1946; Krantz et al. 1971). Applying a mean to an ordinal scale is not a rounding error. It is a claim the scale does not license.

### When precision should stop

Additional precision must stop when its marginal cost exceeds the loss reduction it buys. The procedure is to compare the marginal cost of measuring with the change in the value of information and to stop at the point where they cross; beyond it, every further decimal is paid for in resources the decision would rather spend elsewhere.
