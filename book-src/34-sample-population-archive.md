1. Say what a sample estimates and what it cannot, for a population that was never enumerated.
2. Separate a large archive from a representative one, and give the arithmetic reason the first

## 1. Three different objects

Suppose a mortgage lender fits a default model on $n\_{\mathrm{fit}}$ repaid loans holding complete tax transcripts, pulled from $n\_{\mathrm{arch}} > n\_{\mathrm{fit}}$ screened applicants, and deploys it across $N \gg n\_{\mathrm{arch}}$ households entering a downturn. Rejected applicants never entered the archive, while struggling borrowers stopped filing tax forms and dropped out under listwise deletion. The model looks crisp on cross-validation over $n\_{\mathrm{fit}}$, yet triggers **Collapse Proposition 1 (Blind Undersizing)** ([Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md)) when reserves sized on that false precision hit the recessionary population.

In [Chapter 09](09-sets-relations-functions.md), we saw how measurement pipelines map real-world entities onto tables via non-injective projections. Across the world-to-archive transit ($T\_3$, $P\_1 \to P\_2$ of [Chapter 29](29-aim-and-the-three-planes.md)), three distinct populations govern every inquiry:

- **The target population ($N$):** the real-world group the decision affects (incoming patients, borrowers facing recession, bridge joints in gales), whose exact headcount $N$ is often unknown before action.
- **The archive population ($n\_{\mathrm{arch}}$):** the units recorded inside institutional ledgers after intake rules, sensor cutoffs, and budget limits filter reality.
- **The fitted population ($n\_{\mathrm{fit}}$):** the smaller subset left after models discard incomplete feature rows.

When a finite population of known size $N \ge 1$ is properly listed, Daniel G. Horvitz and Donovan J. Thompson (1952) proved how to recover an unbiased population mean. If every unit $i \in \lbrace 1, \dots, N\rbrace$ receives a **known, design-assigned** inclusion probability $\pi\_i = \mathbb{P}(R\_i = 1) \in (0, 1]$ ($R\_i = 1$ when logged), weighting each observed value by $w\_i = 1/\pi\_i$ restores the true mean:

$$\hat{Y}\_{\mathrm{HT}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum\_{i=1}^{N} \frac{R\_i Y\_i}{\pi\_i}, \qquad \mathbb{E}\bigl[\hat{Y}\_{\mathrm{HT}}\bigr] = \frac{1}{N}\sum\_{i=1}^{N} \frac{\mathbb{E}[R\_i] Y\_i}{\pi\_i} = \bar{Y}\_N.$$

Modern probability-survey engineering took shape simultaneously in the Global South. Prasanta Chandra Mahalanobis (1944, 1946) built optimal sample allocation balancing survey cost against spatial variance in Bengal crop surveys, and created **interpenetrating networks of sub-samples (*IPNS*)**, sending parallel independent teams across the same design to catch field and interviewer errors missed by textbook $1/\sqrt{n}$ formulas.

Unbiased design-variance calculation requires positive pairwise selection probabilities $\pi\_{ij} = \mathbb{P}(R\_i = 1, R\_j = 1) > 0$. When population count $N$ is unknown and replaced by $\hat{N}\_{\mathrm{HT}} = \sum\_{i: R\_i = 1} 1/\pi\_i$, the Hájek ratio estimator $\bigl(\sum\_{i: R\_i = 1} Y\_i/\pi\_i\bigr)/\hat{N}\_{\mathrm{HT}}$ stays ratio-consistent rather than finite-sample unbiased.

Horvitz–Thompson weighting fails along three structural boundaries. First, positivity collapses wherever $\pi\_i = 0$, either from missed units (`Not observed`) or pre-schema exclusions (`Not askable` at $T\_1$, [Chapter 33](33-measuring-and-erring.md)). Second, in observational data where $\hat{\pi}\_i(X\_i)$ must be modeled, selection driven by unobserved outcomes $Y\_i$ biases **both** raw and reweighted averages. Third, unbiased past draws never cross regime shifts without causal transportability rules (Bareinboim & Pearl 2016). Table joins widen that gap further: because SQL `NULL` keys never match ([Chapter 33](33-measuring-and-erring.md) §4), an `INNER JOIN` quietly sheds unlinked rows, shrinking coverage to $0 < f\_{\mathrm{join}} < f < 1$ and biasing averages whenever key absence tracks $Y$.

## 2. Volume is not representation: Meng's Big Data Paradox

Why can a dataset with five million rows be *less* reliable than a well-drawn poll of five hundred people? Ordinary random-sampling noise shrinks as $1/\sqrt{n}$. Yet if people with worse outcomes are slightly less likely to enter the database ($\rho\_{R,Y} \neq 0$), that bias never shrinks with row count. Instead, a massive sample just locks a tight false confidence band around the wrong number.

Xiao-Li Meng (2018) turned that intuition into an exact finite-population identity. Let $0 < n < N$, so sampling fraction $f = n/N = \bar{R} \in (0, 1)$ and recording standard deviation $\sigma\_R = \sqrt{f(1-f)} > 0$. Given population standard deviation $\sigma\_Y > 0$, the **data defect correlation** $\rho\_{R,Y} = \mathrm{Cov}(R, Y) / (\sigma\_R \sigma\_Y) \in [-1, 1]$ measures how strongly entering the table ($R$) correlates with the outcome ($Y$). Note that at a full census $n = N$ ($f = 1$), $\sigma\_R = 0$ and selection error vanishes identically. Writing $\bar{Y}\_n = (\frac{1}{N}\sum\_{i=1}^N R\_i Y\_i)/f$ and $\mathrm{Cov}(R, Y) = (\frac{1}{N}\sum\_{i=1}^N R\_i Y\_i) - f\bar{Y}\_N$, dividing by $f\sigma\_Y$ gives **Meng's identity**:

$$\frac{\bar{Y}\_n - \bar{Y}\_N}{\sigma\_Y} = \rho\_{R,Y}\sqrt{\frac{1-f}{f}}.$$

In plain words, the standardized error equals the product of data defect correlation ($\rho\_{R,Y}$) and the square-root odds of being left out of the archive ($\sqrt{(1 - f)/f}$). Three distinct regimes follow:

1. **Design-controlled recording ($\mathbb{E}[\rho\_{R,Y}] = 0$):** Under simple random sampling without replacement ($N \ge 2$), $\mathbb{E}[\rho\_{R,Y}^2] = 1/(N-1)$, giving standardized root-mean-square error $O(\sqrt{(1-f)/n})$ (or $O(\sqrt{\mathrm{DEFF}/n})$ under clustering in §3, corresponding to variance inflation $\mathrm{DEFF}/n$).
2. **Census limit vs. selective sorting ($f \to 1$ vs. $f \in (0, 1)$):** Selection error disappears only at $n = N$. For $f \in (0, 1)$, $|\rho\_{R,Y}| = 1$ occurs only when $Y\_i = a + b R\_i$ ($b \ne 0$); multi-valued outcomes keep $|\rho\_{R,Y}| < 1$, and bounded support $[y\_{\min}, y\_{\max}]$ limits $|\bar{Y}\_n - \bar{Y}\_N| \le y\_{\max} - y\_{\min}$. Yet whenever $|\rho\_{R,Y}|$ stays away from zero while coverage $f \to 0^+$, $\sqrt{(1-f)/f}$ magnifies bias across $\sigma\_Y$.
3. **Selective archive under partial coverage ($\rho\_{R,Y} \neq 0, f \in (0, 1)$):** Matching squared selection error $(\bar{Y}\_n - \bar{Y}\_N)^2$ against the variance $\sigma\_Y^2 / n\_{\mathrm{Meng}}$ of a clean **infinite-population** simple random sample shrinks the **effective sample size** to:

$$n\_{\mathrm{Meng}} = \frac{f}{(1-f)\rho\_{R,Y}^2}.$$

Read aloud, $n\_{\mathrm{Meng}}$ scales linearly in $f/(1 - f)$ and inverse-quadratically in $\rho\_{R,Y}$, refusing to grow with raw row count $n$ at fixed coverage. Empirically, against the 2021 CoWIN adult first-dose COVID-19 vaccination benchmark in India, the large non-probability Facebook COVID-19 Trends and Impact Survey (median 25,000 weekly responses, 1% response rate) suffered a median data defect correlation of $0.0040$ that collapsed its median weekly effective sample size to 2 (range 1–3; approximately 99.99% reduction from 25,000), whereas the smaller CVoter probability CATI survey (median 2,700 weekly responses, 55% response rate) held a median data defect correlation of $0.0005$ and median effective sample size of 9 (range 6–33; Yang et al. 2023). Because true $\rho\_{R,Y}$ depends on unknown $\bar{Y}\_N$, we evaluate $n\_{\mathrm{Meng}}(\bar{\rho})$ across a declared bound $\bar{\rho} \in (0, 1]$ ($\bar{\rho} \ge |\rho\_{R,Y}|$), which drops to $f/(1-f)$ at $\bar{\rho} = 1$ and creates an unshrinkable Type B uncertainty floor ([Chapter 33](33-measuring-and-erring.md)).

On the outcome scale, $|\rho\_{R,Y}| \le \bar{\rho}$ bounds the true mean inside the Manski–Meng interval $\bigl[\bar{Y}\_n \pm \bar{\rho}\mkern3mu\sigma\_Y\sqrt{(1-f)/f}\bigr]$. Because $\sigma\_Y > 0$ is unobserved under selective intake, evaluating this interval uses a known scale or bounded-support bound such as Popoviciu's $\sigma\_Y \le (y\_{\max} - y\_{\min})/2$, intersecting the resulting interval with Manski's sharp worst-case bounds $[f\bar{Y}\_n + (1 - f)y\_{\min},\mkern3mu f\bar{Y}\_n + (1 - f)y\_{\max}]$ so the estimate never leaves $[y\_{\min}, y\_{\max}]$. Writing the unclamped ratio $r^\star := \frac{\lvert\bar{Y}\_n - \bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{crit}}\rvert}{\sigma\_Y}\sqrt{\frac{f}{1 - f}}$ defines the **breakdown correlation** ($f \in (0, 1), \sigma\_Y > 0$):

$$\rho^\star\_{R,Y} = \min\bigl(1,\mkern4mu r^\star\bigr) = \min\\!\left(1,\mkern4mu \frac{\lvert\bar{Y}\_n - \bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{crit}}\rvert}{\sigma\_Y}\sqrt{\frac{f}{1 - f}}\right).$$

When $r^\star > 1$ ($\rho^\star\_{R,Y} = 1$), the Manski–Meng interval stays strictly on one side of $\bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{crit}}$ even at $\bar{\rho} = 1$, leaving the sign of $\bar{Y}\_N - \bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{crit}}$ identifiable across all correlations. When $\bar{Y}\_n \neq \bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{crit}}$ and $r^\star \le 1$, any selection bias strictly below $\rho^\star\_{R,Y}$ keeps the sign strict, at $\bar{\rho} = r^\star$ the interval touches $\bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{crit}}$ at one endpoint, and once $r^\star < \bar{\rho} \le 1$ the band strictly straddles $\bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{crit}}$ ($\rho^\star\_{R,Y} = r^\star = 0$ if $\bar{Y}\_n = \bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{crit}}$) and makes the verdict unidentifiable. Table size $f$ sits in the numerator while selection $(1 - f)\rho\_{R,Y}^2$ destroys precision in the denominator. If $N$ and $\sigma\_Y$ remain unknown, $\rho^\star\_{R,Y}$ instead defines a frontier of admissible $(N, \sigma\_Y)$ pairs.

## 3. Shared causes, clustering, and weighting efficiency

Even without selection bias, archive rows rarely provide independent evidence. Imagine sampling fifty hospital patients from one single ward sharing one broken ventilator versus fifty patients from fifty independent hospitals: the fifty correlated patients behave closer to one unit than fifty. As [Chapter 14](14-linear-algebra.md) showed for correlated linear spaces, shared variance shrinks effective dimension.

Under a homoscedastic one-way random-effects model, **intra-cluster correlation** $\rho\_I = \sigma\_u^2/(\sigma\_u^2 + \sigma\_e^2) \in [0, 1]$ (distinct from $\rho\_{R,Y}$; ratio estimates carry small-$C$ bias) measures the fraction of variation shared inside groups. Following Leslie Kish (1965) and Brent R. Moulton (1986), let $C \ge 1$ clusters have sizes $m\_1, \dots, m\_C \ge 1$ ($n = \sum\_{c=1}^C m\_c$), size variance $\sigma\_m^2 = \frac{1}{C}\sum\_{c=1}^C (m\_c - \bar{m})^2$, and squared coefficient of variation $\mathrm{CV}^2(m) = \sigma\_m^2/\bar{m}^2 \ge 0$. Effective cluster size is $\tilde{m} = \sum\_{c=1}^C m\_c^2 / n = \bar{m}\bigl(1 + \mathrm{CV}^2(m)\bigr) \ge \bar{m} \ge 1$, defining the **cluster design effect**:

$$\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{cluster}} = 1 + (\tilde{m}-1)\rho\_I \ge 1.$$

For balanced wards ($n = C\bar{m}$, $\tilde{m} = \bar{m}$) and $\rho\_I \in (0, 1]$, cluster-adjusted sample size is $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{cluster}} = C\bar{m}/\bigl[1 + (\bar{m} - 1)\rho\_I\bigr]$, strictly capped below $C/\rho\_I$ for $\rho\_I \in (0, 1)$ (reaching $C$ at $\rho\_I = 1$) no matter how many patients per ward are logged. Time creates the exact same correlation: call-center volumes cluster by hour and day, inflating variance across repeated shifts (Weinberg, Brown, & Stroud 2007; Shen & Huang 2008).

Unequal survey weights $w\_i > 0$ ($n \ge 1$, divisor $n$ in $\mathrm{CV}^2(w)$) inflate variance similarly via Kish's (1965, §11.7) weighting factor:

$$\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{Kish}} = 1 + \mathrm{CV}^2(w) = \frac{\sum\_i w\_i^2}{(\sum\_i w\_i)^2 / n} \ge 1.$$

Notice how cluster correlation and unit weights combine. Under two-component random effects ($\rho\_I \in [0, 1]$) with **unit-level** weights independent inside clusters and of cluster membership, sizes $m\_c$, and random effects $(u\_c, e\_{cj})$ in the large-$C$ limit, **the exact composition is additive**: $\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{total}} = \mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{cluster}} + \mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{Kish}} - 1 = 1 + (\tilde{m} - 1)\rho\_I + \mathrm{CV}^2(w) \ge 1 > 0$, bounded for $\rho\_I \ge 0$ by $\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{cluster}}\cdot\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{Kish}} = \mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{total}} + (\tilde{m} - 1)\rho\_I\mkern3mu\mathrm{CV}^2(w)$. Under shock-independent **cluster-constant** weights ($w\_{cj} = w\_c$), that product equals exact variance when $m\_c \equiv \bar{m}$ or large-$C$ $w\_c$ is independent of $m\_c$ (or size-weighted second-moment orthogonal in $w\_c^2$); when $w\_c^2$ depends on unequal $m\_c$, exact variance uses $\tilde{m}\_w = \sum\_c m\_c^2 w\_c^2 / \sum\_c m\_c w\_c^2$ in $\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{Kish}}[1 + (\tilde{m}\_w - 1)\rho\_I]$ (bounded for shock-independent weights and $\rho\_I \ge 0$ by $\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{Kish}}[1 + (m\_{\max} - 1)\rho\_I]$ with $m\_{\max} := \max\_c m\_c$), while shock-dependent weights require conditional shock-weighted variance.

For $n \ge 1$, $\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{total}} > 0$ fixes the **sampling-variance effective count** $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} = n / \mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{total}} > 0$. To bring both clustering and selection bias onto one scale ($f \in (0, 1)$, bound $\bar{\rho} \in (0, 1]$ with $\bar{\rho} \ge |\rho\_{R,Y}|$), combine sampling variance and squared selection error in parallel as a **harmonic effective sample size** (holding under the unit-level additive regime above):

$$\frac{1}{n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho})} = \frac{1}{n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}} + \left(\frac{1-f}{f}\right)\bar{\rho}^2 = \frac{\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{total}}}{n} + \left(\frac{1-f}{f}\right)\bar{\rho}^2 \mkern5mu\le\mkern5mu \frac{\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{cluster}}\cdot\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{Kish}}}{n} + \left(\frac{1-f}{f}\right)\bar{\rho}^2.$$

Read aloud, just as two bottlenecks in series limit flow by their sum of resistances, total information loss sums the cluster-deflated inverse count and selection-bias inverse count, reducing at full census ($f = 1$) to $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}$ for analytic inference across future cohorts, whereas descriptive finite-population sampling variance vanishes via $(1 - f)$. Feeding $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho})$ into [Chapter 33](33-measuring-and-erring.md)'s **evidential weight** $V\bigl(n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho}), n\_0\bigr) = n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho}) / \bigl(n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho}) + n\_0\bigr) \in [0, 1)$ (Keynes 1921; [Chapter 45](45-world-to-archive-big-data-paradox.md)) anchors inference in design quality, while remaining blind to unaskable groups ($\pi\_i \equiv 0$).

Textual scholarship discovered this exact deflation long ago. In Karl Lachmann's (1850) stemmatic method for reconstructing a lost *archetypon* from medieval manuscript copies (*descripti*), fifty copies copied from one surviving branch ($\rho\_I \to 1$) collapse to $n\_{\mathrm{eff}} \to 1$ witness for that branch. Paul Maas's *eliminatio codicum descriptorum* (Maas 1958 [1927]) operationalizes the classic forensic maxim: *ponderanda sunt testimonia, non numeranda* (weigh independent witnesses rather than counting dependent copies).

## 4. Missingness and dark archives

With missingness flag $M\_i = 1 - R\_i \in \lbrace 0, 1\rbrace$, building on Donald B. Rubin's (1976) formalization of missing at random (MAR), observed at random (OAR), and parameter distinctness, standard missing-data theory divides incomplete records into three mechanisms:

1. **MCAR (Missing Completely at Random):** $\mathbb{P}(M \mid Y\_{\mathrm{obs}}, Y\_{\mathrm{mis}}) = \mathbb{P}(M)$ with $\mathbb{P}(R\_i = 1) > 0$. Dropping missing rows cuts precision without bias.
2. **MAR (Missing at Random):** $\mathbb{P}(M \mid Y\_{\mathrm{obs}}, Y\_{\mathrm{mis}}, \psi) = \mathbb{P}(M \mid Y\_{\mathrm{obs}}, \psi)$. Likelihood or Bayesian inference remains unbiased under MAR plus **parameter distinctness** (Rubin 1976), while propensity reweighting also needs **positivity** ($\mathbb{P}(R\_i = 1 \mid X\_i) > 0$) and accurate models, and MAR stays untestable against MNAR from observed rows alone.
3. **MNAR (Missing Not at Random):** Missingness tracks the unseen value $Y\_{\mathrm{mis}}$ itself. Naive imputation projects survivor traits onto missing rows. Without external fusion structure (Bareinboim & Pearl 2016) or sensitivity bounds in $\bar{\rho}$ (§2), point identification fails and worst-case bounds require a declared **bounded support** $[y\_{\min}, y\_{\max}]$ (collapsing to $(-\infty, \infty)$ under heavy tails).

Moreover, $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho})$ governs the population mean $\bar{Y}\_N$, **not** extreme tail quantiles $q\_p$ ([Chapter 06](06-who-is-missing-from-the-table.md); [Chapter 45](45-world-to-archive-big-data-paradox.md)). On bounded support $[y\_{\min}, y\_{\max}]$ with observed share $f \in (0, 1)$ and population observed conditional CDF $F\_{\mathrm{obs}}(y) = \mathbb{P}(Y \le y \mid R = 1)$, total probability traps true $F\_Y(y)$ between $f F\_{\mathrm{obs}}(y)$ and $(1 - f) + f F\_{\mathrm{obs}}(y)$. That yields sharp Manski MNAR quantile bounds $[q\_L(p), q\_U(p)]$: whenever missing share $1 - f$ exceeds upper tail probability $1 - p$ ($p > f$), the upper quantile bound hits $q\_U(p) = y\_{\max}$ regardless of sample size (and $q\_L(p) = y\_{\min}$ for $p \le 1 - f$).

What if whole populations drop off registers completely, forming the **dark archive** of unlogged casualties or missing units (Lum, Price, & Banks 2013)? Think of cross-checking two independent lists: if almost every person recorded appears on only one list ($f\_1$ singletons) and very few overlap on both lists ($f\_2$ doubletons, where $f\_1, f\_2 \in \mathbb{N}\_0$ count incidence multiplicities, distinct from sampling fraction $f$ in §2), the unseen population missed by both lists can be large. Using Cauchy–Schwarz, Anne Chao bounded those missing units: $\hat{n}\_{00,\mathrm{Chao1}} = f\_1^2/(2 f\_2)$ under single-stream multinomial sampling with replacement for $f\_2 > 0$ (Chao 1984), and across $t \ge 2$ closed-population lists without within-list replacement under **symmetric list incidence** ($p\_{ij} = p\_i$, Model $M\_h$; Chao 1987; Chao 1989; Chao & Colwell 2017) with $f\_2 > 0$ ($n\_{11} > 0$):

$$\hat{n}\_{00,\mathrm{Chao2}}(t) = \frac{t - 1}{t}\mkern3mu\frac{f\_1^2}{2 f\_2}, \qquad \hat{n}\_{00,\mathrm{Chao2}}(2) = \frac{f\_1^2}{4 f\_2} = \frac{(n\_{10} + n\_{01})^2}{4 n\_{11}},$$

switching at $f\_2 = n\_{11} = 0$ to the continuity-adjusted formula $\frac{t - 1}{t}\frac{f\_1(f\_1 - 1)}{2(f\_2 + 1)}$ (or $\frac{f\_1(f\_1 - 1)}{4(n\_{11} + 1)}$ for $t = 2$; Chao & Colwell 2017).

Notice the boundary on two-list capture–recapture ($n\_{11} > 0$, $f\_1 = n\_{10} + n\_{01}$, $f\_2 = n\_{11}$). By AM–GM, $\hat{n}\_{00,\mathrm{Chao2}}(2) \ge n\_{10}n\_{01}/n\_{11} = \hat{n}\_{00,\mathrm{LP}}$ (matching only when $n\_{10} = n\_{01}$). Under symmetric Model $M\_h$, Cauchy–Schwarz guarantees $\mathbb{E}[n\_{00}] \ge \frac{t-1}{t}\frac{(\mathbb{E}[f\_1])^2}{2\mathbb{E}[f\_2]}$ in expectation (and for $t = 2$, under non-negative list dependence aside from finite-sample noise). **When $n\_{10} \neq n\_{01}$ reflects list-varying coverage ($p\_{ij} = p\_j$, Model $M\_t$), heterogeneous unit-and-list capture ($p\_{i1} \neq p\_{i2}$, Model $M\_{th}$), or negative dependence ($p\_{11} < p\_1 p\_2$), this lower bound fails**: AM–GM pushes $\hat{n}\_{00,\mathrm{Chao2}}(2)$ past Lincoln–Petersen and can overshoot true $N$, demanding multi-list models across $t \ge 3$ registries (Lum, Price, & Banks 2013; [Chapter 06](06-who-is-missing-from-the-table.md); [Chapter 45](45-world-to-archive-big-data-paradox.md)).

Database joins dictate those counts before estimation begins. Across two lists ($f\_2 = n\_{11} > 0$), a matched-only `INNER JOIN` drops unmatched rows $n\_{10}, n\_{01}$, forcing $f\_1' = 0$ and $\hat{n}\_{00} = 0$. Keeping unmatched records with `FULL OUTER JOIN` preserves singletons. Yet if $1 \le \Delta < f\_2$ true overlaps ($f\_1 \ge 0$) carry unresolved Codd *A-marks* or *I-marks* on link keys, failed equality splits each match into two fake singletons ($f\_1' = f\_1 + 2\Delta$, $f\_2' = f\_2 - \Delta > 0$). That inflates $\hat{n}\_{00,\mathrm{Chao2}}(2) = (f\_1 + 2\Delta)^2 / \bigl(4(f\_2 - \Delta)\bigr) > f\_1^2 / (4 f\_2)$ (Chao & Colwell 2017), while even outer joins stay blind to `Not askable` exclusions ($\pi\_i \equiv 0$).

Historical preservation filters archives deeply. Taphonomy, founded in paleontology to study what survives decay (Efremov 1940) and extended to archaeology and historical archives, joins Andean *khipu* accountants checking knotted tribute cords across mirrored paired ledgers (Urton 2003) and Ibn Khaldūn's fourteenth-century *Muqaddimah* discrediting chroniclers whose battle tallies breached ecological carrying capacity (Rosenthal 1958). Extending that critique, Michel-Rolph Trouillot (1995) named four moments of archival silence:

1. **Fact creation:** who enters the record.
2. **Fact assembly:** which records physically survive.
3. **Fact retrieval:** how indexes weight surviving records.
4. **Retrospective significance:** how hindsight rewrites history, quarantined only by append-only **bitemporal archives** specializing *valid time* and *transaction time* into event time ($t\_{\mathrm{ev}}$) and append-only availability time ($t\_{\mathrm{disp}} \ge t\_{\mathrm{ev}}$; Snodgrass 2000).

Database internals explain why moments 2 and 4 need architectural custody above storage engines. **B+-trees** overwrite pages in place (Comer 1979); **LSM-trees** append sequentially before rolling-merge compaction drops tombstones and old versions (O'Neil et al. 1996); and **ACID** transactions guarantee consistency of the *current* materialized table (Haerder & Reuter 1983), not historical provenance. Valid `UPDATE` or `DELETE` commands erase earlier knowledge unless append-only bitemporal custody precedes cryptographic sealing ([Chapter 40](40-institution-incentive-metric.md); [Chapter 45](45-world-to-archive-big-data-paradox.md)). Real-time macroeconomic series routinely undergo revisions comparable in magnitude to published growth rates, reversing policy verdicts (Croushore & Stark 2001).

Even bitemporality fixes only moment 4. As James C. Scott (1998) and Georges Canguilhem (1943/1966) show, rigid classification rules lock `Not askable` exclusions ($\pi\_i \equiv 0$) at moments 1–3, saturating internal weight $V \to 1$ while pushing unrecorded harm onto external reception perimeters' capacity to absorb strain ([Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md); [Appendix A](appendix-a-mathematics.md) §A.7). Under [Chapter 29](29-aim-and-the-three-planes.md) §4 and [Chapter 36](36-decision-loss-threshold.md), crisis crews read the worst sealed vintage rather than `INNER JOIN` or overwritten views, leaving multi-list reconciliation off-shift ([Chapter 33](33-measuring-and-erring.md)).

### When the design effect is one by decision

Even a true census ($f = 1$, uniform weights $w\_i \equiv 1$ fixing $\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{Kish}} = 1$) eliminates only descriptive finite-population sampling variance, never `INNER JOIN` fakes or **analytic inference across future cohorts**: past populations retain clustering ($\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{cluster}} > 1$), omit `Not askable` groups ($\pi\_i \equiv 0$), and break under **target-label substitution**, such as training medical risk tools on healthcare spending rather than actual illness (Obermeyer et al. 2019). Declare universe, join rules, weights, and estimand explicitly rather than letting apparent $f = 1$ excuse extrapolation.
