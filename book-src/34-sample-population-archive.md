## By the end of this chapter you can

1. Say what a sample estimates and what it cannot, for a population that was never enumerated.
2. Separate a large archive from a representative one, and give the arithmetic reason the first
   does not buy the second.
3. Classify missingness as ignorable or not, and say what each classification costs.

## 1. Three different objects

Suppose a regional mortgage lender trains an automated default-prediction model on a subset of $n\_{\mathrm{fit}}$ fully repaid loans with complete tax transcripts, drawn from an internal archive of $n\_{\mathrm{arch}} > n\_{\mathrm{fit}}$ historical applicants who passed initial branch screening, and deploys it across a much larger target population of $N \gg n\_{\mathrm{arch}}$ households entering an industrial recession. Applicants denied at the branch window never entered the archive. Borrowers who defaulted mid-year and stopped submitting tax schedules were dropped by listwise deletion. The fitted model scores cleanly under cross-validation on the $n\_{\mathrm{fit}}$ survivors, and fails on the $N$ households it was hired to judge.

Three distinct populations are in play in any empirical investigation. In real institutions they are almost never congruent.

The **target population** ($N$) is the complete universe of cases the decision will govern: every patient
who will arrive in the emergency department next year, every borrower facing recession, every structural
joint in the bridge under gale loads. It is never fully enumerated, and by the time it has passed it is history, not action.

The **archive population** ($n\_{\mathrm{arch}}$) is the set of cases an institution observed and
recorded: the intersection of what physically occurred, what triggered sensor thresholds, what survived
operational cleaning, and what the database schema had a category for.

The **fitted population** ($n\_{\mathrm{fit}}$) is the subset of the archive retained by the statistical
pipeline: the rows with complete feature vectors that survived listwise deletion.

To bridge the archive back to the target population without bias, Daniel G. Horvitz and Donovan J. Thompson (1952) proved that if every unit $i \in \lbrace1, \dots, N\rbrace$ in the target population has a known, strictly positive probability $\pi\_i = \mathbb{P}(R\_i = 1) \in (0, 1]$ of being recorded ($R\_i = 1$), weighting each observed record by its inverse inclusion probability $w\_i = 1/\pi\_i$ recovers an exactly unbiased estimator of the finite-population mean:

$$\hat{Y}\_{\mathrm{HT}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum\_{i=1}^{N} \frac{R\_i Y\_i}{\pi\_i}, \qquad \mathbb{E}\bigl[\hat{Y}\_{\mathrm{HT}}\bigr] = \frac{1}{N}\sum\_{i=1}^{N} \frac{\mathbb{E}[R\_i] Y\_i}{\pi\_i} = \bar{Y}\_N.$$

Horvitz–Thompson reweighting fails along two institutional fault lines. Where an excluded subpopulation has $\pi\_i = 0$, a positivity failure, no finite weight reconstructs the missing stratum. Where the recording indicator $R\_i$ depends on the unobserved outcome $Y\_i$ itself, the true propensities $\pi\_i$ are unknown and unweighted archive averages carry the selection bias.

A claim that fails to distinguish which of these three populations it describes is an unvalidated claim. Most
catastrophic failures in automated machine learning stem from the unmeasured gulf between the fitted sample and
the open target population, a gulf no in-sample goodness-of-fit statistic can detect.

## 2. Volume is not representation: Meng's Big Data Paradox

Suppose a national retail bank collects satisfaction surveys from a small fraction $f = n/N$ of its account holders, and dissatisfied customers are slightly less likely to complete the form, inducing a tiny correlation $\rho\_{R,Y} < 0$ between the response indicator $R\_i \in \lbrace0, 1\rbrace$ and satisfaction $Y\_i$. Management celebrates a multi-million-row archive as a near-census with a textbook standard error shrunk by the square root of the row count. In reality, that massive volume has merely locked in a systematic upward distortion.

The stubborn intuition that massive sample sizes guarantee asymptotic truth holds under one condition:
that the data recording mechanism is statistically independent of the outcome being measured. When recording is
even slightly selective, data volume sharpens and solidifies the error; it does not eliminate it.

Xiao-Li Meng (2018) proved an exact algebraic identity for the error of the sample mean across any finite population. Let $f = n/N = \bar{R} \in (0, 1]$ be
the sampling fraction, $\sigma\_R = \sqrt{f(1-f)}$ the population standard deviation of the binary recording indicator $R$, $\sigma\_Y > 0$ the population standard deviation of $Y$, and $\rho\_{R,Y} = \mathrm{Cov}(R, Y) / (\sigma\_R \sigma\_Y) \in [-1, 1]$ the
**data defect correlation** between $R$ and $Y$. Because the archive mean is $\bar{Y}\_n = (\frac{1}{N}\sum\_{i=1}^N R\_i Y\_i) / f$ and the finite-population covariance is $\mathrm{Cov}(R, Y) = (\frac{1}{N}\sum\_{i=1}^N R\_i Y\_i) - f\bar{Y}\_N$, dividing $\mathrm{Cov}(R, Y) = \rho\_{R,Y}\sqrt{f(1-f)}\mkern3mu\sigma\_Y$ by $f \sigma\_Y$ yields the exact standardized estimation error:

$$\frac{\bar{Y}\_n - \bar{Y}\_N}{\sigma\_Y} = \rho\_{R,Y}\sqrt{\frac{1-f}{f}}.$$

Three regimes follow from the identity, and the third is the one institutions meet.

1. **Ignorable random recording** ($\rho\_{R,Y} \to 0$). When the recording filter is uncorrelated with the outcome, the systematic error $\rho\_{R,Y}\sigma\_Y\sqrt{(1-f)/f} \to 0$, and the estimation error converges to zero at the classical simple-random-sampling rate $O(1/\sqrt{n})$.
2. **Exhaustive census against adversarial filter** ($f \to 1$, $|\rho\_{R,Y}| \to 1$). As $n \to N$ so $f \to 1$, the drop-out factor $\sqrt{(1-f)/f} \to 0$ and the selection bias vanishes because no unit remains unrecorded; conversely, at fixed partial coverage, if selection approaches full sorting ($|\rho\_{R,Y}| \to 1$), the absolute error reaches the whole outcome scale $\sigma\_Y\sqrt{(1-f)/f}$, which grows without bound as coverage thins.
3. **Selective archive under partial coverage** ($\rho\_{R,Y} \neq 0, f < 1$). Equating the squared selection error $(\bar{Y}\_n - \bar{Y}\_N)^2$ to the variance $\sigma\_Y^2 / n\_{\mathrm{Meng}}$ of a genuinely random sample shows that the **effective sample size** of a biased big-data archive collapses to:

$$n\_{\mathrm{Meng}} = \frac{f}{(1-f)\rho\_{R,Y}^2}.$$

Read as an identity in the unknown defect, this is not an estimate of $n\_{\mathrm{Meng}}$ but the graph of a map. $\rho\_{R,Y}$ is defined against $\bar{Y}\_N$, which the archive does not contain, so what an analyst can write down is $n\_{\mathrm{Meng}}(\bar{\rho})$ for a declared bound $\bar{\rho} \ge |\rho\_{R,Y}|$. The map is decreasing in $\bar{\rho}$, and at the only bound the identity itself supplies, $|\rho\_{R,Y}| \le 1$, it gives the floor $n\_{\mathrm{Meng}} \ge f/(1-f)$. At $\bar{\rho} = 0$ it diverges, which is the arithmetic form of the ignorability assumption: no finite archive produces a selection penalty that the analysis has assumed away. Quoting a single $n\_{\mathrm{Meng}}$ is therefore quoting the map at a point the analyst chose.

On the location scale, the same identity fixes a **breakdown correlation** against any declared decision threshold $\bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{crit}}$. Under $|\rho\_{R,Y}| \le \bar{\rho}$, the Manski–Meng partial-identification interval for $\bar{Y}\_N$ is $\bigl[\bar{Y}\_n - \bar{\rho}\mkern3mu\sigma\_Y\sqrt{(1-f)/f},\mkern5mu \bar{Y}\_n + \bar{\rho}\mkern3mu\sigma\_Y\sqrt{(1-f)/f}\bigr]$. Setting the half-width equal to the observed distance $|\bar{Y}\_n - \bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{crit}}|$ gives the critical defect correlation (capped at $1$ by Cauchy–Schwarz, for $0 < f < 1$ and $\sigma\_Y > 0$):

$$\rho^\star\_{R,Y} = \min\mkern-3mu\left(1,\mkern4mu \frac{\lvert\bar{Y}\_n - \bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{crit}}\rvert}{\sigma\_Y}\sqrt{\frac{f}{1 - f}}\right).$$

Whenever $|\rho\_{R,Y}| < \rho^\star\_{R,Y}$, every admissible population mean stays on the same side of $\bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{crit}}$ as $\bar{Y}\_n$; once $\bar{\rho} \ge \rho^\star\_{R,Y}$ with the ratio at most $1$, the interval straddles $\bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{crit}}$ and missingness reverses the decision (or, for $\bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{crit}} = 0$, the sign).

The identity has a consequence that reads as a paradox and is not one. Because the collapse depends on the correlation and the coverage ratio and not on the row count at all, an administrative database of arbitrarily many records can carry the statistical information of a handful of independent random draws while radiating the spurious authority of a massive census: the numerator $f$ is what the institution buys when it grows its volume, the denominator is what its filter destroys, and only their ratio matters. An imperceptible correlation between who is recorded and what is measured is enough to put two orders of magnitude between the row count and the evidence.

## 3. Shared causes, clustering, and weighting efficiency

Administrative and physical records are rarely independent and identically distributed draws. Patients arrive
within clinical wards; transactions cluster by merchant terminal; strain readings cluster by sensor bus. When
observations share an unmeasured cluster-level shock, each additional row in the cluster carries diminishing
information, and ten thousand more rows from the same correlated cohorts add storage without adding evidence.

The **intra-cluster correlation** $\rho\_I \in [0, 1]$ measures the fraction of total variance attributable to differences between clusters, not to differences inside them. Following Kish's unequal-cluster treatment (Kish 1965, §5.5) and Brent R. Moulton (1986), for clusters of effective size $\tilde{m} = \sum\_{c=1}^C m\_c^2 / n = \bar{m}\bigl(1 + \mathrm{CV}^2(m)\bigr)$ (reducing to the average size $\bar{m}$ when clusters are balanced), a one-way random-effects decomposition of variance estimates $\rho\_I$ and yields
the **cluster design effect**:

$$\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{cluster}} = 1 + (\tilde{m}-1)\rho\_I.$$

In the balanced special case of a registry recording $n = C\bar{m}$ patients distributed across $C$ hospital wards of $\bar{m}$ patients each ($\tilde{m} = \bar{m}$), positive
intra-ward correlation $\rho\_I > 0$ yields $\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{cluster}} = 1 + (\bar{m} - 1)\rho\_I > 1$. The effective
sample size shrinks to $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{cluster}} = C\bar{m}/\bigl[1 + (\bar{m} - 1)\rho\_I\bigr] < n$, bounded above by $C/\rho\_I$ however many patients each ward records.

The same arithmetic governs a time series of arrivals. Call-center arrival records of the kind modeled by Weinberg, Brown, & Stroud (2007) and Shen & Huang (2008) cluster by hour and by day, and a demographic survey clusters by household and by census tract; in each case the rows inside a cohort share an unmeasured shock, the design effect divides the count, and the effective sample that remains is the only quantity the evidence can carry.

Similarly, survey post-stratification weights $w\_i$ reduce effective sample size. Leslie Kish (1965) proved that
weight dispersion inflates variance by:

$$\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{Kish}} = 1 + \mathrm{CV}^2(w) = \frac{\sum\_i w\_i^2}{(\sum\_i w\_i)^2 / n} \ge 1.$$

Under the two-component model with weights independent of cluster membership, the exact composition is additive, $\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{total}} = \mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{cluster}} + \mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{Kish}} - 1 = 1 + (\tilde{m} - 1)\rho\_I + \mathrm{CV}^2(w)$, whereas multiplying the two factors ($\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{cluster}}\cdot\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{Kish}} = \mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{total}} + (\tilde{m} - 1)\rho\_I\mkern3mu\mathrm{CV}^2(w)$) serves only as a conservative upper bound that exceeds it by the non-negative cross term. The sampling-variance effective sample size entering standard-error denominators is $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} = n / \mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{total}}$; combining $1/n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}$ with Meng's finite-population selection-bias penalty evaluated at a declared bound $\bar{\rho} \ge |\rho\_{R,Y}|$ (since $\rho\_{R,Y}$ itself depends on the unobserved population mean) yields the harmonic effective count governing Keynesian evidential weight:

$$\frac{1}{n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho})} = \frac{1}{n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}} + \left(\frac{1-f}{f}\right)\bar{\rho}^2 = \frac{\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{total}}}{n} + \left(\frac{1-f}{f}\right)\bar{\rho}^2 \mkern5mu\le\mkern5mu \frac{\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{cluster}}\cdot\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{Kish}}}{n} + \left(\frac{1-f}{f}\right)\bar{\rho}^2.$$

Classical philology reached the same arithmetic from the other direction, under the name *stemma codicum*. In
Karl Lachmann's (1850) methodology, counting how many surviving Renaissance
manuscripts agree on a variant reading (*codices plurimi*) commits a nominal-volume fallacy whenever
a pile of copies derives from a single damaged archetype (*codices descripti*, $\rho\_I \to 1$,
$\mathrm{DEFF}$ as large as the cluster, $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}$ as small as one copy). Maas's *eliminatio codicum
descriptorum* and the maxim *ponderanda sunt testimonia, non numeranda* (witnesses must be
weighed, not counted) is the philological ancestor of dividing raw volume by design effect before
calculating Keynesian evidential weight.

## 4. Missingness and dark archives

Donald B. Rubin's taxonomy separates missing data into three regimes by whether missingness is ignorable (Rubin 1976):

1. **MCAR (Missing Completely at Random)**: $\mathbb{P}(M \mid Y\_{\mathrm{obs}}, Y\_{\mathrm{mis}}) = \mathbb{P}(M)$.
   Observed data is a random subsample. Listwise deletion causes loss of power but zero bias.
2. **MAR (Missing at Random)**: $\mathbb{P}(M \mid Y\_{\mathrm{obs}}, Y\_{\mathrm{mis}}) = \mathbb{P}(M \mid Y\_{\mathrm{obs}})$.
   Missingness is conditional on observed covariates. Recoverable through inverse propensity weighting or multiple
   imputation, but fundamentally unverifiable from the dataset itself.
3. **MNAR (Missing Not at Random)**: Missingness depends on the unobserved value itself. Imputation creates
   dangerous fictions; the only rigorous recourse is partial identification under worst-case bounds.

To bound the **dark archive**, entities that appear zero times in the register, as in human-rights casualty estimation (Lum, Price, & Banks 2013), Anne Chao derived non-parametric Cauchy–Schwarz lower bounds from rare-incidence frequencies: $\hat{n}\_{00,\mathrm{Chao1}} = f\_1^2/(2 f\_2)$ under single-stream multinomial sampling (Chao 1984), and across $t \ge 2$ incomplete registries without within-list replacement (Chao 1987; Chao & Jost 2012),

$$\hat{n}\_{00,\mathrm{Chao2}}(t) = \frac{t - 1}{t}\mkern3mu\frac{f\_1^2}{2 f\_2}, \qquad \hat{n}\_{00,\mathrm{Chao2}}(2) = \frac{f\_1^2}{4 f\_2} = \frac{(n\_{10} + n\_{01})^2}{4 n\_{11}}.$$

The two-list bound is worth reading against the estimator it replaces. Two incomplete registries observe $n\_{11}$ shared units, $n\_{10}$ units unique to the first list, and $n\_{01}$ units unique to the second, so $N\_{\mathrm{obs}} = n\_{11} + n\_{10} + n\_{01}$, $f\_1 = n\_{10} + n\_{01}$, and $f\_2 = n\_{11}$. By AM–GM, $\hat{n}\_{00,\mathrm{Chao2}}(2) = (n\_{10} + n\_{01})^2/(4 n\_{11}) \ge n\_{10}n\_{01}/n\_{11} = \hat{n}\_{00,\mathrm{LP}}$ (with equality if and only if $n\_{10} = n\_{01}$), and under individual catchability heterogeneity or positive list dependence (where shared blind spots inflate $n\_{11}$ relative to $f\_1$), Cauchy–Schwarz guarantees $\mathbb{E}[n\_{00}] \ge (\mathbb{E}[f\_1])^2/(4\mkern2mu\mathbb{E}[f\_2])$. Both estimators then sit below the true unseen count, making $N\_{\mathrm{obs}} + \hat{n}\_{00,\mathrm{Chao2}}(2)$ a defensible floor on the population rather than a census.

Archaeological taphonomy (Efremov 1940) and historical sociology formalize how physical preservation
biases the archive. In *Silencing the Past*, Michel-Rolph Trouillot (1995) identified four successive moments where power
manufactures institutional silence:

1. **Fact creation**: Who enters the historical record at all.
2. **Fact assembly**: What documents survive into the physical archive.
3. **Fact retrieval**: How catalogs and algorithms index and weight what was kept.
4. **Retrospective significance**: How subsequent generations rewrite the record with hindsight, a distortion
   quarantined by an append-only bitemporal record that keeps the value as it stood at each decision instant alongside its later revision. The revision history of real-time macroeconomic vintages that Croushore & Stark (2001) document is the standard evidence that an initial release is not the final historical value: the first announcement of a quarter's growth is routinely revised, sometimes by a magnitude comparable to the growth being reported, and a measurable share of revisions flip the sign of the change. A decision taken on the initial print was taken on a different number from the one the archive will eventually hold, and no later query of the revised series can recover what the policymaker could have known.

### Volume is not representation

Under a small non-response correlation $\rho\_{R,Y}$, a vast respondent count $n$ out of a large population $N$ carries a selection-limited effective count $n\_{\mathrm{Meng}} = f/((1-f)\rho\_{R,Y}^2)$ that can be smaller than the count of a single well-drawn survey wave, while demographic clustering over cells of effective size $\tilde{m} = \sum\_{c=1}^C m\_c^2 / n$ (equal to $\bar{m}$ when cells are balanced) inflates sampling variance by $\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{cluster}} = 1 + (\tilde{m}-1)\rho\_I$ (shrinking the raw count $n$ to $n/\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{cluster}}$, with $\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{total}}$ adding any weight dispersion as in §3). The two penalties compose additively in reciprocal effective sample size—$1/n\_{\mathrm{eff}} = \mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{total}}/n + 1/n\_{\mathrm{Meng}}$—and do not cancel; the resulting harmonic effective sample $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is the quantity the evidence actually rests on.

The unseen share has its own arithmetic. Whenever marginal coverages differ ($n\_{10} \ne n\_{01}$) the two-list bound $f\_1^2/(4f\_2)$ exceeds the Lincoln–Petersen projection by AM–GM, while positive list dependence or shared blind spots inflate doubletons $f\_2$ and depress both projections below the true $n\_{00}$: the dark archive outside both lists can exceed the visible archive inside them, and the visible count plus $f\_1^2/(4f\_2)$ remains a floor, not a census.

More rows do not repair a filter; they make the filter more confidently wrong, and a bad filter with more rows is a bad filter with a wider evidentiary footprint.

### When the design effect is one by decision

The design effect is irrelevant when the estimand is the mean of a census with uniform weights, and in that case it is fixed at one by decision, not by measurement. The procedure is to declare the universe and the weighting rule, and to record the justification instead of reporting a number that the data cannot inform.
