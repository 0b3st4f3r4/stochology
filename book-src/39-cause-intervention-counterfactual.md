## By the end of this chapter you can

1. Separate *what goes with what* from *what happens if we act*, and say which one a decision needs.
2. Read a causal claim and name the identification or invariance assumption that would make it false.
3. Ask a counterfactual question precisely enough to know when data pin it to one answer and when only to an interval.

## 1. Pearl's three-rung causal hierarchy

In [Chapter 18](18-philosophy-of-science.md) and [Chapter 35](35-inference-and-evidence.md), we saw why predicting past correlations never guarantees what an action changes. Judea Pearl (2009) organized causal reasoning into a strict **three-level causal hierarchy**:

```
Level 3: COUNTERFACTUALS  P(y'_{x'} | x, y)  "Had we chosen x' instead of x, would harm y have been averted?"
         ^
Level 2: INTERVENTIONS    P(y | do(x))       "What will happen if we take action x?"
         ^
Level 1: ASSOCIATIONS     P(y | x)           "What does observing symptom x tell us about y?"
```

1. **Rung 1 (Association):** passive observation and historical correlation, the entire world of curve fitting and supervised machine learning. Conditioning passively on post-treatment survivors ([Chapter 38](38-time-and-dependence.md)) opens collider bias instead of isolating causal impact.
2. **Rung 2 (Intervention):** active manipulation written in Pearl's do-calculus as $P(y \mid \mathrm{do}(x))$, which cuts incoming arrows into $X$ to simulate forcing $X = x$ by external action.
3. **Rung 3 (Counterfactuals):** retrospective necessity and blame, asking *given bad outcome $y$ after action $x$, would alternative $x'$ have prevented it?* No prospective experiment observes counterfactuals directly, because one unit cannot live two histories simultaneously.

Policy choices are always Rung 2 or Rung 3 questions. Answering them from Rung 1 correlations without causal assumptions is the single most common category mistake in automated decision-making.

## 2. Invariant Causal Prediction and structural identification

How can an institution identify genuine causal drivers without running a randomized trial? Classical backdoor adjustment requires assuming conditional ignorability ($Y\_x \perp X \mid Z$, meaning no unblocked unmeasured confounding given adjustment set $Z$), which raw records cannot test. Look instead across different environments $e \in \mathcal{E}$ (such as clinics, regions, or policy shifts) where background conditions vary while the outcome's physical mechanism stays fixed. Spurious correlates and downstream symptoms wobble when policies change, whereas direct causal parents $X\_{\mathrm{PA}}$ keep their structural relationship with $Y$ steady everywhere.

Jonas Peters, Peter Bühlmann, and Nicolai Meinshausen (2016) formalized this insight as **Invariant Causal Prediction (ICP)**. Assuming no unmeasured confounding or shifting hidden direct parents affecting $Y$, common support of $X\_{\mathrm{PA}}$ across $\mathcal{E}$, and interventions across $\mathcal{E}$ that never touch $Y$'s own structural equation, the conditional distribution $Y \mid X\_{\mathrm{PA}}$ remains strictly **invariant across every environment**:

$$P(Y \mid X\_{\mathrm{PA}}, E = e\_1) = P(Y \mid X\_{\mathrm{PA}}, E = e\_2) \qquad \forall e\_1, e\_2 \in \mathcal{E}.$$

In plain words, ICP tests every predictor index subset $S \subseteq \lbrace 1, \dots, d\rbrace$ (distinct from §5's risk score $S \in [0, 1]$) for stability across $\mathcal{E}$, collects all invariant subsets into $\mathcal{A}$, and takes their intersection $\hat{S}^\ast = \bigcap\_{S \in \mathcal{A}} S$ (returning an empty refusal output whenever $\mathcal{A} = \emptyset$). At error level $\alpha \in (0, 1)$, Peters, Bühlmann, and Meinshausen (2016) prove conservative parent coverage $\mathbb{P}(\hat{S}^\ast \subseteq X\_{\mathrm{PA}}) \ge 1 - \alpha$; recovering every parent requires rich environmental variation. Notice the two distinct empty outputs: **no** invariant set ($\mathcal{A} = \emptyset$) refutes the structural premise (exposing missing variables, hidden confounding, or direct shocks to $Y$), whereas $\hat{S}^\ast = \emptyset$ with $\mathcal{A} \neq \emptyset$ flags collinear inputs, low statistical power accepting $S = \emptyset$, genuine zero parents ($X\_{\mathrm{PA}} = \emptyset$), or environments $\mathcal{E}$ that fail to perturb $X\_{\mathrm{PA}}$, the agnotological trap guarded in [Chapter 48](48-reactive-worlds-performativity-goodhart.md).

## 3. Counterfactual responsibility: Tian-Pearl bounds

After disaster strikes, accountability asks a Rung-3 question: *did deploying this rule cause the harm, or would harm have occurred anyway?*

Take binary action $X \in \lbrace x, x'\rbrace$ and binary outcome $Y \in \lbrace 0, 1\rbrace$ ($y$ for $Y = 1$, $y'$ for $Y = 0$) satisfying causal **consistency** ($X = x \implies Y\_x = Y$ and $X = x' \implies Y\_{x'} = Y$). Pearl defines joint causal impact through the **Probability of Necessity and Sufficiency (PNS)**, alongside individual necessity $\mathrm{PN} = \mathbb{P}(Y\_{x'} = 0 \mid X = x, Y = 1)$ when $\mathbb{P}(X = x, Y = 1) > 0$:

$$\mathrm{PNS} = \mathbb{P}(Y\_x = 1, Y\_{x'} = 0).$$

Because we never see both $Y\_x$ and $Y\_{x'}$ on the same person, $\mathrm{PNS}$ is only partially identified. From experimental marginals $P(y \mid \mathrm{do}(x))$ and $P(y \mid \mathrm{do}(x'))$ alone, Fréchet–Hoeffding bounds yield a wide interval:

$$\max\bigl(0,\mkern3mu P(y \mid \mathrm{do}(x)) - P(y \mid \mathrm{do}(x'))\bigr) \le \mathrm{PNS} \le \min\bigl(P(y \mid \mathrm{do}(x)),\mkern3mu P(y' \mid \mathrm{do}(x'))\bigr).$$

Can observational behavior shrink those counterfactual bounds without assuming monotonicity? Yes: knowing how people *actually behaved and fared* together with experimental effects constrains the joint probabilities. Assuming cross-source population compatibility (a common population $P(Y\_x, Y\_{x'})$ satisfying the empirical compatibility bounds $P(x, y) \le P(y \mid \mathrm{do}(x)) \le 1 - P(x, y')$ and $P(x', y) \le P(y \mid \mathrm{do}(x')) \le 1 - P(x', y')$, whose failure yields an empty inverted interval refuting pooling; [Chapter 48](48-reactive-worlds-performativity-goodhart.md) §7.2.3), Jin Tian and Judea Pearl (2000) proved that combining observational joints $P(x, y)$ (with $P(y) = P(x, y) + P(x', y)$) with experimental arms tightens the sharp bounds without monotonicity assumptions to:

$$\begin{aligned}
\max\Bigl\lbrace{}0,\mkern4mu P(y \mid \mathrm{do}(x)) - P(y \mid \mathrm{do}(x')),\mkern4mu P(y) - P(y \mid \mathrm{do}(x')),\mkern4mu P(y \mid \mathrm{do}(x)) - P(y)\Bigr\rbrace{} &\le \mathrm{PNS}, \\\\
\mathrm{PNS} &\le \min\Bigl\lbrace{}P(y \mid \mathrm{do}(x)),\mkern4mu P(y' \mid \mathrm{do}(x')),\mkern4mu P(x, y) + P(x', y'),\mkern4mu P(y \mid \mathrm{do}(x)) - P(y \mid \mathrm{do}(x')) + P(x, y') + P(x', y)\Bigr\rbrace{}.
\end{aligned}$$

Read aloud, these inequalities fix the exact honest interval for retrospective responsibility without inventing unverified monotonicity or parametric priors.

## 4. Institutional design: the three pillars, Sosa AAA, and Fricker's injustice

Causal tools predict intervention effects; governance decides who acts and who answers. In principal–agent theory ([Chapter 20](20-legitimacy-and-institutions.md); Holmström 1979; formalized in [Chapter 40](40-institution-incentive-metric.md) §3), accountability rests on **Observability**, **Authority**, and **Consequence**. As the *Challenger* disaster proved (Vaughan 1996), those three pillars stay incomplete without an **anti-closure safeguard** (independent waiver reopening and automatic non-self-extending sunsets) to stop normalized deviance.

Under Ernest Sosa's (2007, 2015) **AAA** and **full-aptness** grading ([Chapter 16](16-what-knowledge-is.md); [Chapter 29](29-aim-and-the-three-planes.md)), agents are judged on non-deviant craft rather than outcome hindsight (Baron & Hershey 1988). That separates genuine competence from Gettier double luck (Gettier 1963) and outcome luck (Williams 1981; Nagel 1979; [Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md)), while commanding the archer to lower the bow whenever causal structure is unidentifiable (§6–§7), except in binary double binds forcing explicit rule challenge ([Chapter 27](27-what-judging-is.md)).

Silence breaks accountability just as sharply as poor statistics. Miranda Fricker (2007, 2020) identified **testimonial injustice** (credibility deficits rooted in identity prejudice, specialized here to discounting frontline warnings by speaker rank $\delta\_{\mathrm{status}} > 0$) alongside **hermeneutical injustice** (structural gaps in collective interpretive resources, specialized here to missing causal or archival categories; [Chapter 18](18-philosophy-of-science.md)). Inside Stafford Beer's (1972) **algedonic channel**, rank discounting $\delta\_{\mathrm{status}} \in (0, 1)$ stretches pre-ceiling detection latency $\tau\_{\mathrm{detect}}(\delta\_{\mathrm{status}})$ by $(1 - \delta\_{\mathrm{status}})^{-1}$ (up to discrete ceiling rounding $\lceil \cdot \rceil$ over shift counts; [Appendix A](appendix-a-mathematics.md) §A.3 #19), pushing recovery past physical survival time ([Chapter 38](38-time-and-dependence.md); [Chapter 40](40-institution-incentive-metric.md)). Given tightly bounded live attention ([Chapter 36](36-decision-loss-threshold.md)), Stochology enforces zero status discounting ($\delta\_{\mathrm{status}} = 0$) paired with corroborated likelihood-ratio filtering to stop unverified alert floods.

## 5. Chouldechova's algorithmic fairness impossibility theorem

Institutions deploying risk scores across populations often demand equal predictive accuracy alongside equal error rates across groups. Why does simple probability arithmetic forbid satisfying both at once whenever group base rates differ?

Let $Y \in \lbrace 0, 1\rbrace$ denote outcome, $\hat{Y} \in \lbrace 0, 1\rbrace$ any binary rule (such as $\mathbf{1}\lbrace{}S > s\_a^\ast\rbrace$ on risk score $S \in [0, 1]$, distinct from §2's predictor index subset), and $A \in \lbrace 0, 1\rbrace$ group membership with unequal prevalences $p\_a = \mathbb{P}(Y = 1 \mid A = a) \in (0, 1)$ ($p\_1 \neq p\_0$) and $0 < \mathbb{P}(\hat{Y} = 1 \mid A = a) < 1$. Whenever positive predictive value $\mathrm{PPV}\_a := \mathbb{P}(Y = 1 \mid \hat{Y} = 1, A = a) > 0$, Bayes' rule links $\mathrm{PPV}\_a$ directly to false positive rate $\mathrm{FPR}\_a := \mathbb{P}(\hat{Y} = 1 \mid Y = 0, A = a)$ and false negative rate $\mathrm{FNR}\_a := \mathbb{P}(\hat{Y} = 0 \mid Y = 1, A = a)$, as Chouldechova (2017) proved (§2.3):

$$\mathrm{FPR}\_a = \frac{p\_a}{1 - p\_a} \cdot \frac{1 - \mathrm{PPV}\_a}{\mathrm{PPV}\_a} \cdot \bigl(1 - \mathrm{FNR}\_a\bigr).$$

In plain words, across groups with unequal prevalences ($p\_1 \neq p\_0$), no imperfect binary rule can satisfy all three criteria simultaneously:

1. **Predictive parity:** $\mathrm{PPV}\_1 = \mathrm{PPV}\_0$.
2. **False Positive Rate parity:** $\mathrm{FPR}\_1 = \mathrm{FPR}\_0$.
3. **False Negative Rate parity:** $\mathrm{FNR}\_1 = \mathrm{FNR}\_0$.

Check the edge cases cleanly: outside perfection ($\mathrm{PPV}\_a < 1, \mathrm{FNR}\_a < 1$, forcing $\mathrm{FPR}\_a > 0$; whereas perfect precision $\mathrm{PPV}\_a = 1, \mathrm{FPR}\_a = 0$ nullifies the middle factor, and $\mathrm{FNR}\_a = 1$ with $\mathrm{PPV}\_a = 0$ clears denominators in $(1 - p\_a)\mkern3mu\mathrm{PPV}\_a\mkern3mu\mathrm{FPR}\_a = p\_a(1 - \mathrm{PPV}\_a)(1 - \mathrm{FNR}\_a)$), locking $\mathrm{PPV}\_1 = \mathrm{PPV}\_0$ across unequal $p\_a$ forces disparities in $\mathrm{FPR}$, $\mathrm{FNR}$, or both. Nor does score calibration ($\mathbb{P}(Y = 1 \mid S = s, A = a) = s$) equalize thresholded $\mathrm{PPV}\_a$ (Chouldechova 2017). How institutions govern that unavoidable trade-off follows in §7.

## 6. Why an attribution score is not an intervention

Popular explainability tools, including SHAP (Lundberg & Lee 2017), LIME (Ribeiro, Singh & Guestrin 2016), and partial-dependence plots, decompose a fitted model $f$ on the archive ($P\_2$), whereas interventions alter mechanisms in the world ($P\_1$; Pearl 2009):

1. **Attribution reads the model, not the world.** Take a confounded linear setup where latent $Z$ drives both cause $X = aZ + \varepsilon\_X$ ($a > 0$, independent noise $\sigma\_X^2 > 0$) and outcome $Y = X + \gamma Z + \varepsilon\_Y$ ($\gamma > 0$) alongside a clean proxy $W$ driven solely by $Z$ (either noiseless $W = Z$ with $\gamma > 1$, or lower-noise $W = bZ + \varepsilon\_W$ with $b > 0$, $b/\sigma\_W^2 > a/\sigma\_X^2$, and large $\gamma$): $\mathrm{do}(X)$ shifts $Y$ by one unit while $\mathrm{do}(W)$ changes nothing. Yet for large $\gamma$, regressions weight $W$ above $X$. Evaluating SHAP (Lundberg & Lee 2017) under the causal-versus-model distinction of Janzing, Minorics, and Blöbaum (2020), both conditional and marginal Shapley attributions on $f$'s inputs inherit that reversed ranking; only estimating $\mathbb{P}(Y \mid \mathrm{do}(x))$ on an identified causal graph restores world order.
2. **Decomposition assumes model accuracy.** Explaining a model where structural discrepancy $\delta(x)$ (distinct from §4's rank discount $\delta\_{\mathrm{status}}$) is huge merely explains the model's mistake.
3. **Importance shifts with coding and distance.** Correlated features split credit arbitrarily; exact Shapley attributions preserve rank under strictly monotone (invertible) input reparameterizations of the same prediction map $g(T(x)) = f(x)$, whereas LIME's local neighborhood weights (Ribeiro, Singh & Guestrin 2016) shift under nonlinear rescaling.

§2 and §3 restore causal honesty cleanly: ICP returns an invariant set $\hat{S}^\ast$, and Tian–Pearl bounds return intervals. Passing Rung-1 scores off as causal transparency feeds the agnotological alibi of **Collapse Proposition 3**, partnering with limited liability and rank discounting ($\delta\_{\mathrm{status}} > 0$) to block accountability.

### How many environments

While ICP's invariance premise holds ($\mathcal{A} \neq \emptyset$), adding genuinely different environments eliminates spurious subsets from $\mathcal{A}$, sharpening intersection $\hat{S}^\ast = \bigcap\_{S \in \mathcal{A}} S$ toward true parents $X\_{\mathrm{PA}}$ (Peters, Bühlmann & Meinshausen 2016), balanced against finite-sample power losses if splits thin out. Few or uniform environments leave competing sets in $\mathcal{A}$ and shrink $\hat{S}^\ast$ toward $\emptyset$, so reporting $\hat{S}^\ast$ without its environment design hides its warrant.

### When the counterfactual is not identified

Tian–Pearl bounds (§3) require no selection or instrumental assumptions, yet fail to bound causation above zero (widening toward $[0, 1]$) when experiments are missing ($0 \le \mathrm{PNS} \le P(x, y) + P(x', y')$; Tian & Pearl 2000), when $P(y \mid \mathrm{do}(x)) \le P(y \mid \mathrm{do}(x'))$, or when consistency fails. Nor does a population bound fix one individual case. Under Sosa's (2015) full aptness, honest analysts name the missing premise, price getting it, and act safely on worst-case bounds meanwhile.

## 7. The two edges of the attribution interval, the remedy and the missing intervention

### Two degenerate arms

Write experimental arms $a = P(y \mid \mathrm{do}(x))$ and $b = P(y \mid \mathrm{do}(x'))$. The Fréchet–Hoeffding interval $[\max(0,\mkern3mu a - b),\mkern5mu \min(a,\mkern3mu 1 - b)]$ collapses to a point only at extreme degenerate arms ($a \in \lbrace 0, 1\rbrace$ or $b \in \lbrace 0, 1\rbrace$): $a = 1$ or $b = 0$ pins $\mathrm{PNS} = a - b$, while $a = 0$ or $b = 1$ pins $\mathrm{PNS} = 0$. Inside $(0, 1)^2$, positive width always remains.

Fixing effect $d = a - b > 0$, upper bound $\min(a, 1 - b) = d + \min(b, 1 - a)$ peaks at $(1 + d)/2$ when $a + b = 1$ and shrinks toward $d$ as $a \to 1$ or $b \to 0$. Quoting only $a - b$ hides maximal ambiguity. Under monotonicity $Y\_x \ge Y\_{x'}$ ($a \ge b$), $\mathrm{PNS} = a - b$ exactly; without monotonicity, worst-case attribution charges $\min(a, 1 - b)$ (which equals $a$ when $a + b \le 1$, and $1 - b < a$ when $a + b > 1$), blowing up relative over-attribution $\min(a, 1 - b)/(a - b)$ as $a - b \to 0^+$ inside $(0, 1)^2$.

### What base rates fix and what consequences can change

§5 separated a classifier's statistical **error frontier** (even group-specific cutoffs $s\_1^\ast \neq s\_0^\ast$ can match $\mathrm{FPR}$ or $\mathrm{FNR}$ only by breaking $\mathrm{PPV}$ parity) from the **institutional consequence ledger**. Across unequal base rates ($p\_1 \neq p\_0$) with imperfect interior rules ($0 < \mathrm{PPV}\_a < 1, \mathrm{FNR}\_a < 1$), only equalizing upstream prevalences ($p\_1 = p\_0$) dissolves the algebraic obstruction.

As Corbett-Davies et al. (2017) show, imposing blind parity constraints on thresholds can hurt the protected group itself, and identical parity ratios flip meaning between punitive burdens and supportive benefits ([Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md)). Institutions must set cutoffs by minimizing declared error costs and publish disparities openly without naturalizing historical base rates. Following Georges Canguilhem's **vital normativity** (Canguilhem 1943/1966), healthy institutions intervene upstream on material conditions and redesign compensation, escalation paths, and liability limits ([Chapter 40](40-institution-incentive-metric.md)) so errors neither go unpaid nor spill past external reception perimeters' capacity.

### When there is no intervention to reason about

Rung-2 and Rung-3 reasoning fails whenever no concrete manipulation defines $\mathrm{do}(x)$. Vague labels like "assign a better teacher" or "upgrade a neighborhood" hide **compound treatments** whose effects vary wildly across implementations unless exact interventions are specified (Hernán & VanderWeele 2011), distinct from structural non-manipulability whose manipulable descendants still yield to $\mathrm{do}(x)$ (Pearl 2019). Analysts must state exact intervention protocols (actor, target, timing, and spillover bounds) and demote vague labels to description, without using messy definitions as an excuse to ignore ongoing harm (§6). In ICP audits, empty $\mathcal{A} = \emptyset$ enforces refusal to answer, whereas $\hat{S}^\ast = \emptyset$ ($\mathcal{A} \neq \emptyset$) flags low power or collinearity ([Chapter 48](48-reactive-worlds-performativity-goodhart.md)).
