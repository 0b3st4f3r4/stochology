## By the end of this chapter you can

1. Derive Meng's identity from the finite-population covariance, and read the effective sample size as a ratio of two defects.
2. Separate the three corrections the harmonic law carries (selection, clustering and weighting) and say which archive feature each one prices.
3. Say why volume alone cannot buy confidence, and which estimand an effective sample size is entitled to govern.

A fallacy travels with large administrative databases, electronic health records and billion-row telemetry streams: *"With millions of records, sampling error is practically zero."*

Mediating Filter I · Axis 1 ($S\_{\mathrm{epi}}$: Epistemic Margin) closes here, pricing raw archive volume, $n$, and single-list administrative visibility against Canonical Operator I: separating the sampling-variance effective sample size $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} = n/\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{total}}$ (governing standard errors) from the bias-penalized harmonic effective count $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho})$ (governing Keynesian evidential weight at a declared selection-defect bound $\bar{\rho} \ge |\rho\_{R,Y}|$). The law carries Keynes's evidential weight ($V$), Meng's Big Data Paradox ($\rho\_{R,Y}$), the Kish–Moulton cluster design effect ($\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{cluster}}$), Horvitz–Thompson and Kish weighting ($\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{Kish}}$), Chao's capture-recapture of the uncounted ($\hat{n}\_{00}$) and bitemporal data vintages (Keynes, 1921; Horvitz & Thompson, 1952; Kish, 1965; Chao, 1984; Moulton, 1986; Rubin, 1976; Croushore & Stark, 2001; Lum & Price, 2013; Meng, 2018):

$$\frac{1}{n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho})} = \frac{1}{n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}} + \left(\frac{1 - f}{f}\right)\bar{\rho}^2 = \frac{\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{total}}}{n} + \left(\frac{1 - f}{f}\right)\bar{\rho}^2, \qquad V\bigl(n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho})\bigr) = \frac{n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho})}{n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho}) + n\_0}$$

## 4.1 Keynes's Evidential Weight $V$ vs. Probability $p$

In Chapter VI of *A Treatise on Probability* (1921), John Maynard Keynes drew a distinction that textbook statistics almost forgot:

> *"As the relevant evidence at our disposal increases, the magnitude of the probability of the argument may either decrease or increase, according as the new knowledge strengthens the unfavourable or the favourable evidence; but **something** seems to have increased in either case, we have a more substantial basis upon which to rest our conclusion."*

A forecast based on a deeply audited cohort of independent households and a forecast of the same probability based on two unverified rumors have the **exact same probability** $p$, yet radically different **evidential weight** $V = V(x/h) \in (0, 1)$:

$$V\bigl(n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho})\bigr) = \frac{n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho})}{n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho}) + n\_0}, \qquad n\_0 := n\_{\mathrm{req}}(\alpha, \beta, p^\star) = \left(\frac{z\_{1-\alpha} + z\_{1-\beta}}{\varepsilon\_{\mathrm{rel}}}\right)^2 \frac{1 - p^\star}{p^\star},$$

where $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho})$ is the harmonic effective count at declared selection-defect bound $\bar{\rho} \ge |\rho\_{R,Y}|$ (combining the sampling-variance effective size $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} = n/\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{total}}$ with Meng's selection-bias benchmark $n\_{\mathrm{Meng}}(\bar{\rho})$) and $n\_0 := n\_{\mathrm{req}}(\alpha, \beta, p^\star)$ is anchored to the effective sample required at error tolerances ($\alpha + \beta < 1$) and relative resolution $\varepsilon\_{\mathrm{rel}} > 0$ around the cost–loss threshold $p^\star = C/(\eta L) \in (0, 1)$ (so $(1 - p^\star)/p^\star = \eta L/C - 1$, reaching $V = 1/2$ precisely when $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho}) = n\_{\mathrm{req}}$). The weight is not a second probability and cannot be averaged with one: it is a statement about the basis, and two institutions holding the same $p$ with different $V$ will behave differently the moment a cheap piece of additional evidence becomes available.

## 4.2 Xiao-Li Meng's (2018) Exact Big Data Paradox Identity: Algebraic Proof

A database of millions of rows can carry *less* effective information than a simple random sample of a few hundred. In his landmark 2018 paper *Statistical paradises and paradoxes in big data (I)*, Xiao-Li Meng proved an exact algebraic identity for the error between the sample mean $\bar{Y}\_n$ of an archive and the true finite-population mean $\bar{Y}\_N$.

### 4.2.1 Proof of the Big Data Identity

Consider a finite population of size $N$ with values $Y\_1, \dots, Y\_N$, population mean $\bar{Y}\_N = \frac{1}{N}\sum\_{i=1}^N Y\_i$, and finite-population standard deviation $\sigma\_Y = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum\_{i=1}^N (Y\_i - \bar{Y}\_N)^2}$.

Let $R\_i \in \lbrace0, 1\rbrace$ be the recording/response indicator for individual $i$. The recorded sample size is $n = \sum\_{i=1}^N R\_i$, the sampling fraction is $f = n / N$, and the observed sample mean is $\bar{Y}\_n = \frac{1}{n}\sum\_{i=1}^N R\_i Y\_i$. Note that the population mean of $R$ is $\bar{R}\_N = \frac{n}{N} = f$, and its variance is:
$$\sigma\_R^2 = \frac{1}{N}\sum\_{i=1}^N (R\_i - f)^2 = f(1 - f)$$
The finite-population covariance between the recording mechanism $R$ and the variable of interest $Y$ is:
$$\mathrm{Cov}\_N(R, Y) = \frac{1}{N}\sum\_{i=1}^N (R\_i - f)(Y\_i - \bar{Y}\_N) = \frac{1}{N}\sum\_{i=1}^N R\_i Y\_i - f \bar{Y}\_N = \frac{n}{N}\bar{Y}\_n - f \bar{Y}\_N = f\bigl(\bar{Y}\_n - \bar{Y}\_N\bigr)$$
By definition of the finite-population Pearson correlation coefficient $\rho\_{R, Y} = \frac{\mathrm{Cov}\_N(R, Y)}{\sigma\_R \sigma\_Y}$:
$$\rho\_{R, Y} = \frac{f(\bar{Y}\_n - \bar{Y}\_N)}{\sqrt{f(1 - f)}\mkern3mu\sigma\_Y} = \sqrt{\frac{f}{1 - f}}\mkern3mu\frac{\bar{Y}\_n - \bar{Y}\_N}{\sigma\_Y}$$
Rearranging directly establishes Meng's exact, non-asymptotic identity:
$$\bar{Y}\_n - \bar{Y}\_N = \underbrace{\rho\_{R, Y}}\_{\text{Data Defect}} \times \underbrace{\sqrt{\frac{1 - f}{f}}}\_{\text{Data Scarcity}} \times \underbrace{\sigma\_Y}\_{\text{Problem Difficulty}}$$

### 4.2.2 The Collapse of Selection-Adjusted Effective Sample Size ($n\_{\mathrm{Meng}}$)

To translate squared selection displacement into a bias-equivalent effective sample size $n\_{\mathrm{Meng}}(\rho\_{R,Y})$, equate $(\bar{Y}\_n - \bar{Y}\_N)^2$ to the sampling variance $\sigma\_Y^2 / n\_{\mathrm{Meng}}$ of a hypothetical infinite-population simple random sample (as Meng 2018 does):
$$\rho\_{R,Y}^2 \left(\frac{1 - f}{f}\right)\sigma\_Y^2 = \frac{\sigma\_Y^2}{n\_{\mathrm{Meng}}} \implies n\_{\mathrm{Meng}}(\rho\_{R,Y}) = \frac{f}{1 - f}\mkern3mu\frac{1}{\rho\_{R,Y}^2}$$

Selection shifts the estimator's location rather than inflating its sampling variance: only $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} = n / \mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{total}}$ enters standard-error denominators, whereas $n\_{\mathrm{Meng}}(\rho\_{R,Y})$ prices squared bias in mean-squared error and enters the harmonic denominator of $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho})$ in Keynes's weight $V$. $n\_{\mathrm{Meng}}$ depends only on the sampling fraction $f = n/N$ and the data defect correlation $\rho\_{R,Y}$, and at a fixed fraction it is decoupled from the raw row count $n$. That decoupling is the paradox and it is not a rhetorical one: an archive covering a substantial share of a population, but carrying even a slight selection correlation, can deliver a bias-adjusted effective sample smaller than a well-drawn survey of a few hundred households, because the defect enters as an inverse square. Under such a defect, standard confidence intervals are displaced from the truth by more than their own width, and empirical coverage is not slightly wrong but substantially absent. Meng's identity predicts that before any data are collected.

**The defect is a hypothesis, not a measurement.** $\rho\_{R,Y}$ is defined against the unrecorded population mean $\bar{Y}\_N$, which is the estimand, so no archive contains the value it would take and the equation above does not estimate it. What the identity identifies is the displacement per unit of defect, $\sqrt{(1-f)/f}$, not the defect itself. The only bound the identity supplies is the finite-population Cauchy–Schwarz one, $|\rho\_{R,Y}| \le 1$, and inserting it gives the sharp worst-case displacement

$$\bigl|\bar{Y}\_n - \bar{Y}\_N\bigr| \mkern5mu\le\mkern5mu \sigma\_Y\sqrt{\frac{1 - f}{f}},$$

which holds for every selection mechanism compatible with the observed marginal and does not shrink as $n \to \infty$ at fixed $f$. In the sense of [Chapter 44](44-underdetermination-partial-identification.md) this is a partial-identification bound and not a confidence interval: its width is structural, so more records through the same door leave it where it was.

Against any critical threshold $\bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{crit}}$ (in particular $\bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{crit}} = 0$ for sign reversal), equating the displacement $\bar{\rho}\mkern3mu\sigma\_Y\sqrt{(1 - f)/f}$ under $|\rho\_{R,Y}| \le \bar{\rho}$ to $|\bar{Y}\_n - \bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{crit}}|$ yields the **breakdown correlation** (for $0 < f < 1$ and $\sigma\_Y > 0$):

$$\rho^\star\_{R,Y} = \min\mkern-3mu\left(1,\mkern4mu \frac{\lvert\bar{Y}\_n - \bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{crit}}\rvert}{\sigma\_Y}\sqrt{\frac{f}{1 - f}}\right).$$

Below $\rho^\star\_{R,Y}$ the threshold verdict is invariant to missingness; at $\bar{\rho} \ge \rho^\star\_{R,Y}$ (when the ratio is at most $1$) the Manski–Meng band crosses $\bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{crit}}$ and overturns it.

Every statement about the effective sample is therefore conditional on a declared bound. Let $\bar{\rho}$ satisfy $\bar{\rho} \ge |\rho\_{R,Y}|$ and be fixed before the archive is read. Composing the inverse sampling-variance effective size $1/n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} = \mathrm{DEFF}/n$ of §4.3 with Meng's selection-bias term evaluated at $\bar{\rho}$ yields $\frac{1}{n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho})} = \frac{1}{n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}} + \frac{1 - f}{f}\bar{\rho}^2$, turning the harmonic effective count into the admissible set

$$n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho}) \in \left[\Bigl(\tfrac{\mathrm{DEFF}}{n} + \tfrac{1 - f}{f}\bar{\rho}^{2}\Bigr)^{-1},\mkern5mu n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}\right],$$

whose upper end $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} = n/\mathrm{DEFF}$ is what the sampling design alone allows for variance and standard-error scaling, and whose lower end is what $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho})$ becomes for MSE and evidential weight $V$ if the declared defect is the true one. Reporting a single number $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(0) = n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}$ is the case $\bar{\rho} = 0$, and that is an assumption about the recording mechanism, not a finding about the archive.

**Sizing reads the same algebra backwards.** Fix the target $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{req}}$ and ask how many rows the archive needs. Solving the composed law for $n$ at the declared bound gives

$$n \mkern5mu\ge\mkern5mu \frac{n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{req}}\bigl(\mathrm{DEFF} + N\bar{\rho}^{2}\bigr)}{1 + n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{req}}\bar{\rho}^{2}},$$

which collapses to the design-effect inflation $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{req}}\mkern3mu\mathrm{DEFF}$ as $\bar{\rho} \to 0$ and, provided the design-effect inflation alone is affordable ($n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{req}}\mkern3mu\mathrm{DEFF} \le N$), is bounded above by the population $N$ for every $\bar{\rho} \le 1$, approaching it as the defect bound and the target grow together. The row count required is a function of a defect the archive cannot measure, so a sample-size target quoted without its $\bar{\rho}$ is quoted for an assumption; and because the requirement saturates at $N$, a declared defect large enough puts the claim beyond any procurement budget, which is the census limit of §4.7 read as a cost, not as a limit.

## 4.3 Kish–Moulton Cluster Design Effects ($\mathrm{DEFF}$) and Unequal Cluster Sizes

Even when $\rho\_{R,Y} = 0$, administrative rows are almost never independent: patients are clustered within hospital wards, and calls arrive in correlated daily waves. The correction that follows is often called the Kish–Moulton correction elsewhere in this book; that label is this book's shorthand, not an eponym the survey literature uses, and the unequal-cluster treatment it names is the one Kish set out and Moulton extended.

When cluster sizes $m\_1, \dots, m\_C$ vary with sample mean $\bar{m} = \frac{1}{C}\sum\_{c=1}^C m\_c$ and variance $\sigma\_m^2 = \frac{1}{C}\sum\_{c=1}^C (m\_c - \bar{m})^2$, Brent R. Moulton (1986) showed that cluster size heterogeneity inflates the effective cluster size from $\bar{m}$ to:
$$\tilde{m} = \bar{m}\bigl(1 + \mathrm{CV}^2(m)\bigr), \qquad \mathrm{CV}(m) = \frac{\sigma\_m}{\bar{m}}$$
The resulting **cluster design effect** is:
$$\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{cluster}} = 1 + (\tilde{m} - 1)\mkern3mu\rho\_I = 1 + \bigl[\bar{m}\bigl(1 + \mathrm{CV}^2(m)\bigr) - 1\bigr]\rho\_I$$
where $\rho\_I$ is the intra-cluster correlation coefficient (ICC). When clusters are highly variable in size ($\mathrm{CV}(m) > 1$), neglecting the Moulton variance correction $\mathrm{CV}^2(m)$ severely underestimates $\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{cluster}}$ and understates standard errors, producing phantom statistical significance. The effective sample size for variance sizing collapses to:
$$n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{cluster}} = \frac{n}{\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{cluster}}}$$

When the weight factor of §4.4 is present as well, the two effects do not multiply. Under the two-component model with weights independent of cluster membership, the exact composition is additive, $\mathrm{DEFF} = \mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{cluster}} + \mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{Kish}} - 1 = 1 + (\tilde{m} - 1)\rho\_I + \mathrm{CV}^2(w)$; the product of the two factors is larger by the cross term $(\tilde{m} - 1)\rho\_I\mkern3mu\mathrm{CV}^2(w)$, so using the product sizes conservatively and calling it an identity overstates the variance of the design.

## 4.4 Horvitz–Thompson / Kish Weighting and Chao's (1984) Lower Bound on the Uncounted

Two instruments recover information about under-recorded strata.

1. **Horvitz–Thompson (1952) & Leslie Kish's (1965) Design Effect:** When recorded unit $i$ has inclusion probability $\pi\_i > 0$, inverse-probability weights $w\_i = 1/\pi\_i$ remove selection bias, but extreme weight dispersion reduces effective sample size to Kish's floor:

   $$n\_{\mathrm{eff, Kish}} = \frac{\bigl(\sum\_{i=1}^n w\_i\bigr)^2}{\sum\_{i=1}^n w\_i^2} = \frac{n}{1 + \mathrm{CV}^2(w)}$$

2. **Anne Chao's (1984, 1987) Capture-Recapture Lower Bound ($\hat{n}\_{00}$):** What if a vulnerable subpopulation has $\pi\_i = 0$ in a single administrative registry? Following Chao (1984, 1987) and Lum, Price, and Banks (2013), single-stream multinomial sampling bounds unseen classes by $\hat{n}\_{00,\mathrm{Chao1}} = f\_1^2/(2 f\_2)$, while cross-referencing $t$ incomplete registries without within-list replacement scales by $(t - 1)/t$—yielding for $t = 2$ lists ($f\_1 = n\_{10} + n\_{01}$, $f\_2 = n\_{11}$) the heterogeneity-robust lower bound on the **completely uncounted population** $n\_{00}$:

   $$\hat{n}\_{00,\mathrm{Chao2}}(2) = \frac{f\_1^2}{4 f\_2} = \frac{(n\_{10} + n\_{01})^2}{4 n\_{11}} \ge \frac{n\_{10}\mkern3mun\_{01}}{n\_{11}} = \hat{n}\_{00,\mathrm{LP}}$$

---

## 4.5 Bitemporal Vintages: Event Time ($t\_{\mathrm{ev}}$) vs. Availability Time ($t\_{\mathrm{disp}}$)

Finally, archives evolve dynamically over time: macroeconomic series, epidemiological counts, and hospital records are revised months or quarters after the event occurred. Every record carries both its **event time** $t\_{\mathrm{ev}}$ and its **institutional availability time** $t\_{\mathrm{disp}} \ge t\_{\mathrm{ev}}$.

Any record with $t\_{\mathrm{disp}} < t\_{\mathrm{ev}}$ is rejected as a causal look-ahead leak, and a decision at time $\tau$ may condition only on the latest vintage satisfying $t\_{\mathrm{disp}} \le \tau$. When models are trained or evaluated on final revised data, they suffer from **retrospective hindsight leakage**:

- The real-time release available to policymakers at time $t\_{\mathrm{disp}}$ often indicates economic expansion ($\Delta Y > 0$), while subsequent benchmark revisions flip the estimate to contraction ($\Delta Y < 0$).
- A non-trivial share of quarters flips sign between initial release and benchmark revision, as the Philadelphia Fed Real-Time Data Set documents (Croushore & Stark 2001), and the share is the kind of quantity that only a vintage archive can measure.
- Retrospective policy simulations using latest-vintage data impute clairvoyant knowledge to the historical decision-maker, leading to severe causal misattribution.

---

## 4.6 Effective Sample Size in Three Real Archives

The three archives this chapter examines are chosen because each one prices a different term of the harmonic law, and the order in which they are read is the order of the terms. Earnings recorded for survey respondents are the demographic case: respondents inside a cell defined by age and education resemble each other, so the cell counts once, and the design effect of the one-way random-effects decomposition shrinks the nominal sample to the effective one. Call volumes are the temporal case: consecutive arrival days resemble each other for reasons that have nothing to do with the question, and the same decomposition, applied to days instead of cells, prices the same defect. In both, the correction is a ratio of two corrections, and the Keynes weight that follows is a statement about the basis and not about the estimate.

The third case is the one a single list cannot see. Chao's capture-recapture estimator takes the units seen once and the units seen twice across two incomplete registries and returns a lower bound on the population neither list records, and Lum, Price, and Banks's (2013) cross-list version returns the intersection estimate. The bound is a lower bound for a structural reason: the units that neither list records are, by construction, absent from the arithmetic, so the estimator can only say how many the lists imply must exist beyond their union, never how many there truly are.

The vintage archive adds the last term. A quarterly series published in real time and revised afterwards carries two numbers per observation, and the pair — not the observation — is the datum. Reading the initial release against the final one gives the revision distribution; reading only the final one gives a decision-maker knowledge nobody had at the time.

---

### The weight that a huge archive cannot buy

An archive with millions of responses can carry the evidential weight of a few hundred independent ones. The weight itself does not announce the collapse: it is computed from the effective sample and compared against a reference scale, and the comparison is what makes two archives of wildly different nominal size commensurable. Report the effective sample next to the nominal one, or report nothing that a reader can act on.

The paradox is arithmetic, not rhetoric: $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is a function of the sampling fraction and of the defect correlation, and a defect in the denominator is enough to make the second term of the harmonic law dominate, after which a larger archive buys nothing. The vintages add the time axis: a number that is revised between its first publication and its settled value is not a number that a decision can be anchored to. A bitemporal reading is the procedure that keeps both versions visible instead of overwriting one with the other.

### When the paradox is harmless

The paradox is harmless when the missingness correlation is zero, and that is testable by comparing vintages or by a design with a forced response. Where the correlation cannot be measured, the procedure is to report the bound on the assumption, not an effective sample that pretends to have measured it.

### Three ways of paying the same price

The three cases pay the same price in different ways, and the differences are instructive. The demographic clustering is the classic one: respondents inside a cell resemble each other, so the cell counts once. The call clustering is the temporal version, where consecutive days resemble each other for reasons that have nothing to do with the question. The vintage adds a third: the same respondent can appear with two different values, and the pair is the observation. In all three the correction is the same and the size of the correction differs, so the design effect has to be measured and not assumed. The three cases also show why the correction cannot be applied after the fact: once the rows are in one file, the clustering is invisible to anyone who does not know how the file was assembled.

## 4.7 The two ends of the sampling fraction, and the quantile the weight cannot see

### Two limits of the harmonic law

The Meng benchmark is $n\_{\mathrm{Meng}}(\rho\_{R,Y}) = \frac{f}{1-f}\mkern3mu\rho\_{R,Y}^{-2}$, and its two ends are the fraction and the defect. Hold the defect fixed and move the fraction: $f \to 1$ drives the prefactor to infinity and the bias-adjusted benchmark with it, while $f \to 0$ leaves only the defect, since $n\_{\mathrm{Meng}} \to 0$ and no archive is smaller. The symbolic reading is that $\rho\_{R,Y}^{-2}$ is a constant multiplier: the defect sets the order of magnitude and the fraction only moves the mantissa, so an archive can grow by a factor of a hundred and improve its selection-adjusted effective sample by the fraction it added and nothing else.

The clustering side has the same pair of ends. As $\mathrm{CV}(m) \to 0$ the Moulton correction vanishes and $\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{cluster}} \to 1 + (\bar{m} - 1)\rho\_I$, and as $\rho\_I \to 0$ it collapses to $1$ at any cluster size, so the correction is a size term times a dependence term and either one alone is harmless. An archive in which both terms are present has a design effect that carries both, and a correction applied to only one of them understates the other.

### The quantile the effective sample cannot deliver

The recommendation of this chapter is to correct the design effect, report the effective sample beside the nominal one, and size the decision from it. Applied to a promise stated as a quantile, that recommendation misleads, and the arithmetic is short. An institution staffing to the upper limit of a confidence interval for the *mean* of a daily series can leave a large share of days short if its promise is stated as a quantile, because the mean's design effect does not control a quantile estimand: under clustering the exceedance indicator $\mathbf{1}\lbrace{}Y\_i \le q\_p\rbrace$ carries its own quantile intra-cluster correlation and the standard error passes through the local density $f\_Y(q\_p)$ rather than the sample standard deviation $s$. Symbolically, even in independent draws $\mathrm{se}(\bar{y}) = s/\sqrt{n}$ and $\mathrm{se}(\hat{q}\_p) = \sqrt{p(1-p)}/(\sqrt{n}\mkern3muf\_Y(q\_p))$ target different functionals of the distribution and scale through different quantities, so one $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}$ for the mean cannot serve both estimands. The dated case where this was learned one step removed is the 1936 *Literary Digest* poll, which collected millions of ballots and was wrong, and whose lesson was taken as being about defect, not about volume (Squire 1988).

### When the archive has no single denominator

The thesis is that the harmonic law supplies the honest denominator for the archive, one number that prices every claim the archive can support. The condition that invalidates it is a single reporting mechanism over a fixed population: a fixed $N$, one inclusion probability per unit, and a defect correlation identified from the same cross-section. Bitemporal archives violate all three at once, because $N$ itself is revised, the availability time of a record is not its event time, and no single vintage identifies $\rho\_{R,Y}$ for the quantity as it will be published. The law then has no computable value, only a range. The procedure when the condition fails: compute the law vintage by vintage, quote the worst vintage's $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}$ as the operative denominator, never mix vintages inside one denominator, and treat a revision of $N$ as a new population; a correction assumes a denominator the next report can inherit, and one that changes without notice cannot be.
