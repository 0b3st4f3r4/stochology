## 6.1 Non-Ergodic Itô Variance Drain and Baker–McHale Epistemic Kelly Shrinkage

A reserve or a capital stock $W\_t$ can evolve multiplicatively over irreversible time ($dW\_t = W\_t(\mu\mkern3mudt + \sigma\mkern3mudB\_t)$). Then the standard move is to optimize the **ensemble average** across hypothetical parallel universes:
$$\mathbb{E}[W\_T] = W\_0 \exp(\mu T) \implies g\_{\mathrm{ensemble}} = \lim\_{T \to \infty} \frac{1}{T}\ln\frac{\mathbb{E}[W\_T]}{W\_0} = \mu$$
Physical, ecological and human institutions are not parallel worlds. An agent inhabits a **single, irreversible trajectory** through time.

### 6.1.1 Proof of the Time-Average vs. Ensemble-Average Divergence

Kiyosi Itô's (1951) Lemma gives the logarithm of wealth $\ln W\_t$:
$$d\ln W\_t = \frac{1}{W\_t} dW\_t - \frac{1}{2 W\_t^2}(dW\_t)^2 = \left(\mu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2\right)dt + \sigma\mkern3mudB\_t$$
Integrating over horizon $T$:
$$\ln\frac{W\_T}{W\_0} = \left(\mu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2\right)T + \sigma B\_T$$
Divide by $T$ and let $T \to \infty$: the Law of the Iterated Logarithm makes the Brownian fluctuations vanish ($\lim\_{T\to\infty} \frac{\sigma B\_T}{T} = 0$ almost surely), and the trajectory's time-average growth rate is
$$g\_{\mathrm{time}}(f) = \lim\_{T \to \infty} \frac{1}{T}\ln\frac{W\_T}{W\_0} = f\mu - \frac{1}{2}f^2\sigma^2 \quad \text{almost surely}$$

The difference $g\_{\mathrm{ensemble}} - g\_{\mathrm{time}}(1) = \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2$ is the **Itô variance drain**:

- Differentiating with respect to leverage $f$ yields John Kelly's (1956) optimal growth leverage $f^\ast = \mu / \sigma^2$, achieving maximum growth $g^\ast(f^\ast) = \frac{\mu^2}{2\sigma^2}$.
- An optimizer that maximizes the ensemble average $\mathbb{E}[W\_T]$ chooses unbounded leverage, $f \to \infty$.
- Yet for any leverage exceeding the ruin threshold:
  $$f > \frac{2\mu}{\sigma^2}$$
  the time-average growth rate turns negative: $g\_{\mathrm{time}}(f) < 0$. Under such leverage **the single trajectory collapses to zero with probability 1** ($\lim\_{T \to \infty} W\_T = 0$ a.s.), while the expectation across universes that do not exist explodes ($\mathbb{E}[W\_T] \to \infty$). Optimizing the ensemble average guarantees individual ruin.

### 6.1.2 Baker–McHale Epistemic Shrinkage Under Finite Estimation Sample Size

Paul Samuelson (1971, 1979) had warned of the same gap from another direction: the true drift $\mu$ is never known, only estimated from a sampling-variance effective sample size $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}$ (Chapter 45) with estimation variance $\sigma\_{\hat{\mu}}^2 = \sigma^2 / n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}$. Plug $\hat{\mu}$ straight into John Kelly's (1956) formula $f^\ast\_{\text{plug-in}} = \hat{\mu}/\sigma^2$ and the position overbets on estimation noise by construction. Rose D. Baker and Ian G. McHale (2013) derived the **epistemic Kelly shrinkage** factor:
$$k\bigl(n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}\bigr) = \frac{1}{1 + 1 / n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}} \in (0, 1], \qquad k\_{\mathrm{BM}} = \frac{z\_{\hat{\mu}}^2}{1 + z\_{\hat{\mu}}^2 + \hat{f}^2 (1 + \tfrac{1}{2}\hat{\gamma}\_2) / n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{vol}}}$$
where $z\_{\hat{\mu}}^2 = n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{vol}} \hat{\mu}^2 / \hat{\sigma}^2$ is the drift signal-to-noise ratio under annual-block volatility-regime clustering ($n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{vol}} = T / \mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{vol}}$) and $\hat{\gamma}\_2$ is excess kurtosis. A raw $T$ of tens of thousands of days still leaves the daily drift with a modest $t$-statistic, so the shrinkage factor pulls the reckless plug-in leverage back before the heavy-tail regime selector takes over. This is not conservatism for its own sake. It says that the leverage maximizing growth *in the population* is not the leverage a sample can defend.

## 6.2 Canguilhem's Vital Normativity, Holling's Resilience Basin, and McEwen's Allostatic Wear

Georges Canguilhem, in *The Normal and the Pathological* (1943/1966), defined health as a **margin of tolerance** for the inconstancies of the environment, the capacity to absorb shocks and institute new norms, and not as conformity to a statistical average. C. S. Holling (1973) gave the ecological version by separating

- **Engineering resilience** ($\lambda\_{\mathrm{recovery}} = -\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\big|\_{x^\ast}$): local recovery speed near equilibrium;
- **Ecological / vital resilience** ($D\_t = x\_{\mathrm{high}}^\ast - x\_{\mathrm{tip}}^\ast$): the width of the stability basin before a fold bifurcation tips the system into irreversible collapse.

Bruce McEwen's (1998) **allostatic load** accumulates under chronic stress, and the vital basin ratio $D\_t / D\_0$ shrinks with it. Marten Scheffer et al.'s (2009) **Critical Slowing Down** lag-1 autocorrelation $\rho\_1 = \exp(-\lambda\_{\mathrm{recovery}}\Delta t)$ then approaches one and warns of the tipping point long before the mean state collapses. That autocorrelation is worth reading because it moves while the mean still sits where the institution expects it. The basin narrows first. The level moves later.

The inference runs one way only. The autocorrelation is likelier under an approaching transition than under a stationary series, and the manager wants the reverse conditional. The gap between the two conditionals is the prosecutor's fallacy (Boettiger & Hastings 2012).

**Domain boundary and rupture under Lévy jumps ($\alpha < 2$).** Scheffer et al.'s (2009) autocorrelation diagnostic presupposes continuous, finite-variance diffusive perturbations relaxing locally inside a narrowing basin, so that a falling recovery rate $\lambda\_{\mathrm{recovery}} \to 0^+$ drives $\rho\_1 = \exp(-\lambda\_{\mathrm{recovery}}\Delta t) \to 1^-$ before the state crosses $x\_{\mathrm{tip}}^\ast$. That diagnostic breaks whenever the shock process carries discontinuous **Lévy jumps with tail exponent $\alpha < 2$** (infinite variance, converging to an $\alpha$-stable law rather than a Brownian diffusion; Gnedenko & Kolmogorov 1954; [Chapter 46](46-non-identical-asymmetry-heavy-tails.md) §5.4). Under heavy-tailed jumps, the system need not exhibit gradual critical slowing down before collapse: a single extreme shock exceeding the basin width ($|\Delta X\_t| > D\_t$) can jump the trajectory directly across the tipping boundary while $\lambda\_{\mathrm{recovery}}$ remains strictly positive and $\rho\_1$ stays bounded away from one. Quiet autocorrelation ($\rho\_1 \not\to 1^-$) therefore provides no safety guarantee against jump-induced ruin in $\alpha < 2$ regimes: lag-1 autocorrelation tracks local diffusive curvature around equilibrium, not the tail probability of crossing $D\_t$ in one jump.

## 6.3 Kingman's Heavy-Traffic Queue Explosion and the Lean Operating Trap

Lean utilization is what destroys hospital emergency rooms, electrical grids and supply chains.
In queueing physics, let $\tau = 1/\mu\_s$ be the mean service time, $\rho = \lambda / \mu\_s \in (0, 1)$ the server utilization, and $c\_a^2, c\_s^2$ the squared coefficients of variation of inter-arrival and service times. John F. C. Kingman's (1962) distribution-free inequality for the general $GI/G/1$ queue bounds the expected wait at every utilization, and tightens as $\rho \to 1^-$ onto Kingman's (1961) heavy-traffic formula (which is already exact at every load for Poisson arrivals $M/G/1$, where $c\_a^2 = 1$ yields the Pollaczek–Khinchine mean $\rho\tau(1+c\_s^2)/(2(1-\rho))$):
$$\mathbb{E}[W\_q] \le \left(\frac{c\_a^2/\rho^2 + c\_s^2}{2}\right)\mkern3mu\frac{\rho}{1 - \rho}\mkern3mu\tau, \qquad \mathbb{E}[W\_q] \approx \left(\frac{c\_a^2 + c\_s^2}{2}\right)\mkern3mu\frac{\rho}{1 - \rho}\mkern3mu\tau.$$

The direction of the sign carries the argument. Below saturation, bursty arrivals ($c\_a^2 > 1$) can exceed the heavy-traffic curve and require the $c\_a^2/\rho^2$ ceiling, while as $\rho \to 1^-$ the two expressions converge onto the same hyperbolic wall $\rho/(1-\rho)$. An operator planning at a quiet-load average wait has planned below a ceiling whose slack disappears where the plan is most exposed.

### 6.3.1 The Hyperbolic Utilization Explosion

The factor $\frac{\rho}{1 - \rho}$ is a hyperbola with a vertical asymptote at $\rho = 1$, and no particular operating point is needed to see what it does. At comfortable utilization ($\rho < 1/2$) the dimensionless multiplier sits below one (scaling expected wait below the dispersion-scaled service unit $\frac{c\_a^2 + c\_s^2}{2}\mkern3mu\tau$); at lean utilization near saturation it diverges as $(1 - \rho)^{-1}$. The ratio between two such points is computable directly as
$$\frac{\rho\_2/(1-\rho\_2)}{\rho\_1/(1-\rho\_1)}$$
so a few percentage points of utilization multiply waiting time several-fold. That asymmetry is the trap. A capacity increment that looks like a small efficiency gain is a large latency loss, because the institution pays for its occupancy with the very quantity its survival depends on.

Lean optimization eliminates "idle headroom" to minimize unit costs and pushes $\rho \to 1$. Service delay scales with $\mathbb{E}[W\_q]$, so system recovery time $\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{recovery}}$ explodes hyperbolically.

### 6.3.2 The Master Survival Inequality: $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} \ge \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{recovery}}$

Herbert Scarf's (1960) $(s, S)$ inventory optimality and David Simchi-Levi et al.'s (2014) supply-chain physics give the same necessary condition. An institution facing a disruption survives only if its **Time-to-Survival Buffer** ($\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} = B\_t / \dot{D}\_{\mathrm{crisis}}$, the buffer divided by the crisis draw-down rate) is at least as long as its **Time-to-Recovery / Steering Delay** ($\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{recovery}}$), instantiated at decision state $\mathcal{I}\_t$ by the three-term **Effective Time-to-Recovery** $\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$:
$$\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{recovery}} := \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t) = \tau\_{\mathrm{detect}} + \tau\_{\mathrm{delib}}(\mathcal{I}\_t) + \tau\_{\mathrm{actuate}}, \qquad \mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} \ge \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t).$$
Here the three intervals partition the response clock without overlap:

- $\tau\_{\mathrm{detect}}$ is the physical and evidential accumulation latency from shock onset until the monitor's threshold fires an alarm, evaluated with zero status discounting ($\delta\_{\mathrm{status}} = 0$);
- $\tau\_{\mathrm{delib}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$ is the institutional and cognitive deliberation latency from alarm trigger to authorized response, conditioned on the sealed information state $\mathcal{I}\_t$ — absorbing cognitive inspection service and queueing ($\tau\_{\mathrm{cog}} = B\_{\mathrm{audit}}/C\_{\mathrm{human}}$ under utilization $\rho\_{\mathrm{cog}}$; [Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md) §8.4), any status-discount drag ($\delta\_{\mathrm{status}} > 0$), and review or retraining delay prior to release;
- $\tau\_{\mathrm{actuate}}$ is the post-authorization physical execution, rollout, and server queue-drain delay (scaling with $\mathbb{E}[W\_q]$ under utilization $\rho$).
Pre-authorized algedonic bypass compresses $\tau\_{\mathrm{delib}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$ toward its minimal verification floor, whereas uncompressed audit panels, status discounting, and lean server utilization inflate $\tau\_{\mathrm{delib}}(\mathcal{I}\_t) + \tau\_{\mathrm{actuate}}$ simultaneously.

A disruption that strikes a system at lean utilization or through congested deliberation channels finds a bloated effective recovery time $\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$ and a thin buffer slack ($\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} < \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$). The pair then fails the inequality as a comparison of two computed times, not as a description of an atmosphere.

### 6.3.3 The Ruin Coefficient Behind the Clocks

Failure of the survival inequality is a first-passage statement, and it can be read without a severity distribution. With replenishment stopped during the recovery window, the reserve is $U(t) = B - S(t)$, where $S$ is the cumulative shock, a process with stationary independent increments and $\mathbb{E}[S(t)] = \lambda\mu t$ for shock intensity $\lambda$ and mean severity $\mu$. The ruin time is $\tau = \inf\{t \ge 0 : S(t) \ge B\}$, and on the fluid path — the path on which the shock arrives exactly at its mean rate — the barrier is reached at $B/(\lambda\mu)$, which is $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}}$. A failed inequality therefore names a sign and not a mood: the fluid reserve is at zero strictly before help arrives, and no refinement of the archive moves the sign.

Where the inequality holds, survival is still not certain: the ruin probability stays positive whenever a shock can exceed the buffer, and the coefficient of Cramér and Lundberg measures how much buffer buys how little of that probability (Cramér 1930; Lundberg 1903). Write $c$ for the net replenishment rate during normal operation and $M\_X(r) = \mathbb{E}[e^{rX}]$ for the moment-generating function of a single shock. The adjustment coefficient $R > 0$ is the positive root of the Lundberg equation
$$\lambda\bigl(M\_X(R) - 1\bigr) = cR,$$
and Lundberg's inequality bounds the ruin probability of the reserve by
$$\psi(B) \le e^{-RB}.$$
A buffer that holds ruin below a declared $\varepsilon$ therefore has to satisfy $B \ge \ln(1/\varepsilon)/R$. Expanding the equation to second order, $M\_X(r) = 1 + \mu r + \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[X^2]r^2 + O(r^3)$, so that $\lambda\mu R + \tfrac{1}{2}\lambda\mathbb{E}[X^2]R^2 = cR + O(R^3)$ and
$$R = \frac{2(c - \lambda\mu)}{\lambda\mkern3mu\mathbb{E}[X^2]} + O\bigl((c - \lambda\mu)^2\bigr).$$
The coefficient vanishes linearly in the net replenishment, so the buffer a fixed $\varepsilon$ requires diverges as $c \downarrow \lambda\mu$. That divergence is the ruin-theoretic form of the wall the utilization hyperbola describes. The queue multiplier grows with the load as $1/(1-\rho)$; the buffer requirement grows as $1/R$ while the safety loading shrinks; both are unbounded at the point where the institution has eliminated its slack. The survival inequality is the degenerate $R = 0$ boundary of the family. It says when ruin is certain. The coefficient says how unlikely ruin is when it is not.

### 6.3.4 The Bound When the Reserve Is an Interval

Everything above assumed the buffer is a number. It is usually an interval. A reserve is a ledger with an unbooked liability, an inventory with an unlogged shortfall, a capital position whose assets were marked by someone else. Two intervals matter at once, the reserve and the severity distribution the archive describes, and both move the exponent and leave the form of the inequality alone.

**The reserve.** Lundberg's bound decreases in the buffer, so over $B \in [B\_L, B\_U]$ the supremal ruin probability is attained at the bottom,
$$\psi \mkern5mu\le\mkern5mu \sup\_{B \in [B\_L,\mkern3mu B\_U]} e^{-\theta B} \mkern5mu=\mkern5mu e^{-\theta B\_L}.$$
Writing $w\_B = B\_U - B\_L$, the interval costs a multiplicative factor $e^{\theta w\_B}$ on the certified probability, and the factor grows with the exponent. A system far from the wall pays most, in probability, for one unbooked unit of reserve. Near the wall the exponent is small and the same interval is nearly free. That is the arithmetic behind a familiar fact: slack is cheap where it is not needed.

**The severity.** Let $f$ be the recorded share of shocks, $R$ the recording indicator — the $R$ of $\rho\_{R,Y}$, not the adjustment coefficient — and $Y$ the severity. With $R$ Bernoulli$(f)$ we have $\mathrm{Var}(R) = f(1-f)$, so the covariance identity $\mathrm{Cov}(R,Y) = f(1-f)\bigl(\bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{obs}} - \bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{mis}}\bigr)$ divided by $\sigma\_R\sigma\_Y$ gives
$$\bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{obs}} - \bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{mis}} \mkern5mu=\mkern5mu \frac{\rho\_{R,Y}\mkern3mu\sigma\_Y}{\sqrt{f(1-f)}},$$
which is the displacement identity of Chapter 45 read on the severity scale. The mean of the recorded shocks fixes the mean of the unrecorded ones, up to the defect. Let $\rho\_{R,Y}$ fall below zero and the unrecorded shocks are the severe ones, their mean rising at the rate $\sigma\_Y/\sqrt{f(1-f)}$: the same Data Defect term that inflates the effective sample, now standing where the ruin exponent can read it.

A mean is not enough for a moment-generating function; the support is. For $X \in [0, x\_{\max}]$ with prescribed mean $m$, the map $x \mapsto e^{\theta x}$ is convex, so its expectation over laws with that mean is maximized at the extreme law that puts mass $m/x\_{\max}$ at $x\_{\max}$ and the rest at zero:
$$M\_X(\theta) \mkern5mu\le\mkern5mu 1 + \frac{m}{x\_{\max}}\bigl(e^{\theta x\_{\max}} - 1\bigr).$$
Composing the two pieces, every severity distribution the archive admits satisfies
$$M\_X(\theta) \mkern5mu\le\mkern5mu f\mkern3muM\_{\mathrm{obs}}(\theta) \mkern5mu+\mkern5mu (1-f)\left[1 + \frac{\bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{mis}}}{x\_{\max}}\bigl(e^{\theta x\_{\max}} - 1\bigr)\right],
\qquad \bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{mis}} = \bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{obs}} - \frac{\rho\_{R,Y}\sigma\_Y}{\sqrt{f(1-f)}}.$$

**The exponent that follows.** Replace $M\_X$ by that ceiling in the Lundberg equation and let $\bar\theta$ be the positive root of $\lambda\bigl(\bar M(\theta) - 1\bigr) = c\theta$. Because $\bar M \ge M\_X$, the function $\lambda(\bar M - 1) - c\theta$ dominates the true one and its crossing comes no later, so $\bar\theta \le \theta$; and Lundberg's inequality survives the substitution, since $\psi \le e^{-\theta B} \le e^{-\bar\theta B}$. The loss is the whole content of the word *opens*: the statement stays an inequality, weaker by exactly the shortfall in the exponent. Differentiating the dominated equation, with $\partial\bar M/\partial\rho\_{R,Y} < 0$ from the sign of $\bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{mis}}$,
$$\frac{d\bar\theta}{d\rho\_{R,Y}} \mkern5mu=\mkern5mu \frac{\lambda\mkern3mu(1-f)\mkern3mu\bigl(e^{\bar\theta x\_{\max}} - 1\bigr)\mkern3mu\sigma\_Y}{x\_{\max}\sqrt{f(1-f)}\mkern5mu\bigl(\lambda \bar M'(\bar\theta) - c\bigr)} \mkern5mu>\mkern5mu 0,$$
the denominator being positive at the smallest positive root. The root exists only where the ceiling law is served, $\lambda\mkern3mu\bar E[Y] < c$ for $\bar E[Y] = f\bar Y\_{\mathrm{obs}} + (1-f)\bar Y\_{\mathrm{mis}}$ the mean of that law. This condition is not the point-identified one: since $\bar Y\_{\mathrm{mis}} = \bar Y\_{\mathrm{obs}} - \rho\_{R,Y}\sigma\_Y/\sqrt{f(1-f)}$ and $\mu = \bar Y\_{\mathrm{obs}}$ at $\rho\_{R,Y} = 0$, the ceiling mean is $\bar E[Y] = \mu - (1-f)\rho\_{R,Y}\sigma\_Y/\sqrt{f(1-f)}$, above $\mu$ in the adverse direction. A declaration of defect therefore raises the loading a system must carry before any exponent exists at all.

The exponent rises with the defect. Recording the severe shocks is protective and missing them is not, so the adverse direction is $\rho\_{R,Y} = -\bar\rho < 0$, and the bound has to be evaluated there. Substituting $\rho\_{R,Y} = -\bar\rho$ into $\bar E[Y]$ gives $\bar E\_{-\bar\rho}[Y] = \bar Y\_{\mathrm{obs}} + \bar\rho\mkern3mu\sigma\_Y\sqrt{(1-f)/f}$. Setting the ceiling loading $\delta(-\bar\rho) = c - \lambda\bar E\_{-\bar\rho}[Y]$ to zero isolates the **vital-margin breakdown correlation** (for $0 < f < 1$, $\lambda > 0$, $\sigma\_Y > 0$, $\bar Y\_{\mathrm{obs}} \in [0, x\_{\max}]$, with $(x)\_+ = \max(0, x)$):

$$\rho^\star\_{R,Y} = \min\!\left(1,\mkern4mu \frac{(c - \lambda\bar Y\_{\mathrm{obs}})\_+}{\lambda\sigma\_Y}\sqrt{\frac{f}{1 - f}}\right).$$

When the point-identified system appears solvent ($c > \lambda\bar Y\_{\mathrm{obs}}$) and the ratio is at most $1$, any defect $\bar\rho \ge \rho^\star\_{R,Y}$ collapses $\bar\theta(-\bar\rho)$ to zero and makes the required buffer $\ln(1/\varepsilon)/\bar\theta(-\bar\rho) + w\_B$ diverge, inverting the safety decision; admissibility on $[0, x\_{\max}]$ simultaneously caps the adverse defect at $\bar\rho\_{\mathrm{supp}} = \min\bigl(1,\mkern3mu \frac{x\_{\max} - \bar Y\_{\mathrm{obs}}}{\sigma\_Y}\sqrt{f(1-f)}\bigr)$.

**The certificate.** Declare $\bar\rho \ge |\rho\_{R,Y}|$, as Chapter 45 does for the effective sample, and hold the reserve at the top of its interval. Then
$$\psi \mkern5mu\le\mkern5mu \exp\bigl(-\bar\theta(-\bar\rho)\mkern3muB\_L\bigr), \qquad
B\_U \mkern5mu\ge\mkern5mu \frac{\ln(1/\varepsilon)}{\bar\theta(-\bar\rho)} \mkern5mu+\mkern5mu w\_B,$$
so a buffer certified at $\varepsilon$ carries both corrections in one inequality: the interval's width added, and the exponent discounted by the declared defect. Expanding $\bar\theta(-\bar\rho) = \theta\_0\bigl(1 - (\bar\theta'(0)/\theta\_0)\bar\rho + O(\bar\rho^2)\bigr)$, with $\theta\_0 = \bar\theta(0)$, gives the requirement to first order,
$$\frac{\ln(1/\varepsilon)}{\bar\theta(-\bar\rho)} \mkern5mu=\mkern5mu \frac{\ln(1/\varepsilon)}{\theta\_0}\left(1 + \frac{\bar\theta'(0)}{\theta\_0}\mkern3mu\bar\rho + O(\bar\rho^2)\right),$$
so the declared defect inflates the reserve linearly, where the same defect inflates the required row count quadratically in that chapter. The asymmetry is not an artefact of the algebra. An effective sample is a variance, and the defect enters it squared. A ruin exponent is a rate, and the defect enters it to the first power. A margin sized on the epistemic side alone is short on the vital side by an amount of order $\bar\rho$, the very correlation it was hedging.

The linear term is uniform in $\bar\rho$ only while it stays small against the leading one, and that is a condition on the loading rather than on the defect. With $\delta = c - \lambda\bar E[Y]$ the loading of the ceiling law, $\theta\_0 \approx 2\delta/(\lambda\bar E[Y^2])$ vanishes with $\delta$, while $\bar\theta'(0)$ stays finite: its numerator carries $e^{\bar\theta x\_{\max}} - 1 \approx \theta\_0 x\_{\max}$, which cancels the $\delta$ that appeared in the denominator. The relative coefficient is therefore $\bar\theta'(0)/\theta\_0 = \lambda(1-f)\sigma\_Y/(\delta\sqrt{f(1-f)})$, diverging as $1/\delta$, and the expansion holds while
$$\bar\rho \mkern5mu\ll\mkern5mu \frac{\delta}{\lambda(1-f)\sigma\_Y/\sqrt{f(1-f)}}.$$
Away from the wall the correction is genuinely first order. Near it the coefficient is large, and the "linear" inflation dominates instead of staying small. It is largest where the required buffer is largest, the opposite of the order in which a practitioner would want the approximation to fail.

**Degenerate checks.** At $f = 1$ the defect term carries the factor $1 - f$ and the bound collapses to the point-identified Lundberg inequality, as it must: a complete archive has no unrecorded severity to place. At $\bar\rho = 0$ with $f < 1$ the mean of the unrecorded shocks is pinned while their shape is not, so a residual width survives — the bound is sharp for the mean and not for the exponent, which is the honest reading of "missing at random". The extreme law also requires the unrecorded mean to lie inside the support, so a declared pair $(f, \bar\rho)$ is admissible only while $0 \le \bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{obs}} - \rho\_{R,Y}\sigma\_Y/\sqrt{f(1-f)} \le x\_{\max}$; past that point the declaration would place the unrecorded shocks outside the range severity can take, which is the one place in this argument where the defect bound can be checked against the archive instead of asserted about it. And the wall is not at $c \downarrow \lambda\mu$: the exponent dies at $c \downarrow \lambda\bar E[Y]$, above $\lambda\mu$ by $\lambda(1-f)\rho\_{R,Y}\sigma\_Y/\sqrt{f(1-f)}$ in the adverse direction, so a declared defect moves the wall outward and the point-identified wall is the optimistic end of the family. A buffer sized against $\lambda\mu$ is short by exactly the loading the declaration added, before the exponent correction is applied — the wall dominates any defect, but the defect moves the wall.

## 6.4 Abraham Wald's (1943) Latent Survivorship Matrix Solver and Sniedovich's Maximin Safeguard

Finally, two classic defenses against Knightian blindness belong to this operator:

### 6.4.1 Abraham Wald's Latent Survivorship Linear Inversion

In 1943 the Statistical Research Group (SRG) asked Abraham Wald where to add armor to Allied combat aircraft. The military had recorded the bullet holes on planes that returned from combat missions. A point optimizer would reinforce the areas with the most holes.

Wald saw that the sample was truncated. Downed planes never returned to base.
Let $c \in \{1, 2\}$ represent two distinct mission cohorts. Let $S\_{c, A}$ and $S\_{c, B}$ be the observed returning survivor hit counts in zone $A$ (wings/fuselage) and zone $B$ (engine/cockpit). Let $M\_c$ be the total number of missing (downed) aircraft in cohort $c$.

Let $\boldsymbol{\theta} = (\theta\_A, \theta\_B)^T$ be the unknown vulnerability odds multipliers on missing aircraft, where $\theta\_z = q\_z / (1 - q\_z)$ represents the ratio of conditional lethality $q\_z = \mathbb{P}(\text{Lost}\mid\text{Hit}\_z)$ to survival probability $1 - q\_z$. Under latent proportionality, the missing aircraft satisfy the linear system:
$$\begin{pmatrix} S\_{1, A} & S\_{1, B} \\ S\_{2, A} & S\_{2, B} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \theta\_A \\ \theta\_B \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} M\_1 \\ M\_2 \end{pmatrix}$$
Provided the determinant $\Delta = S\_{1, A}S\_{2, B} - S\_{1, B}S\_{2, A} \ne 0$, matrix inversion recovers $(\theta\_A, \theta\_B)$ exactly:
$$\begin{pmatrix} \theta\_A \\ \theta\_B \end{pmatrix} = \frac{1}{S\_{1, A}S\_{2, B} - S\_{1, B}S\_{2, A}} \begin{pmatrix} S\_{2, B} & -S\_{1, B} \\ -S\_{2, A} & S\_{1, A} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} M\_1 \\ M\_2 \end{pmatrix}$$
The recovered multipliers are odds ratios, not probabilities, and they map back through $q\_z = \theta\_z / (1 + \theta\_z)$ to conditional lethalities. What the inversion shows is the direction of the inference. A zone in which returning aircraft show almost no hits carries a *high* lethality multiplier, because the aircraft hit there did not come back, and Wald's advice was to armour where the returning sample was empty (Wald 1943). The survivorship-conditioned estimate is the complement of the naive one. The difference between them is the bias a truncated sample always carries.

### 6.4.2 Info-Gap Robustness Guarded by Moshe Sniedovich's Global Wald Maximin

Local Info-Gap robustness around a nominal guess runs into Moshe Sniedovich's (2007, 2012) *No-Man's-Land* invariance property. An option can look locally robust around the nominal and still expose the community to catastrophic global ruin outside the robustness radius. Wald's global Maximin floor is the correction: it asks what happens at the worst point of the uncertainty set, not at the neighbourhood the analyst chose to inspect.

## 6.5 The Two Ledgers on Equity and Grid Archives

The non-ergodic ledger is read off a long return series. A century and a half of daily index returns give an arithmetic drift and a volatility, and together they produce a time-average growth rate below the ensemble drift by the Itô term. The path earns less than the average of the paths, and no additional history closes the gap, because the gap is arithmetic and not statistical. The same series separates the leverage that maximizes growth from the leverage beyond which the time average turns negative. The interesting quantity is neither boundary but the interval between them, where leverage is a decision and not a bet on the ensemble.

The second ledger is read off a demand series with a diurnal cycle. Victorian half-hourly electricity demand supplies a median, an upper quantile and a maximum, and a ramp rate measured at the same percentile; those four numbers describe a system whose stress case is not its mean. Size dispatchable capacity from a multiple of an upper quantile and the system sits at a utilization where the Kingman factor is large; the survival buffer, the distance from the stress quantile to the observed maximum divided by the ramp rate, is then short. Size from a multiple of the maximum and utilization sits at the median, where the Kingman factor is near one and the buffer is long. The grid is the same grid in both readings. Only the headroom differs, and with it the verdict on whether the system survives the disruption it is being asked about.

Both ledgers refuse the ensemble. The return series refuses it because a single trajectory cannot average over its own counterfactuals. The demand series refuses it because a system that fails at the maximum does not get to collect the average. The survival inequality of §6.3.2 is where they meet: a growth rate is worth compounding only if the position survives long enough to compound it.

## 6.6 The recovery term is not one term: a deployment reading

§6.3.2 instantiated the general recovery time $\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{recovery}}$ as the three-term effective latency $\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t) = \tau\_{\mathrm{detect}} + \tau\_{\mathrm{delib}}(\mathcal{I}\_t) + \tau\_{\mathrm{actuate}}$. In any system that
runs on a fitted model those three phases specialize into three deployment acts, and institutions omit the middle and organizational ones:

$$\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t) = \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{detect}} + \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{retrain}} +
\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{roll out}},$$
where $\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{detect}}$ instantiates $\tau\_{\mathrm{detect}}$, while model retraining and release-gate clearance allocate the deliberation and post-authorization actuation latencies across $\tau\_{\mathrm{delib}}(\mathcal{I}\_t) + \tau\_{\mathrm{actuate}}$.

The decomposition matters because different mechanisms govern the three terms, and only the first is
usually instrumented. Take a monitored performance signal that shifts, a one-sided sequential monitor with its slack and its threshold, a reserve that affords a known number of steps of degraded service, and a retraining that takes a known number of shifts. A monitor that fires promptly and a retraining that finishes inside the survival window leave the reserve largely intact. An institution whose retraining takes twice as long recovers after the reserve is gone, on the same drift, with the same monitor and the same reserve. The verdict belongs to the sum, to no single term.

Three readings follow. Each is a margin, not an optimisation.

1. **Without a fallback, detection is worthless.** An institution with no fallback arm exhausts the reserve
   entirely: it never stops serving the degraded model, so its $\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$ is infinite and the
   inequality fails by construction, not by degree. This is Collapse Proposition 2 in its cleanest form: knowing
   the width of the drop exactly, and having no way to stop consuming the reserve because of it.
2. **The monitor's operating point is a trade between two failure modes.** A lower threshold detects earlier and
   raises the false-alarm rate; a higher threshold does the reverse. The trade is auditable only against a
   stationary comparison: with no drift in the world, the same monitor stays silent, so the alarm that fires is
   attributable to the shift rather than to the noise.
3. **The lag that matters is organisational, not algorithmic.** Retraining is cheap; releasing a retrained artifact
   through whatever gates exist is not, and the gates are the term that must be compared with the reserve.

This decomposition is also the right reading of the MLOps practice this chapter's neighbours describe. Continuous
training tries to shrink the second and third terms; drift monitoring tries to shrink the first; a rollback path is
the only mechanism that makes the sum finite once the first two are spent.

### The recovery term is not one number

A stressed system survives for the hours its buffer affords, then needs its own recovery time to come back, and the two are measured on different clocks. The survival time comes off the demand series; the recovery time off the institution's own latency parameters. Nothing in the mean return of the ensemble says so, and the recovery term is not a single number. Discounted, the same nominal recovery is longer, and the inequality fails by more.

Discounting is the part a schedule hides. A recovery that pays back over quarters is not a recovery at the moment the barrier is crossed, and an institution booking the nominal hours books a latency it will not receive. The honest ledger reports the survival time, the discounted recovery time and the inequality between them.

The second error is subtler and lives in the series. The drift across parallel worlds is not the drift along one path, and the difference, the variance drain, is not estimation error. A reserve sized on the ensemble drift overestimates its own accumulation, and no additional history closes a gap that is arithmetic, not statistical.

### When absorption cannot happen

Non-ergodicity is irrelevant when absorption cannot occur inside the horizon. Compute the worst survival time over the horizon. If it exceeds the horizon, use the ensemble mean and record that the choice was made. The test costs one line, and it decides which average the plan may use.

### Two ledgers, one currency

The two ledgers, survival and accumulation, are usually reported by different people, which is why they are usually inconsistent. The risk function reports the survival time. The investment function reports the drift. Nobody reports the difference between ensemble drift and path drift, the quantity that decides whether the reserve exists at the horizon. The three numbers belong in one table, in one currency, and the currency is time: how long the position survives, how long it takes to recover, and how fast the reserve grows along the path instead of across the ensemble. A table with those three columns can be audited by someone who has never seen the model.

## 6.7. The Clock the Inequality Forgets

### 6.7.1 Two limits of the growth ledger

Both formulas of §6.1, $g\_{\mathrm{time}}(f) = f\mu - \frac{1}{2}f^2\sigma^2$ and $g\_{\mathrm{ensemble}} = f\mu$, have degenerate ends, and any series computes them.

The first end is $f \to 0$. Dividing by $f$ gives $g\_{\mathrm{time}}(f)/f \to \mu$: an infinitesimal position earns the arithmetic drift, and the drain vanishes as a fraction of a vanishing stake. The two terms are in ratio $\frac{1}{2}f\sigma^2/\mu = f/(2f^{\*})$, so the drain is second order in the leverage.

The second end is the ruin boundary $f = 2\mu/\sigma^2$. There the two terms of the growth rate are equal, so $g\_{\mathrm{time}} = 0$ while the ensemble drift stays positive. A ledger reporting the ensemble line reports a compounding position; the position is flat; both statements are correct. Between zero and the optimum the path earns less than the ensemble by the variance drain. Beyond the optimum it earns less than the optimum. Beyond the boundary it earns nothing. The interval between the boundaries is the only place where leverage is a decision, not a bet on the ensemble, and it is one octave wide, from $f^{\*}$ to $2f^{\*}$.

### 6.7.2 The reserve the shock owns

The inequality of §6.3.2 treats the buffer as available and the crisis as exogenous. It has a failure mode that no buffer size cures: the shock can destroy a share of the reserve in the same instant that it creates the demand. Write the buffer $B$, the draw-down rate $\dot{D}$, and let the event impair a fraction $c$ of the buffer. The effective survival time is then $(1-c)B/\dot{D}$, and restoring $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} \ge \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$ requires $B \ge \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)\mkern3mu\dot{D}/(1-c)$, a buffer larger than the naive size by the factor $1/(1-c)$, which diverges as the impairment approaches the whole reserve. The rule then recommends buying more of the same, and the quantity it multiplies has just been shown to be correlated with the event.

The failure of the Texas interconnection in February 2021 is the dated case. The planning reserve was a buffer sized against a draw-down rate estimated from another season, and the event that arrived froze the fleet's own fuel supply. The staff reports of the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission and the North American Electric Reliability Corporation document unit trips and fuel-supply failures across the fleet, not a shortage of installed capacity (FERC/NERC 2021). The inequality was satisfied at the planning margin and the deployment failed anyway. The instrument that would have worked is not a larger buffer but a smaller $c$: winterised fuel supply, dual-fuel capability, and a reserve whose availability does not depend on the same weather as the demand.

The rule survives in corrected form, and the correction is one term. An institution that cannot estimate $c$ has not estimated its survival time. It has estimated a number it would like to be true.

### 6.7.3 Recovery time is a function of the position that needs it

The deeper reason the inequality is not a sizing rule: $\mathrm{TTR}$ is not exogenous to $R$. An institution recovers by converting assets into the means of continuing, and the price falls as it converts, so the recovery time of a position depends on how large the position is against the market that must absorb it (Danielsson & Shin 2003). Write $\mathrm{TTR}(A) = \mathrm{TTR}\_0(1 + \kappa A/M)$ with $A$ the amount still to be raised and $M$ the market's absorption rate. The inequality then compares a survival time decreasing in the shortfall with a recovery time increasing in it, and the two curves need not cross at any buffer size.

That is a fixed-point problem with three outcomes the audit has to name. A crossing, where the smallest crossing buffer is the answer. No crossing, where no feasible buffer works and the action is to reduce exposure before the stress, not during it. Or a crossing only at a buffer the institution cannot hold, which is the second case with a price attached. The procedure evaluates the inequality at the stressed recovery time. The Northern Rock episode of September 2007 illustrates it. The recovery clock was set on the liability side, by depositors and wholesale counterparties who repriced at the first news, so the balance sheet was an argument inside the counterparties' recovery function and not a parameter of the bank's own (Danielsson & Shin 2003). Its survival time was measurable. Its recovery time was being set by people reading its survival time.

An audit that reports $\mathrm{TTR}$ as a constant has reported a property of its own model. The defensible report gives the pair at the operating point and at the stressed point and states whether any feasible buffer exists, because the case in which it does not is the case in which a sizing rule is asked for a number it cannot produce and produces one anyway.

## Exercises

1. **Collapse Proposition 2 in an Epistemic-Only Agent:** Consider an agent that keeps the epistemic margin, switches off the vital margin, and keeps governance. Why does it achieve high epistemic coverage yet still hit the ruin barrier? Answer in terms of the survival inequality, and say which of the two margins the coverage figure belongs to.
2. **Baker–McHale Shrinkage Limit:** Verify that $k\bigl(n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}\bigr) = 1/(1 + 1/n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}})$ tends to one half as $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} \to 1$ and to one as $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} \to \infty$, and say what each limit means for a position sized on a single observation.
3. **Two Fractions, One Growth Rate:** Take an excess drift $\mu - r$, a variance $\sigma^2$ and a sampling-variance effective sample size $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}$. Compute the epistemic Kelly fraction $f^\ast = k\bigl(n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}\bigr)(\hat{\mu}-r)/\sigma^2$, the growth rate it delivers, and the growth rate at twice that fraction. Show that the doubled fraction keeps a small share of the growth while carrying the same downside, which is the asymmetry that makes the fraction a barrier rather than a target.
4. **The coefficient behind the clocks:** With shock intensity $\lambda$, exponential severities of mean $\mu$ and net replenishment $c$, solve $\lambda(M\_X(R) - 1) = cR$ for the adjustment coefficient and evaluate the buffer that holds ruin below $\varepsilon$. Then take $c \downarrow \lambda\mu$ and say what the limit does to the buffer and to the safety loading (answer: $R = (c - \lambda\mu)/(c\mu)$ and $B \ge \ln(1/\varepsilon)\mkern3muc\mu/(c - \lambda\mu)$; the buffer diverges and the safety loading vanishes, which is the same wall the utilization hyperbola describes).
5. **The reserve with a defect:** With a recorded share $f$, a declared defect bound $\bar\rho$ and a reserve known only within an interval of width $w\_B$, write the certified buffer and say which correction is additive and which enters the exponent's reciprocal. Then take $f = 1$ and $\bar\rho = 0$ with $f < 1$ in turn, and say what survives (answer: $B\_U \ge \ln(1/\varepsilon)/\bar\theta(-\bar\rho) + w\_B$; the width is additive while the defect discounts the exponent to first order in $\bar\rho$, the two ends being the point-identified Lundberg inequality at $f = 1$ and a bound that is sharp in the mean but not in the exponent at $\bar\rho = 0$).
