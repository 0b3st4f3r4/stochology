## 6.1 Non-Ergodic Itô Variance Drain and Epistemic Kelly Shrinkage

Nicholas Georgescu-Roegen (1971) proved that biological and economic systems live inside irreversible entropic time rather than reversible mechanics. Once a reserve crosses zero, future expected surpluses cannot revive a ruined community.

### 6.1.1 Time-Average vs. Ensemble-Average Divergence

In plain English, averaging across a million parallel worlds is not the same as compounding one fragile world over time. As [Chapter 38](38-time-and-dependence.md) shows ([Appendix A](appendix-a-mathematics.md) §A.2), multiplicative wealth under leveraged stake $f \ge 0$ splits the ensemble expectation rate $g\_{\mathrm{ensemble}}(f)$ from the single-path growth rate $g\_{\mathrm{time}}(f) = r\_{\mathrm{rf}} + f(\mu - r\_{\mathrm{rf}}) - \frac{1}{2}f^2\sigma^2$ through **Itô's (1951) variance drain** ($\frac{1}{2}f^2\sigma^2$). Chasing ensemble expectations rewards pushing leverage up without limit. Along a single path at baseline risk-free rate $r\_{\mathrm{rf}} = 0$ (reserving $r$ for the Lundberg argument in §6.3.3), any leverage above $2\mu/\sigma^2$ collapses almost surely toward zero. Path growth instead peaks at Robert C. Merton's (1969) CRRA rule in its logarithmic ($\gamma = 1$, $r\_{\mathrm{rf}} = 0$) limit and John Kelly's (1956) fraction $f^\ast = \mu/\sigma^2$ (for $\mu, \sigma > 0$).

At the same time, Filter II never elevates log-growth maximization into a universal law ([Chapter 38](38-time-and-dependence.md)). Non-ergodicity separates ensemble averages from time averages whenever paths cannot pool physically (Peters, 2019), finite-horizon variance differs from infinite-time asymptotics (Hulme et al., 2023), and forcing log utility everywhere erases legitimate risk preferences (Ford & Kay, 2023, 2024). Filter II therefore treats variance drain strictly as a **survival barrier** where ruin looms, leaving choices inside the surviving corridor open to community preferences.

### 6.1.2 Epistemic Shrinkage Under Finite Samples and Stevens Scale Invariance

Even when parameters are known, Leo Breiman (1961) proved expected-log betting wins asymptotically ($T \to \infty$), whereas Paul Samuelson (1971, eqs. 4, 20, pp. 2493–2496) showed it fails to dominate over finite horizons under isoelastic ($x^\gamma/\gamma$) or bounded utility ("over-riskiness" and "under-riskiness") and breaks under serial dependence ("Pandora's Box").

In reality, mean drift $\mu$ is also unknown. At baseline risk-free rate $r\_{\mathrm{rf}} = 0$ (writing $\hat{\mu} - r\_{\mathrm{rf}}$ whenever $r\_{\mathrm{rf}} \ne 0$), we estimate $\hat{\mu}$ from a clustered archive whose variance-effective sample size shrinks to $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} > 0$ ([Chapter 45](45-world-to-archive-big-data-paradox.md)). Under known unit-period return variance $\sigma^2 > 0$ and the **unit-period estimation-variance premise** $\sigma\_{\hat{\mu}}^2 := \mathrm{Var}(\hat{\mu}) = \sigma^2 / n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}$ (distinct from fixed-span continuous-time sampling where $\mathrm{Var}(\hat{\mu}) = \sigma^2 / T$, and distinct from Baker & McHale's 2013 SNR-dependent frequentist shrinkage $\bigl(1 + \frac{\sigma^2}{n(\mu - r\_{\mathrm{rf}})^2}\bigr)^{-1}$), plugging noisy $\hat{\mu}$ into $f^\ast\_{\text{plug-in}} = \hat{\mu}/\sigma^2$ overbets on estimation error (Baker & McHale 2013). Replacing $\sigma^2$ with predictive variance $\sigma\_{\mathrm{pred}}^2 = \sigma^2 + \sigma\_{\hat{\mu}}^2 = \sigma^2\bigl(1 + 1/n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}\bigr)$ gives shrunk leverage $f^\ast\_{\mathrm{epi}} = \hat{\mu}/\sigma\_{\mathrm{pred}}^2 = k\bigl(n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}\bigr)\mkern2mu(\hat{\mu}/\sigma^2)$, where dimensionless **epistemic Kelly shrinkage** $k\bigl(n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}\bigr)$ (motivated by Baker & McHale 2013; [Appendix A](appendix-a-mathematics.md) §A.2) is:
$$k\bigl(n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}\bigr) = \frac{1}{1 + 1 / n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}} \in (0, 1),$$

In plain words, finite samples always pull the bet below the ideal fraction: only as effective evidence $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} \to \infty$ does the shrinkage factor approach $1$ (while estimating unknown $\sigma^2$ adds extra uncertainty indexed by $z\_{\hat{\mu}}^2$ below). Two structural limits bind any shrinkage factor here:

- **Stevens ratio-scale invariance ([Chapter 46](46-non-identical-asymmetry-heavy-tails.md)).** Because $f^\ast = \mu/\sigma^2$ carries inverse return units (scaling returns by $a$ scales $f^\ast$ by $1/a$), any valid multiplier $k$—including extensions for estimated $\hat{\sigma}^2$—must stay strictly **dimensionless**. It must be built only from unit-free ratios such as $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}$ and $z\_{\hat{\mu}}^2 = n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}\hat{\mu}^2/\hat{\sigma}^2$, never mixing $\hat{f}^2$ directly against dimensionless constants. Any fourth-moment kurtosis correction also requires finite fourth moments ($\alpha > 4$).
- **Domain breakdown.** Shrinkage never licenses positive exposure when excess drift is zero or negative ($\mu \le r\_{\mathrm{rf}}$) or when variance is infinite ($\alpha \le 2$); both force **Regime III** halt (cutting exposure).

## 6.2 Canguilhem's Vital Normativity, Allostasis, and Resilience Basins

Georges Canguilhem, in *The Normal and the Pathological* (1943/1966), defined biological health as a **margin of tolerance** against environmental inconstancy (*infidélité du milieu*)—the capacity to absorb shocks and forge new operating norms rather than clinging to yesterday's average. Māori *Whakataukī* captures that dependence genealogically, stepping into tomorrow backward with eyes fixed on past ruptures and inherited duties (Rameka 2016): vital slack is the living memory of past storms.

Peter Sterling and Joseph Eyer (1988) named the physiological counterpart **allostasis**—*stability through change*, shifting setpoints proactively ahead of coming loads rather than defending Walter Cannon's rigid thermostat. In ecological dynamics, C. S. Holling (1973; 1996) separated two dimensions of resilience:

- **Engineering resilience** ($\lambda\_{\mathrm{eng}} = -\frac{\partial F}{\partial x}\big|\_{x^\ast}$, where $F(x)$ is the local drift field, kept distinct from Kelly leverage $f$ and Poisson rate $\lambda$): how fast a small bump returns toward equilibrium $x^\ast$.
- **Ecological / vital resilience** ($D\_t = x\_{\mathrm{high}}^\ast - x\_{\mathrm{tip}}^\ast$): the basin width separating normal operation from a tipping point $x\_{\mathrm{tip}}^\ast$, corresponding to physical buffer $B\_t$ above ruin in *The Community and the Reserve*.

Under relentless emergency mobilization, Bruce McEwen's (1998) **allostatic load** erodes regulatory reserve so relative basin width $D\_t / D\_0$ ($D\_0 > 0$) shrinks. Inside organizations, silencing safety warnings fuels `"rumination and a sense of regret"`, wearing down human vigilance ([Chapter 20](20-legitimacy-and-institutions.md); Stephens et al. 2022). When disturbances are small, finite-variance continuous fluctuations inside one basin, Marten Scheffer et al.'s (2009) **Critical Slowing Down** lag-1 autocorrelation $\rho\_1 = \exp(-\lambda\_{\mathrm{eng}}\Delta t)$ climbs toward $1$ as $\lambda\_{\mathrm{eng}} \to 0^+$, signaling danger before the mean moves. Yet inference works in one direction only: elevated autocorrelation is likelier near a bifurcation, whereas treating high $\rho\_1$ as proof of imminent collapse commits the prosecutor's fallacy (Boettiger & Hastings 2012).

**Domain boundary and rupture under Lévy jumps ($\alpha < 2$).** Scheffer et al.'s (2009) warning signal presupposes small continuous diffusive noise. That premise fails whenever infinite variance ($\alpha \le 2$) arrives via **Lévy jumps ($\alpha < 2$)** converging to an $\alpha$-stable law (Gnedenko & Kolmogorov 1954; [Chapter 46](46-non-identical-asymmetry-heavy-tails.md) §5.4). A single extreme jump exceeding basin width ($|\Delta X\_t| > D\_t$) crosses the tipping cliff $x\_{\mathrm{tip}}^\ast$ while $\lambda\_{\mathrm{eng}} > 0$ and $\rho\_1 \not\to 1^-$—quiet autocorrelation offers zero protection against sudden jumps.

## 6.3 Kingman's Heavy-Traffic Queue Explosion and the Lean Operating Trap

Lean efficiency targets routinely break hospital emergency wards, power grids, and reserve supply chains. Take a single-server $GI/G/1$ renewal queue with mutually independent i.i.d. interarrival times (mean $1/\lambda > 0$) and i.i.d. service times (mean $\tau = 1/\mu\_s > 0$, $\mu\_s > \lambda > 0$), utilization $\rho = \lambda/\mu\_s \in (0, 1)$ (distinct from Meng's $\rho\_{R,Y}$), and finite squared coefficients of variation $0 \le c\_a^2, c\_s^2 < \infty$ with $c\_a^2 + c\_s^2 > 0$ ($\alpha > 2$). John F. C. Kingman's (1962) distribution-free bound establishes the global upper ceiling ([Chapter 38](38-time-and-dependence.md); [Appendix A](appendix-a-mathematics.md) §A.2):
$$\mathbb{E}[W\_q] \le \frac{c\_a^2/\rho^2 + c\_s^2}{2}\cdot\frac{\rho}{1 - \rho}\cdot\tau,$$
whose $c\_a^2/\rho^2$ term diverges as $\rho \to 0^+$ when $c\_a^2 > 0$. In heavy traffic ($\rho \to 1^-$), Kingman's (1961) heavy-traffic approximation and the exact Pollaczek–Khinchine $M/G/1$ identity ($c\_a^2 = 1$) tighten that upper ceiling to the heavy-traffic scaling $\mathbb{E}[W\_q] \approx \frac{c\_a^2 + c\_s^2}{2}\cdot\frac{\rho}{1 - \rho}\cdot\tau$.

Three boundary conditions separate physical queues from naive formulas:

1. **Finite variance and stability ($\alpha > 2$, $\rho < 1$):** Under heavy-tailed service ($\alpha \le 2$), $c\_s^2$ diverges, and single-server bounds act only as conservative structural cues inside multi-server networks.
2. **Breakdown of Little's law at saturation:** John D. C. Little's (1961) conservation identity ($L = \lambda W$) requires long-run stability ($\rho < 1$) and fails transiently at overload ($\rho \ge 1$). Peter W. Glynn and Ward Whitt's (1989) generalized law relaxes stationarity only when queues return repeatedly to empty regeneration states ($Q(t\_k) = 0$) with sublinear growth ($n^{-1}W\_n \to 0$)—failing once sustained overload drives linear queue explosion.
3. **Means hide tail waits:** Expected wait $\mathbb{E}[W\_q]$ masks far larger upper-quantile delays whenever convex harm $\Delta\_J$ bites ([Chapter 46](46-non-identical-asymmetry-heavy-tails.md)).

### 6.3.1 The Hyperbolic Utilization Explosion

In the heavy-traffic scaling $\mathbb{E}[W\_q] \approx \frac{c\_a^2 + c\_s^2}{2}\cdot\frac{\rho}{1 - \rho}\cdot\tau$, the congestion multiplier $\frac{\rho}{1 - \rho}$ is a hyperbola with a vertical wall at $\rho = 1$. When utilization sits below $1/2$, the factor stays under $1$. Near saturation it explodes like $(1 - \rho)^{-1}$, and raising utilization from $\rho\_1$ to $\rho\_2$ in that regime scales heavy-traffic wait time by $\frac{\rho\_2/(1-\rho\_2)}{\rho\_1/(1-\rho\_1)}$. Stripping away "idle slack" pushes $\rho \to 1^-$ and drives recovery delay $\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$ straight up the vertical wall precisely when disruption strikes.

### 6.3.2 The Master Survival Inequality: $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} \ge \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{recovery}}$

Both Herbert Scarf's (1960) $(s, S)$ inventory model and David Simchi-Levi et al.'s (2014) supply-chain physics arrive at the exact same race for *The Community and the Reserve* ([Chapter 38](38-time-and-dependence.md); [Appendix A](appendix-a-mathematics.md)). **Time-to-Survival Buffer** $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} = B\_t / \dot{D}\_{\mathrm{crisis}}$ (buffer $B\_t \ge 0$ divided by crisis drain rate $\dot{D}\_{\mathrm{crisis}} > 0$, prior to common-mode impairment $c\_{\mathrm{imp}}$ in §6.7.2 where it shrinks to $(1 - c\_{\mathrm{imp}})B\_t / \dot{D}\_{\mathrm{crisis}}$) must match or outlast **Effective Time-to-Recovery** $\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{recovery}} := \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t) = \tau\_{\mathrm{detect}}(\delta\_{\mathrm{status}}) + \tau\_{\mathrm{delib}}(\mathcal{I}\_t) + \tau\_{\mathrm{actuate}}$ at sealed information state $\mathcal{I}\_t$. Three distinct clocks make up recovery time:

- **Detection latency ($\tau\_{\mathrm{detect}}(\delta\_{\mathrm{status}})$):** time needed to accumulate enough evidence for an alarm. Institutional status discounting $\delta\_{\mathrm{status}} \in (0, 1)$ stretches this delay relative to the undiscounted baseline $\tau\_{\mathrm{detect}}(0)$ derived in [Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md) §§8.2.2, 8.6.1 ([Chapter 20](20-legitimacy-and-institutions.md)).
- **Deliberation latency ($\tau\_{\mathrm{delib}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$):** committee and audit clearance time under limited human triage bandwidth ([Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md) §8.4). Oversized review packets eat directly into remaining detection headroom $T\_{\mathrm{rem}}^{\mathrm{detect}} := \mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} - (\tau\_{\mathrm{delib}}(\mathcal{I}\_t) + \tau\_{\mathrm{actuate}})$ (distinct from [Chapter 95](95-collision-at-ten-bits-per-second.md)'s deliberation headroom $T\_{\mathrm{rem}}^{\mathrm{delib}}$).
- **Actuation latency ($\tau\_{\mathrm{actuate}}$):** execution and queue-drain delay after authorization, which scales up with Kingman congestion $\rho$.

When disruption hits near saturation ($\rho \to 1^-$) or runs through discounted, audit-jammed channels, $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} < \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$ records a concrete failure of two physical clocks.

### 6.3.3 The Ruin Coefficient Behind the Clocks

When the survival inequality fails, ruin is a first-passage fact even before specifying shock shapes. Suppose replenishment halts during recovery so remaining reserve is $U(t) = B - S(t)$ ($B > 0$), where cumulative demand $S(t) = \sum\_{i=1}^{N(t)} X\_i$ is a **compound Poisson** process of independent shocks $X\_i \ge 0$ arriving at intensity $\lambda > 0$ with mean $\mathbb{E}[X\_i] = \mu > 0$ ($\mathbb{E}[S(t)] = \lambda\mu t$), and $\tau\_{\mathrm{ruin}} = \inf\lbrace{}t \ge 0 : S(t) \ge B\rbrace$ is its hitting time. Along the average fluid path, shocks exhaust buffer $B$ at $B/(\lambda\mu) = \mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}}$, so failing the inequality guarantees average ruin before recovery arrives.

Even when the survival inequality holds, random shock clusters can still exhaust $B$. Cramér and Lundberg's ruin coefficient measures how steeply buffer size suppresses ruin risk (Cramér 1930; Lundberg 1903).

Let $c > \lambda\mu > 0$ be normal-period net replenishment rate and $M\_X(r) = \mathbb{E}[e^{rX}]$ be the moment-generating function of shock $X$ at $r > 0$. Assume $c > \lambda\mu$ and the **Cramér condition**: light-tailed shocks whose $M\_X(r) < \infty$ on $(0, r\_{\max})$ blows up smoothly ($\lim\_{r \uparrow r\_{\max}} M\_X(r) = +\infty$, guaranteeing a root—whereas bounded $M\_X(r\_{\max}^-) < \infty$ at large $c$ can leave $\lambda(M\_X(r)-1) < cr$ everywhere). Then the **Lundberg adjustment coefficient** $R > 0$ (distinct from stock $R\_t$ and Brake 2's ceiling $R^\ast$) is the unique positive root of:
$$\lambda\bigl(M\_X(R) - 1\bigr) = cR,$$

so $R$ measures the exponential safety margin of replenishment rate $c$ over light-tailed bursts, bounding infinite-horizon ruin probability $\psi(B)$ by:
$$\psi(B) \le e^{-RB}.$$

Under heavy-tailed shocks without exponential moments ($\alpha < \infty$, such as Pareto claims where $M\_X(r) = +\infty$ for every $r > 0$), Cramér's condition fails and $R$ collapses to zero. When light-tailed moments exist, keeping ruin below $\varepsilon \in (0, 1)$ requires $B \ge \ln(1/\varepsilon)/R$, and expanding $M\_X(r) = 1 + \mu r + \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[X^2]r^2 + O(r^3)$ gives the small-margin approximation:
$$R = \frac{2(c - \lambda\mu)}{\lambda\mkern3mu\mathbb{E}[X^2]} + O\bigl((c - \lambda\mu)^2\bigr).$$

Thus $R$ shrinks linearly as net slack $(c - \lambda\mu) \to 0^+$, so required buffer $1/R$ diverges just like Kingman's multiplier $1/(1-\rho)$: eliminating slack blows up both queues and required reserves. The survival inequality marks the $R = 0$ cliff where fluid ruin is guaranteed, whereas Lundberg's $R$ measures how fast risk fades at $c > \lambda\mu$.

### 6.3.4 The Bound When the Reserve Is an Interval

Everything so far assumed exact buffer $B$ and shock distribution $X$. In reality, both arrive as intervals—reserves carry unbooked liabilities, and archives miss severe shocks—widening the exponent while keeping Lundberg's law intact.

**First, the reserve interval.** Since $e^{-RB}$ shrinks in $B$, across $B \in [B\_L, B\_U]$ ($B\_L \ge 0$, width $w\_B = B\_U - B\_L$) worst-case ruin probability obeys:
$$\psi \mkern5mu\le\mkern5mu \sup\_{B \in [B\_L,\mkern3mu B\_U]} e^{-RB} \mkern5mu=\mkern5mu e^{-RB\_L}.$$

Unverified reserves add zero protection, inflating the worst-case bound by $e^{Rw\_B}$: unbooked width costs steeply far from the cliff ($R$ large) and less near the cliff ($R$ tiny) where slack is already thin.

**Second, the severity interval.** Let $f \in (0, 1)$ denote the recorded shock share, $I\_{\mathrm{rec}} \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(f)$ the recording indicator (written $R$ in Meng's notation $\rho\_{R,Y}$, distinct from §6.3.3's Lundberg $R$ and stock $R\_t$), and $Y$ shock severity ($\sigma\_Y > 0$). Dividing Meng's covariance identity $\mathrm{Cov}(I\_{\mathrm{rec}},Y) = f(1-f)\bigl(\bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{obs}} - \bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{mis}}\bigr)$ by $\sigma\_{I\_{\mathrm{rec}}}\sigma\_Y$ gives [Chapter 45](45-world-to-archive-big-data-paradox.md)'s Data Defect identity on severity:
$$\bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{obs}} - \bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{mis}} \mkern5mu=\mkern5mu \frac{\rho\_{R,Y}\mkern3mu\sigma\_Y}{\sqrt{f(1-f)}}.$$

When $\rho\_{R,Y} < 0$, the archive drops the worst shocks, raising unseen mean severity $\bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{mis}}$ at rate $\sigma\_Y/\sqrt{f(1-f)}$.

With bounded shock support $X \in [0, x\_{\max}]$ ($x\_{\max} > 0$), convexity of $x \mapsto e^{Rx}$ maximizes expectation over all laws sharing mean $m$ by placing mass $m/x\_{\max}$ at $x\_{\max}$ and the rest at zero:
$$M\_X(R) \mkern5mu\le\mkern5mu 1 + \frac{m}{x\_{\max}}\bigl(e^{Rx\_{\max}} - 1\bigr).$$

Combining observed share $f$ (with observed MGF $M\_{\mathrm{obs}}(R)$) and missing share $1-f$ yields the two-part ceiling $\bar{M}(R)$ covering every severity distribution consistent with $\rho\_{R,Y}$:
$$M\_X(R) \mkern5mu\le\mkern5mu f\mkern3muM\_{\mathrm{obs}}(R) \mkern5mu+\mkern5mu (1-f)\left[1 + \frac{\bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{mis}}}{x\_{\max}}\bigl(e^{Rx\_{\max}} - 1\bigr)\right],
\qquad \bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{mis}} = \bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{obs}} - \frac{\rho\_{R,Y}\sigma\_Y}{\sqrt{f(1-f)}}.$$

**Third, the resulting exponent.** Replacing $M\_X$ with $\bar M$ in Lundberg's equation defines conservative root $\bar R \le R$ solving $\lambda\bigl(\bar M(R) - 1\bigr) = cR$, keeping $\psi \le e^{-RB} \le e^{-\bar R B}$ valid. Implicit differentiation along $\rho\_{R,Y}$ gives:
$$\frac{d\bar R}{d\rho\_{R,Y}} \mkern5mu=\mkern5mu \frac{\lambda\mkern3mu(1-f)\mkern3mu\bigl(e^{\bar R x\_{\max}} - 1\bigr)\mkern3mu\sigma\_Y}{x\_{\max}\sqrt{f(1-f)}\mkern5mu\bigl(\lambda \bar M'(\bar R) - c\bigr)} \mkern5mu>\mkern5mu 0,$$

because $\lambda \bar M'(\bar R) - c > 0$ at the smallest positive root: safer $\rho\_{R,Y}$ raises $\bar R$, while adverse under-recording ($\rho\_{R,Y} < 0$) shrinks it.

Positive root existence tightens to $\lambda\mkern3mu\bar E[Y] < c$, where $\bar E[Y] = f\bar Y\_{\mathrm{obs}} + (1-f)\bar Y\_{\mathrm{mis}} = \bar Y\_{\mathrm{obs}} - \rho\_{R,Y}\sigma\_Y\sqrt{(1-f)/f}$ (reducing to $\bar Y\_{\mathrm{obs}}$ when $\rho\_{R,Y}=0$). Any suspected missingness bias raises the loading rate needed before any finite safety margin exists.

Evaluating the adverse boundary $\rho\_{R,Y} = -\bar\rho < 0$ yields ceiling mean $\bar E\_{-\bar\rho}[Y] = \bar Y\_{\mathrm{obs}} + \bar\rho\mkern3mu\sigma\_Y\sqrt{(1-f)/f}$. Setting net loading $\delta(-\bar\rho) = c - \lambda\bar E\_{-\bar\rho}[Y]$ to zero defines the **vital-margin breakdown correlation** $\rho^\star\_{R,Y}$ ($0 < f < 1$, $\lambda > 0$, $\sigma\_Y > 0$, $\bar Y\_{\mathrm{obs}} \in [0, x\_{\max}]$, $(x)\_+ = \max(0, x)$):

$$\rho^\star\_{R,Y} = \min\\!\left(1,\mkern4mu \frac{(c - \lambda\bar Y\_{\mathrm{obs}})\_+}{\lambda\sigma\_Y}\sqrt{\frac{f}{1 - f}}\right).$$

Whenever declared defect $\bar\rho \ge \rho^\star\_{R,Y}$, $\bar R(-\bar\rho)$ vanishes and required buffer $\ln(1/\varepsilon)/\bar R(-\bar\rho) + w\_B$ diverges—reversing apparent solvency $c > \lambda\bar Y\_{\mathrm{obs}}$ (while physical support $[0, x\_{\max}]$ caps the adverse missingness edge $\bar Y\_{\mathrm{mis}} \le x\_{\max}$ at $\rho\_{R,Y} = -\bar\rho \le 0$ via $\bar\rho\_{\mathrm{supp}} = \min\bigl(1,\mkern3mu \frac{x\_{\max} - \bar Y\_{\mathrm{obs}}}{\sigma\_Y}\sqrt{f(1-f)}\bigr)$).

**Fourth, the combined certificate.** Declare $\bar\rho \ge |\rho\_{R,Y}|$ (matching [Chapter 45](45-world-to-archive-big-data-paradox.md)) and size upper reserve $B\_U$ using lower reserve $B\_L$ and discounted exponent $\bar R(-\bar\rho)$:
$$\psi \mkern5mu\le\mkern5mu \exp\bigl(-\bar R(-\bar\rho)\mkern3muB\_L\bigr), \qquad
B\_U \mkern5mu\ge\mkern5mu \frac{\ln(1/\varepsilon)}{\bar R(-\bar\rho)} \mkern5mu+\mkern5mu w\_B,$$

adding reserve width $w\_B$ to the defect-discounted requirement. Expanding $\bar R(-\bar\rho) = R\_0\bigl(1 - (\bar R'(0)/R\_0)\bar\rho + O(\bar\rho^2)\bigr)$ around $R\_0 = \bar R(0)$ gives:
$$\frac{\ln(1/\varepsilon)}{\bar R(-\bar\rho)} \mkern5mu=\mkern5mu \frac{\ln(1/\varepsilon)}{R\_0}\left(1 + \frac{\bar R'(0)}{R\_0}\mkern3mu\bar\rho + O(\bar\rho^2)\right),$$

showing that selection bias $\bar\rho$ inflates physical buffer **linearly** ($\bar\rho^1$), whereas [Chapter 45](45-world-to-archive-big-data-paradox.md) showed it inflates sample size **quadratically** ($\bar\rho^2$): effective sample size scales as inverse variance while a ruin exponent scales as a rate, so buffering solely on epistemic counts under-reserves at first order in $\bar\rho$.

Notice when that linear expansion holds. Expanding around $R\_0 = \bar R(0)$ ($\rho\_{R,Y} = 0$) with baseline net loading $\delta\_0 = c - \lambda\bar Y\_{\mathrm{obs}} > 0$ (distinct from status discount $\delta\_{\mathrm{status}}$ and threshold $\delta\_{\min}$), $R\_0 \sim 2\delta\_0/(\lambda\bar M''(0))$ vanishes as $\delta\_0 \to 0^+$ while $\bar R'(0)$ stays finite ($e^{\bar R x\_{\max}} - 1 \sim R\_0 x\_{\max}$ cancels $\delta\_0$). Hence $\bar R'(0)/R\_0 \sim \lambda(1-f)\sigma\_Y/(\delta\_0\sqrt{f(1-f)})$ blows up as $1/\delta\_0$, restricting the linear regime to:
$$\bar\rho \mkern5mu\ll\mkern5mu \frac{\delta\_0}{\lambda(1-f)\sigma\_Y/\sqrt{f(1-f)}},$$

so near razor-thin replenishment margins ($\delta\_0 \to 0^+$) "first-order" defect inflation explodes earliest where buffers are already largest.

**Finally, three sanity checks.** (1) At full recording ($f = 1$), $1 - f = 0$ and the bound recovers the point Lundberg inequality. (2) At zero bias ($\bar\rho = 0$) with incomplete records ($f < 1$), missing shock means match observed means while shapes remain unbounded—honest "missing at random" leaves residual ambiguity in the exponent. Furthermore, $(f, \bar\rho)$ is physically admissible only while $0 \le \bar{Y}\_{\mathrm{obs}} - \rho\_{R,Y}\sigma\_Y/\sqrt{f(1-f)} \le x\_{\max}$, letting the archive falsify impossible defect claims. (3) The apparent naive-MAR ruin wall $c \downarrow \lambda\bar Y\_{\mathrm{obs}}$ shifts outward to the worst-case bound $c \downarrow \lambda\bar E\_{-\bar\rho}[Y]$ (higher by $\lambda(1-f)\bar\rho\mkern3mu\sigma\_Y/\sqrt{f(1-f)} = \lambda\bar\rho\mkern3mu\sigma\_Y\sqrt{(1-f)/f}$ at $\rho\_{R,Y} = -\bar\rho < 0$, bracketing true mean $\mu = \mathbb{E}[X\_i]$): the wall dominates any defect, yet the defect moves the required replenishment boundary outward.

## 6.4 Abraham Wald's (1943) Latent Survivorship Solver and Sniedovich's Maximin Safeguard

Two defenses against Knightian blindness govern how Filter II reconstructs unseen hazards before sizing reserves.

### 6.4.1 Abraham Wald's Latent Survivorship Inversion and Positive-Cone Guard

In 1943 Abraham Wald, at the Statistical Research Group at Columbia, asked where to armor Allied combat aircraft using damage seen only on returning planes (*Memo CRC 432*; Wald 1943; Mangel & Samaniego 1984). Wald's original wartime calculation worked from single-cohort hit rates under uniform exposure without multiple fatal hits.

Adapting that logic into a pedagogical **two-cohort linear specialization** ([Chapter 38](38-time-and-dependence.md)), let $j \in \lbrace 1, 2\rbrace$ index two mission cohorts. Write $S\_{j, A}, S\_{j, B}$ for returning survivor hit counts in zone $A$ (wings/fuselage) and zone $B$ (engine/cockpit), and $M\_j$ for missing aircraft in cohort $j$. Let $\boldsymbol{\theta} = (\theta\_A, \theta\_B)^T$ denote unknown vulnerability **odds** (not odds ratios) in each zone, where $\theta\_z = q\_z / (1 - q\_z)$ maps conditional lethality $q\_z = \mathbb{P}(\text{Lost}\mid\text{Hit}\_z) \in [0, 1)$ one-to-one onto $[0, \infty)$. Under single-hit fatal proportionality, missing counts obey the $2\times 2$ balance:
$$\begin{pmatrix} S\_{1, A} & S\_{1, B} \\\\ S\_{2, A} & S\_{2, B} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \theta\_A \\\\ \theta\_B \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} M\_1 \\\\ M\_2 \end{pmatrix},$$

matching missing counts to survivor hits weighted by each zone's lethality. Solving requires $\Delta = S\_{1, A}S\_{2, B} - S\_{1, B}S\_{2, A} \ne 0$ and **positive-cone admissibility**, $\mathbf{M} \in \mathrm{cone}(S\_{\cdot, A}, S\_{\cdot, B})$, forcing non-negative odds $\theta\_A, \theta\_B \ge 0$ so $q\_z = \theta\_z / (1 + \theta\_z) \in [0, 1)$ stays valid:
$$\begin{pmatrix} \theta\_A \\\\ \theta\_B \end{pmatrix} = \frac{1}{\Delta} \begin{pmatrix} S\_{2, B} & -S\_{1, B} \\\\ -S\_{2, A} & S\_{1, A} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} M\_1 \\\\ M\_2 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \Delta \ne 0, \quad \theta\_A, \theta\_B \ge 0.$$

Checking $\Delta \ne 0$ alone fails whenever noise yields invalid negative odds ($\theta\_z < 0$); when survivors record **zero** hits in zone $B$ ($S\_{1, B} = S\_{2, B} = 0$), $\Delta = 0$ and Wald's bound assigns maximum missingness mass straight to the silent blind spot (Wald 1943).

### 6.4.2 Info-Gap Robustness Guarded by Moshe Sniedovich's Global Wald Maximin

![Info-Gap Horizons and Sniedovich's Global Wald Maximin Safeguard](figures/fig-02-infogap-sniedovich.svg)

Yakov Ben-Haim's (2006) **Info-Gap decision theory** ranks policy choices $q\_{\mathrm{IG}}$ around a best-guess model $\tilde{u}$ by their local robustness radius $\hat{\alpha}\_{\mathrm{IG}}(q\_{\mathrm{IG}}, r\_c) = \max\lbrace \alpha\_{\mathrm{IG}} \ge 0 : \min\_{u \in \mathcal{U}(\alpha\_{\mathrm{IG}}, \tilde{u})} R\_{\mathrm{IG}}(q\_{\mathrm{IG}}, u) \ge r\_c \rbrace$ (subscripted to separate Info-Gap uncertainty radius $\alpha\_{\mathrm{IG}}$ and payoff $R\_{\mathrm{IG}}$ from Pareto exponent $\alpha$ and Lundberg's $R$ / stock $R\_t$). It asks how far uncertainty can grow around $\tilde{u}$ before payoff $R\_{\mathrm{IG}}(q\_{\mathrm{IG}}, u)$ drops below critical survival floor $r\_c$. Yet Moshe Sniedovich (2007, 2012) proved his **No-Man's-Land** theorem against local horizons. Option A can enjoy a wider local radius around $\tilde{u}$ ($\hat{\alpha}\_{\mathrm{IG}, A} > \hat{\alpha}\_{\mathrm{IG}, B}$) while collapsing completely inside the uninspected outer universe $U \setminus \mathcal{U}(\hat{\alpha}\_{\mathrm{IG}, A}, \tilde{u})$. Option B instead preserves a global **Abraham Wald (1945) Maximin** safety floor $\min\_{u \in U} R\_{\mathrm{IG}}(B, u) \ge r\_{\mathrm{Wald}}$ across all states in $U$.

That theorem explains why [Chapter 43](43-inquiry-openness-multiverse.md) rejected uninspected local Info-Gap horizons. Conversely, John Harsanyi warned that naive Wald Maximin over an unrestricted universe $U$ paralyzes choice by admitting arbitrarily wild fantasies. Stochology resolves both traps together: Filter II runs Maximin strictly over the **partially identified admissible set** inherited from Filter I ([Chapters 43](43-inquiry-openness-multiverse.md)–[45](45-world-to-archive-big-data-paradox.md)). Whenever even that bounded admissible set violates the survival floor, the operator does not fake infinite safety—it executes **Regime III** refusal (*Lowering the Bow*) and routes the gap to attested **$T\_1$ counterfactual reopening** ([Chapter 43](43-inquiry-openness-multiverse.md)).

## 6.5 The Two Ledgers, the Rational Corridor $(0, f^\ast]$, and Exemption Bounds

Both financial return ledgers and physical demand ledgers refuse naive ensemble averaging, because survival and accumulation run on different clocks. Take a return archive at baseline risk-free rate $r\_{\mathrm{rf}} = 0$ with positive mean $\mu > 0$ and volatility $\sigma > 0$, where Kelly's growth optimum is $f^\ast = \mu/\sigma^2$. Perturbing leverage by any offset $\Delta$ around $f^\ast$ produces identical time-average growth $g\_{\mathrm{time}}$:
$$g\_{\mathrm{time}}(f^\ast + \Delta) = g\_{\mathrm{time}}(f^\ast - \Delta) = g\_{\mathrm{time}}(f^\ast) - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2\Delta^2.$$

In plain words, betting above Kelly ($f \in (f^\ast, 2f^\ast]$) earns the exact same trajectory growth as its conservative mirror $\tilde{f} = 2f^\ast - f \in [0, f^\ast)$, while carrying strictly higher variance $f^2\sigma^2$ (raising log-variance rate by $4\mu\Delta > 0$). **Every stake above $f^\ast$ is therefore strictly dominated.** The rational non-ergodic corridor is **$(0, f^\ast]$** (or $[0, f^\ast]$ when abstaining)—never $(f^\ast, 2f^\ast]$.

Nor is the zero-growth boundary $f = 2f^\ast = 2\mu/\sigma^2$ safe or flat. While $g\_{\mathrm{time}}(2f^\ast) = 0$, log-reserve follows $d(\ln W\_t) = \frac{2\mu}{\sigma}\mkern3mudB\_t$, an un-drifted Brownian motion whose variance $4\mu^2 T / \sigma^2$ grows linearly with $T$ and hits every lower ruin floor $b \in (0, W\_0)$ almost surely by one-dimensional Brownian recurrence. Above $2f^\ast$, $g\_{\mathrm{time}}(f) < 0$ and reserves collapse toward zero almost surely.

On a daily physical demand ledger, provisioning near a modest upper quantile pushes utilization into Kingman's vertical wall while shrinking peak headroom, whereas sizing for surge peaks keeps routine load mild and preserves $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}}$. A single history cannot pool across alternate worlds it never enters, and a community ruined at its peak never lives to collect its mean. Reversion to ensemble expectation $\mathbb{E}[W\_T]$ is valid only when all three conditions of [Chapter 38](38-time-and-dependence.md) hold jointly: guaranteed survival across $T$, linear utility ($\Delta\_J = 0$; Samuelson 1971; Hulme et al. 2023), and zero spillover across external reception perimeters ($\Theta\_{\mathrm{basin}}$). Otherwise, survival buffer time, recovery delay, and path growth sit together in one audited table measured in units of time.

## 6.6 The Recovery Term Is Not One Number: Deployment Gates and Status Discounting

In §6.3.2, we broke effective recovery time into three stages: $\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t) = \tau\_{\mathrm{detect}}(\delta\_{\mathrm{status}}) + \tau\_{\mathrm{delib}}(\mathcal{I}\_t) + \tau\_{\mathrm{actuate}}$. Inside model-driven institutions, those stages map directly onto detection delay ($\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{detect}}$), model retraining ($\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{retrain}}$), and organizational release clearance ($\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{rollout}}$):
$$\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t) = \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{detect}} + \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{retrain}} + \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{rollout}},$$

In plain words, $\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{detect}}$ realizes warning latency $\tau\_{\mathrm{detect}}(\delta\_{\mathrm{status}})$, while retraining ($\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{retrain}}$) and rollout approvals ($\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{rollout}}$) consume deliberation and actuation time $\tau\_{\mathrm{delib}}(\mathcal{I}\_t) + \tau\_{\mathrm{actuate}}$. Three operational rules govern this sum:

1. **Without an armed fallback, detection is worthless.** If *The Community and the Reserve* lacks an immediate fallback arm when drift or drawdown hits, reserves keep draining during review, pushing $\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t) = +\infty$—Collapse Proposition 2 in pure form.
2. **Threshold tuning trades alarm latency against stationary false alarms.** Lowering a sequential alarm cutoff detects shifts sooner at the cost of higher false-alarm load, auditable only against a stationary baseline.
3. **Organizational release gates dominate algorithmic retraining speed.** Re-fitting model weights takes minutes; clearing committee sign-off, compliance queues (§6.3.2), and status rank barriers consumes the crisis clock.

Because survival time ticks off physical demand while recovery ticks off organizational clocks, status discounting ($\delta\_{\mathrm{status}} \in (0, 1)$, down-weighting frontline warnings) silently stretches $\tau\_{\mathrm{detect}}(\delta\_{\mathrm{status}})$ relative to its pre-authorized algedonic baseline ([Chapter 20](20-legitimacy-and-institutions.md); [Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md) §§8.2.2, 8.6.1). That stretch drains remaining detection headroom $T\_{\mathrm{rem}}^{\mathrm{detect}} := \mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} - (\tau\_{\mathrm{delib}}(\mathcal{I}\_t) + \tau\_{\mathrm{actuate}})$ before deferred committee reviews ever finish.

## 6.7 The Clock the Inequality Forgets

### 6.7.1 From Leverage Boundaries to Coupled Physical Reserves

In the rational corridor $(0, f^\ast]$, smooth diffusion leaves risk proportional to stake. Physical institutions, however, suffer discrete common-mode shocks that hit both reserve supply and liquidation speed at once—defeating static buffer formulas.

### 6.7.2 The reserve the shock owns

Section 6.3.2 treated reserve buffer $B \ge 0$ as immune to the crisis draining it. In practice, a correlated shock can wipe out impaired fraction $c\_{\mathrm{imp}} \in [0, 1]$ of the buffer when demand surges, shrinking effective survival time to $(1 - c\_{\mathrm{imp}})B/\dot{D}\_{\mathrm{crisis}}$ and forcing $B \ge \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)\mkern3mu\dot{D}\_{\mathrm{crisis}} / (1 - c\_{\mathrm{imp}})$ to sustain $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} \ge \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$. As $c\_{\mathrm{imp}} \to 1^-$, **$1/(1 - c\_{\mathrm{imp}}) \to \infty$**, and at $c\_{\mathrm{imp}} = 1$ no stockpile suffices unless common-mode vulnerability is severed beforehand.

The February 2021 Texas power crisis illustrates common-mode impairment. Post-event staff reports by the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission and the North American Electric Reliability Corporation documented widespread generating-unit outages and frozen natural-gas fuel lines across the grid rather than insufficient nominal MW capacity alone (FERC/NERC 2021). Buying more vulnerable capacity leaves $c\_{\mathrm{imp}}$ intact; lowering $c\_{\mathrm{imp}}$ requires winterizing plants and decoupling fuel supply from the same freeze driving peak heating demand.

Energetic and human reserves also show **recovery hysteresis**. Once deep depletion crosses a low-voltage disconnect (`LVD`) or acute caregiver burnout limit, restarting full load immediately at the cutoff causes destructive chattering. Safe recovery requires a strict wake margin $B\_{\mathrm{wake}} > B\_{\mathrm{cut}} > 0$ coupled with unwinding cumulative operator wear $W\_{\mathrm{allostatic}, t}$ across the sequential loop ([Chapter 42](42-sequential-loop-naive-agent.md)), ensuring premature restart does not drive impairment $c\_{\mathrm{imp}}$ toward $1$ before $\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$ ends.

### 6.7.3 Recovery time is a function of the position that needs it

Even deeper than common-mode impairment is endogeneity: recovery time $\mathrm{TTR}(B)$ grows with the reserve exposure $B \ge 0$ being unwound (Danielsson & Shin 2003). Stabilizing a stressed position from baseline clock $\mathrm{TTR}\_0 > 0$ by liquidating coupled assets $A(B) \ge 0$ into a market with stress- and congestion-dependent absorption $M(B, \mathrm{TTS}) > 0$ (which degrades under fire-sale crowding in $B$ or collapses under system-wide illiquidity) and price-impact sensitivity $\kappa > 0$ stretches recovery to:
$$\mathrm{TTR}(B) = \mathrm{TTR}\_0\left(1 + \frac{\kappa\mkern3mu A(B)}{M(B, \mathrm{TTS})}\right).$$

Comparing impaired survival time $(1 - c\_{\mathrm{imp}})B/\dot{D}\_{\mathrm{crisis}}$ against endogenous $\mathrm{TTR}(B)$ sets up a fixed-point race with three outcomes:

1. **Interior crossing:** a feasible crossing sits inside holding limit $B\_{\max}$ when $\mathrm{TTS}$ rises faster in $B$ than liquidation drag.
2. **No crossing:** $\mathrm{TTS}(B) < \mathrm{TTR}(B)$ across all $B \in (0, B\_{\max}]$—occurring whenever marginal liquidation drag $\frac{d}{dB}\mathrm{TTR}(B)$ stays at or above survival slope $(1 - c\_{\mathrm{imp}})/\dot{D}\_{\mathrm{crisis}}$ (such as superlinear $A(B)$, or steep linear slope $\mathrm{TTR}\_0 \kappa c\_A / M \ge (1 - c\_{\mathrm{imp}})/\dot{D}\_{\mathrm{crisis}}$), or when correlated fire-sale contagion drives market absorption $M(B, \mathrm{TTS}) \to 0^+$, forcing immediate ex-ante leverage cuts.
3. **Infeasible crossing:** the mathematical crossing lies above physical capacity $B\_{\max}$, which behaves operationally just like case (2).

Jón Danielsson and Hyun Song Shin (2003) proved this mechanism for endogenous market risk, and Markus K. Brunnermeier and Lasse Heje Pedersen (2009) derived the resulting margin spirals linking funding liquidity to asset liquidity. The 1998 LTCM unwind illustrates the trap vividly (Lowenstein 2000): synchronized deleveraging puts every firm's fragility inside everyone else's recovery clock. Treating $\mathrm{TTR}$ as an external constant hides the spiral that closes the exit door.

### 6.7.4 Discontinuous Jumps and Unenforceable Stop-Losses

Even without strategic runs, stop-loss orders assume prices move continuously enough for trades to execute along the way. As Benoît B. Mandelbrot (1963) proved for infinite-variance Paretian trajectories ([Chapter 38](38-time-and-dependence.md); [Chapter 46](46-non-identical-asymmetry-heavy-tails.md)), discontinuous jumps leap cleanly past intermediate stop-loss prices without filling. Reactive exit orders therefore push $\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$ past collapse unless an ex-ante vital buffer already stands ready before impact.

## 6.8 Structural Uncertainty, Nonrobustness, and Reflexive Brake 2

Across Filters I and II, deep parameter uncertainty meets physical ruin cliffs directly. When unknown variance or scale must be integrated out under finite data, Gaussian conditionals yield Student-$t$ predictive distributions with polynomial tails whose convergence to thin tails is pointwise rather than uniform (Weitzman 2007, eq. 20, p. 10; eq. 21, p. 11).

In Martin L. Weitzman's (2007, eqs. 38–39, pp. 16–18; Theorem 1, eqs. 42–44, p. 19) Dismal Theorem, let $M$ denote the stochastic discount factor and $\lambda > 0$ denote the value-of-life-style parameter fixing the positive minimum catastrophic consumption floor $D(\lambda) > 0$. As $\lambda$ grows large, expected discounting diverges without bound:
$$\lim\_{\lambda \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[M \mid \lambda] = +\infty.$$

In plain words, when extreme catastrophe cannot be bounded away from zero consumption, finite past data cannot stabilize standard expected-utility discount factors. Critics noted that capping utility eliminates literal infinities, yet Weitzman (2011) replied in his non-robustness section that `"Infinity is a side-show that has unfortunately diverted attention from the main issue"`—showing instead how conclusions stay acutely sensitive to tail assumptions.

Empirical benchmarks anchor that non-robustness warning (Weitzman 2011). Ice-core records across 800,000 years kept atmospheric $\mathrm{CO}\_2$ roughly between 180 and 280 ppm and `"has never been above 300 ppm"` before modern readings stood `"at about 390 ppm"`. Excess concentrations decay slowly after emissions stop, with roughly 70% remaining after 100 years and roughly 40% after 1,000 years above the 280 ppm pre-industrial baseline, while extreme warming of 11–12°C risks breaching the 35°C human wet-bulb thermoregulatory ceiling (Sherwood & Huber 2010). Across competing mixture models, Weitzman (2012, p. 10) further noted that the heaviest branch governs extreme risk because `"thinner-tailed child distributions are discounting their own relevance out of existence."`

Taken blindly as an unrestricted control rule, however, unbounded tail-expectation calculus fails just as badly as naive point certainty. Four adversarial boundary audits constrain Filter II (retaining each paper's local parameter notation):

- **Algebraic dominance, pooling error, and midcourse learning.** William D. Nordhaus (2011, pp. 247–248, 252, 254–255) showed that Dismal divergence requires relative risk aversion $b > 0$ to exceed Pareto tail exponent $a > 0$ by more than $1$ ($b > a + 1$), whereas natural earthquake loss distributions exhibit $a \approx 1$. Nordhaus criticized equal-weight pooling across 22 climate-sensitivity studies and noted that unbounded expected-loss logic would spend USD 4 million per year to erase a $10^{-8}$ annual asteroid risk. Crucially, Nordhaus (2011, p. 255) contrasted irreversible one-shot gambles—such as the 1945 Trinity test or high-energy collider thresholds—against slow-moving hazards where `"The Role of Learning"` enables sequential observation and midcourse correction before thresholds lock in.
- **Outcome tails versus marginal-utility explosions.** Robert S. Pindyck (2010, p. 4; pp. 8–9, note 12) separated `"two kinds of 'fat tails'"`: thick physical outcome tails versus exploding constant-relative-risk-aversion marginal utility near zero consumption. Under benchmark settings ($\eta = 2$, $\alpha = 4/3$, $\lambda = 1/2$), he capped marginal utility $MU$ at a cutoff temperature $T\_m$. A thin-tailed exponential temperature law then produced higher expected marginal utility $E[MU(T\_m)]$ than a heavy-tailed Pareto law up to nearly 10°C at multiplier $\mu = 1$ and up to 6°C at $\mu = 3$ (where exponential $E[MU(T\_m)]$ peaks exactly at $T\_m = 5$°C).
- **Prior fragility and correlated social vulnerability.** Antony Millner (2013, Prop. 1, p. 7; pp. 13–14; Thm 2, p. 22; eqs. 24–28, pp. 24–25) proved optimal total abatement share stays strictly interior, $a^\ast \in (0, 1)$, never exhausting output. He showed Dismal divergence disappears or returns depending on whether one chooses a maximum-entropy exponential prior or an improper $1/s$ scale prior. Bounded HARA utility removes individual divergence by muting sensitivity to tiny tail odds. Yet Millner (2013, eqs. 24–28, pp. 24–25) identified an irreducible social boundary: when shocks correlate across households, Arrow–Lind risk spreading breaks down and Harsanyi social welfare still diverges once joint threshold condition $c^\ast > 0$ is crossed.
- **Empirical tail measurement across integrated assessment models.** Testing the Dismal boundary across $100{,}000$ Monte Carlo runs in the Mimi framework, David Anthoff and Richard S. J. Tol (2021, Tables 1–3, 6, pp. 4, 6–10, 13) measured maximum-likelihood Pareto tail indices $\alpha$. They recorded $6.5847$–$18.5796$ in DICE and $3.224$–$3.578$ in PAGE, whereas equity-weighted FUND at a 0.1% pure rate of time preference dropped to $0.49929$–$0.66252$ (below $1$, leaving expected social cost of carbon undefined). Across $2{,}786$ published social-cost estimates from 148 studies, maximum-likelihood tail indices remain $1.0766$ (author-weighted), $1.2241$ (quality-weighted), and $1.2260$ (unweighted)—inside the infinite-variance regime $1 < \alpha \le 2$ and statistically indistinguishable from $1$ (Anthoff & Tol 2021, Table 6, pp. 4, 13).

Stochology resolves the clash between Weitzman's non-robustness and these four boundary audits through a clean split between **live step control** and **ex-post retrospective diagnosis** ([Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md)). Heavy-tail warnings and empirical indices near $\alpha \le 2$ prove that point expectations fail; they never convert an aggregate into an automated rule or let axes trade off numerically.

- **Online per-step control (Canonical Operator II):** Acting in *The Community and the Reserve* proceeds step by step across lexicographic Regimes I–III, immediately pre-empted by Step 1 survival halt / **Regime III-B (Algedonic Halt)** whenever $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} < \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$ (including queue-overloaded triage delays) or alarm channels fail ([Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md); [Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md); [Chapter 50](50-methodological-families-reflexivity.md)). When no feasible buffer clears the survival race inside $B\_{\max}$ or midcourse learning closes (Nordhaus 2011, p. 255), Canonical Operator II cuts exposure, fires armed fallbacks, or halts drain before impact—refusing Kornai's (1986) soft-budget disguise of exporting deficits outside the perimeter.
- **Strictly ex-post trajectory audit ($\mathcal{D}\_{\mathrm{diag}}$ and Reflexive Brake 2):** After a trajectory ends, co-diagnostic $\mathcal{D}\_{\mathrm{diag}} = (M\_{\mathrm{Leontief}}, \Theta\_{\mathrm{basin}})$ identifies which margin or external reception perimeter failed, while **Reflexive Brake 2** checks the strictly **ex-post** hoarding ceiling $R^\ast$ via pre-anchored $(\kappa\_{\mathrm{prec}}, c\_{\mathrm{urgent}})$ ([Chapter 50](50-methodological-families-reflexivity.md) §9.3.2)—auditing chronic starvation without rationing online slack while $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} < \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$ demands protection.
