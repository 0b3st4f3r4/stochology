## 8.1 Sosa's Virtue Epistemology, Outcome Bias, and Bitemporal Telic Evaluation

In *Moral Luck* (1979/1981), Bernard Williams and Thomas Nagel showed how moral judgment is corrupted by the stochastic outcome of an action after it leaves the agent's hands. Jonathan Baron and John C. Hershey (1988) then produced the same corruption in the laboratory: evaluators suffer a severe **outcome bias**, rating one and the same ex-ante medical or operational decision "competent" when the coin lands heads and "negligent" when an uncontrollable tail risk materializes.

### 8.1.1 Antipater's Archer: Télos vs. Skopós and Sosa's AAA Taxonomy

Outcome bias has to be overcome without collapsing into an ungrounded ethics of good intentions. Stochology formalizes the distinction **Antipater of Tarsus** drew (Stobaeus, *Eclogae* II.76.9–15): the archer (*toxótēs*) aims with consummate care (*télos*, the internal good of doing everything in one's power continuously and undeviatingly), and striking the external target is a separate matter (*skopós*, a *proēgmenon* or preferred indifferent).

Ernest Sosa (2007, 2015) formalized this distinction into his **AAA** (*Accuracy, Adroitness, Aptness*) virtue epistemology, and the operator grades a decision in four cells:

- **Inapt and inaccurate:** negligent ex-ante method and bad outcome;
- **Lucky accuracy only:** a negligent ex-ante method, say running with no buffer, that escaped disaster by luck: must **never** be rewarded with bonuses;
- **Adroit and unlucky:** rigorous ex-ante competence (*télos*) and vital buffering, hit by an exogenous tail shock ($L\_{\mathrm{exog}}$): must **never** be scapegoated;
- **Fully apt performance:** success achieved *because* of competence, or a reflective meta-competence that sees when epistemic weight $V$ is too low and suspends risky action for vital safeguards.

### 8.1.2 Cryptographic Commitment to the Ex-Ante State

To enforce the separation institutionally, the operator seals the Stage-1 evaluation of ex-ante competence $C(\pi\_t \mid \mathcal{I}\_{t\_{\mathrm{disp}}})$ inside a single-opening SHA-256 and RFC 2104 HMAC-SHA256 (Krawczyk, Bellare, & Canetti 1997) cryptographic commitment **before** the outcome $Y\_{t+1}$ is revealed. In the political philosophy of **Hannah Arendt (1958)**, this bitemporal pre-registration is the institutional translation of the **human promise**, which erects "islands of security" in an ocean of future uncertainty. **Simone Weil (1952 [1949])** grounds the refusal of cosmetic impunity when pre-registered pacts are betrayed.

Stochology draws the line hard between **natural crosswinds** and **adulterated air before the shot**. Resultant moral luck and Bernard Williams's no-fault reparative *agent-regret* apply **only** when the arrow is deflected by genuine exogenous contingency ($L\_{\mathrm{exog}}$) after an adroit shot. Where the informational and institutional medium was pre-corrupted by premise-rigging at the top (Simon 1997 [1947]) and retrospective scapegoating (Girard 1986 [1982]), there is no stochastic misfortune. There is deliberate fraud against the social pact.

That boundary defines the **pre-registration limit** of the cryptographic vault itself. A SHA-256 and HMAC-SHA256 commitment proves chronological integrity and non-renegotiation **from the moment the record is written** ($t\_{\mathrm{disp}}$); it cannot detect or prevent premise rigging, selective inclusion, or testimonial suppression that occurred **before** the ex-ante state $\mathcal{I}\_{t\_{\mathrm{disp}}}$ was sealed, because a valid MAC authenticates whatever premise set it is handed. The bitemporal seal is therefore a diagnostic boundary against post-outcome rewriting and a precondition of temporal integrity, never a substitute for pre-registration scrutiny: its reliability depends upstream on open framing in the inquiry transit—tracked via the Simon Premise Rigging Index ($\mathrm{PRI}$) of [Chapter 43](43-inquiry-openness-multiverse.md)—and on an undiscounted algedonic channel (§8.2.2) capable of contesting the record before it is locked.

## 8.2 List–Pettit Discursive Dilemma and Fricker's Testimonial Injustice on Beer's Algedonic Channel

Institutions do not think with a single brain. They aggregate distributed judgments across committees and hierarchies, and when the aggregation is structured naively they approve catastrophic deployments that every individual member would reject.

### 8.2.1 The List–Pettit Discursive Dilemma Matrix

Christian List and Philip Pettit (2011) generalized the legal *doctrinal paradox* into the **Discursive Dilemma**. Let deployment of an autonomous system require all three axes of the Nuclear Triad ($C \iff P\_1 \land P\_2 \land P\_3$), where $P\_1$ is Epistemic Margin, $P\_2$ is Vital Margin, and $P\_3$ is Institutional Governance. Three specialists vote:

| Voter / Specialist | Premise $P\_1$ (Epistemic) | Premise $P\_2$ (Vital) | Premise $P\_3$ (Governance) | Conclusion $C = P\_1 \land P\_2 \land P\_3$ |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Specialist 1 (Epistemologist)** | **True** | **True** | False | **False** (Veto: Gov broken) |
| **Specialist 2 (Physicist/Engineer)** | **True** | False | **True** | **False** (Veto: Vital broken) |
| **Specialist 3 (Governance/Ethicist)**| False | **True** | **True** | **False** (Veto: Epi broken) |
| **Premise-Majority Vote** | **True ($2/3$)** | **True ($2/3$)** | **True ($2/3$)** | **True ($2/3 \land 2/3 \land 2/3$)** |
| **Conclusion-Majority Vote** | — | — | — | **False ($0/3$ Approvals)** |

Every specialist identifies a fatal failure ($0/3$ conclusion approval). **Premise-by-premise majority voting** approves each axis ($2/3, 2/3, 2/3$) and launches the unsafe system. The operator therefore enforces a **constitutional conjunctive veto**, Stafford Beer's $S3^\ast$ sporadic audit channel, so that a detected discursive dilemma blocks deployment whenever any premise is refuted. The alternative is an institution whose aggregation rule is more permissive than every one of its members.

### 8.2.2 Miranda Fricker's Testimonial Injustice on Stafford Beer's Algedonic Channel

In Stafford Beer's (1972) Viable System Model, an **algedonic signal** (pain/distress $e\_t > 1$) bypasses bureaucratic hierarchy to trip the emergency circuit breaker before the Time-to-Survival Buffer ($\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}}$) runs out.

Miranda Fricker (2007) showed that hierarchical institutions inflict **testimonial epistemic injustice** by discounting the credibility of frontline workers, nurses, or marginalized residents (Fricker 2020). Management applies a status-based credibility discount $\delta\_{\mathrm{status}} \in [0, 1)$ to frontline alarms ($e\_{\mathrm{discounted}} = e\_t^{1 - \delta\_{\mathrm{status}}}$), and the shifts required to trip the emergency circuit breaker stretch from $\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{undiscounted}}$ to $\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{discounted}}$:

$$\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{undiscounted}} = \left\lceil \frac{\ln E\_{\mathrm{trip}}}{\ln e\_t} \right\rceil, \qquad \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{discounted}} = \left\lceil \frac{\ln E\_{\mathrm{trip}}}{(1 - \delta\_{\mathrm{status}})\ln e\_t} \right\rceil$$

The discount enters only the denominator, so latency grows as $1/(1 - \delta\_{\mathrm{status}})$ at fixed distress. An institution whose buffer covers the undiscounted latency loses the channel once the status discount pushes latency past the buffer. The alarm still arrives, and it arrives after the reserve is gone. Testimonial injustice is therefore a survival parameter and not a matter of tone, which is why §8.6.1 computes the critical discount instead of describing it. Setting $\delta\_{\mathrm{status}} = 0$ removes rank and credential discounting while leaving signal filtering to corroborated sequential likelihood ratios ($\prod\_{s \le t} e\_s \ge E\_{\mathrm{trip}}$) under the cognitive channel ceiling of §8.4.1.

## 8.3 Innes–Holmström–Milgrom Contracts and Chouldechova's Fairness Trilemma

How do contracts, incentives, and classifiers distribute risk and error across humans?

### 8.3.1 Multitask Distortion, Scapegoating, and the Bateson Double-Bind Trap

1. **Limited Liability, Risk-Shifting, and the Live-or-Die Contract:** Under limited liability an executive who captures upside bonus $B$ while truncating downside ruin loss $-L$ at zero has positive private expected value for a reckless gamble whose social expected value is deeply negative. Jensen and Meckling (1976) showed that this asset substitution follows from a limited-liability equity claim. Robert Innes (1990) proved the contract-theoretic half: under limited liability with ex-ante hidden effort, the constrained-optimal monotone contract takes a *live-or-die* (debt plus call option) form, the payoff the remedy has to flatten. Only **clawback escrow** $\gamma\_{\mathrm{clawback}}$ restores alignment; the escrow is this book's construction, not a result in either paper.
2. **Holmström–Milgrom (1991) Multitask Care:** For frontline workers performing countable volume tasks $a\_1$ and unmeasured care $a\_2$, high-powered piece rates $\beta\_1 \gg 0$ cannibalize care ($\Delta a\_2 < 0$). By Bengt Holmström's (1979) **Informativeness Principle**, the share of tail-deficit variance that arises from executive capacity sizing is attributed to structural leadership, and only the residue to frontline operational noise. The operator reports both shares so that the attribution can be checked.
3. **Simon–Girard Scapegoat Blame Transfer:** When executives rig decision premises at the top (**Herbert Simon 1997 [1947]**) and trigger **René Girard's (1986 [1982]) scapegoat mechanism** by assigning most of the blame downward, the audit reports a **scapegoat blame transfer** of
   $$\Delta\_{\mathrm{scapegoat}} = \max(0, b\_{\mathrm{frontline}} - L\_{\mathrm{oper}})$$
   the excess of the blame assigned to the frontline over the operational share it actually caused, together with the ratio between the two.
4. **Gregory Bateson's (1972/2000) Double-Bind Trap:** Combining proxy coercion ($\beta\_{\mathrm{proxy}, t} \to 1$), status-based testimonial silencing ($\delta\_{\mathrm{status}, t} \to 1$), and a disabled $S3^\ast$ brake ($\mathbf{1}\{\text{brake}\_t\} = 0$) locks operators into a double bind:
   $$\mathrm{DB}\_t = \beta\_{\mathrm{proxy}, t} \cdot \delta\_{\mathrm{status}, t} \cdot \bigl(1 - \mathbf{1}\{\text{brake}\_t\}\bigr)$$
   which vanishes under the full triad because the brake is on and the status discount is zero.

The nexus formalizes the degeneration of institutional authority into alienated *potestas* and sovereign capture. In the political analysis of [Chapter 20](20-legitimacy-and-institutions.md), decision-making power severed from the constituent *potentia* of those who bear the physical risks turns centralized command into an apparatus of capture. Unilateral premise-rigging, testimonial silencing and asymmetric limited liability let sovereign actors hoard private gains while externalizing catastrophic tail risks onto frontline operators and affected third parties. Genuine institutional governance replaces alienated command with horizontal algedonic entitlements, symmetrical liability, and pre-committed cryptographic covenants.

### 8.3.2 Proof of Alexandra Chouldechova's (2017) Algebraic Fairness Impossibility Theorem

An algorithmic classifier deployed across groups with unequal base rates $p\_A \ne p\_B$ is routinely promised simultaneous "fairness" across all criteria. Alexandra Chouldechova (2017) proved the promise impossible.

**Theorem (Chouldechova, 2017, Eq. 2.5):** Let $Y \in \{0, 1\}$ be the true outcome, $\hat{Y} \in \{0, 1\}$ the binary classification, $p = \mathbb{P}(Y = 1)$ the group base rate, $\mathrm{PPV} = \mathbb{P}(Y = 1 \mid \hat{Y} = 1)$, $\mathrm{FNR} = \mathbb{P}(\hat{Y} = 0 \mid Y = 1)$, and $\mathrm{FPR} = \mathbb{P}(\hat{Y} = 1 \mid Y = 0)$. For any binary classifier with $0 < p < 1$ and $\mathrm{PPV} > 0$:

$$\mathrm{FPR} = \frac{p}{1 - p} \cdot \frac{1 - \mathrm{PPV}}{\mathrm{PPV}} \cdot \bigl(1 - \mathrm{FNR}\bigr)$$

*Proof:* By Bayes' rule and the definition of conditional probability:
$$\mathbb{P}(\hat{Y} = 1, Y = 1) = \mathbb{P}(\hat{Y} = 1) \cdot \mathrm{PPV} = p \cdot (1 - \mathrm{FNR})$$
$$\implies \mathbb{P}(\hat{Y} = 1) = \frac{p(1 - \mathrm{FNR})}{\mathrm{PPV}}$$
By the law of total probability:
$$\mathbb{P}(\hat{Y} = 1) = \mathbb{P}(\hat{Y} = 1, Y = 1) + \mathbb{P}(\hat{Y} = 1, Y = 0) = p(1 - \mathrm{FNR}) + (1 - p)\mkern3mu\mathrm{FPR}$$
Equating both expressions for $\mathbb{P}(\hat{Y} = 1)$:
$$\frac{p(1 - \mathrm{FNR})}{\mathrm{PPV}} = p(1 - \mathrm{FNR}) + (1 - p)\mkern3mu\mathrm{FPR}$$
Subtracting $p(1 - \mathrm{FNR})$ from both sides:
$$(1 - p)\mkern3mu\mathrm{FPR} = p(1 - \mathrm{FNR})\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{PPV}} - 1\right) = p(1 - \mathrm{FNR})\left(\frac{1 - \mathrm{PPV}}{\mathrm{PPV}}\right)$$
Dividing both sides by $(1 - p)$ completes the proof:
$$\mathrm{FPR} = \frac{p}{1 - p} \cdot \frac{1 - \mathrm{PPV}}{\mathrm{PPV}} \cdot (1 - \mathrm{FNR}) = \mathrm{odds}(p) \cdot \frac{1 - \mathrm{PPV}}{\mathrm{PPV}} \cdot (1 - \mathrm{FNR}) \qquad \square$$

**Corollary (Fairness Impossibility):** If an imperfect classifier ($0 < \mathrm{PPV}\_g < 1$, $\mathrm{FNR}\_g < 1$) achieves predictive parity ($\mathrm{PPV}\_A = \mathrm{PPV}\_B$) and equal false negative rates ($\mathrm{FNR}\_A = \mathrm{FNR}\_B$) across two groups with different base rates $p\_A \ne p\_B$ in $(0, 1)$, the false positive rate ratio is strictly dictated by the base-rate odds ratio:

$$\frac{\mathrm{FPR}\_A}{\mathrm{FPR}\_B} = \frac{p\_A / (1 - p\_A)}{p\_B / (1 - p\_B)} = \frac{\mathrm{odds}(p\_A)}{\mathrm{odds}(p\_B)} \ne 1$$

No algorithmic engineering removes the disparity. An institution must publicly decide **who pays for classification errors**, under Thomas Scanlon's (1998) contractualist justification.

## 8.4 Zheng–Meister's Throughput Bottleneck, Cognitive Queues, and Gigerenzer's Natural Frequencies

Institutional accountability collapses when automated dashboards overwhelm the human operator.

### 8.4.1 The Two-Tier Cognitive Queue Under Channel Limits

Jieyu Zheng and Markus Meister (2025) put human behavioral and sensory-motor conscious throughput at roughly **ten bits per second**. Andrew Wong et al. (2021) documented in external validation across 27,697 hospitalised patients that the widely deployed Epic Sepsis Model achieved a sensitivity of **$33\%$** and a positive predictive value of **$12\%$** (from which the derived false-alarm proportion is $1 - \mathrm{PPV} = 0.88$, or $88\%$), and with it widespread alert fatigue.

The audit models the human as a server. An operator inspecting $B\_{\mathrm{audit}}$ bits requires cognitive service time $\tau\_{\mathrm{cog}} = B\_{\mathrm{audit}} / C\_{\mathrm{human}}$. The alert arrival rate fixes the cognitive utilization $\rho\_{\mathrm{cog}}$, and actuation latency against the crisis survival window completes the queue. Three panels follow from that one model:

1. **A synchronous summary panel** reporting only the few sufficient statistics the decision needs. Service time is short, cognitive utilization sits far below the Kingman ceiling, the queue contributes a small wait, and total recovery time stays inside the survival window.
2. **A standard epistemic card** reporting an intermediate number of scalars. Service time grows in proportion, utilization rises into the region where the queue term stops being small, and the total recovery time approaches the survival window without exceeding it.
3. **A full uncompressed audit** reporting every scalar. Service time grows past the point where cognitive utilization approaches one, Kingman's factor turns vertical, and total recovery time exceeds the survival window by an order of magnitude: Collapse Proposition 2 arrives through a cognitive queue and not a physical one.

The design conclusion is not "fewer statistics". The number of statistics an audit demands is a capacity decision with a latency consequence, and the sufficient statistics have to be chosen before the crisis and not during it.

### 8.4.2 Gerd Gigerenzer's Natural Frequency Trees Against Conditional Fallacies

Alerts are translated into Gerd Gigerenzer & Ulrich Hoffrage's (1995) integer natural frequency trees. Conditional probabilities in prevalence form are where base-rate neglect is easiest to commit, and frequency counts are where it is hardest:

- In a cohort of shifts with a small crisis prevalence $\pi$, the tree counts the true crises and the non-crises, then splits each by the detector's behaviour.
- A noisy detector with a high true-positive rate and a non-trivial false-positive rate rings on most crises and on a large share of the non-crises. The non-crises outnumber the crises, so the positive predictive value of an alarm collapses and the false-discovery share rises toward one. That is the arithmetic behind the derived false-alarm proportion from Wong et al.'s (2021) measured positive predictive value of $12\%$, and the reason alert fatigue is a property of prevalence and not of detector quality.
- The Turing–Good weight in decibans, $W = 10\log\_{10}(\mathrm{TPR}/\mathrm{FPR})$, makes the same point in the currency of evidence: a detector whose weight is small can be *accurate* per classification and useless per alarm.
- A throughput-filtered detector with a low false-positive rate raises the positive predictive value by an order of magnitude on the same prevalence, not because it detects more crises but because it stops reporting the non-crises. The gain is bought with missed crises, which is the trade the frequency tree makes visible.

## 8.5 The Gate on a Custody Archive

This chapter's gate reads a police race and identity-based data extract: recorded arrests, the decision to release or hold, and the covariates the record happens to carry. The archive holds decisions and not harms, which is what makes it the right test for a governance margin. The classifier question and the liability question are both about a rule the institution applied.

The fairness instrument runs in four strata, and the strata are the finding, not any single number. Full marginal base rates force one false-positive disparity under predictive parity and equal false-negative rates. Holding employment and citizenship identical narrows the base-rate gap and the forced disparity with it, without eliminating either. The intersectional poles, unemployed arrestees of one group against employed arrestees of the other, force the widest disparity of the four: Chouldechova's identity doing what it says. And an empirical rule read off the archive's own release decisions, with no target rate fixed, reproduces the ordering. There the identity stops being a hypothetical and becomes a description of an operating institution.

The second instrument prices testimony. With a distress factor and a survival buffer, an undiscounted algedonic channel trips the circuit breaker inside the buffer, and a substantial testimonial discount delays detection past it; the arithmetic is §8.2.2 and the critical discount is §8.6.1. The third instrument counts the audit's own cognitive cost: an uncompressed display drives the operator's queue past the ceiling, while a short synchronous panel keeps recovery inside the window. The fourth reports the contract terms — the structural share of the variance, the blame transferred downward, and the double-bind index — because those are the quantities a liability regime would have to move.

---

### The channel that records and the channel that hears

The channel works without discounting and fails with it. The same distress trips the breaker inside the buffer at zero discount, and past the buffer at a large one. The deficit is not a delay in the alarm. It is the difference between an institution that hears and one that records.

That distinction is what the two gates are for. An ex-post gate can always reconstruct a decision after the fact, and the reconstruction is where moral luck enters. The same decision looks apt if the outcome was favourable and reckless if it was not, unless the ex-ante state was sealed before the outcome existed. The seal converts luck into a variable the audit can hold constant.

The telic grade is the third object: a decision can be apt and fail, inept and succeed, and the four cells are named so that a promotion or a dismissal can be aimed at the cell rather than at the outcome. Without the names, the only cell an institution can see is the one that happened.

### When there is no third party

The telic evaluation is dispensable when the agent is single, no third party is affected and there is no public record to keep. The procedure is to declare the case and to replace the sealed commitment with a private, dated record: the machinery exists to protect someone who did not choose, and with no such person the record is enough.

### Two gates, two failure modes

The two gates fail differently, and the difference is what an audit is for. An ex-ante gate that is too tight refuses good decisions and leaves no trace, because the refusals never happen. An ex-post gate that is too loose accepts bad ones and leaves a record that reads like justification. The sealed commitment makes the ex-ante state readable after the fact, and the telic grade names which of the four cells the decision occupied. Without the names, the only cell an institution can see is the one the outcome made visible. Gates and grade together are the minimum that lets an institution punish a decision and not an outcome; without them the audit records luck.

## 8.6. Discounted Channels, Chosen Criteria, and Records That Do Not Discriminate

### 8.6.1 Two limits of the discounted channel

§8.2.2 prices testimonial injustice as latency, with two reachable ends.

Write the undiscounted latency $q\_0 = \ln E\_{\mathrm{trip}}/\ln e\_t$. The discounted quotient is $q(\delta) = q\_0/(1-\delta)$, and the latency is its ceiling, so latency scales as $1/(1-\delta)$, and the buffer an institution needs is $q\_0/(1-\delta)$ shifts. Set that equal to the buffer it holds, $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}}$, and the critical discount is
$$\delta^{\star} = 1 - \frac{q\_0}{\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}}}.$$
Below it the channel is an alarm channel; above it, a recorder. The transition is a threshold, not a slope.

The other end is $\delta\_{\mathrm{status}} \to 1$, where the denominator tends to zero and the latency diverges. No buffer covers this and no horizon is long enough for the number to mean anything. The limit of a status-discounted algedonic channel is an absent alarm, not a slow one, and the arithmetic makes the transition hyperbolic and not gradual. A status discount defended as modest is a decision about survival, not tone; conversely, eliminating status discounting ($\delta\_{\mathrm{status}} = 0$) recovers $q\_0$, while corroborated likelihood-ratio sequential filtering ensures that only alarms crossing $E\_{\mathrm{trip}}$ draw on the finite human throughput of roughly ten bits per second (Zheng & Meister 2025).

### 8.6.2 The criterion the algebra cannot choose

Chouldechova's identity holds, and its corollary says that across unequal base rates, predictive parity ($\mathrm{PPV}\_A = \mathrm{PPV}\_B$) and equal false-negative rates ($\mathrm{FNR}\_A = \mathrm{FNR}\_B$) force unequal false-positive rates for any imperfect classifier, so the institution must decide who pays for the errors. The form supplies no content for that decision, and it fails visibly in two steps.

The first is the sign of the label. Hold the numbers fixed, with $p\_A > p\_B$, predictive parity ($\mathrm{PPV}\_A = \mathrm{PPV}\_B$), and equal false-negative rates ($\mathrm{FNR}\_A = \mathrm{FNR}\_B$), so that Chouldechova's identity forces $\mathrm{FPR}\_A > \mathrm{FPR}\_B$ throughout. Let the positive label carry a burden (a detention, a flagged account, a revocation): a false positive is then an unwarranted harm, and the excess false-positive rate falls as a surcharge on the higher-base-rate group $A$. Let the positive label carry a benefit (a bed, a tutoring hour, a grant) at those identical numbers: the excess false positives in group $A$ become windfall allocations to non-qualifying members of $A$, while group $B$ receives fewer windfall benefits — or, if equal false-positive rates ($\mathrm{FPR}\_A = \mathrm{FPR}\_B$) are enforced alongside predictive parity instead, false-negative denials of the benefit concentrate in the higher-base-rate group (§8.3.2). One identity, one classifier, opposite welfare meaning. The arithmetic cannot say whether $\hat{Y} = 1$ harms or helps.

The second is the partition. The identity holds for every grouping variable, so the choice of which partition to report is substantive before it is mathematical. The same archive split by citizenship and split by race yields two different disparities from the same rule, and neither partition is *the* partition: a report naming one and not the other has made a political choice look like a measurement.

The recommendation to carry is the inversion the literature reached. Fix the thresholds by minimising the institution's own cost of error, and report the parity properties that follow. Do not choose a parity property and report the thresholds it dictates (Corbett-Davies et al. 2017). The direction is right because the cost of error is a fact about the institution and the parity property is its consequence.

### 8.6.3 When the sealed record does not discriminate

The ex-post gate rests on a claim about evidence: if the ex-ante state was sealed before the outcome, then apt-and-unlucky is distinguishable from inept-and-lucky, and liability can be aimed at the grade rather than at the outcome. The commitment delivers the sealing. It cannot deliver discrimination, and the condition is easy to state. Two strategies that generate the same sealed record are the same grade, whatever their authors intended.

The invalidation is a likelihood ratio. Let $H$ be that the operator was adroit and $E$ be everything in the sealed record. The grade is identified only if $\mathbb{P}(E \mid H) \ne \mathbb{P}(E \mid \neg H)$; when the two are equal, Bayes's odds identity yields a unit likelihood ratio (zero evidentiary weight) and the posterior returns the prior unchanged. Pre-registration is necessary for identification and not sufficient. That is the part a compliance regime assumes away.

The negative-pressure test on the Macondo well in April 2010 is the dated instance. The test was run and the readings were recorded. The dispute after the blowout was about what the record showed: whether the pressure behaviour was consistent with a competent interpretation, or only looked so in hindsight (National Commission 2011). The record existed and was not sealed as this chapter requires. Even sealed, it would have left the two hypotheses predicting the same numbers on the page. No commitment scheme repairs that. The repair is upstream, in a test whose hypotheses predict different observations.

The procedure makes ex-ante distinguishability a precondition of the ex-post gate. Before the outcome, name the observation that would separate competent from incompetent performance. Where none can be named, record the case as undetermined and allocate liability by institutional design (who chose the option set, who set the clock) and not by the agent's grade. A commitment that stores an indistinguishable record converts moral luck into administrative luck, which is a change of venue, not a remedy.
