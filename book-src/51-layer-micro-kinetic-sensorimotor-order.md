## By the end of this chapter you can

1. Separate what the agent issued from what the environment returned, in a loop whose period is shorter than the time needed to reconsider the
   command.
2. Compare the slack before an absorbing boundary with the time required to recover, and decide from that comparison alone whether the operating
   point is admissible.
3. Name the one quantity a fast loop must never optimise directly, and say why maximising it guarantees the boundary is reached.

## 1. Two times, one inequality

The state evolves faster than the controller can deliberate. Measurement-to-actuation delay dominates, and the horizon is
absorbing: a state can be entered from which no later correction exists. Slack is the distance to the boundary over the rate consuming it; recovery is
the time to restore the margin once eaten. The admissible region is where slack exceeds recovery, and it is computable
before the run.

$$d\_{\mathrm{stop}}(v,\mu) = v\mkern3mu\tau\_{\mathrm{react}} + \frac{v^{2}}{2\mu g} = v\mkern3mu\tau\_{\mathrm{react}} + \frac{v^{2}}{2g}\cdot\frac{1}{\mu}$$

The friction term carries the coefficient in a denominator, a loss of grip enters reciprocally. A small deficit in adhesion buys a
larger proportional increase in stopping distance, and that amplification is what a dry-surface margin cannot price. Reaction latency dilutes the
penalty without removing it, since it adds a term that is indifferent to the surface; a margin calibrated on a dry road therefore carries no
information about a wet one.

The two clocks the order is named for are not two distances. Write the slack before the boundary as $s$ and the rate at which the chosen command
consumes it as $c$, so that

$$\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} = \frac{s}{c}.$$

Recovery is the time the surface needs to restore the state it has been pushed out of, and it enters in one of two forms. Where the restorative
authority is the same channel that is being spent: the tyre that turns is the tyre that must stop, the actuator that accelerates is the one that
must decelerate, and recovery proceeds at a rate $r$ proportional to the same deficit, $\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{recovery}} = s/r$, and the inequality
cancels the slack:

$$\frac{s}{c} < \frac{s}{r} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c > r.$$

That cancellation is the finding. The size of the margin does not appear in the condition of admissibility. It appears only in how long
the agent has to discover that the condition fails. A larger slack therefore buys time and never buys admissibility, and an agent that answers a
marginal violation by raising the reserve has answered a rate problem with a distance. Where recovery instead has a lead time of its own, say a
replacement, a repair crew, a committee — the second clock is a constant $T\_{0}$ and the condition becomes linear in the command:

$$s > c\mkern3mu T\_{0}.$$

Then the clearance required scales with the rate, so doubling the speed doubles the distance that must be held. The two regimes are why a margin
quoted without its clock cannot be read. The same clearance is generous under one and short under the other, and any table that reports a clearance
has already chosen which regime it assumes.

## 2. Two tapes

The command tape is what the agent issued: declared bounds, provisioned capacity, the reserve allocated, the brake applied or withheld.
The response tape is what the environment returned: realised load, realised harm, whether the boundary was crossed. Scoring on the response tape
alone teaches that the cheapest way to look good is to be lucky. Failure is rare, so the score improves while the exposure grows. The check is
whether a good response was caused by a good command; where it was not, the credit is void.

The check must inspect the syntax of the command tape. By Rice's theorem (1953) every non-trivial semantic property of a program's future
execution is undecidable, so a supervisor waiting to verify what a black-box policy will do cannot terminate before the horizon. The decidable
alternative is a conservative syntactic certificate over declared premises, verified in linear time before actuation; Girard's linear logic (1987)
supplies the accounting, since a finite reserve and a single-opening pre-outcome record are linear resources, spent once and never duplicated. The
certificate is blind wherever the tape is not observable before the act, or the boundary after it.

## 3. The model inside the loop

A controller whose delay budget is shorter than its deliberation budget thinks inside the model. It compresses the observation into a latent state,
predicts the next latent under the chosen action, and trains a small policy inside itself (Ha & Schmidhuber 2018). Discrepancy grows with distance from the fitted support,
so an optimiser inside the model finds the direction of largest cheap improvement, which is where the model is wrong.

The structure can be laid out without any apparatus: a linear surrogate fitted on anchors inside a support radius, a policy optimised within it,
and the same actions executed against a world carrying one absorbing constraint the surrogate never saw. The greedy dreamer leaves the support,
destroys the world on the way, and its dreamed return stays a multiple of the realised one, because the surrogate was never asked about the
constraint that ends the episode. This is the adversarial form of Goodhart's law. The policy maximises the proxy it can see, and the proxy is the
model. Raising the exploration temperature converts that committed exploit into an exploratory trajectory and collapses the terminal discrepancy,
and it certifies no model either. The surviving trajectory survives by never committing.

## 4. The margin belongs to the surface

The same audit can be posed on a point-mass vehicle driven around a fixed track under the vector constraint
$a\_{\mathrm{long}}^{2} + a\_{\mathrm{lat}}^{2} \le (\mu g)^{2}$. A model-based controller plans on a friction coefficient identified on the deployed
surface with a safety margin, and completes the lap with no violation. A policy tuned by search inside a drier simulator loses grip, and the same
policy fitted to demonstrations of the model-based controller loses it too, only later: a regressor fitted on the visited distribution has no reason
to be right at its edge. A small friction deficit destroys the model-based lap as well: the margin was never a property of the controller.

## 5. Where the advice is wrong

The form recommends one thing: increase slack. Slack grows as the longitudinal command approaches the limit, and the growth is a circle, not a line. At the
friction limit the lateral authority left over is $a\_{\mathrm{lat}} = \mu g\sqrt{1 - u^{2}}$ at longitudinal utilisation $u$, so a utilisation
scored as excellent leaves a steering authority that a lateral displacement of a few metres inside a one-second crossing at motorway speed exhausts.
The point the score rewards cannot avoid the other boundary at all, so the criterion must be vector-valued: refuse a point when the smaller remaining
authority falls below its requirement.

Both sides were taken as given. When reaction latency depends on the operating point, since a faster loop allocates less attention per
decision, and the boundary moves with the command that consumes it, the inequality becomes a fixed-point condition that may hold everywhere,
nowhere, or on a set that is not an interval. The procedure is to measure latency across the range, test at the upper end, not at the mean,
and report the interval beside the curve that produced it.

## 6. T1Framing: what the surface counts as a state

A sensorimotor surface answers three questions before it measures anything, and the three are one answer: what counts as the state, at what
resolution, and against which intended act. None of them is read off the world. A hand moving toward a cup is a continuum of positions, velocities
and contact forces, and which of these is "the state" depends on what the loop is trying to hold: endpoint error if the act is reach, a force
profile if the act is grasp, a rate of change if the act is catch. The act picks the variable, the variable picks the resolution, and the resolution
picks what a measurement can be a measurement of.

That the declaration precedes the count is Ashby's (1956) point, not a gloss on it. Variety is the logarithm of the number of distinct states
a regulator can distinguish, so the partition is already in place before any variety can be counted; two controllers on the same limb, one counting
joint angle and one counting endpoint error, regulate different objects and their varieties are not comparable quantities. The state is declared and
not discovered, and the declaration is a judgment about a whole situation and not a reading of one of its parts. Vickers's (1965) account of
regulation is one in which an appreciator judges a situation before committing to any part of it.

The resolution has a budget, and the budget is not effort. Zheng and Meister (2025) put conscious behavioural and sensory-motor throughput near
$10\text{ bits/s}$, so a state declared at a resolution finer than the channel can carry is not a finer controller; it is a controller that drops
frames and reports the drop as a state. What survives the budget is what the act made relevant: a tolerance set by what the loop can still correct.
The framing question and the temporal question are the same question asked in two vocabularies.

The cost of getting it wrong is exact, and it is not a modelling error. On the friction surface of §4, an act with a wide tolerance licenses a coarse
state, and at that resolution a small adhesion deficit is a sub-cell difference: the surface reports no change, the loop corrects the error it counts,
and the ratio of §1 is never formed because the rate at which the margin is being spent is not among the variables the state carries. A model can be
exact on the declared state and the declaration can still be the thing that fails, and the failure is invisible from inside, because a variable that
was never declared cannot be reported as missing.

## 7. T7Liability: who carries the reserve the load chooses

In the vocabulary of liability a margin is a quantity one party pays for and another consumes, and the fast loop makes the split unusually clean
because the three roles sit in three places. The party that sets the load chooses the speed, the schedule or the utilisation target. The surface
carries the authority that absorbs the error. The party inside the loop — occupant, patient, pedestrian — carries the consequence. The margin is
sized at the second, chosen at the first, and paid at the third.

The agency form is standard. Jensen and Meckling (1976) split the cost of an arrangement in which one party acts for another into monitoring,
bonding, and a residual loss that survives both, and the residual falls on the party who did not act. In a loop whose period is shorter than its
deliberation budget (§3) the residual cannot be monitored at the moment of the act, because there is no interval in which a supervisor could look.
What is observable is the specification written before the run, and the specification is the load-setter's own document. Power (1997) described the
general shape of that substitution, that what can be verified is what gets produced; here the verified object is the declared clearance, which is
precisely the number the load-setter chose.

Limited liability sharpens the arithmetic without requiring bad faith. A party whose exposure is truncated at the value of its own assets provisions
against the truncated loss, and since a smaller loss buys a smaller reserve at every margin, the difference between the two reserves is exactly the
part of the loss the other party carries; Innes (1990) is the general statement. The legal form is Hand's (1947) comparison of the burden of
precaution against the probability and gravity of the harm, and the fast loop rearranges its partition: the burden is spent on every trip while the
harm is realised once, so the party holding both numbers on its own books compares a certain recurring cost with a rare external one and will find
the precaution expensive each time it is taken.

What remains after the arithmetic is a question of standing, not of size. Frick (2015) states the test — a risk is justifiable only to those
who bear it — and the bearer here is the party who was absent when the utilisation target was set, so a certificate of adequacy issued by the
load-setter has been issued by the one party whose interest runs against the margin. Because the loop is faster than deliberation, the decision
cannot be moved to the instant of the act; it can only be moved in time, to the declared margin before the run or to the record after it. Both are
written by the party that chose the load: the margin as a specification before the run, the record in the form that specification defines. That is
the order's liability finding. In a loop too fast to audit, the payer is structurally absent from the two places where the decision is written down.

2. **Dreaming against the surface claim:** say why raising the exploration temperature repairs the dream exploit
   and leaves the friction deficit untouched, and which of the two failures a syntactic certificate over declared premises can see.
3. Reproduce the stopping distance symbolically at two friction coefficients, and say whether a dry-surface margin with a fixed slack is still
   admissible on wet asphalt (answer: the friction term enters as $1/\mu$, so a small adhesion deficit is amplified into a deficit larger than the
   slack, and the dry margin is short).

   the ancestor cone.
5. **The declaration before the measurement.** Declare the state of one limb twice — as endpoint error, then as the rate at which contact force
   changes — and say which declaration makes the ratio of §1 computable inside the loop, and which of the two a clearance specification is able to
   certify.
