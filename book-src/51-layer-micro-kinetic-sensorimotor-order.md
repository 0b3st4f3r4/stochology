## By the end of this chapter you can

1. Separate the issued command tape (*télos*) from the environmental response tape (*skopós*) in a sensorimotor loop whose period is shorter than conscious deliberation.
2. Compare time-to-survival against effective recovery time—distinguishing concurrent rate balance from finite-latency kinetic excursions—and explain why temporal clearance is necessary rather than sufficient without framing (`T1`) and liability (`T7`).

## 1. T5Temporal: two times, one inequality

Imagine riding a bicycle down a damp street when a parked car door swings open ahead. Your eyes spot the door, your nerves fire, and your tires grip wet asphalt—all before you could finish forming a deliberate sentence.

That split-second stop is the everyday world of micro-kinetic control. Here, $T\_5$ (`T5Temporal`) protects the live race between how fast physical headroom disappears and how quickly perception, command, and braking act. Because the physical state moves faster than conscious planning, a late correction cannot rescue a wheel or limb once it crosses an irreversible boundary.

Measure that stopping margin directly. On level ground, let a rider or vehicle move at speed $v \ge 0$ over a surface with positive friction coefficient $\mu > 0$ under gravitational acceleration $g > 0$. With a perception-reaction delay $\tau\_{\mathrm{react}} \ge 0$, the reaction distance $d\_{\mathrm{react}}(v)$ and braking distance $d\_{\mathrm{brake}}(v,\mu)$ sum to the total stopping distance $d\_{\mathrm{stop}}(v,\mu)$:

$$d\_{\mathrm{stop}}(v,\mu) = v\mkern3mu\tau\_{\mathrm{react}} + \frac{v^{2}}{2\mu g} = d\_{\mathrm{react}}(v) + d\_{\mathrm{brake}}(v,\mu).$$

In plain words, total stopping distance adds the ground covered while you react to the ground needed for your brakes to shed kinetic energy. Because friction $\mu > 0$ sits in the denominator of $d\_{\mathrm{brake}} = v^{2}/(2\mu g)$, losing grip stretches braking non-linearly.

Suppose a slick surface cuts friction by a fraction $\delta \in (0, 1)$, reducing $\mu$ to $(1 - \delta)\mu$. For any positive speed $v > 0$, braking distance grows by $\delta/(1 - \delta) > \delta$—so halving grip doubles braking length. Total stopping distance ($d\_{\mathrm{stop}} > 0$) rises by $\frac{d\_{\mathrm{brake}}}{d\_{\mathrm{stop}}}\frac{\delta}{1 - \delta}$, cushioned by the friction-free reaction term $d\_{\mathrm{react}} = v\tau\_{\mathrm{react}}$, and exceeds $\delta$ once $\delta > d\_{\mathrm{react}}/d\_{\mathrm{stop}}$.

Field measurements across road and trail users confirm how sharply vehicle type and wet surfaces split these margins. Landis, Petritsch, & Huang (2004) measured bicycle perception-reaction times averaging 0.9 s (SD 0.7 s, 85th percentile 1.3 s, $N = 130$) compared with 1.2 s (SD 0.8 s, 85th percentile 2.3 s, $N = 14$) for kick scooters.

Dry braking tests diverged just as sharply across modes (Landis, Petritsch, & Huang 2004). Measured dry braking distances and calculated friction factors shifted from $f = 0.32$ on bicycles (mean 4.8 m, 85th percentile 7.0 m) to $f = 0.09$ on kick scooters (mean 4.9 m, 85th percentile 8.9 m). Under halved wet-pavement friction at an 85th-percentile speed of 29 km/h (18 mi/h), calculated recumbent-bicycle stopping sight distance reached 32.7 m (107 ft), against AASHTO's 38.7 m (127 ft) uniform bicycle standard at a 30 km/h (20 mi/h) design speed (Landis, Petritsch, & Huang 2004). A safety margin calibrated on dry pavement or one vehicle type therefore underprices wet surfaces and slower-reacting modes.

Distance alone still misses the core contest: `T5Temporal` compares two clocks rather than two lengths. Write $s > 0$ for the positive headroom remaining before impact, and $c > 0$ for the rate at which the chosen command consumes that headroom. Dividing the two defines **time-to-survival**:

$$\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} = \frac{s}{c}.$$

Read aloud, time-to-survival is simply how long you can keep going before hitting the boundary if nothing intervenes.

Restoring control is rarely instantaneous. Remaining headroom $s > 0$ can differ from the excursion deficit $D \ge 0$, and hardware pays delay $\tau\_0 = \tau\_{\mathrm{detect}} + \tau\_{\mathrm{delib}}(\mathcal{I}\_t) + \tau\_{\mathrm{actuate}} \ge 0$ ([Appendix A](appendix-a-mathematics.md)) before restoring at rate $r > 0$. Summing both gives effective recovery time $\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}} = \tau\_0 + D/r$, splitting physical admissibility into two clock regimes:

1. *Concurrent shared-channel rate balance ($D = s$, $\tau\_0 = 0$, or continuous net depletion):* When the actuator restores size $s > 0$ at rate $r > 0$ with zero switching delay ($\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{recovery}} := \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}} = s/r$), or drains at net rate $c - r > 0$, $s$ cancels:

$$\frac{s}{c} < \frac{s}{r} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c > r.$$

   If demand outpaces restoration ($c > r$), a larger initial buffer buys warning time ($s/c$ or $s/(c - r)$) without curing the steady-state deficit.

2. *Finite-latency or excursion-limited recovery ($\tau\_0 > 0$ or $D \ne s$):* Under positive sensing-to-actuation delay $\tau\_0 > 0$ or $D \ne s$ (as in braking across clear sight distance), $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} \ge \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}$ becomes

$$s \ge c\left(\tau\_0 + \frac{D}{r}\right).$$

   Required physical slack grows directly with command speed $c$ times total recovery duration: extra space genuinely buys admissibility for finite stops.

Any clearance table omitting which regime applies cannot be verified, and $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} \ge \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}$ certifies only the survival clock (`T5Temporal`), never admissibility without framing (`T1`) and liability (`T7`).

## 2. Two tapes: *télos* and *skopós*

Physical control splits what you intended from what the road did. The command tape (*télos*) records what the controller actually issued: declared state bounds, capacity, reserve allocated, and whether a brake was engaged. The contingency response tape (*skopós*) records what the environment returned: realized grip, external gusts, and whether the barrier was crossed ([Appendix B](appendix-b-architecture-verification.md)).

Scoring on *skopós* alone teaches a dangerous lesson: when crashes are rare, cutting margins looks efficient until luck runs out. Because the acceptance predicate is non-commuting, it audits *télos* and the pre-set guard margins independently of *skopós*. A lucky swerve cannot excuse an unbuffered command, and a well-buffered action struck by an unforeseeable shock is never confused with negligence.

Pre-actuation audits face a strict speed limit: checking *télos* must finish inside the survival clock. Rice's theorem (1953) proves that no universal runtime check can decide non-trivial semantic behavior for arbitrary programs, so an unbounded proof stalls past $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}}$. Fast loops rely instead on a linear-time syntactic certificate over single-use resources, accounting finite reserves and single-opening seals via Girard's linear logic (1987) and the grammar recognizer of [Layer 06](56-layer-formal-computable-type-order.md), while leaving world framing (`T1Framing`) and physical time (`T5Temporal`) to their own guards.

## 3. The model inside the loop

When human deliberation is too slow for real-time action, designers often train controllers inside an internal forward model. In Ha and Schmidhuber's (2018) recurrent world-model architecture, an encoder compresses images into a latent state, a density model predicts transitions under sampling temperature $\tau$, and a compact controller learns inside that simulated dream.

Because simulator accuracy fades outside observed states, an optimizer exploits simulator glitches as cheap rewards. On a simulated cover-taking task, setting near-deterministic sampling ($\tau = 0.10$) let the controller score $2086 \pm 140$ inside its virtual dream, yet it crashed to $193 \pm 58$ in the real environment—worse than a random policy's $210 \pm 108$ (Ha & Schmidhuber 2018). Raising simulator sampling temperature to $\tau = 1.15$ introduced enough noise during offline training to curb exploitation, aligning performance at $918 \pm 546$ virtual versus $1092 \pm 556$ actual (Ha & Schmidhuber 2018).

Notice carefully where that noise belongs: temperature $\tau$ regulates offline simulator training (`T1Framing`), never live physical actuation. Injecting random dithering into a live actuator near a cliff raises state variance and pushes the system closer to ruin. An internal model sharpens anticipation inside its calibrated domain, yet it adds zero road friction and buys zero extra milliseconds before impact.

## 4. Surface margin, the friction circle, and takeover latency

Under the circular contact constraint $a\_{\mathrm{long}}^{2} + a\_{\mathrm{lat}}^{2} \le (\mu g)^{2}$ ($\mu, g > 0$), physical headroom lives at the tire-road interface and human handoff—not inside software weights. Field data from early public-road testing illustrate that gap starkly.

Across 460,097 public-road autonomous miles reported to California DMV from September 2014 through November 2015 (Google 424,331 miles, 92%), disengagement rates spanned 1.1 per 1,000 miles for Google (0.18 late period) to 980 for Mercedes-Benz, with 13 of 69 replayed manual Google disengagements collision-bound and 56 safety-critical (Dixit, Chand, & Nair 2016). Twelve crashes occurred (10 in that window plus Cruise Automation on 8 January 2016 and the lone autonomous-at-fault Google crash on 14 February 2016), tracking cumulative miles ($r = 0.98, p < 0.01$; monthly $0.73, p < 0.01$): Google logged 1 crash per 47,148 miles and 0 fatalities versus 1 per 108 million miles in 2014 CHP highway records, though comparing comprehensive low-speed DMV logs against severe highway CHP records (1 per 2.07 million miles) breaks `T1Framing` and `T3Archive` (Dixit, Chand, & Nair 2016).

Geometry shows why pushing a single performance score erases steering reserve. Define scalar longitudinal utilization as $u = \lvert a\_{\mathrm{long}}\rvert / (\mu g) \in [0, 1]$. Along the circular tire grip limit, remaining lateral steering authority $a\_{\mathrm{lat}}(u)$ satisfies:

$$a\_{\mathrm{lat}}(u) = \mu g\sqrt{1 - u^{2}}, \qquad \frac{da\_{\mathrm{lat}}}{du} = -\frac{\mu g u}{\sqrt{1 - u^{2}}} < 0 \quad \bigl(u \in (0, 1)\bigr).$$

As braking approaches full grip ($u \to 1^{-}$), remaining steering authority drops toward zero and plunges vertically along the circle. Under symmetric bang-bang lateral kinematics ($\Delta y = 2 \cdot \frac{1}{2} a\_{\mathrm{lat}}^{\mathrm{req}}(\Delta t/2)^{2}$) over preview time $\Delta t = d/v > 0$ at sight distance $d > 0$ and speed $v > 0$, clearing offset $\Delta y > 0$ demands $a\_{\mathrm{lat}}^{\mathrm{req}} = 4\Delta y/\Delta t^{2} = 4\Delta y\mkern2mu v^{2}/d^{2}$ (or $2\Delta y/\Delta t^{2}$ under one-way acceleration). Admissibility must therefore reject any command whose smallest directional margin falls below demand.

Human handoff speed also slows with familiarity: across $n = 652$ pooled system failures excluding perception time, alert-to-takeover latency averaged 0.84 s (SD 0.82 s) without significant differences by road type or trigger, yet drifted upward with cumulative miles for both Mercedes-Benz ($0.122, p = 0.007; n = 487$) and Google ($0.161, p = 0.029; n = 165$) (Dixit, Chand, & Nair 2016). Viability $T\_{\mathrm{avail}}(u) \ge \tau\_{\mathrm{react}}(u)$ therefore demands measuring upper-tail latency and publishing its interval beside the response curve.

## 5. T1Framing: what the surface counts as a state

Here, $T\_1$ (`T1Framing`) protects what counts as a surface state, at what resolution, and relative to which intended act before any sensor logs a single bit. A hand reaching for a cup moves through infinite positions, speeds, and contact forces. Whether "the state" means endpoint gap, grip force, or slip velocity depends on whether the act is reach, grasp, or catch—so the intended act picks the variable, and the variable fixes what faults stay invisible.

Ashby (1956) established that counting variety—whether as distinct elements or in base-2 *bits*—already presupposes a chosen state partition. Two controllers on the same arm, one tracking joint angles and another tracking endpoint error, regulate distinct partitions that cannot substitute for each other (Ashby 1956). Choosing that partition is an appreciative judgment of the whole situation before measuring any piece (Vickers 1965).

Biological bandwidth makes that framing cut sharper. While human peripheral nerves collect roughly $10^{9}\text{ bits/s}$, conscious behavioral and sensorimotor throughput saturates near $10\text{ bits/s}$ (Zheng & Meister 2025). Conscious attention therefore drops frames if forced to steer micro-kinetic adjustments directly, leaving millisecond control inside $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}}$ to reflexes, surface compliance, and task tolerances. Yet declaring too coarse a tolerance in §4 turns early tire slip into an unrecorded sub-cell residual: the dashboard stays green while grip bleeds away unmonitored.

## 6. T7Liability: who carries the reserve the load chooses

Here, $T\_7$ (`T7Liability`) guards against moral hazard when the load-setter chooses speed or utilization, the surface absorbs error, and occupants, cyclists, or pedestrians bear crash harm. Under Jensen & Meckling's (1976) agency decomposition, a 0.84 s takeover window (Dixit, Chand, & Nair 2016) leaves no time for live external auditing, shifting oversight onto pre-run rules authored by the load-setter (Power 1997).

Limited liability sharpens that split without requiring malice: capping downside at firm assets $A$ halts precaution against truncated loss (Innes 1990), breaking Hand's (1947) balance $B < PL$ because routine slack hits the operator while rare harm lands outside. Frick's (2015) *ex ante* contractualism (*Decomposition Test*) rejects self-authored certificates where exposed parties lack standing in pre-run specifications and post-run logs.

## 7. The basal keystone `(1, 1, 1)`: collapses and domain boundary

At $\mathcal{G}\_1$'s root ($\mathrm{Anc}^\ast(L\_{01}) = \lbrace{}L\_{01}\rbrace$), `L01` places one non-substitutable guard on every axis ([Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md)):

- **Collapse Proposition 1 (`T1Framing`):** Simulator blind spots or coarse surface tolerances size buffers against false certainty (*falsified if* an unframed coarse loop survives unmodeled surface shocks).
- **Collapse Proposition 2 (`T5Temporal`):** Accurate braking geometry fails whenever recovery latency exceeds survival headroom, $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} < \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (*falsified if* overdue recovery clears the barrier unaided).
- **Collapse Proposition 3 (`T7Liability`):** Truncated-liability operators shift crash harm across external reception perimeters ($\Theta\_{\mathrm{basin}}$) via self-certified rules (*falsified if* capped operators preserve reserves through shocks without risk-bearer standing or alarms).

Two hard boundaries bracket this order. First, when $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}}$ falls below irreducible sensing-to-actuation delay, only passive compliance or physical separation stabilizes control. Second, in wholly reversible low-energy motions with $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} \gg \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}$, no reachable ruin barrier, and no export across external reception perimeters ($\Theta\_{\mathrm{basin}}$), deliberate checking can safely replace pre-allocated physical reserves.
