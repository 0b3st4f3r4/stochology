## 1. T4Formalization: what counts as the state

A queue is the first object in Part V whose buffer sits in its definition, not in a note appended to it. A server, an arrival stream, and a rule for what happens when the server is occupied: those three are the whole of the object, and the formalization site is the one that says so before anything has been counted. Which of the three is treated as random fixes the state, and the state fixes what a measurement can be a measurement *of*. If arrivals are a rate, a customer is a unit of counting and the number in the system is an integer; if they are a fluid, the same queue has a level. Under first-come-first-served a wait belongs to a position in a sequence; under a priority rule the same wait belongs to a class. Three declarations, three objects, and the algebra is exact in each of them.

The load factor is where the declaration becomes visible. Write $\lambda$ for the arrival rate and $T\_{s}$ for the mean service time, so that

$$\rho = \lambda T\_{s}.$$

Nothing in that product is observed. $\lambda$ is a parameter of a model of the arrivals, $T\_{s}$ a parameter of a model of the service, and $\rho$ is their product, so a utilization figure is a statement about two models and not about a machine. Two operators can therefore report the same utilization for the same service and be describing different systems: one has counted sessions and the other requests. The number is exact and its referent is a choice, and the choice was made when the state was declared.

Given the declaration, the classical result bounds the wait and does not compute it. For a single server with general arrivals and general service,

$$E[W] \le \frac{\rho}{1-\rho}\mkern3mu\frac{c\_{a}^{2}/\rho^{2}+c\_{s}^{2}}{2}\mkern3mu T\_{s}, \qquad E[W] \approx \frac{\rho}{1-\rho}\mkern3mu\frac{c\_{a}^{2}+c\_{s}^{2}}{2}\mkern3mu T\_{s},$$

where $c\_{a}^{2}$ and $c\_{s}^{2}$ are the squared coefficients of variation of the inter-arrival and service times: the first relation is Kingman's exact $GI/G/1$ upper bound valid at every load $\rho \in (0, 1)$ (Kingman 1962, with trivial equality at zero wait when $c\_{a}^{2} = c\_{s}^{2} = 0$), and the second is Kingman's heavy-traffic approximation as $\rho \to 1^{-}$ (Kingman 1961), which holds with exact Pollaczek–Khinchine equality at every load for Poisson arrivals ($M/G/1$, $c\_{a}^{2} = 1$). Away from saturation, bursty renewals ($c\_{a}^{2} > 1$) can wait longer than the heavy-traffic curve predicts, which is why the universal ceiling carries $c\_{a}^{2}/\rho^{2}$, whereas arrivals more regular than Poisson wait less; as $\rho \to 1^{-}$, $c\_{a}^{2}/\rho^{2} \to c\_{a}^{2}$ and exact ceiling and heavy-traffic limit meet. A bound that survives distributional ignorance declines to be an equality, and an operator who reads a heavy-traffic approximation as an all-load ceiling has confused an asymptote with a guarantee.

In the heavy-traffic regime ($\rho \to 1^{-}$), the expression factors into a congestion multiplier $\rho/(1-\rho)$ that depends on the operating point alone and a dispersion factor $(c\_{a}^{2}+c\_{s}^{2})/2$ that depends on the process alone (with arrival burstiness penalised still more sharply by $1/\rho^{2}$ in the finite-load ceiling). That factorization is why the order is *meso*: the multiplier is a property of the aggregate, the dispersion a property of the stream, and neither is a property of any single arrival. The bound is linear in the dispersion and hyperbolic in the load, so a proportional error in the estimated dispersion passes through undamped while the same proportional error in the load is multiplied by the congestion factor, largest exactly where the estimate matters most. An operator who measured dispersion in a quiet week and plans a busy one has made the smaller error where the larger one is amplified.

## 2. T6Performative: the announcement that enters the arrival

The formalization treats the arrival rate as a parameter of the stream. In every queue a person can see, it is also a function of what the queue says about itself. A posted wait is a signal, and the arrival decides on it: some join, some balk, some join and then abandon. The effective arrival rate is therefore $\lambda(W)$ while the wait is $W(\lambda)$, and the object is the fixed point

$$W = W\bigl(\lambda(W)\bigr),$$

which need not be unique. A queue that reports a short wait draws arrivals until the wait is no longer short; a queue that reports a long one sheds them until it is. Both solve the same equation, and which one obtains is a fact about the reporting regime, not the capacity. That is what it means for the measurement to enter the object: there is no true wait of the queue independent of what the queue announces, because the announcement is one of the things that determines who is standing in it.

The empirical literature treats this feedback as a modelling primitive, not a correction added later. Brown et al. (2005) analyse a call centre in which the arrival rate varies by time of day and by day of week and in which customers abandon while waiting, so the rate that reaches an agent is not the rate that called. Abandonment is the feedback in its causal form: the waiting time determines who stays, and who stays determines the load that produces the waiting time. An analysis that treats the offered load as exogenous has deleted the term that makes the system a system, and the deletion is invisible in the fit.

Where the report is also a target, the feedback acquires a direction. Goodhart (1975) states the general form: a measure used for control ceases to be a good measure of what it was measuring. Espeland & Sauder (2007) document the mechanism in rankings, where the ranked organisation reorganises itself around the measure, so that the next reading is a reading of a different organisation. Perdomo et al. (2020) give the decision-theoretic form: when the distribution depends on the predictor, stability is a property of the retraining loop's fixed point, not of the model. In a queue the analogous statement is that the discipline is where the performance is enacted. A call-back option, a deflection to self-service, a rolling average that reports the median of a bimodal week: each lowers the reported wait by changing what is being waited for, and each leaves the algebra of §1 intact while changing the service out of recognition.

The queue's own history is not a sample from a fixed population, because the population's composition responds to the history: a week measured while a wait was posted is not exchangeable with a week measured while it was not. And the formalization survives all of this: the fixed point has the same algebra, the bound still bounds conditionally on the rate, and what has changed is that the rate is now endogenous to the display.

## 3. T7Liability: who holds the buffer

A buffer is capacity held idle, and idle capacity is a cost with no visible output. The party that owns the queue therefore has a standing reason to hold less of it than the arrivals would prefer, and the reason is structural: the buffer's benefit is the rupture that did not happen, and a rupture that did not happen appears in no ledger. What appears in the ledger is the cost of the capacity, on a line a budget officer can point at.

The rupture has to land somewhere, and where it lands is fixed by the discipline. In a delay system the arrival that finds the server busy waits, and the cost is its time. In a loss system with a finite waiting room the arrival that finds the room full is refused, and the cost is its entire request. In a system with abandonment the arrival that finds the wait too long leaves, and the cost is borne by the arrival and by whatever it was going to do next. Three disciplines, three allocations of one shortfall, and because the discipline is a parameter the formalization of §1 records, the allocation is decided at the same moment the state is declared. This is the precise sense in which the order is liability-primary: the discipline is where the queue decides who pays.

The incentive is visible in one line. Let $B$ be the buffer, $P(B)$ the probability of rupture at that buffer, and $L$ the loss a rupture imposes. A party that bears the whole loss provisions until the marginal reduction in expected loss equals the marginal cost of capacity,

$$P'(B)\mkern3mu L = 1,$$

while a party that bears only part of it, because the loss falls on the arrival or because its liability is truncated at the value of its assets, provisions against $\min(L, A)$ and stops strictly earlier, since $\min(L,A) \le L$ makes the marginal benefit of the last unit of buffer smaller for it. Limited liability is the clean case of the general result (Innes 1990), and no bad faith is required: the same optimisation, run with a smaller $L$, returns a smaller $B$ every time. The buffer that is missing is not the buffer somebody forgot; it is the buffer nobody's loss required.

The legal form of the same question is the negligence calculus of *United States v. Carroll Towing Co.* (Hand 1947), which compares the burden of precaution against the probability of loss multiplied by its magnitude. The formula is neutral among the parties and the arithmetic is not. $B$ is borne by the party that must act, while the product of probability and loss is borne by whoever is downstream of the failure, so an application that reads both terms off the actor's own books has assumed the answer before it computed it. A queue without a buffer has therefore not failed to compute the optimum; it has computed the optimum of a smaller problem, and the difference between the two problems is exactly the part of the loss the arrival carries.

## 4. The signature: an exact order with no epistemic reading

The signature is $(0, 1, 2)$: no epistemic site, one vital, two governance. The governance readings are the two sites just argued — the order changes its own arrivals by reporting them, and it allocates its own rupture by choosing a discipline. The vital reading is the formalization's, and its content is a margin in the strict sense: the bound of §1 is provisionable, and a party that provisions against it holds capacity it expects to need for a reason it can state.

What is absent is any site that would read an epistemic margin. Nothing declares what the model of arrivals leaves out, nothing prices the error the model would make, and nothing holds a record against which the model could be checked. The bound is linear in a dispersion estimated from a trace, and no site in the order requires the estimate's error to be declared before the bound is used. So the order can be formally exact and performatively live while carrying no epistemic margin at all: exact because the algebra is exact given its premises, live because its own reports move its own arrivals, and epistemically exposed because nothing in its local triple makes the premises answerable to anything.

The cost is precise and it is not the cost of being wrong. It is the cost of not being able to tell two cases apart. A queue that met its bound after a quiet quarter and a queue that met its bound because the quarter happened to resemble the model are indistinguishable from inside this order, and they are indistinguishable again when the next quarter does not resemble it. What the absence does not cost is the buffer. The vital site still binds: a queue provisioned at the ceiling survives a misspecification of its arrival process until the buffer is exhausted, and the exhaustion is a physical event with a date, not an inference. Nor does it cost the possibility of repair, because both governance sites remain open: liability can be relocated by changing the discipline so that the party who chooses the buffer is the party who bears the refusal, and the performative site can be made honest by publishing the wait that was measured and not the one that was targeted. Neither repair improves the model; both make the model's failure belong to someone, which is what a governance site can do and an epistemic site cannot.

## By the end of this chapter you can

1. Derive $\rho = \lambda T\_{s}$ as a ratio of two model quantities, and say why two systems reported at the same utilization need not be the same system.
2. Distinguish Kingman's exact $GI/G/1$ upper bound from the heavy-traffic approximation (exact at every load for Poisson $M/G/1$ and asymptotically tight as $\rho \to 1^{-}$), and explain why a planner facing bursty arrivals needs the direction of the inequality.
3. Show how a posted waiting time makes the arrival rate a function of the wait, and why the resulting fixed point is a fact about the reporting regime and not about capacity.
