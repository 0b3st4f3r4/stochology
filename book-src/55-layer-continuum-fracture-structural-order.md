A continuum is the first object here that is neither a channel, a queue nor a clock: it is a solid, and what
decides its fate is a defect no instrument sees whole. The order carries formalization as its primary site and is
the stratum's keystone: no plane is blank.

## 1. Severity: a crack is a threshold on the flaw before it is a statement about the load

In the vocabulary of severity the governing quantity is not the stress but the energy released per unit of
new surface a defect would create. Griffith (1921) wrote that balance, not a
strength criterion. For a crack of length $2a$ in a plate of unit thickness under tension $\sigma$, with
modulus $E$ and surface energy $\gamma$ per new face,

$$\Pi(a) = \Pi\_{0} + 4\gamma a - \frac{\pi\sigma^{2}a^{2}}{E},$$

where the linear term is the work of two new faces and the quadratic the elastic energy returned, the
compliance of a cracked section growing as $a^{2}/E$. Setting the derivative to zero,

$$\frac{d\Pi}{da} = 4\gamma - \frac{2\pi\sigma^{2}a}{E} = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad a\_{c} = \frac{2\gamma E}{\pi\sigma^{2}} = \frac{1}{\pi}\left(\frac{K\_{Ic}}{\sigma}\right)^{2},$$

with $K\_{Ic}^{2} = 2\gamma E$. Since $\Pi''(a\_{c}) = -2\pi\sigma^{2}/E < 0$,
the critical length is a maximum of the total energy: a barrier, not a strength. And the barrier's height is
$\Pi(a\_{c}) - \Pi(0) = 2\gamma a\_{c}$, so wall and pass stand in fixed proportion and both scale as
$\sigma^{-2}$.

The threshold reading is immediate: below $a\_{c}$ growth costs energy, above it growth pays, so severity
compares the largest flaw present with a length that load, modulus and toughness jointly set. Read the other
way, $\sigma\_{c}(a) = K\_{Ic}/\sqrt{\pi a}$, the strength is a random variable whenever the flaw is one, and
the worst flaw is what an inspection can least bound. A load multiplied by $\lambda$ divides the tolerated
length by $\lambda^{2}$, so a raised working load leaves an inspection matched to the old threshold several
times too coarse: the flaw it could not resolve was harmless before the raise and critical after it, with
nothing in the record changed.

A point load cannot answer the question, because the question asks for the event
$\{a \ge a\_{c}(\sigma)\}$: a probability, not a distance. A point value asserts a definite distance to a definite threshold, a claim about two
distributions disguised as one number. The measurement guide of BIPM,
IEC, IFCC, ILAC, ISO, IUPAC, IUPAP and OIML (2008/2012) states the rule, that a result without its uncertainty
is incomplete because the value alone does not say what the instrument excluded, and Roy (1952) made it a
decision criterion under the name safety first: a constraint on a quantile, $\Pr[\text{loss} \ge d] \le
\alpha$, not on a mean. A design reviewed against a nominal load has certified a point estimate of a
quantity whose severity lives in its tail.

## 2. Formalization: the threshold is a fold, and the population is the same fold counted

A threshold a system can cross in one direction and not in the other is, formally, a fold. Write the
potential with a distance to criticality $\mu$ and an imperfection $h$:

$$V(x) = \frac{x^{4}}{4} - \frac{\mu}{2}x^{2} - h x, \qquad V'(x) = x^{3} - \mu x - h.$$

With $h = 0$ and $\mu > 0$, $x = 0$ is a maximum and $x = \pm\sqrt{\mu}$ are minima: two wells with a barrier
of height $\mu^{2}/4$ between them. As $\mu$ falls the wells approach and the barrier falls with the square of
the distance; at $\mu = 0$ the three stationary points coincide, one well remains, and no threshold is left
to cross. A fold is not a state that has become unlikely but a state that has ceased to exist; read as a memory
and not a structure, it is Hopfield's (1982) energy function.

With $h \ne 0$ the roots never meet: imposing $V' = V'' = 0$ gives $\mu = 3x^{2}$ and $x = -(h/2)^{1/3}$,
hence

$$\mu\_{\max} = 3\left(\frac{h}{2}\right)^{2/3}.$$

The exponent is the finding: scaling $h$ down by $k$ recovers only $k^{2/3}$ of the limit load, so a defect
small by the standard of the tolerance is not small by the standard of the response. And with $h \ne 0$ no value of $\mu$ removes the threshold; the fold is smoothed into a rapid
but continuous loss, so "below critical" never means failure is impossible, only that the barrier is finite.
Finite barriers are crossed. The Griffith case is this algebra with the variables named: $x$ is crack advance,
$\mu$ the distance of the load from criticality, $h$ the initial flaw, and the two wells are the intact and the
separated state once the released energy saturates at the width of the section. The approach is observable:
Scheffer et al. (2009) record critical slowing, with its false alarms. A signal is not a certificate.

The population scale is the same argument counted. Let $N$ members carry load only together, so system
strength is the minimum $R\_{(1)} = \min\_{i} R\_{i}$. With independent members and survival $S(r) = \Pr[R > r]$,

$$\Pr[R\_{(1)} > r] = S(r)^{N},$$

and for the Weibull (1939) member law $F(r) = 1 - \exp[-(r/r\_{0})^{m}]$ this is $\exp[-(r/r\_{1})^{m}]$ with
$r\_{1} = r\_{0}N^{-1/m}$: the minimum stays in the member's family, same shape, scale falling as the inverse
$m$-th power of the count. That is the weakest-link law, the mechanical ancestor of the Leontief minimum this
book uses for its three margins.

The two are one argument at two scales. The fold says a member's threshold is where its basin stops
existing; the minimum says the system's threshold is the first member whose basin stops existing. Both
concern the smallest of a set of thresholds, and the composition of folds across a population is an order
statistic. The exponents play the same role in different algebras: $2/3$ governs how fast margin is spent as
the imperfection grows, $m$ how fast survival falls as the load grows, and neither is a property of the
load; both belong to the flaw population, the one thing the design does not know. The mean strength of the
members enters neither expression, which is one fact stated twice: a minimum reads the lower tail, and
extrema are governed by the tail of the parent law and not by its body (Embrechts, Klüppelberg &
Mikosch 1997).

## 3. Liability: the margin is paid by a party who does not choose the load

In the vocabulary of liability a margin is a quantity one party pays for and another consumes. A structure
designed at nominal load is priced against a value that is not a distribution: the party who chooses the load
captures the benefit of the last increment without carrying the probability that the flaw population exceeds
the threshold that increment implies, and the party who carries it is the occupant, the passenger, the
public. A party who owns the upside and is insulated from the downside under-provides care by
construction: Innes (1990) for limited liability, Taleb (2018) for the absence of skin in the game.

The legal form of the transfer is an inequality whose three quantities are held by two parties. Hand (1947),
in *United States v. Carroll Towing Co.*, states the test of negligence as a comparison of the burden of
precaution $B$ with the probability of harm multiplied by its gravity:

$$B < PL \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{the burden was owed.}$$

The algebra is one line; the information is split. $B$ is known to the party who must spend it — designer,
operator, inspector — while $P$ and $L$ are realized on the party who bears the harm. Each side evaluates it with the numbers
it holds, and no arithmetic repairs the partition: a party who never sees $P$
will always find $B$ large. Holmström (1979) states the general form, that liability and incentive attach to
the observable signal and not to the act.

What makes the arrangement durable is that the artifact which survives is not the load decision but the
inspection record. A load is chosen in a meeting and leaves nothing that says "we chose this"; an
inspection leaves a signed sheet with a resolution, a date and a name. Power (1997) described the
consequence: what can be verified is what gets produced, and the ritual of verification displaces the object
it was meant to check. Liability settles
where the record is kept, and the record is kept by the party with the least authority over the load: the
margin was chosen upstream and is defended downstream.

The tail goes unpriced, and its classical case is the one Dowie (1977) reported and the
California Court of Appeal (1981) tried: a fix weighed against an expected harm by a party that did not bear
it, at a per-death valuation that Schwartz (1991) traced to the regulator's own societal-cost estimate. The value that decided it was never printed in the design. The court did not repair the arithmetic; it
relocated the liability after the fact. The case establishes that the calculation was possible, not that it
was wrong: an inequality whose loss is priced before the harm by anyone other than the party who suffers it will be satisfied, and
satisfied where the record is cleanest.

Vaughan (1996) supplies the temporal form: each operation that returned made the previous estimate of the
tail look conservative, so the record of survival became the evidence for the higher load, and the standard
of care migrated without anyone deciding to move it — a drift Perrow (1984) and Leveson (2011) place in the
system, not in the person. Douglas (2000) is the epistemic statement underneath: the acceptance threshold is
a value judgment, so "the code says it passes" is a decision already taken about whose loss counts. Frick
(2015) states the constraint the order cannot meet from inside: a risk is justifiable only to those who bear
it, and the bearer of a flaw population's tail is not the signer of the inspection.

## 4. Keystone: all three readings at (1, 1, 1)

The three readings come out equal, and the equality is not a score. Severity is the threshold on the flaw;
formalization the fold that makes a threshold a threshold; liability the owner of the threshold. Read back to
back they are one object at three planes, and the signature records occupancy: at no site is an axis silent.
The stratum carries the signature more than once, and a signature says which planes are occupied, not how
well any is served; it ranks nothing.

What the full triple buys is addressability. Because three different arguments occupy the three sites, a
failure has three distinguishable diagnoses: a flaw the inspection did not resolve, a fold the analysis
smoothed away, or a margin whose cost was carried by a party who did not choose the load. The evidence separating them differs, and so does the repair; and the order
itself becomes a calibration point, where a reading that collapses the three planes can be shown the
collapse.

What it does not buy: not a ranking, for the reason given. Not a sum: the triad is a
Leontief minimum, so a full triple does not raise the floor, it only means no term of the floor is missing.
Not a warranty: the smoothed fold says there is no load below which failure is impossible, so passage of a
code is a comparison at a point, and severity lives in the distribution the point was drawn from. Not a
control law: the triple is folded from a finished trajectory and reports which plane failed, while acting is
done per step by the canonical operator. What it leaves the rest of the stratum: which threshold, produced by which
fold, owned by whom.

## By the end of this chapter you can

1. Derive the Griffith critical length from the energy balance of a cracked body, and say why the result is
   a condition on the flaw and not a statement about the load.
2. Show that the fold algebra and the Weibull weakest-link form are one argument at two scales, and identify
   which exponent carries the imperfection and which carries the population.
3. Say where the liability for a margin settles when the load and the inspection belong to different
   parties, and why the record, not the decision, becomes the evidence after a failure.

## Exercises

1. Carry out the energy balance for a crack of length $2a$ in a plate of unit thickness, recover $a\_{c}$, and
   then compute what happens to the tolerated length when the remote tension is multiplied by $\lambda$. Say
   what the answer implies for an inspection whose resolution was fixed before the load was raised.
2. Show that the barrier height at the critical length is $2\gamma a\_{c}$, and say why a criterion stated as a
   critical length and a criterion stated as a critical energy release rate are the same criterion only while
   the load is held fixed.
3. Take the quartic potential with imperfection $h$, derive the limit load $\mu\_{\max} \propto h^{2/3}$, and
   say what the exponent does to the intuition that a flaw below tolerance is a flaw without consequence.
4. Compose $N$ independent Weibull members into a chain and derive the system scale $r\_{0}N^{-1/m}$. Say why
   the mean strength of the members does not appear, and what that implies for a test programme that samples
   members from the middle of the population.
5. Write the negligence comparison as an inequality, mark which of its three quantities is known to which
   party, and say what happens to the comparison when the burden is borne by one party and the loss by
   another. Then say which of the three diagnoses of §4 the resulting failure would receive.

## Bridge

This chapter delivered a threshold that belongs to the flaw, not to the load, a fold that is the
formal shape of that threshold and that survives an imperfection only by being smoothed, and a margin whose
liability settles where the inspection was recorded, not where the load was chosen. The algebra that
composes a population of such thresholds into one system threshold is an order statistic, and the order that
follows takes up the case in which the procedure itself is the resource being consumed:
[Chapter 56, *Layer 06: Formal Logic, Computability & Linear Resource-Type Order*](56-layer-formal-computable-type-order.md).
