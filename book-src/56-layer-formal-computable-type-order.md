## 1. Why the audit must be syntactic

Safety engineering keeps asking a monitor to verify, before a policy acts, that its future execution will never violate an invariant. By Rice's
theorem (1953) every non-trivial semantic property of the partial functions an arbitrary program computes is undecidable, so
no procedure decides in finite time whether an unconstrained policy will exhaust a reserve on an unseen input, and a semantic proof awaited
inside a loop with a finite horizon is a contradiction.

The decidable escape is a linear-time $LL(1)$ syntactic certificate over declared premises, in which a sentence is well formed only if its framing
mode, interval bounds $[L\_t, U\_t]$ with $L\_t \le U\_t$, effective sample size and survival brake appear on the control tape before any write to
the world is licensed. The recogniser is a finite production system over a declared grammar, not a verifier over programs, and that restriction is
what puts its cost inside the loop's horizon.

The framing is the first thing the audit decides, and it decides whether an answer exists. Asked whether a program does what its authors meant,
the auditor is asked for a semantic property of the partial function the program computes, and the theorem cited above closes the route: no
procedure separates the programs that mean a given thing from those that do not, so a monitor awaiting that separation awaits it past any
horizon. Turing's (1936) halting problem is the ancestor of the case and not a neighbouring one. Asked instead whether a write respects the
resource discipline, the auditor is asked about a derivation: whether each resource in the context is consumed the number of times its type
permits, and whether the sentence is produced by the grammar. That question is about the shape of a finite object, and for a grammar with one
token of lookahead it is answered in time linear in the sentence.

The two framings are not two grades of the same demand. They sit on opposite sides of the line Rice drew, and the framing is what places the audit
there: a question about a program's meaning admits no finite certificate, a question about a tape's shape admits one, and the second is weaker in
exactly the respect that makes it affordable. The weakness is the chapter's recurring cost. A well-formed derivation constrains what may be written
on the tape and not what the tape means, so a declaration can be impeccable and the write it licenses wrong — which §4 shows twice.

## 2. Linear logic and affine resources

Girard's linear logic (1987) and the affine ownership disciplines of typed programming languages forbid duplicating a resource and, in a strict
linear discipline, dropping it without an explicit consumer; classical logic permits both. The three axes read as three linear resources: a vital
reserve is spent once, a pre-outcome seal is a single-opening typestate whose
move into verification consumes it, and an algedonic warning must be handled and not dropped by an
intermediate signature.

Formally the discipline is the withdrawal of two structural rules. Classical and intuitionistic logic admit weakening, which lets a premise be
dropped unused, and contraction, which lets one premise serve twice; linear logic withdraws both, and an affine discipline re-admits weakening
while continuing to forbid contraction. The tensor rule shows where the count lives:

$$\frac{\Gamma \vdash t : A \qquad \Delta \vdash u : B}{\Gamma, \Delta \vdash \langle t, u\rangle : A \otimes B}$$

The context is split, not shared, so no resource name may occur on both sides of the line. The invariant the recogniser checks is therefore a
property of the derivation tree — for each resource $r$, at most one leaf consumes it — and it is read off the tree, not off a run.

## 3. Bounded enumeration and the coverage ledger

Searching guard configurations across $7$ sites with $g$ levels requires $g^{7}$ evaluations, so an unbounded
search would stall the deliberation loop and induce Collapse Proposition 2 through verification latency alone. A generative search therefore
carries a mandatory coverage ledger: above a pre-registered cap $C$ the search halts inside the survival budget and flags its result as truncated,
because a search that reports what it did not reach reports a population it never sampled. The contract is
$c(g) = \min(1, C/g^{7})$, so coverage is full through the largest level count the cap admits and falls away as $C/g^{7}$ beyond it. Publish the
fraction beside the result, treat the uncovered region as unscanned and not clean, and downgrade the verdict to an abstention.

The ledger's claim is a closure predicate and not a sample. Write $V$ for the set of violation shapes the grammar can produce, $V\_{\mathrm{rej}}$
for those a production rejects, and $U$ for the declared uncovered set; the claim is that the first is contained in the union of the other two,

$$V \subseteq V\_{\mathrm{rej}} \cup U, \qquad U \cap V\_{\mathrm{rej}} = \varnothing,$$

with the uncovered set disjoint from the rejected one so that the two verdicts cannot both be earned by the same shape. A repertory of sampled
cases can only exhibit the shapes it contains and so supports no such inclusion; a finite enumeration over the grammar's own productions can, and
that is the difference between sampling and closure. The obligation is falsifiable in one way only: exhibit a derivation the recogniser accepts
whose write violates the discipline.

## 4. The three collapses, and two declarations that licensed a wrong write

1. **Collapse Proposition 1** ($\neg S\_{\mathrm{epi}}$, silent domain narrowing): assuming an input lies inside a narrow type range without
   bounding out-of-range states, the mechanism of the 1996 Ariane 5 Flight 501 loss, where a 64-bit horizontal velocity was cast into a 16-bit
   signed integer under the unverified premise that Ariane 4's envelope still held (Lions 1996).
2. **Collapse Proposition 2** ($\neg S\_{\mathrm{vital}}$, unbounded proof latency): running an undecidable verification routine inside a
   real-time loop until the bureaucratic delay exceeds the survival horizon.
3. **Collapse Proposition 3** ($\neg S\_{\mathrm{gov}}$, ambient authority): letting an agent write outside its authorised contract scope,
   spending common reserves without an attested control tape and without a declared opening state for the pact it acts under.

The inversion of the first proposition is Mars Climate Orbiter, lost during orbit insertion in 1999, where thruster firings were declared in
pound-force seconds and consumed as newton-seconds (NASA 1999). Both institutions held written contracts, checked every premise's form, and
every tape parsed. A pound-force second exceeds a newton-second by the factor the definition of the pound-force fixes, so the
declaration understated every impulse by a constant factor over the whole cruise, and the certificate had no
atom where the dimension could live.

Both cases close the same way. The pass is evidence about form and not
about content: a recogniser built on the discipline of §2 would accept both tapes again, and a chapter claiming otherwise would be claiming a
semantic guarantee from a syntactic instrument.

## 5. What a type can say, and what it cannot

The formal object of this chapter is a judgement $\Gamma \vdash e : A$ in which $\Gamma$ is a multiset of resources and not a set: each item
is distinct, and a derivation may neither duplicate one nor silently discard one. That is the whole of Girard's linear logic (1987) read as a
discipline over contexts, and it is what the three readings of §2 share — a reserve spent once, a seal whose single opening is the verification,
a warning that may be raised or not raised but never copied.

What the judgement fixes is the arity of consumption. It cannot fix three other things. First,
it does not fix the identity of the consumer: a derivation in which the party that wrote the declaration also consumes the token, and also
certifies the ledger, is well typed, because no judgement form in the calculus has a place to write a name. Second, it certifies that a token
moved and not that the token's content was true when it moved; a false premise and a true one share a type, since a type records shape and not
truth. Third, a well-formed derivation can license a write the declaration did not intend, and the two losses of §4 are that third silence made
concrete: Ariane 5's inertial reference was held in a narrower type than the value it received and every conversion in the chain was well formed
(Lions 1996); Mars Climate Orbiter's interface carried a number and no dimension and every field in it was present (NASA 1999).

The habit worth breaking is the expectation that a type system's metatheory is a statement about the world. Soundness says that a well-typed
term's evaluations preserve the type and progress says that such a term is not stuck; neither says whether the state the calculus names is the
state the plant is in. The distance between those two is the distance between the tape and the write, and the next two sections measure it.

## 6. Expiry: the proof that was valid at issuance

Every certificate in this chapter is a sentence about premises at one time, and the write it licenses happens at another. Lamport's
happens-before relation (1978) says precisely why the certificate does not close that gap by itself: two events are ordered only when one can
causally reach the other, so a declaration and a distant write are concurrent unless the tape carries a message between them, and the recogniser
reads only the tape.

What happens to a premise between two readings has a name in econometrics. Croushore and Stark (2001) keep every series as it was first published,
so that a vintage is part of the value: the same quarter's output, read at the moment of a decision and read after the annual revision, is two
different numbers under one name. A declaration's premises are vintages of that kind. The derivation does not change when the premise is revised,
and that is the danger, not the reassurance: the sentence stays well formed at use while the statement it certifies has quietly become
false, with no step in the derivation invalidated.

Let each premise carry a window $[a\_i, b\_i]$ inside which it is licensed. A write at time $t$ is admissible only if $t$ lies in every window,

$$\mathrm{licence}(t) = 1 \iff \Bigl( t \in \bigcap\_{i=1}^{n} [a\_i, b\_i] \Bigr) \wedge \mathrm{wellformed},$$

and the quantity that decides whether the discipline is being kept is the count of lapsed premises, $\ell(t) = \#\{i : t > b\_i\}$. A re-check is
forced at a write exactly when $\ell(t) > 0$, so over $m$ writes at most $m$ re-checks are forced, and fewer only where the windows straddle
consecutive writes. That bound is the reason the check can be repeated at all: it is a re-reading of a finite object, priced in the same units at
every repetition, which a semantic verification is not.

The institutional form of the same failure is the precedent that is valid when it is set and is then re-read as a licence. Vaughan's (1996)
account of the Challenger launches describes that trajectory: each deviation was accepted against the record of a previous flight that had not
failed, so the evidence for acceptability was the absence of a prior failure and not a present measurement of the margin. Expiry is therefore
not a nuisance appended to the discipline. It is the part of the discipline that keeps the certificate about the write instead of about the
declaration.

## 7. The empty governance axis and the licensed wrong write

The triple puts its two vital sites on the formal object and on the clock and holds no governance site, and the signature prints that as the zero
in $(1, 2, 0)$. Read through Collapse Proposition 3, the zero is not a section someone forgot to write. It is the absence, from the judgement
itself, of any place where an authority could be named: a type states how often a resource may be consumed and has no form in which to state who
may relax the count, who may declare a violation shape uncovered, or who may sign the ledger that records either.

Stated as a condition on the checker, the governance axial exposure is exact. Let the recogniser accept a derivation $D$ and licence a write $w$ when the tape is
well formed, and let the ledger's closure claim be the inclusion of §3. That inclusion is satisfiable by any party free to choose $U$, since every
shape can be moved into the uncovered set while the derivation stays well formed. Add the condition that the author of $D$ also fixes $U$ and also
certifies the ledger, and the checker becomes uninformative in precisely the case the audit exists for: the declaration passes, the write is wrong,
and the record shows a pass. The tape cannot distinguish "no violation found" from "no violation looked for", because both are the same tape.

Power (1997) names the arrangement when he describes verification becoming ritual, performed in form while its substance is displaced, and stable
in that form because the party under check supplies the categories of the check. Strathern (1997) locates the mechanism in the measure itself: in
an audit culture the indicator takes the place of the performance, so improving the indicator is the rational act and the improvement is real as a
report. The Horizon case is where the displacement reaches a court, since Christie (2020) documents a legal presumption that computer evidence is
dependable unless shown otherwise, applied to records generated by the system whose dependability was the question: the operator's ledger serving
as the evidence of the operator's correctness, with no record falsified anywhere in the chain.

Holmström (1979) gives the reason better intentions do not repair the arrangement: where the action and the observation of it come from the same
source, the incentive constraint loses its purchase, because a principal cannot condition on what it cannot separately observe, and Jensen and
Meckling (1976) name the residual loss. The agnotological reading is the one the collapse proposition states: the truncation fraction of §3 stays
available after the fact as an alibi, so the figure that should downgrade the verdict upgrades the defence, and McGoey (2012) describes strategic
unknowns as produced and not found. What the type cannot supply the institution must, and the predicate has a shape worth writing down:
author, uncovered set and certifier held by three parties, each separation attested and not assumed. Ostrom (1990) is the closest available
grammar for that arrangement, and North (1990) states why it cannot be folded back into the type: rules and their enforcement are one unit, and a
rule with no enforcer is a sentence with no truth value. The zero in the signature is that unit's absence, and stating it is not the same as
supplying it.
