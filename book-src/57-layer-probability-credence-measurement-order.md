## By the end of this chapter you can

1. State the frame every credence presupposes, separate the four empty-field and out-of-domain statuses under *ex ante* authorship, and explain why two readings of one register differ in width.
2. Read an imprecise credence as severity: derive the decision interval from the loss differential, and explain sample-size invariance under worst-case bounds.

## 1. Framing: a credence is a sentence before it is a number

Picture opening a hospital database and spotting a blank cell in the white-blood-cell column for half the patients. Did the lab lose the sample, was the result zero, or did no doctor think to order the test for a sprained ankle? Until someone states what a blank means, no probability percentage computed from that table can be trusted.

Here, $T\_1$ (`T1Framing`) protects probability and measurement claims by locking down the domain, event grammar, and blank-cell meanings *before* a single statistic is calculated. Built on [Layer 06](56-layer-formal-computable-type-order.md)'s syntactic types and [Layer 05](55-layer-continuum-fracture-structural-order.md)'s structural thresholds, this order bridges formalization and physical metrology.

A probability number is always attached to a sentence inside an explicit language. Kolmogorov (1933) states axioms over a sample space and event family $(\Omega, \mathcal{F})$ without choosing $\mathcal{F}$. Cox (1946) embeds Boolean plausibility into **finitely** additive probability (unique up to monotone rescaling) only under universal comparability, density, and smooth $C^2$ rules—assumptions that break on finite Boolean domains without extra structure (Halpern 1999; [Probability as Language](30-probability-as-language.md)). Savage (1954) takes world states as primitive and acts as functions over them, leaving unrepresented states invisible.

Blank table fields require four mutually exclusive meanings fixed *ex ante* ([Measuring and Erring](33-measuring-and-erring.md) §4). Consider an authored frame $(\Omega, \mathcal{F})$, conditioning domain $B \in \mathcal{F}$, and $n = n\_{\mathrm{obs}} + n\_{\mathrm{mis}} + n\_{\mathrm{inapp}} > 0$ rows (with eligible rows $n\_{\mathrm{elig}} = n - n\_{\mathrm{inapp}} = n\_{\mathrm{obs}} + n\_{\mathrm{mis}} > 0$ and $k \in \lbrace{}0, \dots, n\_{\mathrm{obs}}\rbrace$ recorded events among $n\_{\mathrm{obs}} > 0$ observations):

- **`MEASURED_ZERO` ($x\_i = 0$ in $B$):** Counts toward $n\_{\mathrm{obs}}$ and $n\_{\mathrm{elig}}$ without raising event count $k$ or uncertainty width.
- **`NOT_OBSERVED` ($n\_{\mathrm{mis}} \ge 0$ eligible missing rows):** Remains inside $n\_{\mathrm{elig}}$, yielding Manski's interval $[k/n\_{\mathrm{elig}},\mkern3mu(k + n\_{\mathrm{mis}})/n\_{\mathrm{elig}}]$ of width $n\_{\mathrm{mis}}/n\_{\mathrm{elig}}$ (the upper endpoint reaching $1$ as $(n\_{\mathrm{obs}} - k)/n\_{\mathrm{elig}} \to 0$, and the width approaching $1$ as $n\_{\mathrm{obs}}/n\_{\mathrm{elig}} \to 0$, whereas zero-imputation fakes $k/n\_{\mathrm{elig}}$).
- **`INAPPLICABLE` ($n\_{\mathrm{inapp}} \ge 0$ rows outside $B$):** Contracts to $k/n\_{\mathrm{obs}}$ only if $n\_{\mathrm{mis}} = 0$ and $B$ was fixed upfront; reclassifying `NOT_OBSERVED` as `INAPPLICABLE` afterward erases $n\_{\mathrm{mis}}/n\_{\mathrm{elig}}$ on unchanged data.
- **Outside domain ($\omega \notin \Omega$ or $A \notin \mathcal{F}$):** Carries no probability at all—not zero—leaving zero trace in $n$ until an attested `CounterInductiveReopen` ([Appendix B.1](appendix-b-architecture-verification.md#b1-the-grammar-of-aim--syntax-layer-and-acceptance-predicate)).

Hospital records show how much signal hides in whether a field is blank or filled (Agniel, Kohane, & Weber 2018). Across 669,452 patients and 8,867,400 observations spanning 272 laboratory tests—with 227,505 patients (34.0%) getting a white blood cell count during their final year—merely knowing *whether a test was ordered* (ignoring its numeric result) predicted 3-year survival in 233 of 272 tests (86%) at Bonferroni-adjusted $P < 0.05$ ($P < 0.000184$). Adjusted for age, sex, and race, mortality odds rose ($\mathrm{OR} > 1$) in 211 tests (78%) and fell ($\mathrm{OR} < 1$) in 22 (8%). Specifically, 3-year survival fell from 95.0% to 92.9% when a white blood cell count was ordered ($\mathrm{OR} = 1.45$, $P < 0.001$) (Agniel, Kohane, & Weber 2018). Identical rows yield opposite widths when empty entries go unauthored.

Walley (1991) captures incomplete knowledge using a **credal set** $\mathcal{P} \ne \varnothing$ of admissible probability distributions $P$ over $(\Omega, \mathcal{F})$. For any event $A \in \mathcal{F}$, lower and upper previsions bracket the plausible range:

$$\underline{P}(A) = \inf\_{P \in \mathcal{P}} P(A), \qquad \overline{P}(A) = \sup\_{P \in \mathcal{P}} P(A).$$

Read aloud, $\underline{P}(A)$ is the tightest pessimistic chance and $\overline{P}(A)$ is the tightest optimistic chance compatible with the frame. Shrinking $\mathcal{P}' \subset \mathcal{P}$ without evidence fakes precision, while conditioning on $B \in \mathcal{F}$ ($\underline{P}(B) > 0$) can widen (*dilate*) intervals. Under signature `(2, 1, 0)`, lacking an *ex ante* authorship log naming who fixed $(\Omega, \mathcal{F})$, $B$, and blank-field codes allows post-hoc frame trimming to counterfeit epistemic certainty.

## 2. Severity: the width of a credal set is an evidential distance

Here, $T\_2$ (`T2Severity`) protects against fake precision by refusing to shrink uncertainty intervals below what the missingness and evidence genuinely support. An imprecise credence $[\underline{P}(A), \overline{P}(A)]$ is not a normal estimate waiting for more samples: while parametric prior spread narrows as $n$ grows, identification ambiguity from missing or selected records stays wide no matter how big $n$ gets.

Decision theory separates ambiguity from decision margin. Consider two acts $d\_0, d\_1$, bounded measurable loss $L(d, \omega)$, and loss differential $\Delta(\omega) = L(d\_0, \omega) - L(d\_1, \omega)$ across credal set $\mathcal{P} \ne \varnothing$. Act $d\_1$ beats $d\_0$ under distribution $P$ when $\int \Delta \mkern3mu dP > 0$. Walley's (1991) maximality rule issues a strict preference only when both ends of the expected differential agree on sign:

$$\inf\_{P \in \mathcal{P}} \int \Delta \mkern3mu dP \qquad \text{and} \qquad \sup\_{P \in \mathcal{P}} \int \Delta \mkern3mu dP.$$

In plain words, if $d\_1$ beats $d\_0$ under both the worst and best admissible priors in $\mathcal{P}$, the choice is decisive; if the interval crosses zero, the evidence is ambiguous. Under weak compactness (or Walley's linear previsions), infimum and supremum attain $\min/\max$ for continuous $\Delta$, though discontinuous $\Delta$ can miss attainment under Borel weak compactness.

Two numbers summarize that bracket: $\sup\_{P}\int \Delta\mkern3mu dP - \inf\_{P}\int \Delta\mkern3mu dP$ measures **epistemic ambiguity**, while under a shared strict sign $\min(\lvert\inf\_{P}\int \Delta\mkern3mu dP\rvert,\lvert\sup\_{P}\int \Delta\mkern3mu dP\rvert)$ gives the **margin to reversal**. Pretending ambiguity is zero claims unearned precision rather than test severity.

Mayo (2018) fixes `T2Severity`: a test is severe only if it would rarely pass a false claim, never because a prior assumption looked tidy. Under arbitrary missingness on bounded outcomes, Manski's (1990, 2003) worst-case interval stays flat as $n$ rises, while Imbens & Manski (2004) add $O(1/\sqrt{n})$ sampling scatter via an adaptive critical constant.

Choice rules still depend on which tail drives harm. Under $\Gamma$-minimax ($\min\_d \sup\_{P \in \mathcal{P}} \mathbb{E}\_P[L(d)]$, analogous to Gilboa & Schmeidler 1986), when loss $L(d, \omega)$ rises with adverse event $A$ ($L(d, A) > L(d, A^c)$), worst-case loss sits at **upper** prevision $\overline{P}(A)$, **not** $\underline{P}(A)$ (which governs decreasing loss or gain). Rejecting paralyzing universal maximin (Harsanyi 1975; [Inference and Evidence](35-inference-and-evidence.md) §6; [The Triad and the Minimum](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md) §2, §6), Stochology routes intervals into three regimes ([Decision, Loss, Threshold](36-decision-loss-threshold.md) §3; [The Triad and the Minimum](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md) §5):

- **Regime I — Buffered Action ($\inf\_{P}\int\Delta\mkern3mu dP > 0$, e.g., $\underline{P}(A) > p^\ast\_{\mathrm{CL}}$) / Safe Abstention ($\sup\_{P}\int\Delta\mkern3mu dP < 0$, or non-lethal $U\_t \le C$):** Apply Walley maximality when both ends agree on sign.
- **Regime II:** Under reversible ambiguity straddling zero, apply **Minimax Regret** $a^\ast\_{\mathrm{MMR}} = (U\_t - C)/(U\_t - L\_t)$.
- **Regime III:** Under irreversible stakes straddling zero, escalate or halt.

Locking the command frame in *télos* ([Layer 06](56-layer-formal-computable-type-order.md)) and routing out-of-frame shifts through attested reopening avoids silent dynamic inconsistency under dilation. Keynes (1921) separated probability value from evidential weight: extra rows inside the frame shrink random scatter while Manski ambiguity stays untouched. Framing (`T1`, $P\_3 \to P\_2$) and severity (`T2`, $P\_2 \to P\_1$) are distinct crossings in [Aim and the Three Planes](29-aim-and-the-three-planes.md), with severity inheriting its bounds from the frame.

## 3. Formalization: the scale is a commitment, and post-processing cannot undo it

Here, $T\_4$ (`T4Formalization`) protects measurements from illegal arithmetic—such as averaging survey ratings or ordinal grades as if numbers shared physical units. Stevens (1946) classified scales by which transformations preserve their meaning, later axiomatized by Krantz, Luce, Suppes & Tversky (1971), separating **invariant** from **equivariant** statistics ([Measuring and Erring](33-measuring-and-erring.md) §2–§3; [The Non-Identical, Scale Invariance, Convexity, and Heavy Tails](46-non-identical-asymmetry-heavy-tails.md) §5.2):

- **Nominal:** Bijections. Modes, counts, contingency tables and entropy are invariant; arithmetic operations—first the mean—are forbidden.
- **Ordinal:** Strictly increasing maps. Order statistics (odd-$n$ medians or quantile intervals), ranks and dominance are admissible; means and rank distances are forbidden.
- **Interval:** Positive affine maps $x \mapsto ax + b$ ($a > 0$). Difference ratios and Pearson $r$ are invariant, means and standard deviations equivariant; unit fixed, zero arbitrary, raw ratios forbidden.
- **Ratio:** Similarity maps $x \mapsto ax$ ($a > 0$). Raw ratios ($x > 0$) and coefficients of variation ($\mu \ne 0$) are invariant, geometric means ($x > 0$) equivariant; natural zero required.

Those bans bite immediately in data analysis. For any strictly increasing relabeling $f$ and observations $x = (x\_1, \dots, x\_n)$,

$$\operatorname{mean}\bigl(f(x)\bigr) \neq f\bigl(\operatorname{mean}(x)\bigr) \quad \text{in general}, \qquad \operatorname{median}\bigl(f(x)\bigr) = f\bigl(\operatorname{median}(x)\bigr) \quad (n \text{ odd}).$$

In plain words, stretching an ordinal scale flips sample averages unpredictably, while the middle observation (odd-$n$ median) moves cleanly with the scale. For even $n \ge 2$ using the midpoint $\frac{1}{2}(x\_{(n/2)} + x\_{(n/2+1)})$, nonlinear $f$ still breaks equivariance and can flip group rankings, confirming Stevens's (1946) ban on averaging ordinal ranks unless the median stays an order-statistic pair.

Empirical practice routinely breaks that ban. Reviewing 2016 psychology articles using Likert items in *JPSP* (38), *Psychological Science* (20), and *JEP:G* (20; 78 found, 10 excluded), Liddell & Kruschke (2018) found all 68 of 68 studies (100%) analyzing ordinal outcomes used metric models and zero used ordinal models. On Amazon *Drama* movie ratings (36 movies, 502 to 111,232 reviews), metric averages produced opposite-direction inversions (Cases 5 and 6) and lost power even under nearly equal metric standard deviations (Liddell & Kruschke 2018).

Nonparametric rank comparisons avoid that trap within clear limits. The Mann–Whitney index $\hat{\theta}\_{\mathrm{MW}}(X,Y)=\frac{1}{mn}\sum\_{i=1}^m\sum\_{j=1}^n(\mathbf{1}\lbrace x\_i>y\_j\rbrace+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{1}\lbrace x\_i=y\_j\rbrace)$ ([Probability as Language](30-probability-as-language.md); $m,n\ge 1$) stays invariant under **common strictly** increasing $f$ (preserving inequalities $\mathbf{1}\lbrace f(x\_i)>f(y\_j)\rbrace=\mathbf{1}\lbrace x\_i>y\_j\rbrace$, though weaker than first-order stochastic dominance and potentially intransitive), while clinical rubrics demand calibrated survival cutoffs ([The Non-Identical, Scale Invariance, Convexity, and Heavy Tails](46-non-identical-asymmetry-heavy-tails.md) §5.7.2).

Ordinal scoring also blocks causal point identification under `T4Formalization` (Goff 2022). Let observed category $R\_i=r(H\_i,V\_i)$ map latent outcome $H\_i=h(X\_i,U\_i)$ through weakly increasing style $V\_i\in\mathcal{V}$ ($r(h,v)\le r\iff h\le\tau\_v(r)$) under Stevens's (1946) monotone group. Even with shared $r(\cdot)$, the latent average treatment effect $\mathbb{E}[H\_i\mid X\_i=x']-\mathbb{E}[H\_i\mid X\_i=x]=\int\_0^1\lbrace Q\_{H\mid X=x'}(u)-Q\_{H\mid X=x}(u)\rbrace\mkern3mu du$ cannot even be signed from $\mathbb{E}[R\_i\mid X\_i=x']-\mathbb{E}[R\_i\mid X\_i=x]=\int\_0^1\bar{r}'\_{x',x}(u)\lbrace Q\_{H\mid X=x'}(u)-Q\_{H\mid X=x}(u)\rbrace\mkern3mu du$ if quantiles cross, because weights $\bar{r}'\_{x',x}(u)\ge 0$ encode unknown thresholds (Goff 2022). Under $\lbrace X\_i\perp V\_i\rbrace\mid W\_i$ and $\lbrace X\_i\perp U\_i\rbrace\mid(W\_i,V\_i)$, continuous derivatives $\partial\_{x\_j}\mathbb{E}[R\_i\mid x,w]$ identify marginal effects only up to the positive scale factor $\mathbb{E}[\sum\_r f\_H(\tau\_{V\_i}(r)\mid x,V\_i,w)\mid W\_i=w]$ (and $\sum\_r f\_H(\tau\_v(r)\mid x,v,w)$ at fixed $V\_i=v$). Consequently, single slopes lack units, derivative ratios cancel the scale to identify relative effects, discrete contrasts need §2 partial-identification bounds, and treatment-shifted styles $V\_i$ inject threshold bias (Goff 2022).

Subgroup differences in scoring cutoffs or slopes—differential item functioning (DIF)—mimic real latent gaps unless audited anchor-free. Across ordinal items $Y\_{ij}\in\lbrace 1,\dots,M\_j\rbrace$ ($m=1,\dots,M\_j-1$) and latent classes $\xi\_i\in\lbrace 0,\dots,K\rbrace$ ($\theta\_i\mid\xi\_i=k\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu\_k,\sigma\_k^2)$, baseline $\mu\_0=0$, $\sigma\_0^2=1$, $\delta\_{1j0}=\delta\_{2j0}=0$), Wallin & Huang (2026) fit $\operatorname{logit}(P(Y\_{ij}\le m\mid\theta\_i,\xi\_i))=\tau\_{jm}-(a\_j+\delta\_{2j\xi\_i})\theta\_i+\delta\_{1j\xi\_i}$ to spot uniform ($\delta\_{1jk}$) and slope ($\delta\_{2jk}$) DIF without pre-verified anchor items or named demographic groups.

Identification still relies on structural sparsity (Wallin & Huang 2026). Without anchors, affine shifts $\theta=b\_k+c\_k\theta^\ast$ ($c\_k>0$) leave likelihoods identical via $\delta^\ast\_{1jk}=\delta\_{1jk}-b\_k(a\_j+\delta\_{2jk})$, $\delta^\ast\_{2jk}=c\_k(a\_j+\delta\_{2jk})-a\_j$, $\mu^\ast\_k=(\mu\_k-b\_k)/c\_k$, and $\sigma^\ast\_k=\sigma\_k/c\_k$, confounding latent location and scale with DIF. Adding $\ell\_1$ penalty $\lambda\sum\_{j=1}^J\sum\_{k=1}^K(\lvert\delta\_{1jk}\rvert+\lvert\delta\_{2jk}\rvert)$ restores identification only when DIF is sparse: pervasive DIF spawns fake classes, latent classes remain exploratory clusters rather than demographics, and post-selection refits break naive tests (Wallin & Huang 2026).

Cardinal comparability therefore rests on institutional commensuration (Espeland & Stevens 1998). In [Appendix A.4](appendix-a-mathematics.md#a4-leontief-structural-complementarity-of-the-nuclear-triad), for $\tau\in(0,1)$ and $C^1$ strictly increasing maps $\psi\_a:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ ($\psi\_a(1)=1$), viable-box volume $\prod\_a(1-\psi\_a(\tau))$ transforms by $\prod\_a\psi\_a'(S\_a)$ and—within affine rescalings $\psi\_a(x)=\alpha\_a x+(1-\alpha\_a)$ ($0<\alpha\_a\le 1$)—recovers $(1-\tau)^3$ only at $\alpha\_a=1$, whereas nonlinear warps can mimic $(1-\tau)^3$. Box volume is a chart-dependent measure, not an invariant.

Ultimately, a scale $\sigma$ collapses states into symbols, and post-processing cannot undo an information loss. Whenever $\sigma(s\_1)=\sigma(s\_2)$ for distinct states $s\_1\neq s\_2$, every downstream formula sees $T(\sigma(s\_1))=T(\sigma(s\_2))$. Without an *ex ante* rule fixing $\sigma$, merging categories later counterfeits margin; Hacking (1995), Porter (1995) and Scott (1998) trace how scales reshape classified people, strip context from numbers, and blind central maps to unmeasured local variation.
