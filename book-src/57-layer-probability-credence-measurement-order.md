This order sits where the corpus stops treating a number as a report and starts treating it as a sentence
somebody uttered in a language somebody chose. The type order fixes what can be said, the metrological order
decomposes the uncertainty of what was said, and the credence between them is what both presuppose.

## 1. Framing: a credence is a sentence before it is a number

A credence does not attach to the world. It attaches to a proposition, and a proposition exists only inside a
language that individuates it. Kolmogorov (1933) states the axioms over a measurable space $(\Omega,
\mathcal{F})$, and the space is an argument of the theory: the axioms constrain values on members of
$\mathcal{F}$ and say nothing about which subsets belong to $\mathcal{F}$. Cox (1946) reaches the same
priority from the other side, since an algebra of plausibility that respects Boolean structure is isomorphic
to a probability, and Savage (1954) makes it explicit by taking states as primitive and acts as functions on
them, so no act is contingent on a state the space does not contain.

The frame decides which propositions can carry a value at all: a proposition
whose truth set is not in $\mathcal{F}$ is not assigned a small probability but none, and the absence is not a
reading. A small number and no number look alike in a table, and only one of them is evidence.

A frame that excludes a state has already answered the question it claims to leave open. If $\omega$
lies outside $\Omega$, every act returns the same value on $\omega$ as on nothing at all, so no decision can
depend on it; the model has asserted that $\omega$ does not matter, with the authority it later uses to report
a probability. The assertion is about the world, it was made before any evidence arrived, and no later
evidence reviews it, because the evidence is collected inside the frame the exclusion produced.

A blank field has no semantics until someone declares one. Read it as *not applicable* and the credal
set is a singleton at the observed fraction; read it as *not recorded* and every blank row may belong to the
event, so the upper prevision rises toward one. Same data, different width, and the difference is the price of
a field whose empty value was never defined. A declared semantics for the empty symbol is the premise, and without it
the arithmetic has no subject.

Walley (1991) gives the general form. A credence is a set $\mathcal{P}$ of distributions over the frame, with

$$\underline{P}(A) = \inf\_{P \in \mathcal{P}} P(A), \qquad \overline{P}(A) = \sup\_{P \in \mathcal{P}} P(A),$$

so the interval summarises a set defined relative to $(\Omega, \mathcal{F})$. Widen the frame and the infimum
ranges over distributions that must also price the states just admitted; narrow it and the interval contracts
with no observation added. The contraction is not evidence but the arithmetic of a smaller question, and a
report that shows the interval without the frame has shown the answer to a question it did not name.

## 2. Severity: the width of a credal set is an evidential distance

An imprecise credence $[\underline{P}(A), \overline{P}(A)]$ is routinely read as a point credence with an
error bar, and the reading costs decisions. Were the width an error bar it would shrink with the sample and be
a property of the estimator. It does neither: it states how far the evidence must move before a decision taken
on it changes, and that is what this order means by severity.

The form is exact. With two acts, a loss $L(d, \omega)$ and $\Delta(\omega) = L(d\_0, \omega) - L(d\_1,
\omega)$, the second act is preferred under one distribution when $\int \Delta \mkern3mu dP > 0$; under a
credal set the ranking is decided only when the sign is the same throughout, that is when

$$\min\_{P \in \mathcal{P}} \int \Delta \mkern3mu dP \qquad \text{and} \qquad \max\_{P \in \mathcal{P}} \int \Delta \mkern3mu dP$$

fall on the same side of zero. What matters is therefore not the width of $A$ but the projection of the credal
set onto the loss differential, and its extent is the distance a datum must travel.
The same credal set can be severe for one decision and vacuous for
another, because the projection depends on $\Delta$. And a point credence is the degenerate case in which the
distance is zero — not modesty but the strongest severity claim available, since it asserts that no admissible
distribution lies off the point. Finite data plus a point is a claim of unlimited discriminating power,
invisible in the numeral.

Mayo (2018) supplies the vocabulary: severity is a property of a design, not of a result, and the question is
how probable a less favourable outcome would have been had the claim been false. The credal width is that
quantity one level up, fixed before the data by the frame and its premises and unmoved by accumulation inside
it. Manski's (1990, 2003) worst-case bounds show the invariance cleanly: with a bounded outcome and arbitrary
selection the identification width does not depend on sample size. Imbens & Manski (2004) let the two widths
meet, since the critical value for a partially identified parameter is a function of that width measured in
standard errors.

A decision on the lower bound is a decision on the worst case the frame allows, and the phrase carries two
commitments. Minimax: where the loss is monotone in the event, the act with the smallest worst-case expected
loss over $\mathcal{P}$ is the act chosen at $\underline{P}$. And the worst case is relative to
$\mathcal{P}$, so the adversary the rule plays against is the one the frame admits. A frame that excludes a
catastrophe produces a mild worst case and a confident decision, and the confidence is an artefact of the
exclusion. Keynes (1921) separates the value of a probability from its weight, and severity needs the first
while the second says whether more searching would help: the first two sites are one mechanism, and severity
borrows its bound from framing.

## 3. Formalization: the scale is a commitment, and post-processing cannot undo it

Every number above is a reading on a scale, and the scale is where the order becomes formal. Stevens (1946)
defines a scale by its group of permissible transformations, and the definition states which statistics are
admissible: a statistic not invariant under the group reports the labelling as though it were a property of
the measurand (Stevens 1946; Krantz, Luce, Suppes & Tversky 1971).

- **Nominal.** All one-to-one substitutions. Counts, modes and equivalence are licensed; every arithmetic
  operation is forbidden, the mean first among them.
- **Ordinal.** The strictly increasing maps. Medians, quantiles and rank statistics are licensed; the mean and
  every function using the distances between ranks are forbidden, because those distances were never measured.
- **Interval.** The positive affine maps $x \mapsto ax + b$. Means, variances and standard deviations are
  licensed; a unit is required, the origin is arbitrary, and a ratio of two readings is forbidden.
- **Ratio.** The similarity maps $x \mapsto ax$. Ratios, geometric means and coefficients of variation are
  licensed; a natural zero is required, and a conventional one does not qualify.

The prohibitions are the operative half. For strictly increasing $f$ and a sample $x$,

$$\operatorname{mean}\bigl(f(x)\bigr) \neq f\bigl(\operatorname{mean}(x)\bigr) \quad \text{in general},
\qquad \operatorname{median}\bigl(f(x)\bigr) = f\bigl(\operatorname{median}(x)\bigr),$$

so two codings equivalent under the scale's own group return different means and the same median. The repair
is the dominance index of Chapter 30, which counts the pairs on which one sample exceeds the other and is
invariant across the group, because $\mathbf{1}\lbrace{}f(x\_i) > f(y\_j)\rbrace = \mathbf{1}\lbrace{}x\_i > y\_j\rbrace$ for every
increasing $f$, while the arithmetic mean moves with the relabelling. Averaging a triage code fabricates an
interval whose unit is whatever spacing the coder happened to use.

Cardinality is not a stronger version of order but a different commitment. An interval scale needs a common
unit before two readings can be added, and a ratio scale needs a zero belonging to the measurand rather than
to the convention. Where two sites are measured in different units an aggregate requires a commensuration,
which is an act, not a discovery (Espeland & Stevens 1998). Appendix A.4 carries the arithmetic: the
volume of the viable box is $\prod\_a \bigl(1 - \varphi\_a(\tau)\bigr)$ under a change of chart and equals
$(1-\tau)^3$ only in the unit chart, so it is a Lebesgue measure in the declared chart and not an invariant of
the institution.

The last clause is the one no later step repairs. A scale is a map $\sigma$ from states to symbols, and every
statistic is a function of $\sigma(x)$. If $\sigma(x\_1) = \sigma(x\_2)$ for two states the scale does not
separate, then $T(\sigma(x\_1)) = T(\sigma(x\_2))$ for every statistic $T$: post-processing cannot invert a
non-injective map. A distinction destroyed at the scale is destroyed for every estimator, model and audit
downstream, and no quantity of data recovers it, because the data no longer contain it. That is what makes the
choice of scale a formal commitment and not a reporting convention, and why this is the primary site of
the order. Hacking (1995), Porter (1995) and Scott (1998) each describe the same commitment arriving
on the world side, as a classification that feeds back into the classified, a number that travels without the
judgment behind it, and an administration that measures what it can see.

## 4. The governance axial exposure: a signature with no third coordinate

The triple is `{T1Framing, T2Severity, T4Formalization}` and the signature is `(2, 1, 0)`: two sites on the
epistemic axis, one on the vital axis, none on the governance axis. The zero is not a low grade. It states what
the order cannot represent, and what it cannot represent is the act by which the frame was chosen.

A governance coordinate would carry three readings the order does not. Ex ante it would record who authorised
the frame and the premise that narrowed the bound, $g\_{\text{ex-ante}}$ in Appendix A.3; in real time, who may
contest the reading while the decision is still open; ex post, who answers for the decision taken at the lower
bound. The axis score is the minimum of the three, so an order with no site on the axis has no reading on any
of them, and the predicate's demand for a guard on every axis is met only because the axis is not in the
triple. The trajectory is well formed and the transit is absent, which is the quieter fault.

The absence is not innocence. It is unopposed capture, in three steps.

First, framing is a decision, and a decision with no site is a decision with no author in the record. The
frame's exclusions are made somewhere, by whoever defines the register, drafts the categories or chooses the
outcome, and with no governance coordinate that act is not a step in the model. It is therefore not judged,
not contestable and not attributable. The record will show a probability and will not show the hand that
decided which states could have one.

Second, severity is bounded by the frame, so the capture is not visible as a number. The worst case the lower
bound reports is the worst case among admitted states, and an excluded catastrophe does not raise it. An order
can be maximally severe about its own frame and silent about the state that would have reversed the decision,
and the silence does not appear as a low reading; it does not appear at all. A weak governance reading is a
number that can be argued with, and an absent one leaves nothing to argue with.

Third, formalisation ratifies both. Once the scale has destroyed a distinction no later statistic recovers
it, so the capture is not merely unrecorded but irreversible within the order. March & Simon (1958) name the
mechanism: uncertainty absorption, by which a number absorbs the uncertainty of those who supplied it where it
is aggregated, and the absorber is the one who then decides. The absorber needs no authority to absorb; the
act is invisible by construction. It is the form capture takes when nobody has to be corrupted.

This is the collapse the triple yields, and Chapter 56 records the exposure regime that names it. Cynical
capture needs no villain inside the order. It needs an order in which the doubt is expressible and the frame is
not, and then the doubt becomes the alibi: the interval is quoted, the width displayed, the decision taken at
whichever end the taker prefers, because nothing says who may choose the end. Vickers (1965) called that
missing function appreciation; Beer (1972) called the missing path the algedonic channel; Goodhart (1975) and
Espeland & Sauder (2007) describe what follows when a measure becomes the only channel and is optimised
not reported.

So the order can be formally clean and severe while nothing in it can be captured, because nothing in it can
record a capture. A governance coordinate would not make the order more rigorous; it would make it answerable,
and answerability is what distinguishes an unopposed frame from an agreed one. The zero in the signature is
not a gap in the argument but a fact about it: the order can say what may be believed and what a decision can
survive, and cannot say who decided which states were admissible.

## By the end of this chapter you can

1. State the frame decision every credence presupposes, and show with the empty-field case why two readings of
   one register differ in width while sharing every observation.
2. Read an imprecise credence as a severity statement: derive the decision-relevant interval from the loss
   differential, and say why its width is invariant to sample size under worst-case bounds.
3. State the invariance group of each measurement scale, the statistic it forbids, and why a non-injective
   scale cannot be repaired by any later statistic.

## Bridge

The order now has its three sites: a credence is typed by the frame that admits it, its width is the distance
evidence must travel before a decision moves, and its scale fixes the group under which the reading is
invariant. The next chapter is [Chapter 58, *Layer 08: Metrological & Information-Geometric Order*](58-layer-metrological-information-geometric-order.md).
