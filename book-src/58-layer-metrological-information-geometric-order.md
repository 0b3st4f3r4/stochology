## 1. Type A and type B uncertainty

Imagine weighing a steel bracket ten thousand times on a digital scale whose calibration weight is slightly chipped. Averaging ten thousand readings shrinks random jitter toward zero, yet every measurement remains off by the exact same silent offset.

Here, $T\_3$ (`T3Archive`) protects metrological records by keeping sample scatter separate from calibration bias, instrument drift, and unmeasured flaws. The **GUM** and **VIM** (BIPM/JCGM 2008/2012) divide **evaluation methods** rather than random versus systematic error ([Chapter 33, *Measuring and Erring*](33-measuring-and-erring.md)): **type A** evaluates uncertainty statistically from repeated observations, while **type B** evaluates certificates, scale resolution, drift, and definitional limits. Known bias $b \neq 0$ is subtracted directly, leaving only residual correction error inside $u\_B(y) \ge 0$ (uncorrected bias adds linearly).

To combine both parts for a scalar estimate $y$, look first at general smooth functions $y = f(x\_1,\dots,x\_m)$ with sensitivities $c\_i = \partial f/\partial x\_i$ and input variances $u^2(x\_i)$, where GUM sets combined variance $u\_c^2(y) = \sum\_i c\_i^2 u^2(x\_i) + 2\sum\_{i<j} c\_i c\_j u(x\_i, x\_j)$. For a single averaged quantity ($m=1, c\_1=1$) over $n > 0$ readings with baseline unit-level type-A variance $\sigma\_A^2 \ge 0$ (prior to cluster and weight inflation $\mathrm{DEFF} \ge 1$; [Chapter 09](09-sets-relations-functions.md) sets; [Chapter 34](34-sample-population-archive.md) archive weights), and uncorrelated type-B variance $u\_B^2(y)$, total squared uncertainty $u\_c^2(y; n)$ is:

$$u\_c^2(y; n) = \frac{\sigma\_A^2\mkern3mu\mathrm{DEFF}}{n} + u\_B^2(y),$$

where $\mathrm{DEFF}$ tracks cluster and weight inflation ([Chapter 34](34-sample-population-archive.md); [Chapter 45](45-world-to-archive-big-data-paradox.md)). Read aloud, taking more readings shrinks only the first term (as $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} = n/\mathrm{DEFF} \to \infty$ under finite variance $\alpha\_{\mathrm{tail}} > 2$), while the type-B floor $u\_B^2(y)$ never budges. Worse, fixed cluster count $C\_{\mathrm{cl}}$ with intra-cluster correlation $\rho\_I > 0$ (distinct from Meng's $\rho\_{R,Y}$ in §3) hits a cluster ceiling, while positive shared drift correlation ($r\_{AB} > 0$, reaching $|u\_A| + |u\_B|$ at $r\_{AB} = +1$ or under unknown-correlation conservative bounding, whereas $r\_{AB} < 0$ subtracts covariance) pulls combination toward linear sums $|u\_A| + |u\_B|$. Finite-$n$ certificates must always report $u\_c(y; n)$ beside $u\_B$.

Vector measurements extend this rule and reveal serious practice gaps. For vector $\mathbf{Y} = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{X}) \in \mathbb{R}^p$ with Jacobian $\mathbf{C} = (\partial f\_k/\partial x\_j)$ and input covariance $\mathbf{U}\_X$, the first-order law of propagation of uncertainty (LPU) gives $\mathbf{U}\_Y = \mathbf{C}\mkern2mu\mathbf{U}\_X\mkern2mu\mathbf{C}^\top$. LPU couples shared inputs cleanly, yet fails under curvature, non-Gaussian inputs, and non-ellipsoidal bounds. **GUM Supplements 1 and 2** (JCGM 101:2008; JCGM 102:2011) instead propagate input density $g\_X(\boldsymbol{\xi})$ via Monte Carlo (MC) to check LPU when moments are finite and $\mathbf{f}$ holds.

Training across European metrology labs lags badly behind those multivariate tools (Klauenberg et al. 2024). Across 43 collected courses (with 34 surveyed following European NMIs and the JRC; overlapping 15 NMI, 24 calibration/testing-lab, 6 legal, and 16 university), LPU coverage spanned 13/15, 23/24 (>90%), 6/6, and 15/16. Yet MC propagation covered only 7/15, 8/24 (33%), 1/6, and 9/16; LPU-vs-MC validation covered 7/15, 8/24 (33%), 1/6, and 6/16; and multivariate LPU+MC dropped to 3/15, 2/24 (<10%), 0/6, and 2/16 (Klauenberg et al. 2024).

Three non-quadrature forms in this book echo that split between scatter and bias:

- **Selection paradox:** Meng's (2018) identity ([Chapter 45](45-world-to-archive-big-data-paradox.md)) adds variance to squared selection bias $\frac{1-f}{f}\bar{\rho}^2\sigma\_Y^2$, staying $O(1)$ at fixed coverage $f \in (0, 1)$.
- **Partial identification:** Imbens & Manski's (2004) margin adds interval width $U\_t - L\_t$ **linearly** ([Chapter 44](44-underdetermination-partial-identification.md); [Appendix A.1](appendix-a-mathematics.md)).
- **Discrepancy and custody:** Governance ([Chapter 48](48-reactive-worlds-performativity-goodhart.md)) pairs noise with signed Kennedy & O'Hagan (2001) discrepancy $\delta\_{\mathrm{KOH}}(x)$, while bitemporal traceability (Snodgrass 2000; [Chapter 29](29-aim-and-the-three-planes.md)) enforces the **lower bound** $u\_{\mathrm{down}} \ge u\_{\mathrm{up}}$.

## 2. The Fisher–Rao manifold, Bregman projections, and `T6Performative`

Here, $T\_4$ (`T4Formalization`) protects distance measurement across curved probability spaces, while $T\_6$ (`T6Performative`) protects calibration ledgers from labs gaming reported scores.

On smooth regular families ($p > 0$, shared support, positive-definite $g\_{ij}$), extending C. R. Rao's (1945) differential geometry of parameter spaces, Čencov's (1972) theorem fixes the Fisher–Rao metric $g\_{ij}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}\_\theta[\partial\_i\ln p(X;\theta)\mkern3mu\partial\_j\ln p(X;\theta)]$ as unique **up to positive scale $c > 0$** under **Markov morphisms and sufficient statistics** (originally on finite spaces, with coordinate invariance holding generally).

Compare the 1D and 2D Gaussian cases cleanly. On the **1D known-$\sigma$ Gaussian slice** ($\sigma > 0$), P. C. Mahalanobis's (1936) covariance-normalized metric turns squared distance $(\hat{\mu} - \mu\_0)^2/\sigma^2$ to zero-drift target $\mu = \mu\_0$ (distinct from §1's correlation $r\_{AB}$ and [Chapter 47](47-irreversible-time-vital-margin.md)'s $f = 2f^\ast$) into $d\_{\mathrm{FR}}^2$, matching unsquared radius $d\_{\mathrm{FR}} = |\hat{\mu}-\mu\_0|/\sigma$ to Type-A resolution $1/\sqrt{n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}}$. On 2D $\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2)$, Poincaré hyperbolic curvature shortens Fisher–Rao geodesics between equal-variance endpoints $(\hat{\mu}, \sigma)$ and $(\mu\_0, \sigma)$ ($\hat{\mu} \neq \mu\_0$) strictly below $|\hat{\mu}-\mu\_0|/\sigma$ (whereas unequal variances $\hat{\sigma} \neq \sigma$ at $\hat{\mu} = \mu\_0$ yield positive geodesic distance $\sqrt{2}\lvert\ln(\hat{\sigma}/\sigma)\rvert > 0$). Strictly on that 1D fixed-$\sigma^2$ slice, predictive variance $\sigma\_{\mathrm{pred}}^2 = \sigma^2(1 + 1/n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}})$ yields Kelly shrinkage $k(n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}) = (1 + 1/n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}})^{-1}$ (motivated by Baker & McHale 2013; [Chapter 47](47-irreversible-time-vital-margin.md) §6.1.2), whereas unknown $\hat{\sigma}^2$ in 2D needs stronger shrinkage.

Divergence projections formalize model mismatch geometrically. Take a **Legendre-type** (strictly convex, essentially smooth) generator $\phi$ (Legendre conjugate $\phi^\ast$, ensuring $\nabla\phi(p^\ast)$ exists on $\mathrm{ri}(\mathrm{dom}\mkern2mu\phi)$), point $y \in \mathrm{ri}(\mathrm{dom}\mkern2mu\phi)$, and closed convex set $C$ meeting $\mathrm{ri}(\mathrm{dom}\mkern2mu\phi)$ with bounded sublevels. Bregman's (1967) divergence $D\_\phi(x \parallel y) = \phi(x) - \phi(y) - \langle\nabla\phi(y), x - y\rangle \ge 0$ reduces to Kullback–Leibler (1951) divergence from negative entropy **strictly on the normalized simplex** ($\sum x\_i = \sum y\_i = 1$). Under finite $\mathbb{E}[|X|],\mathbb{E}[|\phi(X)|] < \infty$ (failing for quadratic $\phi$ at $\alpha\_{\mathrm{tail}} \le 2$), Jensen's (1906) gap equals $\mathbb{E}[D\_\phi(X \parallel \mu)] = \Delta\_J \ge 0$. Under $e$/$m$ duality, $I$-projection $p^\ast = \arg\min\_{p \in C} D\_\phi(p \parallel y)$ satisfies the Pythagorean **inequality** $D\_\phi(q \parallel y) \ge D\_\phi(q \parallel p^\ast) + D\_\phi(p^\ast \parallel y)$ for all $q \in C$, because the three-point identity gives $D\_\phi(q \parallel y) - D\_\phi(q \parallel p^\ast) - D\_\phi(p^\ast \parallel y) = \langle \nabla\phi(p^\ast) - \nabla\phi(y),\mkern3mu q - p^\ast \rangle \ge 0$: **equality holds if and only if $\langle \nabla\phi(p^\ast) - \nabla\phi(y),\mkern3mu q - p^\ast \rangle = 0$** (identically across all $q \in C$ for affine subspaces meeting $\mathrm{ri}(\mathrm{dom}\mkern2mu\phi)$ or when $y \in C$, and also non-trivially for boundary points $q \in C \setminus \lbrace p^\ast \rbrace$ lying on an exposed supporting face orthogonal to $\nabla\phi(p^\ast) - \nabla\phi(y)$ on non-affine closed convex sets; bounding Type-B mismatch against population $y$, not sample $\hat{p}\_n$), while exponential MLE executes dual $m$-projection.

Forecast scoring turns that geometry into calibration audits. Pre-sorting $n$ forecasts $p$ against outcomes $y$ across levels $z\_1 < \dots < z\_k$ before Pool-Adjacent-Violators (PAV) gives the **CORP** split of mean proper score $\bar{S}(p, y) = \mathrm{MCB} - \mathrm{DSC} + \mathrm{UNC}$ (Dimitriadis, Gneiting & Jordan 2021; keeping score $\bar{S}$ distinct from Triad coordinates $S\_{\mathrm{epi}}, S\_{\mathrm{vital}}, S\_{\mathrm{gov}}$). Under quadratic Bregman Brier loss ($D\_\phi(p, y) = \frac{1}{n}\Vert p - y\Vert^2$, where PAV computes the isotonic projection onto the monotone cone $\lbrace \pi\_1 \le \dots \le \pi\_k \rbrace$), dual-cone Moreau orthogonality zeroes $\langle y - \hat{\pi}, \hat{\pi} - \bar{y}\mathbf{1}\rangle = 0$ inside $\mathrm{DSC}$. By contrast, miscalibration $\mathrm{MCB} = \mathrm{BS}(p) - \mathrm{BS}(\hat{\pi}) = \frac{1}{n}\Vert p - \hat{\pi}\Vert^2 + \frac{2}{n}\langle p - \hat{\pi}, \hat{\pi} - y\rangle$ keeps a **non-negative cone cross-term** when PAV merges bins. It matches Murphy's 1973 $\mathrm{REL}$ only when empirical rates increase monotonically ($\bar{o}\_1 \le \dots \le \bar{o}\_k$; [Chapter 37](37-model-fit-and-error.md)) and needs out-of-fold debiasing ([Chapter 48](48-reactive-worlds-performativity-goodhart.md)).

Thus **$T\_6$ (`T6Performative`)** governs metrology directly: rewarding low $u\_B$ or $\mathrm{MCB}\_{\mathrm{CORP}}$ (Goodhart 1975) invites *certificate shopping*, performative shift $\mathcal{D}(\theta)$ (Perdomo et al. 2020), and trivial climatological forecasts ($\mathrm{MCB}\_{\mathrm{CORP}} = \mathrm{DSC}\_{\mathrm{CORP}} = 0$). Brake 1 ([Chapter 50](50-methodological-families-reflexivity.md)) and independent inter-laboratory key comparisons block that gaming.

Three sharp limits bound uncertainty sums:

1. **Resolution crossover:** For digitizer step $\delta > 0$, quantization error $u\_{\mathrm{res}} = \delta/\sqrt{12}$ catches statistical scatter $s\_{\mathrm{sample}}\sqrt{\mathrm{DEFF}/n}$ (with sample standard deviation $s\_{\mathrm{sample}}$, distinct from score $\bar{S}$ and Triad $S\_a$) at sample count $n^\star = 12\mkern2mu\mathrm{DEFF}\mkern2mu s\_{\mathrm{sample}}^2/\delta^2$ (while i.i.d. noise $s\_{\mathrm{sample}} > \delta$ dithers the step).
2. **Boundary curvature:** On $\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\theta)$ ($\theta \in (0, 1)$), Fisher information $1/[\theta(1-\theta)]$ gives geodesic $|2(\arcsin\sqrt{\theta\_1} - \arcsin\sqrt{\theta\_0})|$, resolvable at $1/\sqrt{n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}}$ inside $(0, 1)$ while failing at endpoints $\lbrace 0, 1\rbrace$.
3. **Circular traceability:** Calibration chains enforce $u\_{\mathrm{down}} \ge u\_{\mathrm{up}}$ only inside the calibration archive's $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{F}\_{\mathrm{cal}}$.
