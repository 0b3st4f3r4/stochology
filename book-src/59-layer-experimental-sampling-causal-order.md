## 1. The fundamental problem, and the three gates

Imagine two surveys trying to measure public support or vaccine uptake: a random probability poll of a few thousand households and a giant opt-in web survey ten times larger. When self-selected participants skew toward one group, the massive dataset gets the answer confidently wrong while the tiny random poll stays close to the mark.

Here, `L09` examines why pure methodological rigor—without physical or institutional guardrails—still breaks. Causal inference studies missing potential outcomes (Neyman 1923; Fisher 1935; Rubin 1974). Given binary treatment $D\_i \in \lbrace 0, 1\rbrace$, no interference and one version of treatment (SUTVA), and consistency ($Y\_i = Y\_i(D\_i)$), every unit's individual effect $\tau\_i = Y\_i(1) - Y\_i(0)$ stays partly unobserved.

Randomization restores comparison by forcing $(Y\_i(1), Y\_i(0)) \perp D\_i$ under positive assignment odds ($0 < \pi\_i := \mathbb{P}(D\_i = 1) < 1$). Uniform odds identify average treatment effect $\mathrm{ATE}$ via difference in means $\hat{\tau}\_{\mathrm{DM}} = \bar{Y}\_1 - \bar{Y}\_0$; unequal odds require Horvitz–Thompson (1952) weights $w\_i = D\_i/\pi\_i + (1-D\_i)/(1-\pi\_i)$ (with variance governed by pairwise inclusion probabilities). Under Fisher's sharp null ($Y\_i(1)=Y\_i(0)$), effect variance $S\_\tau^2=0$; otherwise, under a fixed-arm completely randomized experiment ($\pi\_i \equiv n\_1/N$ under sampling without replacement, distinct from unequal-$\pi\_i$ Horvitz–Thompson designs), Neyman's finite-population variance ($N=n\_1+n\_0$; $n\_1,n\_0\ge 2$; finite-population variances $S\_1^2, S\_0^2, S\_\tau^2$ distinct from Triad axis $S\_{\mathrm{epi}}$) equals $\mathrm{Var}(\hat{\tau}\_{\mathrm{DM}}) = S\_1^2/n\_1 + S\_0^2/n\_0 - S\_\tau^2/N$, making $s\_1^2/n\_1 + s\_0^2/n\_0$ conservative whenever $\tau\_i$ varies since $\rho(Y(1),Y(0))$ stays unobserved. Non-experimental archives lack randomization and require three epistemic gates.

The first gate is framing ($T\_1$, `T1Framing`), which protects against cherry-picking one flattering model variant by auditing all defensible specifications upfront ([question before the data](32-the-question-before-the-data.md); [inquiry openness](43-inquiry-openness-multiverse.md)). Because raw archives allow many valid coding choices, reporting one $p$-crossing invites $p$-hacking. Steegen et al. (2016) inspect full processing multiverses while refusing scalar collapse, whereas Simonsohn, Simmons, and Nelson (2020) rank specification curves and test median effects, sign shares, and Stouffer statistics under correlation. Reporting the closed estimate range $[\min\_k \hat{\beta}\_k,\mkern3mu \max\_k \hat{\beta}\_k]$ alongside Wang and Ramdas's (2022) $e$-BH over pre-registered specification *$e$-values* $E\_1, \dots, E\_K$ controls false discoveries under arbitrary dependence. Simple confirmation ratios fail under near-duplicate specs, confuse sign flips with low power, and miss Type III omitted groups; multiverse sweeps belong offline before live decisions ([Chapter 43](43-inquiry-openness-multiverse.md)).

The second gate is severity ($T\_2$, `T2Severity`), which protects against claiming causal proof when unmeasured confounding leaves wide [partial identification](44-underdetermination-partial-identification.md) bounds. On Dehejia–Wahba's `NSW` training subset (185 treated, 260 controls), randomization yields $+\unicode{x24}1{,}794$, while non-random `CPS` comparison rows give $-\unicode{x24}8{,}498$ (Dehejia & Wahba 1999)—reconcilable by propensity overlap trimming only under untestable conditional ignorability. Without ignorability, Manski's (1990, 2003) bounds on bounded outcome $Y \in [L, U]$ ($L < U$, $R \equiv 1$) keep width $U - L$ invariant to sample size $n$, while Lee's (2009) monotone trimming ($R(1) \ge R(0)$, positive retention) assumes randomization and bounds only always-observed units.

The third gate is the archive ($T\_3$, `T3Archive`), which protects sample counts from inflating precision when clusters echo or selective missingness skews the database ([sample and archive](34-sample-population-archive.md); [Big Data paradox](45-world-to-archive-big-data-paradox.md)). Survey design theory drew from P. C. Mahalanobis's (1944, 1946) cost–variance designs and interpenetrating sub-samples in India, Kish (1965) on unequal selection weights, and Moulton (1986) on within-cluster correlation in $\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{cluster}}$. Under random-effects variance inflation with non-negative intra-cluster correlation $\rho\_I \in [0, 1]$ (distinct from Meng's $\rho\_{R,Y}$ below; $\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{cluster}} \ge 1$, ensuring $\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{total}} \ge 1$ and variance contraction $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} \le n$, distinct from fixed-cluster equicorrelated negative-ICC models bounded only by positive-semidefiniteness $1 + (m\_{\max} - 1)\rho\_I > 0$; [Appendix A](appendix-a-mathematics.md) §A.1), and unit-level weights independent within clusters and uncorrelated with cluster membership and cluster effects in the large-$C$ limit, **the exact composition is additive** ($\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{total}} = \mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{cluster}} + \mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{Kish}} - 1$, [Chapter 34](34-sample-population-archive.md) §3; [Appendix A](appendix-a-mathematics.md) §A.1); under cluster-constant or shock-correlated weights, multiplying both factors yields only a conservative upper bound.

Effective sample counts shrink along two margins. First, design clustering reduces nominal count $n$ to variance-effective count $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} = n/\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{total}} > 0$. Next, under selective reporting indicator $R\_i \in \lbrace 0, 1\rbrace$ (distinct from physical vital reserves; $f = n/N \in (0, 1)$, spread $\sigma\_Y > 0$), Meng's (2018) bias scales as $\rho\_{R,Y}\sqrt{(1-f)/f}\mkern3mu\sigma\_Y$ ([Chapter 34](34-sample-population-archive.md) §2; [Chapter 45](45-world-to-archive-big-data-paradox.md)). That bias compresses $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}$ down to harmonic count $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho}) < n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}$ at declared $\bar{\rho} \ge |\rho\_{R,Y}| > 0$, while excluded $p\_i^{\mathrm{samp}} \equiv 0$ strata vanish completely.

Real-world vaccine tracking reveals how fast big datasets collapse under selection bias (Yang et al. 2023). Paralleling US overestimation against smaller probability polls (Bradley et al. 2021), India's COVID-19 Trends and Impact Survey (`CTIS`; median 25,000 weekly responses, 1% response rate) deviated from CoWIN uptake (median 33.24%, range 15.08–59.95%) by weekly median error 0.39 [0.24, 0.43] and weighted `ddc` 0.0040 (absorbing $\sqrt{\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{Kish}}}$), whereas CVoter's smaller probability survey (median 2,700 responses, 55% response rate) held median error to 0.16 [0.06, 0.20] and `ddc` 0.0005 (Yang et al. 2023).

Selection crushed CTIS's median weekly $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}$ down to 2 (roughly 99.99% loss) versus 9 for CVoter. Inside India's `CTIS`, tracking week-to-week successive differences and relative successive differences recovered median weekly $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}$ of 1,922 (10.18% of $n$, median error $-0.003$) and 2,929 (14.94% of $n$, $-0.039$), respectively—unless selection $\rho\_{R\_t,Y\_t}$ jumped across waves (Yang et al. 2023).

A single scalar $\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{total}}$ also mis-scales hierarchical Bayesian models (Lee, Williams, & Savitsky 2026). For grand mean $\mu$, fixed effects $\beta\_k$, and $J\ge 2$ centered group random effects $\theta\_g = \theta\_g^{\mathrm{tot}} - \mu$, comparing sandwich covariance $V\_{\mathrm{target}}=H^{-1}J\_c H^{-1}$ (penalized posterior Hessian $H$, stratified cluster score covariance $J\_c$) to unadjusted pseudo-posterior covariance $\Sigma\_{\mathrm{MCMC}}$ defines parameter-level ratio $\mathrm{DER}\_k=[V\_{\mathrm{target}}]\_{kk}/[\Sigma\_{\mathrm{MCMC}}]\_{kk}$.

Under balanced Gaussianity, shared $\mathrm{DEFF}$, prior precision $a > 0$, group likelihood precision $\tau\_{\mathrm{prec}} > 0$ (distinct from treatment effects $\tau\_i$), and data-precision shrinkage weight $B = \tau\_{\mathrm{prec}}/(a + \tau\_{\mathrm{prec}}) \in (0, 1)$, $\mathrm{DER}\_{\beta\_k}=\mathrm{DEFF}(1-\omega\_k)$ (between-group covariate share $\omega\_k\in[0,B]$), $\mathrm{DER}\_{\theta\_g}^{\mathrm{cond}}=B\cdot\mathrm{DEFF}$, $\mathrm{DER}\_{\theta\_g}=B\cdot\mathrm{DEFF}\bigl(1-(J(1-B)+B)^{-1}\bigr)$, and $\mathrm{DER}\_\mu+\mathrm{DER}\_{\theta\_g}^{\mathrm{cond}}=\mathrm{DEFF}$ (Lee, Williams, & Savitsky 2026). Algebraically, simply rescaling likelihood precision by $1/\mathrm{DEFF}$ ($\tau\_{\mathrm{prec}} \mapsto \tau\_{\mathrm{prec}}/\mathrm{DEFF}$) without scaling prior precision $a$ inflates conditional group-effect variance by $(a+\tau\_{\mathrm{prec}})/(a + \tau\_{\mathrm{prec}}/\mathrm{DEFF}) = \mathrm{DEFF}/\bigl(1+(\mathrm{DEFF}-1)(1-B)\bigr)>1$ ($\mathrm{DEFF}>1, B\in(0,1)$) even where $\mathrm{DER}\_{\theta\_g}^{\mathrm{cond}}<1$. Worse, $\mathrm{DER}\le 1$ tests variance alone (missing $\rho\_{R,Y}\ne 0$) and excludes hyperparameters and heterogeneous $\mathrm{DEFF}\_j$ (Lee, Williams, & Savitsky 2026).

## 2. Two reports, two populations

The NSW–CPS contrast compares two distinct populations rather than two estimates of one group. Inside a randomized trial on $Y \in [L, U]$ ($L < U$) with arm attrition rates $a\_t, a\_c \in [0, 1)$, Manski attrition width equals $(a\_t + a\_c)(U - L)$ regardless of $n > 0$. External `CPS` controls add observational confounding spanning width $U - L$, placing $-\unicode{x24}8{,}498$ outside the experimental interval (Dehejia & Wahba 1999) and leaving `L09` exposed on $S\_{\mathrm{vital}}$ and $S\_{\mathrm{gov}}$ without physical slack or institutional vetoes.

### Two limits, an estimate that passed every gate, and a design effect with nothing to inflate

Sampling precision behaves differently from identification ambiguity. While attrition width $(a\_t + a\_c)(U - L)$ stays fixed in $n$, the Imbens–Manski (2004) sampling margin adds $c\_\alpha \hat{\sigma}/\sqrt{n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}}$ ($n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} > 0$) outside $[\hat{\theta}\_L, \hat{\theta}\_U]$ under equal endpoint standard deviations $\hat{\sigma}\_L = \hat{\sigma}\_U = \hat{\sigma}$ (or conservative $\hat{\sigma}\_{\max} = \max(\hat{\sigma}\_L, \hat{\sigma}\_U)$ when they differ; [Appendix A.1](appendix-a-mathematics.md)). Quadrupling $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}$ halves that sampling margin at $\hat{\Delta}\_{\mathrm{id}} = 0$ and large-set limits—and shrinks it strictly faster than half for $\hat{\Delta}\_{\mathrm{id}} > 0$ as $c\_\alpha \in [z\_{1-\alpha}, z\_{1-\alpha/2}]$ drops with $\sqrt{n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}}\mkern2mu\hat{\Delta}\_{\mathrm{id}}$—yet any positive bias bound $\bar{\rho}>0$ ($f\in(0,1)$) compresses $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\bar{\rho})$ and hierarchical parameters still demand `DER`.

Even an unbiased internal trial misses target population shifts. Given subgroup effects $\tau\_1, \tau\_0$ and enrolled versus target shares $q\_e, q\_t \in [0, 1]$, averages diverge by $(q\_e - q\_t)(\tau\_1 - \tau\_0)$ no matter how large $n$ grows. External transportability demands overlap ($0 < q\_e < 1$ wherever $q\_t > 0$), location invariance ($\tau\_s^{\mathrm{enrolled}} = \tau\_s^{\mathrm{target}}$), component homogeneity, and zero scale-up congestion ([Layer 02](52-layer-meso-buffered-queue-order.md)) or network spillovers across external reception perimeters ([Layer 03](53-layer-macro-coupled-topology-order.md); $\Theta\_{\mathrm{basin}}$) (Hotz, Imbens & Mortimer 2005; Bareinboim & Pearl 2016).

Design effects also assume finite variance. Whenever heavy tails satisfy Pareto exponent $\alpha\_{\mathrm{tail}} \le 2$ (distinct from Imbens–Manski level $\alpha$), support is **unbounded**—any bounded $[L, U]$ retains finite moments—so $\mathrm{Var}(Y) = \infty$ breaks normal intervals, Manski bands explode to $(-\infty, \infty)$, and calm caps create an anti-Gettier trap ([model error](37-model-fit-and-error.md); [partial identification](44-underdetermination-partial-identification.md)). Analysts must check tail index first, limit $\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{total}}$ to weight–cluster diagnostics and bounded-support bounds to physical ceilings ($-\infty < L < U < \infty$), and send unbounded tails to Filter II's Hill/GPD and $\mathrm{CVaR}\_p$ or $\mathrm{CRPS}$ ($\alpha\_{\mathrm{tail}} > 1$; [heavy tails](46-non-identical-asymmetry-heavy-tails.md)).

Confidential archives add a custody split ([Chapter 45](45-world-to-archive-big-data-paradox.md); [Chapter 74](74-layer-critical-archival-transitional-justice-order.md)). Pure $\varepsilon$-differential privacy ($\varepsilon>0$) bounds single-record likelihood shifts by $e^\varepsilon$ via Laplace noise scale $\Delta\_1 q/\varepsilon$ (Dwork, McSherry, Nissim, & Smith 2006b), while $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-differential privacy adds tail slack $\delta\in[0,1)$ risking exposure unless $\delta\ll 1/n$ rather than $\delta\sim 1/n$ (Dwork, Kenthapadi, McSherry, Mironov, & Naor 2006a).

Pre-estimation perturbation carries sharp statistical costs. Adding noise breaks commutation with nonlinear estimators ($\hat{\theta}(\mathcal{K}(X))\ne\mathcal{K}(\hat{\theta}(X))$), erases multi-list doubletons $f\_2$ in Chao's $\hat{n}\_{00}$, and—under mutually uncorrelated zero-mean post-sampling noise $Z$ ($\sigma\_Z>0$, $\mathrm{Cov}(Y, Z) = \mathrm{Cov}(R, Z) = 0$)—inflates variance $\sigma\_{Y+Z}^2=\sigma\_Y^2+\sigma\_Z^2$ and attenuates selection correlation $|\rho\_{R,Y+Z}|=|\rho\_{R,Y}|\sigma\_Y/\sqrt{\sigma\_Y^2+\sigma\_Z^2}<|\rho\_{R,Y}|$ ($\rho\_{R,Y}\ne 0$) without lowering bias. Differentially private releases should publish $\varepsilon,\delta,\sigma\_Z^2$ for aggregates while keeping record linkage, $f\_2$, and $\rho\_{R,Y}$ audits under off-shift sealed custody.
