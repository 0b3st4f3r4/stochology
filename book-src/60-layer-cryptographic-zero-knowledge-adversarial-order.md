## 1. The strategic adversary, and perfect secrecy

Picture proving at a door that you are over twenty-one without revealing your birthday, name, or address—while a clever eavesdropper tries not only to trick your math protocol, but to time how many microseconds your chip takes to answer or hammer neighboring memory cells to flip bits.

Communication theory fights random static; cryptography fights a live opponent who adapts to public rules. Here, $T\_6$ (`T6Performative`) protects protocol guarantees against an adversary who reads the algorithm and actively games every public step. Starting with al-Kindī's ninth-century statistical cryptanalysis in Baghdad—which broke substitution ciphers using empirical letter frequencies across long texts (Mrayati, Meer Alam & at-Tayyan 2003)—adversarial modeling has forced security premises into the open. Under Kerckhoffs (1883), secrecy lives solely inside the **secret key**; under the Dolev–Yao model (1983), an active network attacker intercepts, replays, and recombines messages over unbroken symbolic ciphers.

Shannon (1949) defined perfect secrecy by requiring ciphertext $C$ to tell an attacker nothing new about message $M$: $P(M = m \mid C = c) = P(M = m)$, or entropy equality $H(M \mid C) = H(M)$ for independent key $K$. Unique decoding ($H(M \mid K, C) = 0$) enforces $H(K) \ge H(K \mid C) \ge H(M)$, breaking secrecy immediately if a key is reused once total message entropy exceeds $H(K)$. Against polynomial-time attackers, one-way functions buy pseudorandomness, commitments, and zero-knowledge proofs, while trapdoor functions power public-key cryptography.

## 2. Soundness as an e-process, and the commitment seal

Here, $T\_2$ (`T2Severity`) ensures verification rounds evaluate over frame-indexed event algebras $(\Omega, \mathcal{F})$ and severity-bounded rejection cutoffs inherited from [Layer 07](57-layer-probability-credence-measurement-order.md) like a genuine test-side *e*-process bounding a cheating prover's odds, while $T\_7$ (`T7Liability`) locks commitments down so nobody can alter a sealed claim after challenges land.

Interactive proofs let a prover convince a polynomial-time verifier without leaking secret witnesses (Goldwasser, Micali, & Rackoff 1985, 1989). Completeness accepts true claims; soundness blocks **computationally unbounded** cheaters (unlike computational *arguments*); and zero-knowledge guarantees an efficient simulator for **every** cheating verifier $V^\ast$ carrying auxiliary knowledge.

Sequential challenges enforce strict anytime error control. With fresh verifier coins and per-round fraud probability $\mathbb{P}\_{H\_0}(\text{pass}\_j \mid \mathcal{F}\_{j-1}) \le \varepsilon\_{\mathrm{sound}} \in (0, 1)$ across rounds $j = 1, \dots, k$ (since adaptive provers correlate steps), define $E\_0 = 1$, set $E\_j = 0$ as an **absorbing** state for round $j$ and all subsequent rounds once any step fails, and set $E\_j = \varepsilon\_{\mathrm{sound}}^{-j}$ only when rounds $1, \dots, j$ all survive. Under false null $H\_0$, $E\_j$ forms a nonnegative test supermartingale ($\mathbb{E}\_{H\_0}[E\_j \mid \mathcal{F}\_{j-1}] \le E\_{j-1}$)—matching the cryptographic analog of [Layer 11](61-layer-biochemical-kinetic-molecular-order.md)'s proofreading ratio. By Ville's inequality (Ville 1939), stopping at $1/\alpha$ ($\alpha \in (0, 1)$, $1/\alpha > 1$) bounds false acceptance **at most $\alpha$** ($\le \alpha$), whereas an additive leakage $\nu > 0$ ($\mathbb{P}\_{H\_0}(\text{pass}\_j \mid \mathcal{F}\_{j-1}) = \varepsilon\_{\mathrm{sound}} + \nu$) inflates conditional step growth on surviving trajectories ($E\_{j-1} > 0$) to $1 + \nu/\varepsilon\_{\mathrm{sound}} > 1$ and breaks the supermartingale. Sequential rounds preserve $\varepsilon\_{\mathrm{sound}}^k$ and zero-knowledge at $k$-round latency; parallel repetition shrinks delay to $O(1)$ yet can break black-box simulation and $\varepsilon^k$ decay in arguments.

Under `T7Liability`, commitment $\mathrm{Com}(m; r)$ pairs **binding** (locking out $m' \ne m$) with **hiding** (masking $m$ with fresh randomness $r$ or a keyed MAC per Krawczyk, Bellare, & Canetti 1997, since bare hashes expose low-entropy guesses). No non-interactive commitment enjoys both unconditionally. Paired with linear typestate ([Layer 06](56-layer-formal-computable-type-order.md)) and temporal vaults ([Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md)), seals prove **post-seal** custody from dispatch $t\_{\mathrm{disp}}$ forward.

## 3. Side channels, silicon degradation, and microarchitectural attestation

Ignoring physical timing and silicon physics triggers **Collapse Proposition 2 (*Impotent Lucidity Before Ruin*)**. Data-dependent branches, modular shortcuts, and cache hits leak keys over timing side channels omitted from abstract proofs (Kocher 1996). Asymmetric latency breaks $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} \ge \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$ before checking finishes, and cheap request floods saturate expensive verifiers unless stopped by stateless cookies, MAC pre-filters, or rate limits.

Physical failure reaches down to whether silicon DRAM even keeps bits steady. Testing $129$ commercial DRAM modules manufactured from $2008$–$2014$, Mutlu & Kim (2019) triggered RowHammer bit flips—caused by hammering neighbor rows so electrical charge leaks before the $64\text{ ms}$ refresh window—in $110$ modules, letting unprivileged code corrupt keys or page tables beneath kernel walls. Refresh margin, strong multi-bit ECC (since standard single-error-correcting, double-error-detecting (SECDED) misses multi-bit RowHammer faults), and row isolation are vital prerequisites ($S\_{\mathrm{vital}}$): otherwise proofs verify software invariants on mutating hardware.

When kernel compromise blinds local guards, systems turn to **remote microarchitectural attestation**. Under **HammerWatch** (Herrmann et al. 2026), a prover hashes ECC Machine-Check Exceptions (`MCE`s) and DDR5 Per-Row Activation Counting (`PRAC`) Alert Back-Off (`ABO`, pre-recovery up to 180 ns) via SHA-256 into a RowHammer-immune `TPM` `PCR` seeded post-reboot by a verifier nonce and transmits RSA-encrypted logs over fresh TLS. Across 20,000 simulated patterns (10,000 benign, 10,000 malicious), thresholds below three `MCE`s and three `ABO` alerts posted zero false positives and zero false negatives—yet unexposed `PRAC` blocks runtime cost measurement, `Rowpress` rarely trips `ABO` (demanding physical refresh and row-isolation margins rather than `ABO` counters alone), and reboot log wipes reopen `T3Archive` blind spots (Herrmann et al. 2026). Under [Layer 07](57-layer-probability-credence-measurement-order.md)'s missingness taxonomy, a reboot-wiped log is `NOT_OBSERVED` rather than `MEASURED_ZERO`—requiring pre-reboot monotonic fault counters persisted in non-volatile `TPM` NVRAM or external `T3Archive` escrow so volatile resets never masquerade as clean hardware.

## 4. Two arithmetics, one seal, and the challenge that is not independent

Given per-round soundness bound $\varepsilon\_{\mathrm{sound}} \in (0, 1)$ and target false-acceptance risk $\alpha \in (0, 1]$ ($\alpha = 1$ gives $k^{\star}(1) = 0$; Ville's strict $1/\alpha > 1$ needs $\alpha \in (0, 1)$), the *e*-process $E\_k = \varepsilon\_{\mathrm{sound}}^{-k}$ crosses $1/\alpha$ at round count $k^{\star}(\alpha)$:

$$k^{\star}(\alpha) = \left\lceil \frac{\ln(1/\alpha)}{\ln(1/\varepsilon\_{\mathrm{sound}})} \right\rceil.$$

Read aloud, pushing false acceptance down by a factor of ten adds a constant number of verification rounds fixed by the per-round error rate. Each round costing time $\tau\_{\mathrm{round}} > 0$ must satisfy $k^{\star}(\alpha)\mkern3mu\tau\_{\mathrm{round}} \le \mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}}$.

Verification servers obey queue congestion too. In a $GI/G/1$ verifier queue (i.i.d. arrival rate $\lambda > 0$, service rate $\mu > 0$, load $\rho = \lambda/\mu \in (0, 1)$, finite second moments $c\_a^2, c\_s^2 < \infty$ under tail exponent $\alpha\_{\mathrm{tail}} > 2$), Kingman's (1961) heavy-traffic asymptote as $\rho \to 1^{-}$ scales expected wait as $(1/\mu)\cdot\frac{c\_a^2 + c\_s^2}{2}\cdot\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}$ ([Layer 02](52-layer-meso-buffered-queue-order.md)). For slack $s = 1 - \rho \in (0, 1)$, halving slack drives $f(\rho) = \rho/(1-\rho)$ to $f(1 - s/2) = 2f(1 - s) + 1 > 2f(1 - s)$, more than doubling delay; when $1 < \alpha\_{\mathrm{tail}} \le 2$ under $\rho < 1$ ($c\_s^2 = \infty$), waiting times remain stationary under Lindley's recursion yet expected wait diverges ($\mathbb{E}[W\_q] = \infty$) and Kingman's finite-variance asymptote fails, whereas $\rho \ge 1$ or infinite-mean $\alpha\_{\mathrm{tail}} \le 1$ destroys stationarity altogether.

Unfiltered dispute floods into human review ([Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md); [Layer 33](83-layer-distributed-consensus-sre-order.md)) trigger **Compliance DoS** across Beer's algedonic channels ($S3^\ast$, distinct from Triad $S\_{\mathrm{vital}}$), burning $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}}$. Attestation logs display this `(1, 0, 2)` trap directly: valid-MAC, valid-TLS oversized logs from compromised provers bypass unauthenticated pre-filters so RSA decryption and `PCR` replay push $\rho \to 1^{-}$ into `Compliance DoS`, while dropping large logs creates benign false alarms and pruning weak indicators barely cuts decryption load (Herrmann et al. 2026)—requiring strict per-prover attested log-size and compute ceilings whose breach trips deterministic **Regime III-B** fail-closed isolation rather than flooding human auditors ($\approx 10\text{ bits/s}$; Zheng & Meister 2025).

At the archival seam, a digest or MAC certifies **post-seal** custody from $t\_{\mathrm{disp}}$ onward, remaining blind to pre-seal exclusions (`T3Archive` missing across `L06` and `L07` in $\mathrm{Cone}(L\_{10})$) and premise rigging ($\mathrm{PRI}$, [Chapter 28](28-the-machine-and-the-deception.md); [Chapter 43](43-inquiry-openness-multiverse.md); [Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md)), which is why sealing depends on an external $T\_1 \leftrightarrow T\_3$ authorship and capture–recapture lock ahead of $t\_{\mathrm{disp}}$. Inside post-seal custody, an $n$-bit hash separates **collision** ($\sim 2^{n/2}$), **second-preimage** ($\sim 2^n$), and **preimage** ($\sim 2^n$) resistance. In the first practical SHA-1 collision, two distinct PDFs shared one $160$-bit digest in $2^{63.1}$ compressions—roughly $6{,}500$ CPU years and $100$ GPU years, over $100{,}000$ times faster than the $2^{n/2} = 2^{80}$ birthday bound (Stevens, Bursztein, Karpman, Albertini & Markov 2017). Cryptographic rotation therefore requires fixed non-self-extending sunsets and bitemporal reopening under Brake 3 ([Chapter 50](50-methodological-families-reflexivity.md)), especially against *harvest-now, decrypt-later* threats where migration time ($\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{migration}}$) races secrecy lifetime ($\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{secrecy}}$) under `T5Temporal`.

Replacing live verifier coins with a transcript hash turns interactive proofs into computational arguments in the **random-oracle model** (Fiat & Shamir 1986), losing unconditional soundness. Trying $Q \ge 1$ offline hashes raises per-try fraud $\varepsilon \in (0, 1)$ to $1 - (1 - \varepsilon)^Q \le \min(1, Q\mkern3mu\varepsilon)$. Worse, hashing each of $k$ binary rounds ($\varepsilon\_{\mathrm{sound}} = 1/2$) separately collapses grinding cost from $2^k$ queries down to $k/\varepsilon\_{\mathrm{sound}} = 2k$. For parallel composition of 3-round $\Sigma$-protocols (or special-sound public-coin proofs), sound Fiat–Shamir binds domain, public statement $x\_{\mathrm{stmt}}$, and all $k$ initial commitments inside one joint hash ($Q\mkern3mu\varepsilon\_{\mathrm{sound}}^k \le \alpha$), whereas adaptive sequential $k$-round proofs whose later commitments depend on prior challenges require **chained prefix hashing** of the running transcript under round-by-round soundness. It likewise forbids **nonce reuse** $r$ in prime-order-$q$ Schnorr $\Sigma$-protocols ($z \equiv r + c\mkern2mu x\_{\mathrm{sk}} (\mathrm{mod}\mkern3mu q)$), where distinct challenges $c\_1 \not\equiv c\_2 \pmod q$ expose secret key $x\_{\mathrm{sk}} \equiv (z\_1 - z\_2)(c\_1 - c\_2)^{-1} (\mathrm{mod}\mkern3mu q)$ (keeping response $z$ distinct from queue slack $s$ and secret key $x\_{\mathrm{sk}}$ distinct from statement $x\_{\mathrm{stmt}}$).

Multi-party threshold signing removes single-key points of failure while sharpening that nonce discipline. Combining verifiable secret sharing (Feldman 1987) with two-round threshold Schnorr signatures (`FROST`; Komlo & Goldberg 2021; Connolly et al. 2024) binds every signing nonce atomically to one ceremony tuple and consumes it on stable storage before sending round-two shares—so power loss cannot trigger key-leaking replay. Honest signers verify every share directly instead of trusting a coordinator, turning equivocation or bad shares into self-proving fraud evidence distinct from network timeouts, while hardware modules pair signing with immediate post-sign verification against physical fault injection.

Microarchitectural telemetry remains dual-use: probes built to detect silicon faults can enable bit-flip exploits or covert spying. Under `T7Liability` ([Chapter 27](27-what-judging-is.md); [Chapter 40](40-institution-incentive-metric.md)), governance confines attestation to authorized defensive audits over minimal logs—barring offensive exploits, unconsented export across external reception perimeters ($\Theta\_{\mathrm{basin}}$), and warrantless surveillance—while refusing to let compliance lists replace live accountability (Brake 4, [Chapter 50](50-methodological-families-reflexivity.md)).
