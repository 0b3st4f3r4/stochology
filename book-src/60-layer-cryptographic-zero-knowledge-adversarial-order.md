## 1. The strategic adversary, and perfect secrecy

Classical communication theory models noise as passive entropy in a memoryless channel; cryptographic engineering begins with an adaptive
adversary in control of that channel. Under Kerckhoffs' formulation (1883) a cryptosystem must remain secure even if the
adversary knows everything except the private key, and in the Dolev–Yao model (1983) that adversary controls the network, synthesising and
altering arbitrary messages, so a guarantee must withstand active strategic performativity.

Shannon's foundation (1949) is the entropy identity: perfect secrecy requires $P(M = m \mid C = c) = P(M = m)$, equivalent to $H(M \mid
C) = H(M)$, which forces the key equivocation to be at least the message entropy and hence non-reusable keys. Computational cryptography
relaxes the absolute bound to polynomial-time adversaries through one-way trapdoor functions.

## 2. Soundness as an e-process, and the commitment seal

An interactive proof convinces a polynomial-time verifier and leaks nothing beyond the validity of the claim (Goldwasser, Micali & Rackoff 1989): an honest verifier accepts a
true assertion, no malicious prover convinces it of a false one with per-round soundness error above $\varepsilon\_{\mathrm{sound}} \in (0, 1)$ — hence cumulative error at most $\varepsilon\_{\mathrm{sound}}^k$ ($\le 2^{-k}$ when $\varepsilon\_{\mathrm{sound}} \le 1/2$) across $k$ independent rounds —
and a simulator reproduces the transcript. Soundness is a test supermartingale: under the null $H\_0$ that the claim is false, starting at $E\_0 = 1$, setting $E\_k = 0$ on any failed challenge, and multiplying the evidence capital to $E\_k = \varepsilon\_{\mathrm{sound}}^{-k}$ when $k$ challenges are survived gives $\mathbb{E}\_{H\_0}[E\_k \mid \mathcal{F}\_{k-1}] \le E\_{k-1}$, so by Ville's maximal inequality the probability that $E\_k$ ever reaches $1/\alpha$ (falsely certifying the claim at level $\alpha$) across any stopping rule is at most $\alpha$.

On the liability axis a commitment scheme binds an agent to a hypothesis before the environment reveals its state. A digest over the sealed
content, a single-opening record that is consumed when opened, and a verified seal on the sentence that carries the claim are the three
mechanisms, and together they make retrospective rationalisation detectable.

## 3. Why the layer carries a vital axial exposure

The layer unites severity, performativity and liability, completing its stratum's coverage of all seven sites, and holds zero vital margin sites.
A system that relies on cryptographic guarantees while neglecting physical time falls to **Collapse Proposition 2** in three ways. Timing side
channels leak keys through memory access latency, because a mathematical proof of correctness assumes an instantaneous abstract machine (Kocher
1996). Asymmetric verification latency exceeds the survival horizon of the physical process, so an industrial robot or a microgrid reaches ruin
before the signature is validated. Denial of service drives the verification queue toward saturation, where Kingman delays explode. A proof of
knowledge says what an adversary cannot do and nothing about how long the honest party takes to be convinced; between offering a signature and
validating it lies a queue made of the seconds the process is consuming.

## 4. Two arithmetics, one seal, and the challenge that is not independent

The guarantee is priced in adversary advantage and spent in time. Do the division before the design is frozen. With per-round
soundness error $\varepsilon\_{\mathrm{sound}}$ the evidence after $k$ rounds is $\varepsilon\_{\mathrm{sound}}^{-k}$, so the anytime-valid rule
at level $\alpha$ rejects first at

$$k^{\star}(\alpha) = \left\lceil \frac{\ln(1/\alpha)}{\ln(1/\varepsilon\_{\mathrm{sound}})} \right\rceil$$

rounds. A verification horizon of fixed duration at a fixed per-round cost admits a fixed number of rounds, so the margin between
what the protocol requires and what the horizon can pay is a factor of the kind a protocol review does not print.

The queue is where the rounds are spent. A stream of proofs arriving at a verifier is a queue with arrival rate $\lambda$, verifier capacity
$\mu$ proofs per window and mean service time $1/\mu$, so utilisation is $\rho = \lambda/\mu$ and Kingman's heavy-traffic form (Kingman 1961) multiplies $1/\mu$ by the combined arrival-and-service dispersion $(c\_a^2 + c\_s^2)/2$ and by $\rho/(1-\rho)$. Halving the remaining idle slack $1 - \rho$ near saturation more than doubles the heavy-traffic multiplier, so the comparison against
the survival horizon is decided by load slack, not by the security parameter.

The seal fails the same way. A commitment makes retrospective rationalisation detectable because the content is bound to a digest before the
outcome is known, and the first public SHA-1 collision is the case where the form applies and the advice does not: two PDFs with different
visual contents shared one digest, at a cost of roughly $2^{63.1}$ calls to the compression function (Stevens, Bursztein, Karpman, Albertini &
Markov 2017). For an $n$-bit digest, collision resistance is bounded by $2^{n/2}$ and preimage resistance by $2^{n}$, so a factor of $2^{n/2}$ ($2^{80}$ for $160$-bit SHA-1) separates "cannot find a
second document" from "cannot reopen the hypothesis", and a seal needs both. Price the digest's collision resistance against
the horizon the commitment must hold over, and treat rotating a broken digest as a governance event, not an engineering chore.

The soundness account rests on a conditional expectation, and it lapses when the challenge is not independent of the transcript. Fiat–Shamir
replaces the verifier's coin with a hash of the transcript (Fiat & Shamir 1986), so the challenge becomes measurable with respect to the past and
soundness survives only in the random-oracle model, where a prover can re-draw its commitment until the derived challenge is favourable: $g$
rejections buy it a factor $g$ of advantage the martingale never counted. State soundness in the random-oracle model, price the grinding budget
inside the adversary's advantage, and forbid challenge reuse, the only part of that surface a seal check can see.
