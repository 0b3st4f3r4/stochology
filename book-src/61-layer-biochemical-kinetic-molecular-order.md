This order descends from the basal layer and from metrology: a molecular step is the smallest thing in the
book that decides, and the decision is read by an instrument that has its own resolution. It opens the edge
the immunological order takes up — [Layer 13](63-layer-immunological-self-governance-order.md)
stands on this order and on the one after it — the stratum begins here and not at the
chemistry: what a single step can certify, and what it costs in time to certify it, is the question every
later order of the living stratum restates at a larger scale.

1. Derive the Michaelis–Menten saturation law from the mass-action law and say what its ceiling is a
   property of.
2. Show why an irreversible step buys discrimination that an equilibrium step cannot, and where the price

3. Say what a kinetic readout constitutes and does not observe, and why the specificity it reports is not a
   property of the molecule alone.

## 1. Severity: what a step can certify before the product leaves

A binding site cannot be both fast and exact. The affinity between an enzyme and its substrate is set by
the same free-energy difference that sets how long the two stay together, so a site that binds the wrong
substrate only weakly also releases the right one quickly; specificity bought at the level of binding is
bought with a rate. The mass-action law states the constraint instead of evading it. For a substrate $S$
and an enzyme $E$ forming a complex and releasing product,

$$\frac{d[ES]}{dt} = k\_1[E][S] - (k\_{-1} + k\_2)[ES], \qquad v = k\_2[ES],$$

and imposing the steady state $d[ES]/dt = 0$ with the conservation $[E]\_0 = [E] + [ES]$ gives

$$v = \frac{V\_{\max}[S]}{K\_M + [S]}, \qquad V\_{\max} = k\_2[E]\_0, \qquad K\_M = \frac{k\_{-1} + k\_2}{k\_1},$$

which is the saturation law of Michaelis & Menten (1913). The output saturates above $K\_M$: the rate
stops reporting the substrate, so the instrument has a ceiling that is a design parameter and not an
empirical limit. And $K\_M$ is not the affinity. It contains $k\_2$, the rate at which the complex commits
to product, so a measure of binding read off a rate is a measure of binding contaminated by the step that
follows it: the first place in this book where the act of reading is inside the quantity read.

The quantity $f = [S]/(K\_M + [S])$ is the fraction of the enzyme that is occupied, and it is the same
variable a queue carries as its utilization: a server whose sites are filled a fraction $f$ of the time is
the enzyme's steady state read at a different scale. That shared occupancy variable is where the analogy
breaks, not an identity between the two laws: steady-state Michaelis–Menten saturation is a deterministic
hyperbolic ceiling rather than a Kingman queue driven by arrival and service variability,
and the difference is the one that matters here: the enzyme has finitely many sites, so $f$ approaches one
without reaching it and the throughput saturates, while a queue with an unbounded buffer diverges as its
utilization approaches one. Both are statements about a ceiling; only one of them is a statement about
collapse.

Severity enters when the step must discriminate and not merely bind. Hopfield (1974) and Ninio (1975)
showed that a single reversible binding event cannot much improve on the ratio of the two affinities, and
that an irreversible step inserted between binding and release can. If each such step passes the correct
substrate with probability near one and the wrong one with probability $\varepsilon\_1$, then $N$ steps
that each reset on early unbinding pass the wrong substrate with probability $\varepsilon\_1^{N}$ while the
right one survives, so the discrimination compounds multiplicatively in the number of steps (McKeithan
1995). Read as a test, the cascade is a product of per-step likelihood ratios: the quantity is nonnegative
and its expectation under the null of no discrimination is bounded by one, which is what makes the $N$-step
output a severity statement and not a score. What the step can certify is the error rate it rules out,
and that is the quantity the triple's first site names.

The severity is therefore structural, not statistical: no accumulation of observations improves it, because
the ceiling is in the mechanism. And because it is structural, it can be certified before the outcome
exists. A test that reports how strongly it could have failed has a severity in the sense this book uses,
and the $N$-step cascade has one: its per-step ratio is known from the mechanism, so the error rate the
cascade rules out is fixed at the moment of design and not after the measurement. What the reader of a
kinetic experiment is entitled to conclude is bounded above by that number, whatever the experiment
returns. An assay can average more molecules and shrink its sampling error, and the
discrimination of the cascade will not move, because the discrimination was never a sampling property. That
is the difference between this order and the experimental one: the experimental order improves a reading by
taking more of it, and this order cannot.

## 2. Time: the irreversible step, and the price written as a delay

The rate constant is a ratio between a barrier and a temperature, and that is why the order is temporal
before it is chemical. Boltzmann (1877) gives the weight of a state as $e^{-E/k\_BT}$; the fraction of a
population whose energy exceeds a barrier $E\_a$ is the tail of that weight,

$$f(E\_a) = e^{-E\_a / k\_B T},$$

so a process whose forward move requires crossing the barrier and whose attempt frequency is $\nu$ proceeds
at

$$k = \nu\, e^{-E\_a / k\_B T},$$

which is the form Arrhenius (1889) fitted to reaction rates before the statistical reading of it existed.
Nothing in that expression is a property of the molecule alone. Raise the temperature and the same
molecule is fast; change the solvent and the barrier moves with it; a catalyst is a different path with a
lower $E\_a$ and not a different substrate. The invariance this order can claim is invariance under a change
of variables: a rate is a rate at a temperature. A claim of invariance that drops the temperature is
a claim about the apparatus, not about the chemistry.

The temporal site has a second and harder content, which is that discrimination is purchased with time and
the purchase is irreversible. At equilibrium, detailed balance forces the ratio of forward to backward
fluxes to be the same for both substrates: whatever a step distinguishes on the way forward, it gives back
on the way back, so a reversible cascade cannot amplify the discrimination it starts with. The proof is one
line. If the two substrates share a catalyst and the cycle is closed, the product of rate constants around
the cycle is the same for both, so the ratio of their occupancies at equilibrium equals the ratio of their
binding constants: the cascade returns the affinity ratio it began with and adds nothing. Irreversibility
breaks the cycle: a step that cannot run backwards, followed by a reset that destroys the mark, is a step
whose discrimination is not refundable. McKeithan (1995) makes the reset explicit for the T-cell receptor:
phosphatase action on early unbinding turns a series of phosphorylations into a filter, and the
reset is the part that costs the time.

The purchase also has a currency, and the currency is why the cascade is a mechanism and not a rule. Each
irreversible step consumes a high-energy bond that the reset then hydrolyses, so the discrimination is paid
for in a quantity the cell must keep supplying, and a cell that stops supplying it loses the discrimination
without losing the enzyme. The exchange rate between error and energy is set by the same algebra: to lower
the error by a factor, the cascade must spend a bond per step per substrate, and the cost therefore grows
linearly in $N$ while the benefit grows exponentially in it. No step of that trade is a choice about how
carefully to look; the looking is the spending. This is the sense in which the order is temporal before it
is chemical, and it is also why a mechanism of this kind is inherited by any institution that needs a
discrimination cheaply: the institution finds it cheap because the price was paid earlier, by something
else, in a currency it does not keep accounts in.

So the two sites are one decision read from opposite ends. Making the error rate $\varepsilon\_1^{N}$ requires
$N$ steps, each of which takes a mean time, so the delay grows with $N$ while the error falls exponentially
in it; a mechanism that buys three more orders of discrimination buys them at the price of a longer wait
before the verdict exists. An institution that inherits such a cascade inherits both numbers and cannot
choose one. What it can choose is where in the cascade to read: read early and the verdict is fast and
weak, read late and the verdict is strong and stale, and the record of the read does not say which of the
two it is. That is the shape the epidemiological order restates as a serial interval against a tracing
delay, and the restatement is exact because the mechanism is the same.

## 3. Performance: the mark as verdict, and the readout that makes the population

The product of the cascade is a mark, a phosphate or a conformational state, and it is not a
description of the molecule. It is an instruction to the next reader, written in a form the next reader can
act on, and it exists because something downstream has a threshold that turns the mark into a decision. A
molecule is a signal only relative to that threshold: the same concentration is saturating for one reader
and invisible to another, and neither reader is wrong. The identity of the substrate — this is the same
kind of thing as that — is therefore enacted at the readout and not found in the structure. What the
cascade certifies is not that two molecules are alike; it is that a reader with this threshold cannot tell
them apart before the mark and can afterwards.

The readout also constitutes the population it reports on. A rate measured in bulk is a mean over a
distribution of single-molecule rates, and the mean is what the model then carries as *the* rate constant;
the distribution disappears into a number and the number is treated as a property of the species. Both
consequences are performative in the strict sense. The description changes what the described thing is
taken to be. An assay performed at a chosen substrate concentration selects the
regime in which the law looks first-order, and the linearity it reports is a property of the choice of
window; a selection that favours fast variants raises the mean the next assay reports, so the constant
drifts with the practice of measuring it and not with the chemistry.

There is a third loop, and it is the one that makes the mark a verdict and not a label. A kinase that
phosphorylates a site has converted a binding event into a state that persists after the event is over, and
the persistence is what allows a later reader to act on a decision it did not witness. The archive of the
cell is written in covalent marks, and like every archive it records the decision and not the evidence for
it: a mark left by a correct discrimination and a mark left by a mistaken one are the same mark. The order's
third site is not decoration. The severity of §1 is a property of a mechanism, the time of
§2 is the price of that severity, and the mark of §3 is what survives both — and what survives is what the
next order reads, with no way to recover from it which of the two it was.
