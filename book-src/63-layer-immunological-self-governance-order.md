## 1. A test with two arms that must fire in one encounter

The system has to answer, for every structure it meets, whether that structure is its own. It has no arbiter to appeal to: no court, no calibration set that is not itself the body, and no external party that could rule on the answer and be believed. What it has is a test, run continuously, on material it cannot step outside of.

Bretscher and Cohn stated the form that test takes: a response requires specificity against the presented structure and a second, costimulatory signal, and the two must arrive in the same encounter (Bretscher & Cohn 1970). Janeway's later account keeps the conjunction and changes what the second signal is taken to mean, without relaxing the requirement that it be present (Janeway 1989). Which molecule carries either arm is not the issue. Neither arm alone is a decision.

Write the encounter as a pair $(s, c)$, specificity and costimulation, and the rule as a conjunction: respond if and only if $s \ge \theta\_{s}$ and $c \ge \theta\_{c}$, both evaluated on the same encounter. The obvious alternative is a weighted sum, $s + \lambda c \ge \theta$, and the difference between the two rules is not a matter of tuning. Under the conjunction the probability of firing on a structure that is the organism's own is bounded without any assumption about the dependence between the arms:

$$P\bigl(s \ge \theta\_{s} \wedge c \ge \theta\_{c} \mid \text{self}\bigr) \mkern5mu\le\mkern5mu \min\bigl\lbrace{}P(s \ge \theta\_{s} \mid \text{self}),\mkern5mu P(c \ge \theta\_{c} \mid \text{self})\bigr\rbrace.$$

The bound is a monotonicity fact, not an independence fact, and that is why it survives contact with the biology. The two arms are positively associated: the same encounter that raises specificity also raises costimulation, so an additive rule has no bound it can state without knowing the association, while the conjunction has one that holds at every level of it. A test whose false-positive rate cannot be bounded in advance is not a test; it is a disposition.

The conjunction also does the work that severity does in an experimental design. A response is evidence that the structure is foreign only if the rule would not have fired on self. That counterfactual is what the second arm purchases: specificity without costimulation describes a great many of the organism's own molecules, so arm one alone does not discriminate and cannot be severe. The severity of the test is therefore located in the pair, and the pair is where this order's epistemic site sits.

That placement is worth stating against a natural misreading. The order might have been filed under the archive, since the immune system plainly keeps a record of what it has met. It might have been filed under framing, since what counts as a structure is not given. It is filed under severity because the question the system asks is not *how much evidence is there* but *would this rule have fired anyway*. The archive enters as the reference against which the rule is run, and the reference is the subject of the next section; the test itself is a threshold pair whose credibility depends on where the thresholds sit relative to the two error costs.

Those thresholds are not free parameters, and the way they drift is the inversion this site names. A severe rule fixes its threshold before the encounter and reports the outcome whatever it is. A rule whose threshold moves after each false negative is no longer testing anything: it is averaging its encounters and calling the average a criterion. The mark of the second is that the rule can no longer fail, and a rule that cannot fail has stopped being a guard and become a description of what happened.

## 2. The reference is a sample of the body, and the margin is time

Anderson and colleagues showed that the reference set is assembled, not given: the thymic epithelium projects a shadow of the body's own proteins, and the clones that recognise what is projected there are removed or diverted (Anderson et al. 2002). The self against which the test is run is therefore a sample of the self. It is drawn from what the projecting tissue expressed, in the window in which projection happened, and it is not the body.

A sample drawn from a population that is not exhaustively enumerable leaves classes uncovered, and an uncovered class is not a misclassification. This distinction is the whole of the epistemic difficulty here. A clone that meets a tissue absent from the shadow has not made an error of judgement about a self structure; it has met a structure about which its reference says nothing, and its rule, applied correctly, returns foreign. The failure is a coverage failure, and no improvement in the threshold repairs it, because the threshold was never the thing that was wrong.

The repertoire is revised after the developmental window, but slowly and at the margin, so the order carries an archive whose sampling frame was fixed early and inherited late. That inheritance is the reason the temporal site cannot be treated as decoration. What the test cannot see is the trajectory: each encounter is adjudicated on its own, and the quantity that decides whether the organism is well is not any single verdict but the margin between the capacity to restrain a response and the load that provokes one.

Write that capacity $C(t)$ and that load $L(t)$, and the margin $m(t) = C(t) - L(t)$. The organism does not fail when a single verdict is wrong; it fails when the margin is exhausted before restoration can arrive, which is the survival inequality stated in the vocabulary of tissue and not of inventory:

$$\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}}(m) \mkern5mu\ge\mkern5mu \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{restore}}(C),$$

where the left side is the time the remaining margin buys at the current draw-down rate and the right side is the time the suppressive arm needs to return the capacity to its baseline. An organism can be lucid about exactly what it is attacking (the response is specific, the target is known) and still cross the barrier, because the margin was thin and the restoration slow.

The two sides are not independent, and this is where the temporal site earns its place. Chronic mobilisation consumes the capacity that would otherwise restore tolerance: the same mediators that sustain a response under repeated demand impose a cumulative cost on the tissue that has to end it (Sterling & Eyer 1988; McEwen 1998). So $C$ falls as $L$ rises, and a margin sized on a snapshot is short by the coupling it ignored. A snapshot reading of tolerance treats a trajectory as a state, and states do not fail; margins do.

Canguilhem's account of health is the same claim without the algebra: health is a margin of tolerance for the inconstancies of the environment, and the capacity to institute new norms and not to hold one (Canguilhem 1943/1966). Autoimmunity, read in that vocabulary, is not the loss of a threshold. It is the collapse of the norm — the organism's own regulation becomes the destruction of its own tissue, and the destruction is carried out competently. The threshold is still applied; the reference no longer includes what the organism is.

This is why the same signal tested at two moments is two different tests. The rule is unchanged, and both of its inputs have moved: the reference set has been revised by everything met in between, and the restoration capacity has been drawn down by however much was met. An order that reports tolerance as a property of the system, without the window over which the property was measured, has reported a number that belongs to a different question.

## 3. The cost lands on the party that did not decide

The two errors are not symmetric, and the asymmetry is not a fact about the errors. It is a fact about who pays. A false positive destroys the organism's own tissue; a false negative leaves an infection to run. The clone that fires bears neither loss. It has no reserve to draw on, no tissue to lose, and no horizon over which either consequence is its own. The decision is made in one place and settled in another, and the place where it is settled is the body.

This is the structure the liability site names, and it appears here in its hardest form because there is no institution to shift the cost to. The shift is not a manoeuvre by a self-interested actor; it is the arrangement. The cortex of a lymph node does not consult the tissue it will damage, and the tissue has no channel through which to object before the damage. What an institution would call risk-shifting upward is here simply the geometry of the circuit.

Sakaguchi and colleagues described the arm that restrains the response: a regulatory population that holds the effector response in check (Sakaguchi et al. 1995). Read against the vocabulary of viable systems, this is an algedonic brake — a channel that can halt an operation from inside the operation, without waiting for the hierarchy above it to notice (Beer 1972). The design has a consequence that is easy to miss and hard to avoid: a brake built on the channel it disciplines can only be pulled by the response itself.

So the signal that ends an attack is made of the damage the attack has already done. The halt is late by construction, and its lateness is not a defect of the brake but its definition. This is why self-tolerance is better described as a governor than as a gate. A gate decides before the fact and can be checked before the fact; a governor acts on a quantity that exists only after the operation has begun.

The liability has a further feature that no institutional analogue reproduces cleanly. The system cannot state its reasons. There is no minute, no signature, no classification of the ground on which the decision was taken. What survives as a record is the trace of what the system responded to — a titre, a histology, a pattern of deposited complement — and that trace is read after the fact by a party standing outside the circuit. The obligation to answer therefore exists and has no addressee, which is a stronger condition than the citation problem of a later order: there the addressee is lost in transmission, and here no genre ever carried one.

Competence and aptness come apart. The test can be severe and the thresholds correctly set and the response still be an attack, because the reference set was incomplete; the failure belongs to a different site, and the liability site cannot repair it. The only available audit is audit by outcome, and audit by outcome is the outcome bias the liability site exists to name. A clinician reading a titre is judging an act by its result, and the act's own competence is not visible in the result.

## 4. One site per axis, and no second channel

The signature $(1, 1, 1)$ says that each axis carries exactly one site: severity on the epistemic, time on the vital, liability on the governance. Every axis is present, which is what the keystone label records, and none is doubled, which is what makes the combination thin.

No site has a backup. Where an axis carries two sites, a failure at one can be partially absorbed at the other; here a failing site is a failing axis, and the circuit has nothing on that axis to fall back on. The triad is intact and it has no redundancy, and the two facts are compatible because they are about different things: the signature counts sites, not reserves.

The repair path is forced. A better threshold does not repair a sampled reference; a longer window does not repair an unrepresented tissue; a liability rule does not repair either, because the party that would be held to answer has no capacity to answer. Each site's repair is local, and the local repairs do not compose into a repaired circuit.

The repairs interfere. Loosen the costimulation threshold until the uncovered tissues stop being attacked and the false-positive rate rises, paid by the tissue; strengthen the regulatory arm until the attacks stop and the false negatives rise, paid by the organism; attribute the attack to the clone that carried it out and nothing changes, because attribution without capacity is a sentence with no addressee. Nothing in this circuit stops the response, and that is what the vital margin would have to do: halt an operation whose cost is already being incurred. The halt exists, it is late, and lateness is what the order is.

Read this way, the failure the order names is the second collapse in its tissue form. The organism is not ignorant of what it is doing and it is not unable to act; it is unable to stop in time, and the interval between the response and the halt is the margin. When the draw-down outruns the restoration, the system is lucid and ruined at once.

The residue is the thing this order pays for that no other order in the corpus pays. It is the only place where the decision-maker and the cost-bearer are the same organism and still not the same party — where the identity that would make liability automatic is present in the biology and absent in the structure. A theory of aim that keeps the decision and the cost in one account has to be able to say why they come apart here, and the answer is not bad faith. It is a reference set drawn from a sample, a margin measured over a window, and a brake that can only be pulled after the fact.

## By the end of this chapter you can

1. State why the self/non-self decision is a conjunctive test rather than a weighted sum, and derive the bound on its false-positive rate without assuming the two arms are independent.
2. Explain why the reference against which the test runs is a sample of the body, and why an uncovered tissue produces a coverage failure rather than a misclassification.
3. Say why the cost of both errors is paid by a party that did not decide, and why a brake built on the channel it disciplines makes the halt necessarily late.

## Bridge

Layer 13 closes with a decision that cannot be audited by its own record: the test is severe, the reference is a sample, and the cost of both errors is paid where the decision was not made. What the chapter hands on is the asymmetry itself — a competence that can be perfect while the act is not apt, because the reference set was never the whole body.
