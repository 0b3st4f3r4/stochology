## By the end of this chapter you can

1. Explain why the choice of characters is a framing decision that precedes the evidence, and why a procedure answering a similarity question can be precise and irrelevant at once.
2. State why a name is anchored to a type rather than to a kind, and why the count of names is not the count of kinds.
3. Say why the name is the vital object of the order, and why its erasure falls hardest at the edges of the kind.

## 1. The character is chosen before it is found

Picture two biologists sorting beetles out of a museum tray. One groups them by overall shell color and shape, putting two shiny black beetles side by side; the other ignores color and compares shared ancestral jaw structures, linking one black beetle to a dull brown cousin instead.

No ruler or DNA sequencer settles that disagreement, because both biologists are answering different questions chosen before measurement began. Here, $T\_1$ (`T1Framing`) protects classification by forcing the character frame and biological question into the open before specimens are sorted.

Hennig's phylogenetic criterion divides apparent similarity into three distinct regimes, not two (Hennig 1966). Only **synapomorphies** (shared newly evolved traits) prove **monophyletic** sister clades. **Homoplasies** (convergent traits evolved twice) form **polyphyletic** bundles, while **symplesiomorphies** (ancient shared traits kept from distant ancestors) create **paraphyletic** grades—like grouping scaly reptiles apart from birds—that mirror real biology yet fail as evidence of closest kinship. Trait direction must be tested via modern outgroup comparison and trait congruence rather than assumed upfront.

Disputes over which measurements count as discrete characters—a framing choice no caliper resolves—create the **wrong-question case**: phenetic clustering asks *who looks most similar overall* rather than *who shares nearest ancestry*, yielding an exact, repeatable answer to the wrong question.

A small trait matrix shows how raw similarity misleads. Give specimens a binary trait matrix $X \in \lbrace 0, 1 \rbrace^{n \times m}$ and positive weights $w\_k > 0$ ($k = 1, \dots, m$). Their weighted Hamming distance is $d\_{H,w}(u, v) = \sum\_{k=1}^m w\_k \mkern2mu \mathbf{1}[X\_{uk} \neq X\_{vk}]$, so merely changing weights $w \mapsto w'$ swaps nearest neighbors under zero lab noise.

Consider even uniform weights ($w\_k \equiv 1$) on a three-taxon tree $((A, B), C)$ rooted at $0^m$ with zero reversals. Suppose $A$ and $B$ share $k\_{\mathrm{syn}} \ge 1$ derived traits while $B$ adds $k\_{\mathrm{aut}} > k\_{\mathrm{syn}}$ unique mutations ($k\_{\mathrm{syn}} + k\_{\mathrm{aut}} \le m$), $A$ adds none ($k\_{\mathrm{aut},A} = 0$), and $C$ stays ancestral. Then $d\_H(A, C) = k\_{\mathrm{syn}} < k\_{\mathrm{aut}} = d\_H(A, B) < k\_{\mathrm{syn}} + k\_{\mathrm{aut}} = d\_H(B, C)$, so unpolarized phenetic distance falsely pairs $(A, C)$ against true sisters $(A, B)$ with perfect data (Hennig 1966). Meanwhile, for $n \ge 2$ labeled species, possible rooted binary trees explode super-exponentially:

$$(2n - 3)!! = \prod\_{j=1}^{n-1}(2j - 1).$$

Read aloud, tree possibilities multiply by odd steps ($1, 3, 15, 105, \dots$), quickly outrunning character tables and forcing careful recension—used downstream in philology by [Layer 22](72-layer-philological-epigraphic-textual-order.md)—to test trait congruence rather than raw likeness.

Hacking's looping effects explain when labels change the labeled object itself, within a strict scope (Hacking 1995; 1999): Hacking (1995) analyzed **human kinds** whose subjects react to classification, and Hacking (1999) sharpened that distinction into **interactive kinds** versus **indifferent kinds**. Because looping demands self-awareness, indifferent kinds spare dead beetles in a drawer; when taxonomy reshapes wild organisms, it acts through external laws and conservation quotas (`T6Performative`). Formalization inherits that initial frame cut, so tightening rules inside a broken character list builds internal precision while answering the wrong question.

## 2. The name is anchored to one dated individual

Here, $T\_3$ (`T3Archive`) protects naming ledgers from museum collection skew, duplicate synonyms, and silent dumping of unplaced specimens into "other" buckets. Under zoological and botanical rules (`ICZN` and `ICN`), formal names attach not to population averages, but to one **type** specimen (holotype, lectotype, or neotype) inside a dated archive. That specimen is **onomatophoric** (name-bearing): it anchors *which group keeps the name* during splits or merges without freezing species descriptions or hiding population variation. Yet anchoring certainty to a sample of size one (`T3Archive`) embeds colonial collecting geography ($\rho\_{R,Y} \neq 0$)—locking Global South holotypes inside distant European drawers while leaving endemic species uncollected.

Names map **many-to-many** onto biological concepts through **synonymy** (many names for one species), **homonymy** (one spelling colliding across different types), and **chresonymy** (one binomial shifting meaning across papers). Across $N\_{\mathrm{lex}} \ge 1$ valid name slots, issuing $k$ independent names uniformly at random ($1 \le k \le N\_{\mathrm{lex}}$) incurs collision probability $P\_{\mathrm{hom}} = 1 - \prod\_{i=0}^{k-1}(1 - i/N\_{\mathrm{lex}})$—strictly a **lower bound** whenever root or stem frequencies are non-uniform—scrambling archives unless registries cross-check namespaces.

Synonym inflation bends global diversity curves directly. Examining 4,861 named North American fossil mammal species in the Paleobiology Database, John Alroy (2002) counted 3,350 (68.92%) currently accepted names ($a = 3{,}350/4{,}861$) and 1,511 invalid names—including 1,268 junior synonyms ($j = 1{,}268/1{,}511$), leaving $1{,}511 - 1{,}268 = 243$ *nomina dubia*. Modeling ongoing per-worker-year invalidation rate $q > 0$ and revalidation rate $r > 0$, Alroy's bidirectional **flux-ratio equilibrium** defines true validity $f$, apparent taxonomic inflation $\mathrm{infl}$, and subsampling correction $m(s)$ at survival chance $s \in (0, 1]$ ($0 < f \le a \le 1$, $j \in [0, 1]$):

$$f = \frac{r}{q + r}, \qquad \mathrm{infl} = \frac{a}{f} - 1, \qquad m(s) = 1 - (1 - j + j s)\left(1 - \frac{f}{a}\right).$$

In plain words, true validity reflects the tug-of-war between experts splitting synonyms ($q$) and reviving old names ($r$), inflating observed species counts whenever $a$ outpaces $f$.

Numerical estimates show how dramatically that synonym bias distorts deep-time trends (Alroy 2002). Under fixed one-way **simple decay** without revalidation, only 40.7% of published names are truly valid and 40.9% of accepted names fail. Under bidirectional flux-ratio equilibrium, true validity reaches 50.09% (95% CI 47.87–52.16%). That still leaves at least 27.3% (95% CI 24.3–30.5%, abstract 24–31%) of accepted names invalid, inflating apparent diversity by 37.6% (95% CI 32.1–44.0%) (Alroy 2002).

Historical cohorts and rock intervals expose the same skew (Alroy 2002). Across 27 five-year cohorts (1850–1859 and 1865–1989; 4,486 species; 2,992 or 66.7% accepted versus 2,399 or 53.5% valid), accepted share rises with recency ($r\_S = +0.654$, $P < 0.001$) while true validity stays flat ($r\_S = +0.311$, $t = 1.635$). Across Cenozoic rocks, taxonomic inflation rises with geological age ($r\_S = +0.557$, $P < 0.001$) because older Paleogene names stayed poorly vetted, shifting the Cenozoic log-diversity slope from $-0.00647$ ($\mathrm{SD} = 0.00126$) to $-0.00891$ ($\mathrm{SD} = 0.00123$) once corrected via $m(s)$ (Alroy 2002).

Whatever specimens fail to fit named boxes spill into a residual bin. Across $N \ge 1$ specimens partitioned into $K$ classes $C\_1, \dots, C\_K$ plus residual set $R\_{\mathrm{inc}}$ (*incertae sedis* or "other"), fixed total $N = \sum\_{i=1}^K |C\_i| + |R\_{\mathrm{inc}}|$ fixes residual fraction $r\_{\mathrm{res}}$ and its discrete tradeoff rate:

$$r\_{\mathrm{res}} = \frac{|R\_{\mathrm{inc}}|}{N} = 1 - \sum\_{i=1}^K \frac{|C\_i|}{N}, \qquad \left.\frac{\Delta r\_{\mathrm{res}}}{\Delta |C\_i|}\right|\_N = -\frac{1}{N} < 0.$$

Read aloud, every specimen rejected by strict metropolitan classes adds directly to the uncounted residual bucket. Bowker and Star (1999) proved every classification scheme forces a decision on edge cases: tightening rules dumps tropical taxa and non-standard records into *incertae sedis* ($R\_{\mathrm{inc}}$), creating archival invisibility and selection bias (recording indicator $R$ in $\rho\_{R,Y} \neq 0$, distinct from residual set $R\_{\mathrm{inc}}$) when excluded from totals.

## 3. The name travels without its evidence

Here, $T\_4$ (`T4Formalization`, primary site `T4*`) protects classifications from **arithmomorphic rigidity** (Georgescu-Roegen 1971)—slicing smooth living variation into discrete codes so labels move cleanly across spreadsheets without ever inspecting the drawer again.

Modern ontologies embed the set relations of [Chapter 09](09-sets-relations-functions.md) (§2)—instantiation ($x \in C$), `is_a` subtype ($\subseteq$), mereological `part_of` (`BFO:0000050`, $\le\_{\mathrm{part}}$), and `T3Archive` holotypes—inside **Basic Formal Ontology (`BFO`)** and the **OBO Foundry**. At the class level, `A part_of B` holds **existentially**: every instance of class $A$ stands in individual parthood $\le\_{\mathrm{part}}$ to some instance of class $B$. Keeping `part_of` separate from `is_a` blocks category errors between clades (historical wholes whose members are `part_of` a lineage) and universal kinds (`is_a`): `A part_of B` together with `B is_a C` implies `A part_of C`—never `A is_a C`, so a bird's feather is `part_of` a vertebrate rather than a vertebrate—just as `A is_a B` together with `B part_of C` preserves existential parthood (`A part_of C`, since every $A$-instance is a $B$-instance and thus part of some $C$-instance).

In `BFO`, persisting objects (continuants) and unfolding processes (occurrents) never cross under `part_of`; continuants *participate in* occurrents. Across over 40 OBO ontologies importing `Plan Specification` (`IAO:0000104`) and 60+ importing `Planned Process` (`OBI:0000011`), direct realization accounts (`RC-account`) fail whenever an intended process aborts or drifts. Because a `Plan Specification` (`GDC`) cannot execute itself, failed `Occurrent` runs sever links to realizables (`SDC`) unless mediated by `CCO` `prescribed by` or `Plan Creation Process` (Duncan et al. 2024).

Large ontology audits—extending [Layer 06](56-layer-formal-computable-type-order.md)'s lesson that syntactic well-formedness cannot guarantee semantic adequacy—confirm that formal axioms alone guarantee neither consistency nor open governance:

- **Name collisions:** Across 53 OBO candidates (318,872 terms by September 2010), 16 (30%) reused external terms while 51 (96%) carried 9,992 overlapping preferred names. That 3.1% overlap left only 2 strictly orthogonal ontologies, and 109 of 135 overlaps (81%) among 6 core ontologies connected `XAO` and `ZFA` (Ghazvinian, Noy, & Musen 2011).
- **Dashboard limits:** After 2018 triage of 201 ontologies (145 active, 5 inactive-in-use, 45 obsoleted, 6 orphaned), automated checks cut errors across 223 active ontologies (Wilcoxon $P = 0.0005$). Yet by May 2021 all 175 active ontologies passed syntactic format, URI, and authority checks (`FP02`, `FP03`, `FP11`, `FP20`; [Layer 06](56-layer-formal-computable-type-order.md)), whereas only 19 (about 11%) passed `FP06 Textual Definitions`, leaving scope (`FP10`) and community consensus (`FP16`) unautomated (Jackson et al. 2021).
- **Duplicate identifiers and skew:** The Vaccine Ontology (`VO` 2026-03-06) holds 15,661 terms, including 1,158 imported from 22 ontologies and 10,929 vaccines or candidates under a single-asserted-parent hierarchy checked with `ELK` reasoning. Still, queries return 1,430 `OHDSI` influenza concepts for 1,429 vaccines due to duplicate `OMOP` IDs, while `VO` *"primarily focuses on the representation of vaccines in English-speaking countries"* (Zheng et al. 2026).

Separating partial orders from ordinal scales clears another naming fallacy. A cladogram is a branching tree poset, whereas Linnaean ranks form an **ordinal scale** obeying $r\_1 < r\_2 \iff g(r\_1) < g(r\_2)$ under monotone relabeling $g$ (Stevens 1946). Ranks record nesting along one branch without providing an evolutionary clock across sister groups (forbidding rank-level arithmetic), yet `ICZN` and `ICN` still enforce rank-bound homonymy priority—making first publication timestamp legally binding until rare Plenary Power or *nomina conservanda* appeals overturn it.

Mathematical geometry exposes how single-specimen types fail edge populations. Take a trait $X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$ ($\sigma > 0$) with tolerance half-width $c\sigma$ ($c > 0$) centered around one ($n = 1$) displaced holotype $x\_0 = \mu + \delta\sigma$ ($\delta \in \mathbb{R}$). Using standard normal cumulative distribution $\Phi$ and density $\phi$, captured population coverage $P\_{\mathrm{cov}}(\delta, c)$ and its shift rate obey:

$$P\_{\mathrm{cov}}(\delta, c) = \Phi(c - \delta) - \Phi(-c - \delta), \qquad \frac{\partial P\_{\mathrm{cov}}}{\partial \delta} = \phi(-c - \delta) - \phi(c - \delta).$$

In plain words, coverage peaks when the holotype hits the center ($\delta = 0$), and drops steadily as metropolitan bias ($\delta > 0$) pulls the type specimen away—erasing the opposite tropical or peripheral tail first. That parallels `VO`'s English-speaking focus (Zheng et al. 2026) and Canguilhem's (1943/1966) insight that biological vitality lives across non-average margins. **Domain failure boundary:** bifurcating tree-and-type rules break under horizontal gene transfer, hybridization, incomplete lineage sorting, ring species, and clonal reproduction.

## 4. Two epistemic sites and no one to answer for the cut

Signature `(2, 1, 0)` places `T1Framing` and `T3Archive` on $S\_{\mathrm{epi}}$, `T4Formalization` on $S\_{\mathrm{vital}}$, and leaves $S\_{\mathrm{gov}}$ locally empty ($k\_3 = 0$). Along $\mathcal{G}\_1$, [Layer 06](56-layer-formal-computable-type-order.md) supplies syntax checks without semantic truth (Rice 1953), [Layer 12](62-layer-morphogenetic-developmental-order.md) supplies morphogenetic sameness (`T1`) with `T5Temporal` and `T6Performative`, and Stratum I suppliers ([Layer 01](51-layer-micro-kinetic-sensorimotor-order.md), [Layer 02](52-layer-meso-buffered-queue-order.md), [Layer 03](53-layer-macro-coupled-topology-order.md)) supply `T7Liability` across $\mathrm{Cone}(L\_{14})$ ([Appendix B.2](appendix-b-architecture-verification.md#b2-the-multilayer-graph--35-orders-7-strata-5-exposure-regimes)). `L14` introduces `T3Archive`, yet **`T2Severity` is missing across all of $\mathrm{Cone}(L\_{14})$**, leaving trait congruence and statistical intervals without an internal severity bound until coupled laterally across $\mathcal{G}\_2 = J(7, 3)$ (e.g., to [Layer 09](59-layer-experimental-sampling-causal-order.md) or [Layer 22](72-layer-philological-epigraphic-textual-order.md)).

In practice, `(2, 1, 0)` breeds **Collapse Proposition 3 (*Cynical Capture*)** whenever biological names decide protection, trade quotas, or legal status without accountable governance. Surveying $110$ taxon-specific specialist groups, $6$ stand-alone Red List Authorities, and $6$ national specialist groups ($110 + 6 + 6 = 122$ contacted; $122 - 6 = 116$ responding), Garnett et al. (2026) found overlapping practices (indexed to the 122 contacted groups, summing to $5 + 14 + 14 + 62 + 33 = 128$): 5 (4%) reported settled taxonomy, 14 (11%) used dedicated taxonomy committees, 14 (11%) vested authority in a single person, 62 (51%) decided collectively, and 33 (27%) adopted outside lists uncritically. Meanwhile, 68 of 82 (83%) CITES-relevant groups skipped CITES reference lists (which take 3 to 10 years to revise) and 103 (84%) diverged from Catalogue of Life (Garnett et al. 2026).

Two missing governance guards drive that vulnerability:

- **Under `T6Performative`:** Beyond Hacking's (§1) human-kind feedback, biological lists shape conservation rules directly. Delisting species or gaming ontology dashboards without semantic checks (Jackson et al. 2021) shifts legal thresholds silently.
- **Under `T7Liability`:** Bowker and Star's (1999) residual category absorbs uncounted labor without ownership, distant museums lock holotypes away from source regions, and code revisions crawl slowly. Closing this gap requires bitemporal custody separating event time from revision time (Snodgrass 2000), a **provisional protective moratorium** (`Regime III` / Brake 3 freeze) while disputed splits, lumps, or synonym audits remain pending so extractive lobbies or captured chairs cannot stall CITES/Red List protection across multi-year cycles ($\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{species}} < \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{review}}$), and an out-of-band appeal giving field parataxonomists and local curators undiscounted voice ([Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md)) gated by corroborated sequential *e*-process filtering ($E\_t \ge E\_{\mathrm{trip}}$; [Chapter 95](95-collision-at-ten-bits-per-second.md)) so automated or industry-funded filing floods cannot saturate review boards outside live escalation queues.

Tightening codes blindly swells $|R\_{\mathrm{inc}}|$; loosening categories erases resolution; and missing governance lets harms spill across ecological and social reception perimeters ($\Theta\_{\mathrm{basin}}$; [Chapter 40](40-institution-incentive-metric.md); [Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md)). **Falsification condition:** Collapse Proposition 3 fails if a curatorial `(2, 1, 0)` regime lacking allocative power—or paired externally with bitemporal logs, conflict disclosure, provisional protective moratoria during pending disputes, and corroborated *e*-filtered frontline appeals—keeps edge species intact without shifting harm across external reception perimeters (scored *ex post* by $\Theta\_{\mathrm{basin}}$).
