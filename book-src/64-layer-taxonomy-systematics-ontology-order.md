## 1. The character is chosen before it is found

A classification opens with a question nobody writes down. On the page it is *what is this*; the question actually put to the material is *which differences between these specimens are to count*. The second must be answered before the first can be, and the classifier answers it.

Hennig's criterion is the sharpest available statement of what that answer has to satisfy. A grouping is phylogenetic when it rests on shared derived characters; a resemblance that arose independently in two lineages is not evidence of grouping at all (Hennig 1966). The criterion does two things at once, and the second is the one that costs. It tells the systematist which characters may be used, and it tells them that a resemblance is not a character until its history has been settled. A feature that looks like a shared derived character and is in fact a convergence is a homoplasy. A grouping built on it is built on nothing: the resemblance is real, and it is not evidence.

Read as a claim about framing, this says that the evidence for a cut is selected before it is weighed. Whether a difference counts as a character is not decided by the specimen. The framer decides it, before any measurement is taken. Two systematists can agree on every measurement of every specimen and disagree about which of those measurements are characters, and no further measurement will settle it, because the disagreement is not empirical. They are arguing about what the question is.

The framing has a failure mode specific to it, and from inside it does not look like error. Call it the wrong-question case: a procedure that answers *which specimens are most similar* is well defined, computable, and irrelevant to the question of which share a history. It returns a precise, reproducible answer about something else. Nothing in the output marks the substitution. A similarity matrix does not know what it was built to measure.

Hacking's account of looping effects gives the reason this is constitutive rather than a choice of method, for the kinds where it applies (Hacking 1995). A classification of human kinds feeds back into the classified: people sorted into a kind come to act, be treated, and understand themselves through the category, and the category is revised in response. For those kinds the framing is not external to the object, and the choice of characters is a choice about what the object will become. State the scope rather than assume it. The looping is not a property of every kind, only of kinds that can read their own classification, and the classifications this order governs land on such kinds as often as on specimens in a drawer.

There the framing site meets the formal site, and that meeting is why the order is hard to repair. The formal apparatus inherits the framing. Choose the character set to make a grouping and the test that follows cannot fail; its rigour is real and aimed at the wrong thing. No amount of formal correctness recovers a question settled before the apparatus was switched on.

## 2. The name is anchored to one dated individual

A formal name is not attached to a kind. It is attached to a type: a specimen, or a series of them, deposited in a collection, described in a publication, and fixed by that act as the reference for the name while the name stands. Everything the name is used for refers back to that material, and the material is finite, dated, located.

One specimen stands for a kind — a sample of size one — and the gap between the two is the archive's characteristic gap. It is not a defect of the description. It is what makes the name resolvable at all. A name attached to a population would have no address, and a name with no address cannot be checked, disputed, or pointed at. The archive buys resolvability with coverage, every time.

Coverage is where the site does its work. The specimens in the drawer are what was collected, in the places and seasons where collecting happened, by the parties doing the collecting. That is a design, and its biases are systematic, not random: a species known from a single valley is not a random draw from its range, and the type series is not a random draw from the kind. A classification resting on such material inherits its coverage, and revising the code does not repair the inheritance. The code never had the missing specimen.

The catalogue compounds the problem in a form that can be stated exactly. The map from names to kinds is many-to-one: several names may denote one kind, so the count of names is not the count of kinds. Counting names counts the dictionary. An archive that reports its holdings as a number of names has reported the size of its vocabulary, and the vocabulary grows for reasons that have nothing to do with the fauna — a description published in ignorance of an earlier one, a subspecies raised and then sunk, a name kept alive by priority after its kind was merged.

Bowker and Star described what happens to material the scheme cannot place. A residual category absorbs it, and the category grows until it carries the work the classification was built to avoid (Bowker & Star 1999). Nothing conspiratorial is required. Every scheme must decide each case, and the cases it cannot decide have to go somewhere, so the somewhere fills. The residue is the archive's honest record of its own incompetence, and also the place where an institution's unmeasured work accumulates, because work done in the residual category is work the scheme does not count.

## 3. The name travels without its evidence

A formal language promises public clarity: a summary usable by someone who has not seen the material. The name is that summary in its purest form. It can be written into a regulation, printed on a label, entered in a database, cited in a paper by a party who has never opened the drawer and never will. This is the formalisation site, and here it is the vital one, because the name is the object that acts.

The inversion is the one the guard site names: arithmomorphic rigidity, and the erasure of the convex harm the summary cannot carry. The code must decide every case, so a penumbra of continuous variation has to be presented as a boundary. A boundary is not a summary of the variation. It is a decision about where the variation stops mattering, taken once and then inherited by every use.

Two properties of that inheritance follow from the form of the code, not from any case.

The first: a hierarchy is an ordinal structure, and ordinal structure is invariant under monotone relabelling. Ranks fix seniority, not the cut. The same set of taxa is compatible with different rank assignments, and no rank carries information about where the boundary was drawn, because a rank is a position in an order and an order has no metric. The part of the apparatus that looks most like a verdict — the nested hierarchy — says least about the boundary. The boundary lives in the character choice, one site back. The hierarchy records only that some cut was made.

The second: priority turns a timestamp into an authority. The earliest published name governs, and the earliest publication is an event in the history of the archive rather than a property of the kind. A name proposed for a local population in one place binds the kind wherever it is later found, and binds it in a form written before the rest of the kind was known. One specimen and one description stand for a variable population. The variance is what the name erases.

The erasure is not uniform, and that is why the site is vital rather than stylistic. A name handles badly exactly the cases at the edge of the kind: the cryptic ones, the extremes, the populations that differ in characters the description did not mention. It performs best where the material is typical, which is where the harm is smallest, and worst where the material is atypical, which is where the harm is largest. A summary that fails at the tail, and fails by construction, is the shape this site exists to name.

So the name is not a summary of the evidence. It is the instrument through which the evidence is bypassed, and a good instrument: portable, checkable in principle, usable in practice by parties who cannot check it. A record becomes usable without its archive the moment it is named, and at that step the order's output enters every other order that uses kinds.

## 4. Two epistemic sites and no one to answer for the cut

The signature $(2, 1, 0)$ puts framing and archive on the epistemic axis, formalisation on the vital axis, and nothing on governance. The absence is not an oversight in the architecture. It is what the order exposes.

What would a governance site have to do here? Give the classified a channel through which the classification could be contested by the party it lands on, before it takes effect. The code has procedures, and none of them is that. Priority disputes are decided between authors. A later revision can sink a name. A commission can rule on a case. Every one of those is an operation on the archive, conducted with archival materials, on a timescale the archive sets. None is an answer to a party. The classified is not a party to the procedure that names it, and in the human cases the naming may be the most consequential thing that happens to it.

The second feature is sharper. The performative loop is real, and the order has no site at which it can be registered. Hacking's looping describes a classification that changes what it classifies; this order's triple contains no performative guard, so the effect is present in the world and absent from the order's account of itself. A classification whose most consequential act is invisible to its own signature cannot govern that act. It cannot even state that there is an act to govern. That is the precise sense in which the deficit is exposed rather than merely missing: the order's own success makes it consequential, because a name that travels is a name that acts where its authors are not.

The third feature is the residue read as an accountability structure. The residual category holds what the scheme could not place, and it grows, and nobody answers for the growth. Accountability here attaches to names, not to omissions: a name can be traced to an author, a publication and a type, and an omission cannot be traced to anything. The party that would have to answer for the residue is the party that decided what the categories would be, and that party has no site at which the decision is reviewed as a decision.

This is what a governance axial exposure looks like when it is not a scandal. Capture needs no bad faith and no bribe. It needs only that the decisions allocating the residue be made where the cost of the residue is not felt. The classifier who chooses the characters bears a wrong character as professional embarrassment; the party classified as residual bears it as a life. The asymmetry is structural, and it is why the third collapse proposition is the one this order exposes. Epistemic doubt about the classification is available, and it is available as an alibi rather than as a check, because there is no site at which it would have to be answered.

Repair, as always in a local axial exposure, runs into the signature. Tighten the code and the residue grows, because more cases fall outside the categories. Widen the kinds until nothing is residual and the names stop travelling, because a name that covers everything distinguishes nothing. Add a contestation procedure and it becomes an archival procedure, absorbing objections on the archive's timescale. None of these repairs the order, because the missing axis is not a missing procedure. It is the absence of a party. The order can be paired with an order that has a governance site, and whether that pairing covers the residue or merely relocates it is the architecture's business. This chapter does not answer the question, and it is not this order's to answer.

What the order contributes is a demonstration: a classification can be epistemically careful and formally rigorous while its most consequential decision has no addressee. The name is the point at which the record becomes an instrument, and there is no one on the other side of it.

## By the end of this chapter you can

1. Explain why the choice of characters is a framing decision that precedes the evidence, and why a procedure answering a similarity question can be precise and irrelevant at once.
2. State why a name is anchored to a type rather than to a kind, and why the count of names is not the count of kinds.
3. Say why the name is the vital object of the order, and why its erasure falls hardest at the edges of the kind.

## Exercises

1. **Framing before evidence.** Two systematists agree on every measurement and disagree about which measurements are characters. Say what kind of disagreement this is, and why no further measurement settles it.
2. **The many-to-one map.** With names mapping many-to-one onto kinds, say what an archive's holdings count measures, and give the two distinct reasons the count can rise without a kind being added.
3. **Ordinal invariance.** Show that a nested hierarchy is compatible with different rank assignments, and say what the hierarchy therefore cannot record about the cut.
4. **The tail.** Explain why a name handles typical material better than atypical material, and why that pattern is a property of the summary rather than of the material.
5. **Reading the signature.** Explain why $(2, 1, 0)$ is a governance axial exposure, and why importing a contestation procedure would be an archival operation rather than a governance site.

## Bridge

Layer 14 closes with an instrument that acts where its authors are not: the name is anchored to one dated individual, it travels without the material it summarises, and the cases it handles worst are the ones at the edge of the kind. What the chapter hands on is the residue — the category that grows because every scheme must decide each case, and the party that bears the growth is not a party to the decision.
