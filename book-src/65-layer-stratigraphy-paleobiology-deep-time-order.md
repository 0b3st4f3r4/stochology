## 1. The interval is chosen before it is measured

Picture standing before a limestone cliff trying to pinpoint the exact bed where an ancient marine ecosystem died out. The rock prints no timestamp along its seams, and the highest fossil shell you find almost certainly lived earlier than the species' actual last day, so where you draw the frame decides how sudden the extinction looks.

Here, $T\_1$ (`T1Framing`) protects deep-time claims by fixing the designated boundary horizon and comparison interval before a single mineral grain is dissolved. A geological date answers a question, and nothing in raw stone carries a label: a chronostratigraphic boundary is a ratified point chosen for global correlation, so the age assigned to it dates that designated frame first.

Meishan's Permian–Triassic Global Stratotype Section and Point (GSSP) illustrates that priority directly (Burgess, Bowring, & Shen 2014). Ratified at the first appearance of *Hindeodus parvus* at the base of Bed 27c, single-zircon CA-ID-TIMS U–Pb ages date volcanic ash Bed 25 to $251.941 \pm 0.037/0.28\text{ Ma}$ ($n = 16$, $\mathrm{MSWD} = 1.3$) and Bed 28 to $251.880 \pm 0.031/0.28\text{ Ma}$ ($n = 13$, $\mathrm{MSWD} = 0.76$), where the slash separates internal analytical scatter from full tracer-plus-decay calibration error. Stratigraphic interpolation dates Bed 27c to $251.902 \pm 0.024\text{ Ma}$ and narrows the main extinction pulse across Bed 25–28 to $0.061 \pm 0.048\text{ Ma}$ ($61 \pm 48\text{ ka}$, summarized as $60 \pm 48\text{ ka}$) (Burgess, Bowring, & Shen 2014). That is why framing sits as primary site `T1*`: the horizon fixes the object before any measurement begins.

Three framing cuts precede every geological tally:

- **Interval denominator:** Since a rate counts losses per duration, widening a window post hoc flattens extinction into routine turnover, while narrowing it sharpens turnover into catastrophe.
- **Lineage identity:** Counting a species across beds imports the morphogenetic sameness rule from [Layer 12](62-layer-morphogenetic-developmental-order.md) before [Layer 14](64-layer-taxonomy-systematics-ontology-order.md) assigns a taxonomic name.
- **Deposit inclusion:** The boundary decides which rock beds enter the section and which never reach the ledger.

No dataset confined inside a single cliff can test its own horizon choice, because a section drawn around a fauna reports that fauna by construction. Correlated outcrops or diagnostic fossils below the ratified seam break that circle, triggering an attested counter-inductive reopening of `T1` inside bitemporal quarantine rather than silent retrospective edits ([Chapter 32, *The Question Before the Data*](32-the-question-before-the-data.md)), bounded by strict non-self-extending deadlines and interim protective moratoria ([Chapter 93](93-fracturing-the-frame-before-the-seal.md); [Chapter 97](97-the-hijacked-kairos-and-exception.md)) so operators cannot contest baseline frames indefinitely.

## 2. The record is the only access

Here, $T\_3$ (`T3Archive`) protects deep-time archives by treating missing fossil horizons, facies shifts, and washed-out drill cores as structural preservation filters rather than random noise. A rock section is an envelope in time: whatever eroded or dissolved without leaving a fossil has no untouched backup copy sitting in storage.

Preservation behaves as a rate: fossils survive only where burial beats destruction, so observed spans underestimate true durations most severely where rock layers thin (Signor & Lipps 1982; Strauss & Sadler 1989). Suppose $H \in \mathbb{Z}\_{\ge 2}$ fossiliferous horizons scatter independently, continuously, and uniformly across a species' true lifespan, leaving observed range $R\_{\mathrm{obs}} > 0$ spanning $H - 1 > 0$ interior gaps. Comparing one missing end-gap against those $H - 1$ interior gaps fixes probability $(1 + a)^{-(H-1)}$ that the true endpoint sits further than $a R\_{\mathrm{obs}}$ past the last find. Setting that tail probability to $1 - C$ for one-sided confidence $C \in (0, 1)$ gives positive extension multiplier $a$:

$$a = (1 - C)^{-1/(H-1)} - 1,$$

extending $R\_{\mathrm{obs}}$ by $a R\_{\mathrm{obs}}$ past the outermost fossil (Strauss & Sadler 1989). In plain words, you price the unseen tail beyond your highest fossil in multiples of the average gap between the fossils you actually found.

Look at how fast uncertainty widens when finds are sparse. Applying single-end $a$ to both ends drops two-sided coverage to $C\_2 = 1 - 2(1 + a)^{-(H-1)} + (1 + 2a)^{-(H-1)} < C$. With just two horizons ($H = 2$), $a = C/(1 - C)$ blows up as $C \to 1^{-}$, whereas large $H$ shrinks $a$ toward $-\ln(1 - C)/(H - 1)$; for single finds ($H \le 1$, $R\_{\mathrm{obs}} = 0$), the gap multiplier fails altogether.

Both simulations and late-Quaternary excavations demonstrate how heavily those sampling gaps shift apparent extinction dates:

- **Simulations:** Under uniform fossilization at $0.005$ per lineage per million years across $20{,}787$ extinct taxa on branching trees, branch-by-branch Strauss–Sadler 95% intervals stayed computable on just $7.42\\%$ of lineages ($H \ge 2$, versus $85.36\\%$ under global continuous Poisson model `Glo`). On that feasible subset, intervals averaged $244.0\text{ Ma}$ wide against $26.1\text{ Ma}$ under exact simulation truth (`Ref`) ($26.0\text{ Ma}$ across all extinct lineages) (Didier & Laurin 2021).
- **Australian megafauna:** Across $659$ screened fossil ages covering $16$ genera alongside $436$ *Homo sapiens* records in Table 1 ($438$ in the methods text, screened from $6{,}349$ dates), Signor–Lipps modeling pulls peak megafaunal extinction forward from the raw youngest-genus median of $50.8\text{ kyr}$ (25th–75th percentiles $45.9\text{–}57.1\text{ kyr}$) to $42.1\text{ kyr}$ (25th–75th percentiles $36.7\text{–}48.1\text{ kyr}$). That corrected extinction falls well after human arrival at $55.6\text{ kyr}$ (25th–75th percentiles $55.4\text{–}55.7\text{ kyr}$) (Saltré et al. 2016).

Direction adds a second preservation filter: shells outlast soft tissues, shallow seabeds outlast eroding uplands, and sea-level sequence breaks bunch apparent extinctions artificially along single surfaces. Treating directional preservation as a parameter informs reconstruction; treating it as random noise turns preservation breaks into fake mass extinctions.

Vertical resolution bounds what questions rock layers can answer (Burgess, Bowring, & Shen 2014). At Meishan, condensed Bed 25–28 records an apparent sedimentation rate of $0.36\text{ cm/ka}$ ($0.17\text{ cm/ka}$ lower bound, upper bound unconstrained), Bed 28–33 records $0.58\text{ cm/ka}$ ($0.34\text{–}0.95\text{ cm/ka}$), and Bed 33–34 jumps to $6.8\text{ cm/ka}$ ($2.5\text{ cm/ka}$ lower bound, upper unconstrained). Because individual $2\sigma$ boundary errors on Bed 25 and Bed 28 overlap (despite their $0.061 \pm 0.048\text{ Ma}$ quadrature interval in §1) while Bed 33–34 bounds cross zero duration, upper rate limits stay unbounded, barring point rate ratios across beds, while condensed intervals hide missing hiatuses inside [Layer 04](54-layer-geophysical-chaos-data-assimilation-order.md)'s coarse-grain and bitemporal data-assimilation framework.

Subsurface boreholes introduce **drill-core recovery loss** (*core loss*) alongside **facies hiatuses** at `T3Archive`. Because weak, fractured, or water-logged seams wash out selectively, missing core segments create non-random missingness (recording indicator $R$ in $\rho\_{R,Y} \ne 0$, distinct from observed stratigraphic range $R\_{\mathrm{obs}}$ above; Meng 2018), so unrecovered runs must stay logged permanently as **missing archival windows**. Computable range extensions cover only taxa with $H \ge 2$ uniform horizons, returning no bound for singletons ($H = 1$), absences ($H = 0$), hiatuses, or washed-out cores absent an external model.

## 3. The date is a measurement with its own error

Here, $T\_5$ (`T5Temporal`) guards crystal survival, decay-horizon exhaustion, and physical outcrop survival before heat, fluids, or erosion erase the clock altogether, building on Type-A/Type-B radiometric error decomposition inherited from `T3Archive` and [Layer 08](58-layer-metrological-information-geometric-order.md). Every radiometric date is an interval combining separate uncertainties that cannot trade against one another.

In live decay counting like radiocarbon under background-free, large-count conditions ($N \gg B, 1$), a count $N > 0$ carries Poisson scatter $\sigma\_N = \sqrt{N}$ and relative error $\sigma\_N / N = 1/\sqrt{N}$ (halving error demands four times the decays), while exponential decay $N \propto e^{-\lambda t}$ pushes age uncertainty up like $1/(\lambda\sqrt{N}) \propto e^{\lambda t/2}$, accelerating under $\sigma\_N = \sqrt{N + B}$ to $\sigma\_t \propto e^{\lambda t}$ once background $B > 0$ dominates near the dating ceiling (`T5Temporal`). By contrast, CA-ID-TIMS U–Pb dating dissolves zircons to measure accumulated daughter-to-parent ion ratio $r = {}^{206}\mathrm{Pb}^\ast/{}^{238}\mathrm{U} = e^{\lambda t} - 1 > 0$, mixing counting shot noise with lab blank and tracer uncertainties. For positive ratio $r > 0$, decay constant $\lambda > 0$, and age $t > 0$, radiometric age $t(r, \lambda)$ splits into Type-A analytical ratio uncertainty $\sigma\_{t,\mathrm{ratio}}$ and Type-B decay-constant uncertainty $\sigma\_{t,\lambda}$:

$$t(r, \lambda) = \frac{1}{\lambda}\ln(1 + r), \qquad \sigma\_{t,\mathrm{ratio}} = \frac{1 - e^{-\lambda t}}{\lambda}\frac{\sigma\_r}{r}, \qquad \sigma\_{t,\lambda} = t\mkern3mu\frac{\sigma\_\lambda}{\lambda}.$$

Read aloud, this archival metrology (`T3`/`L08`) shows that counting ions longer shrinks only analytical error $\sigma\_r/r$, leaving mineral loss and shared calibration limits untouched.

Crystal history and tracer baselines set hard systematic floors (Burgess, Bowring, & Shen 2014). Lead loss biases zircons younger (removed at Meishan by annealing grains at 900 °C for 60 hours and leaching in $29\text{ M}$ hydrofluoric acid for 12 hours), while inherited detrital cores bias dates older. Adopting the `EARTHTIME ET2535` mixed tracer shifted U–Pb dates younger by up to 0.2% ($200\text{–}500\text{ ka}$), leaving irreducible calibration errors of 0.03% on tracer composition and 0.05% on the $^{238}\mathrm{U}$ decay constant. That shared budget generates the $\pm 0.28\text{ Ma}$ outer error on individual Meishan ages. In a within-section difference $\Delta t = t\_1 - t\_2$ sharing tracer and constant, correlated terms shrink differentially to $e^{-\lambda\bar{t}}\mkern2mu\frac{2\sinh(\lambda|\Delta t|/2)}{\lambda}\frac{\sigma\_{\mathrm{tr}}}{\mathrm{tr}}$ (exact to first order, and reducing for small intervals $\lambda|\Delta t| \ll 1$ to $e^{-\lambda\bar{t}}\mkern2mu|\Delta t|\mkern2mu\sigma\_{\mathrm{tr}}/\mathrm{tr}$) and $|\Delta t|\mkern2mu\sigma\_\lambda/\lambda$. That cancellation leaves the Bed 25–28 gap tight at $0.061 \pm 0.048\text{ Ma}$, whereas cross-method comparisons absorb the full $\pm 0.28\text{ Ma}$ calibration width (Burgess, Bowring, & Shen 2014).

Every date therefore carries its mineral and calibration history, so overlapping intervals form a partially ordered poset rather than a list of midpoints. Beyond interval metrology, `T5Temporal` enforces two irreversible physical clocks: thermal or fluid resetting above a mineral's closure temperature erases crystal clocks permanently, while erosion, quarrying, or river dredging drains remaining outcrop volume $V\_{\mathrm{outcrop}} > 0$ at rate $\dot{E} > 0$, destroying sections whenever $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{outcrop}} := V\_{\mathrm{outcrop}}/\dot{E} < \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{sampling}}$ before field recovery and lab preparation finish.
