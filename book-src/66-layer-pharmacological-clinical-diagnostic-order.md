## 1. The dose is the variable, and the protocol is what fixes it

A drug is not a substance. It is a function. *Does it work* has no answer, because the question has no coordinates; *at this dose, for this duration, in this population, on this endpoint* has one, and the protocol exists to name them. That is why formalization is primary here: a clinical claim is a point on a surface, and the protocol is the instrument that says which point, in a form another team can occupy.

The protocol fixes four coordinates — the contrast, the population, the horizon, the endpoint — and buys two things with them. The claim becomes falsifiable. The data become auditable. An inference whose coordinates are stated can be repeated, attacked, repaired; an inference whose coordinates are conventions cannot be attacked at all. That is the order's power, and it is not a small one. A medicine that cannot say what it is a claim about is indistinguishable from a testimony.

The cost is the same coin. Everything outside the four coordinates goes unmeasured, and the unmeasured region is not scattered at random around the point. That is where the rare event lives, and the interaction, and the long horizon. A point that has been licensed is then spoken of as a drug that has been licensed, and the grammar of the sentence does work the data did not do.

The endpoint is a coordinate like the others and the most consequential, because it can be precise and wrong. The Cardiac Arrhythmia Suppression Trial lowered the arrhythmia it targeted. The endpoint of arrhythmic death or non-fatal cardiac arrest ran 4.5 per cent on the active arms and 1.2 per cent on placebo, and total mortality moved the same way (CAST Investigators 1989). Nothing about the trial was sloppy. The formal object was correct, the contrast randomized, the analysis reported. The coordinate named a symptom, and the symptom was not the outcome. This is formalization's own failure mode, and it is not imprecision. It is precision about the wrong variable, which is harder to see from inside the design, because the design is what made it visible.

The same form appears at the level of doses. The therapeutic index is the ratio of the toxic to the effective dose,

$$\mathrm{TI} = \frac{\mathrm{TD}\_{50}}{\mathrm{ED}\_{50}},$$

and $\mathrm{TI} \le 1$ says there is no window: the dose that helps a population is not below the dose that harms it. The index divides two chosen quantities, so it belongs to the pair as much as to the drug. A wide index licenses a buffered step. An index near one licenses a probe whose size is set by the gap between the two doses.

## 2. Severity, and the negative result

Severity asks whether a false hypothesis would rarely have produced the result in hand. For a trial, the answer is a number fixed before the data arrive: the power at the contrast the protocol chose. With $n$ subjects per arm, a within-arm standard deviation $\sigma$, a true difference $\delta$ and a two-sided level $\alpha$,

$$\text{power} = \Phi\!\left(\frac{\delta\sqrt{n/2}}{\sigma} - z\_{1-\alpha/2}\right),$$

so severity is bought with subjects and with effect size, and at fixed $n$ a smaller true difference is indistinguishable from no difference at all. The instrument's reach defines its silence.

Harm is worse, and the asymmetry is structural, not rhetorical. A trial observing no event of a given kind bounds that event's rate by $-\ln(0.05)/n$, which is close to three over $n$; a programme of a few thousand subjects therefore excludes only rates above the per-mille level. The sentence *no evidence of harm* is a statement about $n$ before it is a statement about the drug. Harm rates are smaller than benefit rates, so a design powered for benefit is systematically non-severe for harm: the trial that can see a benefit of the size it was built to see cannot see the harm it was not built to look for.

A third difficulty: the endpoint itself may be a stand-in. Where the measured variable is a surrogate for the one that matters, the trial's severity rests on a claim the trial does not test, namely that moving the surrogate moves the outcome. From inside the design the conditionality is invisible, because the design was built to be severe about the surrogate. It becomes visible when a trial large enough to measure the outcome contradicts it. That is the arrhythmia case — a surrogate moving in the right direction while the outcome moved in the wrong one — and the lesson is not that surrogates are worthless. Their warrant is a separate claim, and it needs its own evidence.

Two objects get confused here, routinely. A negative result excludes an effect larger than the instrument's reach. A null result is the absence of a detectable effect, which may mean the effect is small, the reach short, or the coordinate wrong. Reporting the second as the first is not a statistical error. It is a claim about scope that the data do not carry, and at that point the order's severity becomes the alibi of its governance.

## 3. The window is a margin in time

The index is a ratio at a moment. The body is not at a moment. Accumulation sets the upper edge of the window: a parent drug is converted to a reactive metabolite at a rate, and the conjugate that detoxifies the metabolite is consumed at a rate. Let formation outrun conjugation and the protective pool depletes, and injury follows — the structure of acetaminophen's dose-dependent hepatic necrosis, and of glutathione's protective role against it (Mitchell et al. 1973).

So the margin is temporal, not vertical. The same dose is inside the window at one duration and outside it at another, and the clock that decides is the time to deplete the protective pool against the time to clear the drug. The margin survives while clearance outruns depletion. It closes when the two rates cross. *Therapeutic window* names a projection of that set of trajectories onto the dose axis. Two patients at the same dose are therefore not in the same place, because their rates differ, and a level inside the projected window is not evidence that the trajectory is inside the margin.

Here the temporal site adds what the formal one cannot. The protocol fixes a duration because a trial must end. The body does not end its exposure when the trial does, and accumulation is a function of the exposure's length, not of its size. Dose-finding that holds duration fixed and follows the level is measuring the projection, and a projection mistaken for the surface licenses exactly the exposure the surface forbids.

## 4. The circuit, and the office that is missing

Formalization fixes the coordinates, severity prices the test at those coordinates, the clock prices the exposure. The signature $(1, 2, 0)$ reads one epistemic site, two vital, governance empty. Why the exposed defect is the empty one, and not either of the two that are full, is what the chapter still owes.

The empty site would have to hold a duty and a ledger. The duty is to look for the harm the design was not sized to see: someone obliged to run the study after the licence, on the population the licence reached, for the horizon the trial did not cover. The ledger prices false-alarm cost and missed loss together, because the diagnostic threshold belongs to consequences, not to evidence. The Pauker–Kassirer threshold

$$p^{\ast}\_{\mathrm{PK}} = \frac{C\_{\mathrm{FP}}}{C\_{\mathrm{FP}} + L\_{\mathrm{FN}}}$$

is rational for whoever bears both terms (Pauker & Kassirer 1975). Where the false-alarm cost falls on an institution and the missed loss falls on a patient, each party has a threshold, both are correct, and no office reconciles them.

None exists, and that absence makes this a governance order rather than an epistemic one. The sponsor owns the database and owes no duty to look for what its own design excludes. The regulator licenses a point, not a surface. The patient who bears the rare harm cannot commission the study, and their report enters the record as an anecdote to be discounted. The trial's finite reach, an honest property of an instrument, becomes the sentence *the evidence does not show harm* — true, and non-responsive to the question the exposed population is asking.

Collapse Proposition 3 then applies without strain ([Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md)). Liability is limited on one side and unlimited on the other. The negative trial supplies the agnotological excuse. The testimony of the harmed is discounted until it is discounted to nothing. And the reserve, which here is the population's exposure, is treated as free, because the cost of the exposure is paid by people who are not parties to the licence. The arithmetic of the threshold is right and the ledger is split. That is the governance defect in its purest form: not a wrong calculation, but a calculation performed on two ledgers, with no office holding both.

The residue this order contributes is the pricing of the unmeasured region. A claim about a drug is a claim about a point, and the scope of a negative result is exactly the coordinates the protocol fixed. Everything the order says about dose, window and threshold is a way of keeping that scope attached to the sentence. The empty governance site is why the scope is so easy to lose.
