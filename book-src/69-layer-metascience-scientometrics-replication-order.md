## 1. The value of a result is a property of the field

Picture a hospital lab team reading a freshly published study claiming a striking treatment link at a conventional significance cutoff ($p < \alpha$). Should the team stake multi-year public research funding on that finding? Before trusting a single *p*-value, you need to know how common true hypotheses are in that field and how easily flexible analysis can turn a null experiment into an apparent hit.

Guard site `T2Severity` protects scientific inference by checking whether a published hit survives the field's true false-positive and flexibility risks rather than taking one paper in isolation. A significant finding's worth belongs to its field's selection ecology, not an isolated paper.

Let $R > 0$ be the pre-study odds of true to null relationships, with statistical power $1 - \beta \in (0, 1]$ ($\beta \in [0, 1)$) and significance threshold $\alpha \in (0, 1)$. Write $u \in [0, 1]$ for the share of non-findings turned into positive reports through analyst freedom or selection (Ioannidis 2005). Write the positive denominator $D\_{\mathrm{PPV}} = (1-\beta)R + u\beta R + \alpha + u(1-\alpha) > 0$. Then the positive predictive value $\mathrm{PPV}$ (Ioannidis 2005) and its sensitivity to bias $u$ satisfy:

$$\mathrm{PPV} = \frac{(1-\beta)R + u\beta R}{D\_{\mathrm{PPV}}}, \qquad \frac{\partial\mkern3mu\mathrm{PPV}}{\partial u} = \frac{R(\alpha + \beta - 1)}{D\_{\mathrm{PPV}}^{2}}.$$

In plain words, $\mathrm{PPV}$ is the fraction of published "positive" claims that are actually true, and that fraction strictly shrinks as bias $u$ grows whenever a study has nontrivial power ($1 - \beta > \alpha$).

At zero bias ($u = 0$), true positives outnumber false ones only if $(1-\beta)R > \alpha$ (Ioannidis 2005). Once $u > 0$, the term $u(1-\alpha)$ puts a floor $\alpha + u(1-\alpha) \ge u$ under false positives. Tightening $\alpha \to 0^+$ at fixed $u \in (0, 1)$ therefore caps $\mathrm{PPV}$ strictly below $((1-\beta)+u\beta)R/(((1-\beta)+u\beta)R + u) < 1$ while also cutting fixed-sample power.

## 2. Selection sets the published effect size

Guard site `T3Archive` monitors the unpublished file drawer so journals are not misread as an unbiased sample of conducted experiments. Suppose true effects are zero, so noisy estimates follow $\hat{\theta}\_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma\_i^2)$ with standard error $\sigma\_i > 0$. Two-sided filtering ($|\hat{\theta}\_i| \ge c\sigma\_i$, $c > 0$) preserves zero mean while inflating magnitude $|\hat{\theta}\_i|$, and directional filtering ($\hat{\theta}\_i \ge c\sigma\_i$) inflates the signed average linearly in $\sigma\_i$, peaking in the noisiest studies. Halving $\sigma\_i$ halves those published effects, proving the archive tracks selection correlation $\rho\_{R,Y} \ne 0$ (where $R \in \lbrace 0, 1\rbrace$ denotes the publication-selection indicator, distinct from Section 1's pre-study odds $R$; [Chapter 45](45-world-to-archive-big-data-paradox.md); [Layer 09](59-layer-experimental-sampling-causal-order.md)) rather than nature.

Rosenthal (1979) priced file-drawer tolerance across $k \ge 1$ published studies (distinct from Section 5's order-statistic rank $k$) with test statistics $Z\_i$ and critical cutoff $z\_\alpha > 0$. When $\sum\_{i=1}^{k} Z\_i > z\_{\alpha}\sqrt{k}$, adding $N \ge 0$ unpublished $Z = 0$ studies until $\sum\_{i=1}^{k} Z\_i/\sqrt{k + N} = z\_{\alpha}$ yields the fail-safe number $N\_{\mathrm{fs}} > 0$:

$$N\_{\mathrm{fs}} = \frac{\bigl(\sum\_{i=1}^{k} Z\_i\bigr)^{2}}{z\_{\alpha}^{2}} - k.$$

Read aloud, $N\_{\mathrm{fs}}$ counts how many zero-effect studies sitting in file drawers it would take to push the combined *Z*-score back down to significance cutoff $z\_\alpha$.

Because $N\_{\mathrm{fs}}$ uses unweighted $Z\_i$, it ignores effect size, study precision, and heterogeneity, measuring Stouffer significance tolerance rather than true hidden study counts (Rosenthal 1979). Under directional null filtering, suppressed negative draws pull cancelling counts toward linear scaling in $\sum Z\_i$.

## 3. The parameter no archive can supply

$\mathrm{PPV}$ demands pre-study odds $R$, power $1 - \beta$, and bias $u$, yet published archives filter out the denominator of $R$. Large replication programs reveal how steeply published claims shrink once tested symmetrically.

In psychology, the Open Science Collaboration (2015) replicated 100 published studies. While 97 percent of originals reported significance (93 with $p < .05$ and four borderline up to $p = 0.0567$), only 36 percent of replications succeeded (35/97 positive originals, 36 percent overall). Across 99 pairs, mean correlation $r$ dropped from $0.403$ to $0.197$ (Open Science Collaboration 2015).

Preclinical cancer biology further separates **repeatability** from **replicability**. Across 193 planned experiments in 53 papers, public data existed for only 4 (2%; 68% unshared), code for 1/78 (1%) analyses, and sufficient protocol detail for 0 (0%), leaving only 50 experiments (26%) in 23 papers completable (Errington et al. 2021a). Across 158 evaluated effects (136 positive, 22 null), median positive standardized effect fell 85% from 2.96 to 0.43, with 92% [108/118] smaller and 97% [94/97] numerically smaller (Errington et al. 2021b). Only 43% (42/97) replicated same-direction significance (12% animal, 54% non-animal), and 46% (51/112) passed three or more of five criteria (40% positive, 39/97; 80% null, 12/15) (Errington et al. 2021b).

To avoid guessing unknown odds, evaluate $\mathrm{PPV}$ across an admissible rectangle $(R, u, 1-\beta)$, anchoring $R$ from Stage-1 Registered Reports sealed at $t\_{\mathrm{disp}}$ with audited pre-seal pilots. Where registries are absent, replace point $\mathrm{PPV}$ with the $R$-invariant null false-positive rate $\alpha + u(1-\alpha)$ and publication likelihood ratio $\mathrm{LR}\_{\mathrm{pub}} = ((1-\beta) + u\beta)/(\alpha + u(1-\alpha))$ ranging over an admissible sensitivity rectangle in $(u, 1-\beta)$ rather than a single guessed point.

## 4. The correction that overcorrects

Even when zero publication bias exists, common trim-and-fill corrections spuriously adjust meta-analyses whenever true between-study heterogeneity is present, which behaves like [Layer 08](58-layer-metrological-information-geometric-order.md)'s non-vanishing **Type-B systematic floor $u\_B$** across protocols rather than Type-A random scatter that larger study counts shrink away, and is the routine state of meta-analyzed literatures (Terrin, Schmid, Lau & Olkin 2003). True variance spreads precise studies unevenly, and practical sample-size planning often couples sample size inversely to expected effect size (Terrin, Schmid, Lau & Olkin 2003). Sensitivity analysis must therefore bound estimates over a declared weight family spanning zero to strong bias, recognizing that parametric selection models suffer convergence failures and never substitute for prospective record retention (Terrin, Schmid, Lau & Olkin 2003).

## 5. Discovery control that survives dependence and peeking

Within a closed family of $K \ge 1$ hypotheses at error target $\alpha \in (0, 1)$, the *e*-BH procedure (Wang & Ramdas 2022; [Chapter 30](30-probability-as-language.md)) guards false discoveries without requiring independent tests. Assign each hypothesis an *e*-value $E\_j \ge 0$ satisfying $\mathbb{E}\_{H\_0}[E\_j] \le 1$ across the $K\_0$ true nulls in $\mathcal{H}\_0$. Sorting them as $E\_{(1)} \ge \dots \ge E\_{(K)}$, reject the $N\_{\mathrm{rej}} = k^{\ast}$ largest ($k^{\ast} = 0$ if none qualifies):

$$k^{\ast} = \max\Bigl\lbrace{}k \in \lbrace{}1,\dots,K\rbrace : E\_{(k)} \ge \frac{K}{\alpha k}\Bigr\rbrace.$$

In plain words, *e*-BH accepts the largest subset of top *e*-values whose values clear the step-up bar $K/(\alpha k)$.

For $N\_{\mathrm{rej}} \ge 1$, every rejected null obeys $E\_j \ge E\_{(N\_{\mathrm{rej}})} \ge K/(\alpha N\_{\mathrm{rej}})$, so $\mathbf{1}\lbrace{}j \text{ rejected}\rbrace/\max(N\_{\mathrm{rej}},1) \le \alpha E\_j / K$ even under arbitrary dependence. Summing expectation across true nulls bounds the False Discovery Rate ($\mathrm{FDR}$):

$$\mathrm{FDR} \le \sum\_{j \in \mathcal{H}\_0} \frac{\alpha\mkern3mu\mathbb{E}[E\_j]}{K} \le \frac{\alpha K\_0}{K} \le \alpha.$$

Read aloud, the expected false-discovery share stays capped at $\alpha$ without assuming studies or outcomes are independent.

Unlike Benjamini and Hochberg's (1995) *p*-value rule, *e*-BH holds under arbitrary dependence (Wang & Ramdas 2022); however, optional stopping across filtration-adapted stopping times $\tau\_j$ ($\lbrace\tau\_j \le t\rbrace \in \mathcal{F}\_t$; Ramdas, Grünwald, Vovk & Shafer 2023) is valid **only** when static fixed-horizon *e*-values $E\_j$ are replaced by genuine stopped $(\mathcal{F}\_t)$-adapted ***e*-processes** $E\_{j,\tau\_j}$ (generated by $(E\_{j,t})\_{t \ge 1}$ obeying $\mathbb{E}\_{H\_0}[E\_{j,\tau\_j}] \le 1$, never by peeking at pointwise fixed-$n$ *e*-values; [Chapter 43](43-inquiry-openness-multiverse.md)). That design trades some fixed-sample power for anytime peeking validity while staying competitive in stopping time. Uniform weights ($w\_j = 1$) lose power when exploratory and confirmatory hypotheses mix without pre-sealed prior weights $w\_j \ge 0$ ($\frac{1}{K}\sum\_{j=1}^{K} w\_j = 1$, replacing $E\_j$ by $w\_j E\_j$).

Because *e*-BH only guards pre-enumerated closed families ([Chapter 43](43-inquiry-openness-multiverse.md)), file-drawer omissions ($K\_{\mathrm{pub}} < K$) break $\mathbb{E}\_{H\_0}[E\_j\mid\mathrm{pub}] \le 1$, unlogged automated forks bypass $K$, and *e*-BH never triggers an operational halt (§6). Pairing confirmatory *e*-BH with multiverse auditing (Steegen et al. 2016; Simonsohn, Simmons & Nelson 2020; [Layer 09](59-layer-experimental-sampling-causal-order.md)) exposes analyst branching without inflating $K$.
