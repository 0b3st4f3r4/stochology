## 1. The slot the grammar does not let the speaker leave empty

A hedge is an option. A speaker who says *probably* has chosen to say it; a speaker who says nothing has chosen otherwise. Silence about the source is informative only where saying something was available. Grammatical evidentiality is the case where it is not. Aikhenvald's survey documents languages in which a declarative verb cannot be produced without a morpheme whose value is the speaker's way of knowing, witnessed, deduced, inferred or heard, and in which Tuyuca and Quechua fill that slot with different values according to how the speaker came to know what is asserted (Aikhenvald 2004). The speaker does not decide to register the source. The language requires it, and a bare assertion is ungrammatical rather than cautious.

That changes what the sentence is. Where marking is optional, the pair (proposition, no mark) is a possible utterance, and its meaning is ambiguous between confidence, haste and evasion; a reader who wants the source must ask afterwards. Where marking is obligatory, the pair is not in the language at all. Write $M$ for the evidential value carried by a declarative sentence. In an obligatory system $P(M = \varnothing) = 0$: the map from utterance to provenance is total, and total by construction, not by the speaker's diligence. In an optional system $P(M = \varnothing) > 0$, and the unmarked sentence is confounded: it may be a claim whose source is certain, or one whose source is being withheld, and the two are typographically identical.

This is the archival site, in its purest form. What makes a record a document is not that it is written down but that its provenance is fixed at creation and cannot be revised afterwards without producing a different document. Trouillot located the manufacture of silence in the creation of sources and their assembly into an archive (Trouillot 1995); Stoler showed the bureaucratic categories deciding what is legible before any historian reads the file (Stoler 2009). Both describe a deposit that decides provenance from outside the sentence: the form asks for a name, a date, a classification, and the answers are the administrator's. The evidential morpheme performs the same function from inside the sentence, with a completeness the form cannot reach, because the form can be left blank and the verb cannot. Every declarative utterance arrives already documented, and the documentary question has an answer the speaker had no power to withhold: *how does this speaker know*.

The claim is not that such languages are more truthful, though it is easy to slide there. A grammar that forces a speaker to mark hearsay also forces the speaker to mark hearsay as hearsay, which is exactly what a rumour wants to be called. The claim is structural and narrower. The grammar removes one degree of freedom from the speaker, and the one it removes is the one bureaucratic prose uses to launder an inference into a fact. March and Simon named that laundering uncertainty absorption: an inference communicated upward while the evidence that could qualify it is discarded on the way (March & Simon 1958). The absorption requires a syntax in which a bare declarative is well formed. Where the morpheme is obligatory there is no such syntax, so absorption has to be performed by another operation: by dropping the sentence, not by stripping its slot.

## 2. The mark is inside the act, not a comment on it

The second site decides what kind of thing the morpheme is. It is tempting to read the evidential particle as a comment on the assertion: the assertion goes through, the particle adds a footnote about its reliability. That reading is wrong, and the error is instructive. Austin's account of the performative and Searle's constitutive rules put it precisely: an utterance does not describe an act and then perform it, it counts as the act in virtue of the rules in force (Austin 1962; Searle 1969). An assertion made with the visual morpheme and the same proposition asserted with the hearsay morpheme are not one act with two comments attached. They are two acts. The marking is a component of the act's identity, the way the tense of a verb is a component of the claim rather than a remark about when it is made.

Once the mark is inside the act, three consequences follow that no optional-hedging account can produce. First, the mark cannot be corrected without re-performing the act. An erratum about a footnote repairs the sentence; an erratum about an evidential morpheme is a new assertion, and the record now holds two acts instead of one corrected act. Second, the mark cannot report on itself. The slot records how the speaker came to know the proposition. There is no second slot recording how the speaker came to know the provenance of the provenance, so the marking certifies one hop and stays silent about the chain the hop sits in. Where a claim crosses $k$ reporting hops the warrant it carries is a product,

$$w\_k = \prod\_{i=1}^{k}\bigl(1 - \varepsilon\_i\bigr),$$

which decays geometrically whenever the per-hop losses are positive; the marginal hop costs most nearest the observation, where $\varepsilon\_i$ is largest. The morpheme marks the value of $k = 1$. The document then travels with $k$ unrecorded and unmarkable, because the grammar that would have marked it has exactly one slot and the slot is already spent on the first hop.

The third consequence costs the most in an institution. Because the mark is part of the act, a record that carries it cannot also carry a running commentary on the reliability of the acts that quoted it. A minute that says *the officer reported that the prisoner had been seen* has performed its own act correctly and has said nothing about whether the officer's report was itself a report. The formalization that looks like the remedy, a separate field for source reliability filled at each level of the hierarchy, is not the same object. A field can be omitted, and it is filled by the level above, which is the party whose interest the field would constrain. The grammar cannot be omitted, and it is filled by the party who speaks. No documentary form reproduces it, and the cost is paid in corrigibility: the act is final in a way a footnote is not.

## 3. Who answers when the declared source is false

The third site is liability, and its question is not epistemological. Suppose the marked source is false: the speaker asserts, with the hearsay morpheme, that the bridge is closed, and there is no closure. The content came from someone else. The act was the speaker's. Naming the source does not move the obligation to answer, and the reason is structural, not moral. The author of an act is fixed by the event of utterance; the source of a content is a variable recorded inside the act. Two objects, and the record keeps both. So the record can always say where the claim came from and never says who is bound by it.

Fricker's account of testimonial injustice sharpens this, against the intuition that declaration exonerates (Fricker 2007). Credibility is allocated by the hearer to the speaker, and the status-based discount the hearer applies lands on the speaker, not on the source the speaker names. A witness whose standing is discounted by $\delta\_{\mathrm{status}} \in (0,1)$ does not recover credibility by attributing the claim: the attribution is itself testimony, and it inherits the status discount. Declaring a source adds a claim rather than transferring one, so the declaration cannot lighten the speaker's position. It can only add a second party to the record.

The institutional form of that structure is where the vital margin would have to sit, and where it is absent. At the point of speech the obligation is perfect: the speaker performed the act and can be asked. At the point of citation the obligation is empty, because the archive holds the sentence and not the speaker, and the citation quotes the sentence without its morpheme — not out of bad faith, but because the quoting genre has no slot for it. Stoler's point returns with a different object: the categories of the receiving institution decide what is legible, and an evidential morpheme is legible only to a reader of the language (Stoler 2009). A ministry reading a translated summary has a sentence with no mark and no speaker, and the two absences compose. Trouillot's third and fourth moments — retrieval into narrative and the retrospective assignment of significance — are the moments at which the mark is dropped and the liability is not re-attached (Trouillot 1995).

Nothing in this circuit stops the citation. That is what the signature says.

## 4. The three sites are one circuit

Read as three literatures, the sites look independent: a fact about grammar, a fact about speech acts, a fact about responsibility. They are one circuit, and the guard combination states that fact.

The record fixes provenance, and fixes it totally, which is what makes the utterance a document. The act fixes who is bound, and fixes it by the event rather than by the content, which is what makes the marking unrepairable. Liability survives the declaration, and survives because the declaration is itself an act, which is what makes the record a record of obligations and not only of sources. Repair at any one site undoes another. Make the marking optional and the document property is lost, because silence becomes possible and silence is then uninformative. Make the mark a comment that can be inspected and corrected separately and the act is lost, because the comment needs a mark of its own and the regress has no floor. Make the declaration transfer liability and accountability at the point of utterance is lost, because the speaker becomes a conduit and the act has no author. Accountability at the point of citation is not gained either, since the named source is not in the room.

The signature $(1, 0, 2)$ says what the combination does not contain. One site is epistemic, two are governance, and the vital margin is absent: nothing in the circuit carries a stop-work trigger. A language can mark every source perfectly, an institution can record every act faithfully, and a jurisprudence can hold every speaker to account at the moment of speech — and the chain of citation can still run to the point where the mark has been translated away and the obligation has no addressee. That is the second collapse, and the defect the order names is not a gap in the analysis of meaning. It is the absence of a margin that would halt the citation at the hop where the mark stops being readable.

The residue after the general case is what this order contributes that no other order pays for. Chapter 28 has already argued that obligatory marking defeats uncertainty absorption at the point of speech. The remainder is that the defeat is local. The slot is filled once, at the act, and the act is the only place in the circuit where the filling is compulsory. Everything downstream (the file, the summary, the citation, the policy) is a genre with an optional slot, and an optional slot in a chain of genres is a slot that empties.

## By the end of this chapter you can

1. Say why an obligatory evidential morpheme makes an utterance a document, and distinguish that from a speaker's optional hedge by the probability that the source slot is left empty.
2. State the cost of locating the mark inside the speech act rather than beside it — unrepairability and the one-hop limit — using the geometric decay of warrant across reporting hops.
3. Explain why declaring a source does not transfer liability, and identify the point in the citation chain at which the obligation loses its addressee.

## Bridge

Layer 21 closes the stratum's account of the warrant a sentence carries before anyone reads it: the provenance is filled by the grammar, the filling is part of the act, and the obligation it creates belongs to the speaker who is no longer present when the sentence is quoted. What it leaves is the case in which the source is not a person but a copy, where the question is no longer how the speaker came to know, but how many independent times the thing was known at all. [Chapter 72](72-layer-philological-epigraphic-textual-order.md) states [Layer 22](72-layer-philological-epigraphic-textual-order.md), *Philological, Epigraphic & Textual Order*, and begins from the witness that has no memory and no liability: the manuscript.
