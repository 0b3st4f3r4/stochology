## 1. The witnesses are fewer than the copies

A text survives as copies, and copies are not observations. Lachmann's method turns on that distinction: the witnesses are collated, the manuscripts that demonstrably derive from another surviving manuscript are eliminated, and what remains is arranged in a tree whose branchings record the points at which the transmission divided (Lachmann 1850). The tree is not a convenience of presentation. It is the argument, because it converts a pile of documents into a count of independent acts of copying, and only the second is evidence about what the archetype carried.

The arithmetic of the conversion is the arithmetic of clustering. Counting copies is not a rough approximation to the right quantity. It is a different quantity. Let $n$ be the number of surviving witnesses and a cluster a set of witnesses descending from one damaged exemplar without an independent check. With $\rho\_I$ the intraclass correlation of their readings and $m$ the average cluster size, the effective number of witnesses is

$$n\_{\mathrm{eff}} = \frac{n}{1 + (m-1)\rho\_I},$$

so a family of copies that all descend from a single archetype has $\rho\_I \to 1$ and $n\_{\mathrm{eff}} \to 1$. The *codices descripti* the method exists to eliminate are the special case in which the common archetype is itself a damaged copy still in hand; the collapse is the same in both, but only there can the collator remove a witness instead of discounting one. A collation of many manuscripts that are all copies of one damaged copy carries the evidential weight of one manuscript, and more collation does not move it, because the additional witnesses are not additional observations. The discipline's maxim states the same result without the algebra: witnesses are to be weighed, not counted. The number of documents is a nominal volume, and nominal volume is what a design effect divides.

What the stemma records is precise. What it does not record is more precise still. It records the transmission the survivors attest: branchings that left traces in at least two surviving lines, readings a surviving family shares, the elimination of copies that carry nothing new. It does not record the text that was never copied. A reading that died at the first copy of the archetype has no descendant and therefore no node, and a tree fitted to the survivors cannot show a branch that left no leaf. This is not a defect of Lachmann's procedure. It is the boundary of what a tree over a surviving set can say about a transmitted set that contains it. The correct object is a tree over everything that was copied; the object the method has is a tree over everything that was found. Every inference from the second to the first extrapolates from topology, and the topology was fitted to the second.

The consequence is a limit with a sharp form: the stemma cannot be evidence about its own completeness. Where two independent lines survive, the archetype's reading at a locus is identified up to the resolution the lines provide. Where one line survives, the archetype is the surviving line and the tree is a path. The method returns the text of the exemplar and calls it the archetype, which is true and uninformative. The case is the philological form of an estimator with no overlap: the quantity is not zero and not infinite, it is undefined, and the honest report is that the witnesses are not linked.

## 2. Severity is graded on a scale the witnesses supply

The second site is the one that makes the tree possible, and it is usually passed over. A stemma is not built from agreement in readings. It is built from agreement in error. Two witnesses sit on the same branch when they share a reading judged to be a corruption, because shared corruption is far less likely to have been invented twice than shared correctness. The judgment that a reading is a corruption is therefore prior to the tree, and it is graded. An orthographic slip, a haplography at a line end, a harmonization to a parallel passage, a substantive interpolation: all are errors, and they do not carry the same weight in the argument.

The problem is that the scale on which they are graded is reconstructed from the same witnesses it grades. Which readings count as errors is decided by comparison across the collation; the tree is estimated from that error set; and the reading at the root — the archetype's reading, which is the edition — is defined as the one the tree places there. Written out, with $\sigma$ the error indicator over the variant readings and $\tau$ the tree, the procedure is a pair of maps

$$\tau = \hat{\tau}(\sigma), \qquad \sigma = \hat{\sigma}(\tau),$$

and what the method returns is a fixed point of their composition. A fixed point identifies only when it is unique. Where two trees are each consistent with the error classification each of them implies, the pair $(\tau, \sigma)$ is not identified, and the ambiguity is not a small perturbation around a good answer. It is an ambiguity about which readings are corrupt, and therefore about what the text says.

This is where the severity site does real work and where it is weakest. The grading lets the method prefer one shared reading over another, and the grading is the least stable part of the procedure. Its criteria — which reading is harder, which is more characteristic of the author, which is more likely to have been smoothed by a copyist — are judgments about the author made from the witnesses whose relation to the author is exactly what is being established. A philologist who grades a reading as a corruption because it does not sound like the author has imported the conclusion into the premise. The method survives this only because the import is visible: the apparatus prints the rejected readings, so a reader can re-grade them and obtain a different tree.

The epigraphic half of the order has the same structure with a different instrument, and the difference is instructive. On stone, damage is measured, not graded. The physical dimensions of the break and the regularity of the carving grid fix how many character slots are missing, and the convention prints the restoration inside brackets whose width is the bound (van Groningen 1932). On a regular grid a gap of exactly $k$ slots admits $A^k$ strings over an alphabet of $A \ge 2$ signs, while non-empty restorations of $1$ through $k$ signs number $\sum\_{j=1}^k A^j = (A^{k+1}-A)/(A-1)$; the bracket is a width rather than a grade, and the stone sets the width rather than a judgment about the author. That is why the epigraphic half is the stronger one, and why its limit is different. The bound is honest and the restoration can still be wrong, because a bound on the number of characters is not a bound on the number of sentences.

## 3. Recension is an ordering, and the ordering does not choose

The third site is formalization, and recension is where it is most visible. The procedure is a sequence of operations on a finite set: collate the witnesses at every locus, eliminate the copies that carry no independent reading, arrange the remainder by shared error, read the archetype off the root. Each operation is formal, reproducible, open to inspection, and that is the whole of its authority. A reader who disputes the edition can dispute a step rather than a taste.

What the formalization buys is bounded, and the bound can be counted. A stemma is a rooted tree with the witnesses as leaves, and the number of rooted binary trees on $n$ labelled leaves is

$$(2n-3)!! = \prod\_{j=1}^{n-1}(2j-1),$$

which grows faster than any exponential in $n$, while the number of characters available to discriminate among them grows linearly in the length of the text. For any collation of more than a handful of independent witnesses the space of admissible trees is vastly larger than the evidence can separate. The procedure does not return a tree. It returns the set of trees that minimize the number of inferred changes. Parsimony is an ordering, and an ordering on a set that large returns minimal elements, not an element.

This is not a technicality about search. Where two trees are both maximally parsimonious and disagree about the archetype's reading at one locus, no step of the procedure decides between them. The decision is made by grading one of the two readings as the more likely corruption, which returns the argument to the severity site of §2, with its reconstructed scale. The formal order does not close the question it appears to close. It localizes the question at a smaller number of loci and hands it back. The honest output of a recension is a text with an apparatus, and the apparatus is the set of places where the ordering did not choose.

The limit has a precise shape. Formalization converts a dispute about readings into a dispute about trees, a dispute about trees into a dispute about error classification, and there it stops, because error classification is not a formal operation. A procedure that claimed otherwise would have to derive the severity scale from the same data it grades, which is the fixed point of §2 and not a derivation. So what recension achieves is two-part: it eliminates the witnesses that add no information, and it identifies the archetype's reading wherever the tree is unique. Both parts are real. Neither is the constitution of the text.

## 4. The circuit and the missing governance site

The three sites are one circuit, and the guard combination states the fact. Transmission among survivors is what the record contains, and its completeness is not a property the record can report. Severity is the scale that makes the record readable, and the scale is fitted to the record. Formal ordering is what makes the reading reproducible, and the ordering returns a set at exactly the loci where the scale is contested. Repair at one site undoes another. More witnesses without a severity scale change nothing, because the design effect divides the additional copies until they are one. A refined scale without a formal order produces a tree nobody can check. A formal order without a governance site publishes a set as though it were an element.

The signature $(2, 1, 0)$ says what the circuit does not contain. Two sites are epistemic, one is vital, and the governance axis is empty: nothing assigns the office that may declare the text settled, and nothing assigns the office that must report what the record does not contain. So the edition's silence about the uncopied text is not a scandal. No party's duty would be to state it; the edition states its witnesses, a list of what was found, and does not state its non-witnesses, the set that has no list because it has no members to list. An empty governance axis is not the absence of capture but its cheapness. With no office holding the power to declare the text settled, there is nothing for an interested party to capture and no one who must answer for the record's silence, so the interest that would otherwise buy the office spends nothing and the silence costs it nothing. The bracket is the only place the uncertainty is printed, and a bracket is a typographic convention, not a decision rule. It marks a width on stone and a doubt in a text, and it obliges no one to act on either.

The comparison with the archival order is where the difference becomes exact. An archive of lives can be corrected with a bound because the population is at least nominally defined: there are people, two registers can count them twice, and the overlap between the registers estimates what neither contains. A text has one register. There is no second list of the manuscripts that were never copied, so the estimator that repairs the archive has no input here, and the honest report is a statement of the frame rather than a bound. That is the residue this order contributes to the stratum: the case in which the dark figure exists, is large, and is not estimable, so the only defensible procedure is to print the frame and refuse the number.

## By the end of this chapter you can

1. Convert a collation into an effective number of witnesses, and say why eliminating the *codices descripti* is a design correction rather than an editorial preference.
2. Show that the severity scale which makes a stemma possible is reconstructed from the witnesses the stemma orders, and say what the method returns when the fixed point is not unique.
3. State the limit of parsimony as an ordering — the count of admissible trees against the number of discriminating readings — and say what the apparatus prints in place of a decision.

## Exercises

1. **Nominal against effective volume.** With $n$ witnesses, average cluster size $m$ and intraclass correlation $\rho\_I$, compute $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}$ for the case in which all witnesses descend from one damaged exemplar, and say what a collation of thirty such copies can establish about the archetype.
2. **The fixed point.** Write the pair of maps $\tau = \hat{\tau}(\sigma)$ and $\sigma = \hat{\sigma}(\tau)$ and construct a two-tree case in which each tree implies an error classification that supports it. Say what the edition can print and what it cannot.
3. **Counting the trees.** Using $(2n-3)!!$, say how the number of admissible stemmata grows with the number of independent witnesses, and why a longer text does not close the gap at the same rate.
4. **Stone against manuscript.** Contrast the epigraphic bracket, whose width is set by the physical break, with the philological grade, whose scale is set by the collation. Say which of the two is bounded and why the other is not.
5. **The missing office.** Name the decision that the circuit's empty governance site leaves unassigned, and say what a printed apparatus would have to carry for a reader to make it without re-doing the collation.

## Bridge

Layer 22 closes the stratum's account of the copy: how many independent times a text was known, how the corruption that orders the witnesses is graded on a scale the witnesses supply, and where a formal recension stops and hands the decision back. What it leaves is the case in which the record is not a copy of a text but a residue of a process — where the question is not how the witnesses relate to one another but which of them the ground chose to keep.
