## By the end of this chapter you can

1. Distinguish a taphonomic filter from a collection defect, and say why no improvement in excavation removes the bias the filter produces.
2. Derive the dependence of a survival rate on the observed window, and say what a durability comparison conducted on young deposits licenses and what it does not.
3. Keep a corpus share and a population share apart in the sentence that carries them, and state the three things a claim must print for a later reader to recompute its frame.

## 1. The record is a residue, not a population

Imagine excavating a medieval coastal town and unearthing hundreds of glazed stoneware bowls beside only three scraps of woven linen. Without remembering how soil bacteria rot plant cloth while leaving fired clay untouched, an analyst would conclude that townspeople barely used textiles. What sits in the museum tray is the output of a physical filter, not a faithful cross-section of the town.

Guard site `T3Archive` separates pre-discovery destruction from recovery error during excavation. In historiography, Ibn Khaldūn already insisted in his *c.* 1377 *Muqaddimah* that chroniclers' tales satisfy demographic, ecological, and social coherence before entering evidence (Intro./Book I; Rosenthal 1958). Efremov then made fossilization an active physical process with its own laws (Efremov 1940), turning every archive into a filtered output rather than a random sample.

### The nested survival chain

Consider four strictly nested stages without mixing ($S \subseteq B \subseteq D \subseteq A$): what lived ($A$), what died ($D$), what entered burial ($B$), and what was recovered ($S$). Assume conditional Markov independence within each material class $c$, with positive transition probabilities $P(D \mid A, c) > 0$ and $P(B \mid D, c) > 0$ so conditionals are well defined. Then net admission probability into the record factors across all three stages:

$$P(S \mid A, c) = P(D \mid A, c)\mkern3muP(B \mid D, c)\mkern3muP(S \mid B, c).$$

In plain words, reaching a museum or archive shelf requires surviving death, burial, and discovery in sequence—and a zero at any stage wipes out the entire product.

Unweighted averages fail across mixed classes, and reworked fossils, stratigraphic mixing, contamination, or later codex interpolation break the nested inclusion $S \subseteq B \subseteq D \subseteq A$. Where nesting holds, soft tissues or uncopied oral speech vanish from $S$ no matter how abundant they were in $A$, and sharper excavation cannot multiply zero back into existence.

Two distinct failures sit at different links. A collection defect enters recovery $P(S \mid B, c)$ through coarse sieving or lost provenience—creating selection bias ($\rho\_{R,Y} \ne 0$, Meng 2018) and permanent loss in dug beds, remediable only in unexcavated deposits or uncatalogued stores. By contrast, the taphonomic filter operates upstream inside $P(D \mid A, c)\mkern3muP(B \mid D, c)$ via soil chemistry, transport, and routine disposal (palimpsests, binding scrap, scheduled shredding, distinct from interested suppression in [Chapter 74](74-layer-critical-archival-transitional-justice-order.md)). Because that filter acts before any observer arrives, better recovery cannot undo it.

## 2. Deep time: the rate belongs to the window

Guard site `T5Temporal` tracks how exponential decay over an observation window warps apparent abundance—and clocks how long fragile bone or paper will endure before crossing physical ruin.

### Exponential attrition and apparent growth

Attrition compounds over time. If fraction $p \in (0, 1]$ of a class survives one interval, then $p^{n}$ survives $n \in \mathbb{Z}\_{\ge 0}$ intervals. In continuous time $T \ge 0$ with non-negative hazard $\lambda(u) \ge 0$, cumulative survival follows $S(T) = \exp\bigl(-\int\_0^T \lambda(u)\mkern3mu du\bigr)$. Under a stationary constant hazard $\lambda > 0$, that integral reduces to:

$$S(T) = e^{-\lambda T}$$

Read aloud, surviving share shrinks exponentially with deposit age $T$ at constant decay rate $\lambda$. This simple curve fails whenever episodic catastrophes (fires, library dissolutions, flood pulses) or biphasic decay (rapid early *post-mortem* DNA cleavage or fast discard of ephemera before monastic custody) concentrate losses upfront. Under constant $\lambda > 0$, a site's survival rate belongs strictly to age $T$, not to a timeless material property.

Time windows also skew comparisons between classes and create false growth trends. Compare two classes with positive admission baselines $\alpha\_1, \alpha\_2 > 0$ and decay hazards $0 < \lambda\_1 < \lambda\_2$, so class one is more durable ($S\_i(T) = \alpha\_i e^{-\lambda\_i T}$). Over window $T \ge 0$, their survival ratio becomes:

$$\frac{S\_1(T)}{S\_2(T)} = \frac{\alpha\_1}{\alpha\_2}\mkern2mu e^{(\lambda\_2 - \lambda\_1)T},$$

In plain words, the durable class outlasts the fragile class by a ratio that starts at $\alpha\_1/\alpha\_2$ as $T \to 0^+$ (equaling one only when $\alpha\_1 = \alpha\_2$) and grows exponentially without bound as $T$ increases. Comparing durability on young deposits therefore understates divergence on old ones: although absolute survival $S\_i(T)$ drops for both, corpus share shifts relentlessly toward durable goods.

Across a formation span $[t\_1, t\_2]$ ($0 < t\_1 < t\_2$, distinct from guard-site labels $T\_1, T\_2$), strict convexity of $e^{-\lambda T}$ guarantees that window-averaged survival $(e^{-\lambda t\_1} - e^{-\lambda t\_2})/\bigl(\lambda(t\_2 - t\_1)\bigr)$ exceeds midpoint survival $e^{-\lambda(t\_1 + t\_2)/2}$. Depositional **time-averaging** across $[t\_1, t\_2]$ also mixes remains of different ages inside one layer, blurring sharp pulses before differential decay acts.

The exact same gradient distorts historical series. Fix present calendar date $T\_0$ and suppose true historical production rate at past date $t \le T\_0$ is $R(t) > 0$ (distinct from registration indicator $R$ in $\rho\_{R,Y}$), filtered across elapsed age $T\_0 - t \ge 0$ by constant survival $e^{-\lambda(T\_0 - t)}$. Observed surviving records $O(t) = R(t)e^{-\lambda(T\_0 - t)}$ then satisfy:

$$\frac{d}{dt}\ln O(t) = \frac{d}{dt}\ln R(t) + \lambda.$$

Read aloud, the apparent growth rate of surviving documents equals true historical growth plus the decay rate $\lambda$. Even if historical output never changed ($\frac{d}{dt}\ln R(t) = 0$), attrition manufactures an illusion of exponential growth $+\lambda > 0$ toward the present unless decay is removed.

Windows also truncate both ends of a timeline. Estimating unobserved pre-discovery origination and post-discovery survival bounds belongs to [Chapter 65, *Layer 15: Stratigraphy, Paleobiology & Deep-Time Order*](65-layer-stratigraphy-paleobiology-deep-time-order.md), where horizon counts fix range extensions as confidence intervals rather than dated events.

### Ancient DNA decay across thermal and site windows

Ancient DNA in subfossil bone measures this exponential decay directly. Across 158 radiocarbon-dated moa left tibiotarsi (602–7839 years BP) from three New Zealand sites within 5 km at 13.1°C (286.25 K), decay of a 242 bp mitochondrial DNA fragment follows an exponential curve ($R^2 = 0.386, p < 0.001$) (Allentoft et al. 2012). That exponential model outperforms linear (0.002) and inverse (0.002) alternatives. Its fit yields a 521-year half-life and a per-bond cleavage rate $k = 5.50 \times 10^{-6}\text{ yr}^{-1}$ (governing fragment-level decay hazard $\lambda$), 384 times slower than *in vitro* depurination at pH 5 (Allentoft et al. 2012).

Illumina `HiSeq` sequencing of two *Dinornis robustus* extracts confirmed differential decay between compartments (Allentoft et al. 2012). Mitochondrial DNA decayed 2 and 2.5 times slower than nuclear DNA's mapped shotgun rates of $2.13 \times 10^{-5}$ and $2.71 \times 10^{-5}\text{ yr}^{-1}$, realizing $S\_1(T)/S\_2(T) = (\alpha\_1/\alpha\_2)e^{(\lambda\_2 - \lambda\_1)T}$. Extrapolating to -5°C ($k = 1.5 \times 10^{-7}\text{ yr}^{-1}$) extends the 30 bp half-life to 158,000 years and drops mean fragment length to 1 bp at 6,830,000 years (Allentoft et al. 2012).

Site microclimates shift baseline admission $\alpha\_i$ and window geometry even when decay slopes match. Inside that 5 km New Zealand cluster, covariance analysis found no significant slope gap across deposits, justifying a pooled $k$ (Allentoft et al. 2012). Yet baselines diverged sharply: Pyramid Valley retains 19.6% and Rosslea 0.3% (roughly 330 times fewer, alongside older deposit ages) of Bell Hill Vineyard's template copy counts. Across the 1882-year overlap between Pyramid Valley and Bell Hill Vineyard (915–2797 years BP), the baseline log-copy quantitative PCR cycle-threshold ($C\_T$) gap holds ($t = 3.8, \mathrm{d.f.} = 72, p < 0.001$, unequal variances) (Allentoft et al. 2012).

After log-transformation, age explains 74.9% of copy-number variance at Rosslea ($n = 8, p = 0.005$) versus only 5.8% at shorter-window Bell Hill Vineyard ($n = 47, p = 0.10$), reflecting window width rather than distinct decay rates (Allentoft et al. 2012). Regional pools averaging across deposits therefore confound baseline offsets with observation windows—a spread of regimes that never shrinks as more sites are added.

## 3. Liability: survival read as representativeness

Guard site `T7Liability` holds researchers accountable when they present a surviving corpus share as if it were the true historical share of the past. A corpus share describes the archive, a population share describes the lost world, and crossing between them requires an explicit filter argument.

Let $n\_1$ count class one inside a surviving sample of size $n > 0$, with $n\_c$ counting each class $c$. The corpus share $p\_{\mathrm{corpus}, 1} = n\_1/n$ measures the archive directly, whereas the living share $p\_{\mathrm{assemblage}, 1} = P(\text{class } 1)$ lacks an observed denominator. Writing $P(S \mid \text{class } c)$ for survival and recovery across §1's nested chain $S \subseteq B \subseteq D \subseteq A$ (distinct from triad coordinates $S\_a$), recovering population shares via Bayes' rule and the normalized Hájek estimator $\hat{p}\_{\mathrm{assemblage}, 1}$ requires strictly positive survival probability $P(S \mid \text{class } c) > 0$ for **every** living class $c$:

$$\hat{p}\_{\mathrm{assemblage}, 1} = \frac{n\_1 / P(S \mid \text{class } 1)}{\sum\_{c} n\_c / P(S \mid \text{class } c)}.$$

In plain words, each surviving class count is scaled up by the inverse of its survival probability before dividing by the reconstructed total population.

Two mathematical constraints govern that reweighting. First, inverse-survival weights $w\_c = 1/P(S \mid \text{class } c) \ge 1$ inflate sampling variance by Kish's weighting design effect $\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{Kish}} = 1 + \mathrm{CV}^2(w)$ (Kish 1965; [Chapter 45, *From World to Archive: Evidential Weight, Big Data Paradox, and the Uncounted*](45-world-to-archive-big-data-paradox.md)). That penalty shrinks effective sample size well below nominal count $n$. Second, if any class $c\_0$ vanishes completely ($P(S \mid \text{class } c\_0) = 0$) with true share $p\_0 \in (0, 1)$ ($1 - p\_0 > 0$), then in the large-sample limit whenever $p\_{\mathrm{assemblage}, 1} > 0$ and all surviving classes hold $P(S \mid \text{class } c) > 0$, normalizing over survivors recovers only $p\_{\mathrm{assemblage}, 1}/(1 - p\_0) > p\_{\mathrm{assemblage}, 1}$. Remaining shares thus inflate upward even when $P(S \mid \text{class } 1)$ is exact.

### Unseen works and binding scrap in Middle Dutch literature

Capture–recapture on medieval texts shows both the reach and limits of survival bounds. Across Middle Dutch chivalric romances, 75 surviving works span 167 witnesses ($f\_1 = 45$ singletons, $f\_2 = 13$ doubletons; Kestemont & Karsdorp 2020; [Chapter 45](45-world-to-archive-big-data-paradox.md)), giving `Chao1` no fewer than 152.42 original works (bootstrap CI 110.11–222.98; roughly 49% work survival; Chao 1984) and `Minsample` 2047.77 minimum original manuscript documents (bootstrap CI 1064.19–4006.42; roughly 8.15% survival; Kestemont & Karsdorp 2020). Binding maculature fragments (141 documents across 181 scraps, $f\_1 = 118, f\_2 = 14$) yield `Chao1 = 635.54` (bootstrap CI 449.85–947.25), indicating roughly 31% of projected documents recycled into bindings (Kestemont & Karsdorp 2020).

Non-random custody still breaks uniform attrition assumptions (Kestemont & Karsdorp 2020, p. 52). Composite *convolutes* share correlated survival, inducing cluster inflation $\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{cluster}} > 1$ akin to *codices descripti* in [Chapter 72](72-layer-philological-epigraphic-textual-order.md) and selection bounds in [Chapter 45](45-world-to-archive-big-data-paradox.md). Preferential sparing of illuminated codices and size-driven recycling also leave `Chao1` and `Minsample` blind to $\pi\_i = 0$ classes without a filter model ($\rho\_{R,Y} \ne 0$, Meng 2018).

### Duties on the page and archive boundaries

Three editorial duties belong on the printed page: state the deposit age and window span, state which classes chemistry or custody favored, and keep corpus shares labelled as corpus shares unless a verified filter supports reweighting. Signing a population share under `T7Liability` requires an offline pre-registered inclusion-rule seal deposited in bitemporal metadata so later readers can audit frames without stalling live adjudication ([Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md)).

Two boundaries waive reweighting: purely archival inventories hold preserved share equal to one without sliding into historical prevalence, and verified uniform survival ($\rho\_{R,Y} = 0$, $P(S \mid \text{class } c) > 0$) aligns corpus and population shares.

## 4. One circuit, one guard per axis

Taphonomy, stratigraphy, and source criticism examine separately what `{T3Archive, T5Temporal, T7Liability}` integrates as a `(1, 1, 1)` keystone of Stratum V:

- **`T3Archive` ($S\_{\mathrm{epi}}$):** Guards admission against $\rho\_{R,Y} \ne 0$, $\mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{cluster}} > 1$, and zero-survival dissolution ($P(S \mid c\_0) = 0$).
- **`T5Temporal` ($S\_{\mathrm{vital}}$):** Requires physical artifact survival $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}}$ before DNA depurination or paper acidification to exceed operational salvage and backup latency $\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$ ([Chapter 47](47-irreversible-time-vital-margin.md) §6.3.2).
- **`T7Liability` ($S\_{\mathrm{gov}}$):** Bars promoting durable or elite remains as general history across external reception perimeters ($\Theta\_{\mathrm{basin}}$; [Chapter 41, *The Triad and the Minimum*](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md)).

In $\mathcal{G}\_1$, `L23` inherits deep-time bounds from [Layer 15](65-layer-stratigraphy-paleobiology-deep-time-order.md) and adds `T7Liability` locally before feeding [Layer 29](79-layer-official-statistics-forensic-accounting-order.md) and [Layer 31](81-layer-criminal-proof-forensic-epistemology-order.md), while `T2Severity` stays open across its ancestor lineage ([Appendix B](appendix-b-architecture-verification.md)), leaving severe causal hypothesis testing among rival decay or selection explanations deferred until `L23` enters `L31` (or pairs laterally across $\mathcal{G}\_2 = J(7,3)$ with `L29` via its `L08`/`L09` ancestors).
