## 1. The harm is framed before the archive is sought

A commission does not go looking for the past. It goes looking for a case, and the case is a decision taken
before any file is opened. Fricker's account of epistemic injustice gives the decision its name: hermeneutical
injustice is what happens when the collective interpretive resources lack the category, so an experience is not
merely discounted by listeners but unavailable as a reportable wrong even to the person who underwent it (Fricker
2007). The lack is structural because the resources are produced collectively, and the parties with the least
power over their production are exactly the parties whose experience needs the missing category.

Trouillot's first moment is where that lack becomes a deposit. He located the production of silence at four
moments — the creation of facts, their assembly into an archive, their retrieval into narrative, and the
retrospective assignment of significance (Trouillot 1995) — and the first of the four is not a filing decision.
It is the moment at which something is constituted as an event of a kind. What is not of a kind is not
under-recorded. It is not a candidate.

The arithmetic is small and it is the whole point. Let $\mathcal{C}$ be the set of case types a mandate
recognises, and let $\mathbf{1}[\cdot]$ indicate membership. The probability that an event of type $e$ enters the
record at all is bounded by the indicator:

$$P(e \in \text{record}) \le \mathbf{1}[e \in \mathcal{C}],$$

so an event outside the mandate's category set has probability zero, and no later stage of collection multiplies
it. The bound is not a defect of the investigators, who are usually diligent, and more testimony does not repair
it, because testimony arrives already shaped by the same resources that lacked the category.

The choice is visible in what a mandate asks for. A mandate that frames the harm as discrete criminal acts by
identifiable perpetrators sends investigators to incident logs, chain-of-custody records, witness statements
about dated events. A mandate that frames the harm as structural deprivation sends them to administrative
routine: budget lines, classification schemes, the standing orders that made a population legible and its losses
lawful. The two mandates do not sample the same population differently. They sample different populations, and
each will report a complete and internally consistent archive of the wrong it went looking for.

Framing is therefore not a preface to the analysis. It is the part of the analysis that fixes the support, and
whoever writes the terms of reference decides it. That is why the framing site is an epistemic site and, at the
same time, always an institutional one.

## 2. The archive of the wrong

The second site is the deposit, and the deposit in this order is not geological. Stoler's reading of colonial
archives supplies the grain: the categories of the bureaucracy decide what is legible, so the shape of the record
is the administrator's question made durable, and the file exists because someone needed to be able to say
something (Stoler 2009). An archive built that way does something the previous order's filter does not. It does
not merely fail to register. It registers in order not to.

Consider what a security apparatus produces in the ordinary course. A form that certifies a lawful reason for a
deprivation. A ledger that records the requisition of a vehicle and not the persons the vehicle carried. A
category label that closes a file without asserting a death. Each of these is a record, each is authentic, each
is admissible, and each is a device for producing the appearance of accounting while withholding the accounted
quantity. The archive of the wrong is therefore not the absence of an archive. It is a well-preserved archive of
the wrong variable.

The consequence for the bias is a sign, and the sign is what matters. Where the state's exposure rose with the
harm, registration falls where the harm is greatest, so the correlation between being recorded and the outcome
of interest is negative over the range a tribunal cares about:

$$\rho\_{R,Y} < 0,$$

taken over the range where exposure was greatest, and the correction the earlier chapter derived for the
opposite sign now moves the estimate the wrong way. A body count assembled from official registries is biased
downward not by carelessness but by construction, because the registry was the instrument by which the harm was
made administratively invisible. The earlier chapter established a bias term enlarged by $\sqrt{(1-f)/f}$; here
the bias has a direction, and not only a size, that is a property of the deposit's purpose.

Two archives therefore coexist, and they are not two samples of one thing. The archive of the crime is
clandestine, thin, oral, preserved by the risk its keepers took. The archive of the alibi is official, abundant,
well-housed, complete, because it was produced in order to survive and to be cited. Their survival probabilities
differ for a reason that is not chemical: durability here is a function of institutional interest, which is a
selection on precisely the variable under study. A historian who weighs the two by volume will find the alibi
heavier, and the finding will be an artefact of who was preserving what for whom.

The practical rule is a refusal of a proxy. The density of a fond is not evidence of the density of the event. A
thick file can be a thick silence, and the test of a file is not its weight but the question it was built to
answer.

## 3. The question transitional justice puts

The third site is liability, and its content is a substitution of the question. A criminal trial asks whether
this person did this act, to a standard of proof, with a defence, before a court that can acquit. Transitional
justice asks something else: whether the record is the record. Is the register complete, is the file authentic,
is the testimony admissible, was the silence produced — and if it was produced, by whom, and what does the
institution owe for having produced it.

Fricker's testimonial injustice names the mechanism by which a valid testimony is voided without being
contradicted. The discount is applied to the speaker rather than to the evidence, so a witness whose credibility
is marked down is not refuted. She is not heard, and the record closes without her (Fricker 2007). The remedy is
procedural, and it is not kindness. A procedure in which credibility does the work of corroboration will
reproduce the same distribution of silence the violence produced, and will do so while keeping every rule. The
site requires that the credibility heuristic be prevented from standing in for the corroboration it cannot
supply. That is a design requirement on the institution, not a virtue to be hoped for in the judge.

The second substitution follows from what a remedy can attach to. A reparation is paid to a person, entered
against a name, discharged when that name is satisfied; a residual cannot receive a payment. So the legal
quantity is the named list, and the corrected total is a planning quantity. A commission that pays against the
total pays nobody in particular; a commission that plans against the list under-provisions by exactly the dark
figure. The two are not rival estimates of one thing. They answer different questions with different instruments,
and the defect this site names is the confusion of them: a programme sized on the corrected total over-commits
when registries repel each other and grants impunity when registries copy each other, because the estimator's
error changes sign with the practice of the collectors and not with the truth.

The liability, then, is not individual guilt, and it is not collective guilt either. It is answerability for the
record's validity: the institution that holds the file answers for what the file can and cannot show. That is a
governance site in the strict sense, a place where a seal is set, where a claim about the record is signed by a
body that can be asked to account for it.

## 4. The circuit, and the margin it does not carry

The three sites form one circuit, and the signature says what the circuit lacks.

The frame decides which archive is sought, and the sought archive is the one that gets built. The deposit
decides what the built archive can show, and its purpose sets the sign of the bias. The question of validity
decides what may be concluded and who answers for the conclusion. Repair at one site is undone at another. A
perfectly framed mandate over an archive of the alibi produces a scrupulous account of the wrong variable. An
immaculate file, authenticated and complete, answers a question about administrative regularity rather than
about harm. And a named list that is honest about its own incompleteness still cannot be used to plan a remedy,
because the planning quantity is the one it deliberately does not report.

What the signature `(2, 0, 1)` prints is the absence. Two sites are epistemic, one is a site of governance, and
the vital margin is empty: nothing in this circuit carries a clock, and nothing carries a stop-work trigger. So
the order has a characteristic failure, and it is the second collapse rather than the first or the third. A
commission can be exact about the width of its own uncertainty, can publish the dark figure with its bound, can
distinguish the list from the total — and still complete its work after the population that could have received
the remedy has died. Truth-seeking outlives survivor survival. The knowledge is lucid and the margin is absent,
which is precisely Impotent Lucidity Before Ruin: the width is known, the ruin arrives anyway, because nothing in
the circuit was ever going to stop the process on account of the clock.

The absence is visible in the apparatus as a missing site, not a missing argument. The temporal guard is not in
the triple. Not because the commission forgets time, but because the combination has no place to put it: time
enters as a condition on the claim rather than as a guard on the process. The condition is severe. The thesis
that the archive is a censored sample, and that the dark figure can be recovered, presupposes a closed universe,
a population that holds still while it is being estimated. Where the violence is ongoing the estimand is a rate,
and any figure published at a date describes a set that grew after the date. The register then holds the people
who were reachable before the cut-off, which is a property of the search and not of the population.

What a circuit without the vital margin can still do is print the condition. A register that publishes its
inclusion rule lets a later reader recompute the frame. A count labelled with its cut-off stops pretending the
universe has closed. An exposure denominator — cases per person-time at risk — carries the shrinkage a bare total
hides. Each of these is a repair to the claim, and none is a stop-work trigger. That is the honest statement of
what this order achieves: it makes the record answerable, and it leaves the clock outside.

## By the end of this chapter you can

1. Show how the framing of a harm fixes the support of its archive, and why an event outside the mandate's category set has retrieval probability zero rather than a low one.
2. Distinguish an archive of the alibi from an archive of the crime, and say what the sign of the missingness correlation does to a correction that assumed the other sign.
3. State why the legal quantity and the planning quantity of a transitional-justice record are different objects, and name what each is destroyed by when they are confused.

## Bridge

Layer 24 hands forward a record whose making has been made answerable: a frame that bounds which archive can exist, a deposit whose purpose sets the sign of what it omits, and a validity question that the institution must answer for by name. What it does not hand forward is any account of what happens once the record is complete and someone must act on it before the next fact arrives, and that is where [Chapter 75, *Media Ecology, Information Retrieval & Performative Order*](75-layer-media-ecology-information-retrieval-order.md) begins.
