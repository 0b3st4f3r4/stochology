## By the end of this chapter you can

1. Say what an index decides before it measures — unit, term and match — and show why recall is a ceiling set by coverage rather than a property of the ranking.
2. State an archive's bias term as a ratio over the registration share, and say which of the composed selection factors a given corpus is silent about.

## 1. The frame is decided before the first measurement

Picture a journalist or doctor typing a query into a search engine for rural health warnings, assuming the top ten results mirror everything published. Before any relevance score runs, however, the search system has already decided what counts as a document, how sentences are split into tokens, and which languages or file types even enter its index.

Guard site `T1Framing` exposes these upfront indexing choices that set a hard ceiling on recall before ranking begins. A medium is never a neutral pipe: Innis observed that a civilization's dominant medium favors certain social relations and taxes others before any message is written (Innis 1951). A retrieval index works the same way, fixing what counts as a document, a term, and a match inside a support that later rerankers can only reorder.

### The coverage ceiling and sparse vs. dense retrievers

Let $I$ denote admission into the index and $Rk$ survival of the ranking under index-gated retrieval ($\text{retrieved} \subseteq I$). Whenever $P(\text{relevant}) > 0$, inclusion gives $P(\text{retrieved} \mid \text{relevant}) \le P(I \mid \text{relevant})$ unconditionally. Assuming $P(I, \text{relevant}) > 0$, conditioning splits recall into two factors:

$$P(\text{retrieved} \mid \text{relevant}) = P(I \mid \text{relevant}) \cdot P(Rk \mid I, \text{relevant}).$$

In plain words, total recall multiplies the chance a relevant record enters the index by the chance an indexed record wins the ranking. If a schema omits a record class so $P(I \mid \text{relevant}) = 0$, recall stays zero no matter how clever the ranker becomes.

Sparse and dense retrievers split that frame differently ahead of chunking. Sparse `BM25` assigns score $S\_{\mathrm{BM25}}(q, d)$ to query $q$ against document $d$ of length $|d| > 0$ given corpus mean length $\mathrm{avgdl} > 0$. The formula combines inverse document frequency $\mathrm{IDF}(t)$, term count $f(t, d) \ge 0$, saturation parameter $k\_1 > 0$, and length normalization $b \in [0, 1]$ (Robertson & Zaragoza 2009):

$$S\_{\mathrm{BM25}}(q,d)=\sum\_{t\in q}\mathrm{IDF}(t)\mkern3mu\frac{f(t,d)\mkern3mu(k\_1+1)}{f(t,d)+k\_1\bigl(1-b+b\mkern2mu|d|/\mathrm{avgdl}\bigr)}.$$

Read aloud, `BM25` sums weighted term matches across query words while damping repeated terms and adjusting for document length. Exact zero counts ($f(t, d) = 0$) trigger **lexical mismatch** (`T1Framing`) on paraphrases alongside corpus `IDF` skew (`T3Archive`).

Dense passage retrieval (`DPR`) instead trains $768$-dimensional `BERT` bi-encoders ($\mathrm{sim}(q, p) = E\_Q(q)^\top E\_P(p)$) over $21{,}015{,}324$ disjoint $100$-word Wikipedia passages (Karpukhin et al. 2020). It beats `BM25` by $9\\%$–$19\\%$ top-$20$ accuracy on open QA ($78.4\\%$ Single vs. $59.1\\%$ on Natural Questions), yet loses on high-overlap `SQuAD` v1.1 ($63.2\\%$ Single vs. $68.8\\%$) and drops rare names (`"Thoros of Myr"`) via **embedding-space collapse** (`T1`/`T3`, training bias $\rho\_{R,Y} \neq 0$; Karpukhin et al. 2020). Hybrid sparse–dense retrievers and rerankers raise $P(Rk \mid I, \mathrm{relevant})$ over already indexed items without overcoming $P(I \mid \text{relevant}) = 0$.

Chunking, links, and tokenization embed that cut downstream. Splitting documents drops cross-boundary context, while decoupling weights from a vector store allows `RAG` hot-swaps yet risks retrieval collapse when generators ignore fetched context (Lewis et al. 2020). Across $\ge 24$ million pages and $518$ million links, users inspect only top results, concentrating visibility on PageRank's stationary eigenvector at damping $d = 0.85$ with uniform $(1 - d)$ teleportation inside an ad-oriented black art (Brin & Page 1998). Subword tokenizers add disparities up to $15\times$ across parallel languages—and $>4\times$ at character or byte level—giving identical testimony unequal truncation and index reach $P(I)$ (Petrov et al. 2023). A pre-framed question and a pre-chunked document are opposite halves of one selection ([Chapter 32](32-the-question-before-the-data.md)).

## 2. What the record retains, and who is outside it

Guard site `T3Archive` audits how deposit omissions and index filters multiply together—and flags hyperlink shortcuts that surface pages never actually checked. Trouillot (1995) locates archival silence across fact creation, assembly, retrieval (§1), and institutional citation, while Stoler (2009) tracks administrative categories hardening into record geometry.

The bias is one exact quantity, not a vague worry. Write $R = 1$ for archival registration, $Y$ for a target outcome with finite spread $\sigma\_Y > 0$, and $f = P(R = 1) \in (0, 1)$ for interior coverage share. Meng's (2018) identity ([Chapter 45](45-world-to-archive-big-data-paradox.md)) expresses selection displacement as $\mathbb{E}[Y \mid R = 1] - \mathbb{E}[Y] = \rho\_{R,Y}\sigma\_Y\sqrt{(1 - f)/f}$ (at census $f = 1$, bias vanishes and $\rho\_{R,Y}$ is undefined). Post-hoc reweighting inside the archive cannot recover either factor: tiny $f$ magnifies bias without bound, and $\rho\_{R,Y}$ captures collection tilt rather than analyst effort.

Assume strict nesting $\text{retrieved} \subseteq \text{indexed} \subseteq \text{deposited}$ with positive probabilities $P(\text{deposited}) > 0$ and $P(\text{indexed}, \text{deposited}) > 0$. Retrieval then compounds a second selection layer onto initial deposit:

$$P(\text{retrieved}) = P(\text{deposited}) \cdot P(\text{indexed} \mid \text{deposited}) \cdot P(\text{retrieved} \mid \text{indexed}).$$

In plain words, overall retrieval probability multiplies survival at deposit, indexing, and ranking; a zero at any stage absorbs the product, so faithful ranking cannot fix an incomplete deposit.

Yet anchor propagation breaks that nesting when pointers bypass custody. Indexing over 259 million anchor texts across 24 million crawled pages lets a search engine return uncrawled pages never checked for validity—and even pages that never existed at all solely because external links pointed to them (Brin & Page 1998). Here $\text{indexed}$ escapes $\text{deposited}$, tempting readers to mistake retrieved coherence for world prevalence—repeating the taphonomic fallacy of [Chapter 73](73-layer-taphonomic-historiographical-order.md).

## 3. The ranking writes the corpus it ranks

Guard site `T6Performative` tracks the feedback loop in which published rankings and AI retraining reshape future documents, shrinking variance until synthetic text feeds on itself. Searches do not observe a stationary world: Espeland and Sauder named this reactivity (Espeland & Sauder 2007), and Perdomo et al. (2020) formalized it by distinguishing the deployment-stable fixed point $\theta\_{\mathrm{PS}}$ from the performative optimum $\theta\_{\mathrm{PO}}$. Whereas [Chapter 37](37-model-fit-and-error.md) tracks leakage inside a static fit, this layer tracks how the loop rewrites the archive itself.

### Truncated slices and performative instability

Take a score $s \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^{2})$ ($\sigma > 0$) and fix a deterministic cutoff $\tau = \mu + \sigma\alpha$, keeping population fraction $1 - \Phi(\alpha) \in (0, 1)$. Write standard normal density $\varphi(\alpha)$, inverse Mills ratio $\lambda(\alpha) = \varphi(\alpha)/\bigl(1 - \Phi(\alpha)\bigr) > 0$, and variance factor $\delta(\alpha) = \lambda(\alpha)\bigl(\lambda(\alpha) - \alpha\bigr) \in (0, 1)$. Then the truncated mean $\mathbb{E}[s \mid s \ge \tau]$ and truncated variance $\mathrm{Var}(s \mid s \ge \tau)$ satisfy:

$$\mathbb{E}[s \mid s \ge \tau] = \mu + \sigma\lambda(\alpha), \qquad \mathrm{Var}(s \mid s \ge \tau) = \sigma^{2}\bigl(1 - \delta(\alpha)\bigr).$$

Read aloud, selecting only above cutoff $\tau$ shifts the surviving average upward by $\sigma\lambda(\alpha)$ while squeezing variance down by factor $1 - \delta(\alpha)$. Reapplying one fixed $\tau$ is idempotent after step one, yet ratcheting $\tau$ upward or gaming features concentrates future deposits ($R\_{t+1}$) around the score and amplifies $\rho\_{R,Y}$ (`T6` rewriting `T3`).

With distribution sensitivity $\varepsilon$, loss strong convexity $\gamma > 0$, and joint gradient Lipschitz bound $\beta\_{\mathrm{joint}} > 0$, breaching Perdomo et al.'s (2020) sufficient contraction bound $|\varepsilon| < \gamma/\beta\_{\mathrm{joint}}$ forfeits its uniform guarantee. Actual oscillation occurs once the exact RRM contraction modulus reaches unity ($\kappa\_{\mathrm{RRM}} \ge 1$) or no stable fixed point $\theta\_{\mathrm{PS}}$ exists ([Chapter 48](48-reactive-worlds-performativity-goodhart.md)). Microsoft Tay—shut down after one day and over 93,000 tweets—showed this empirically as live retraining on public replies enacted symbiotic agency capture outside the platform's goal (Neff & Nagy 2016).

### Recursive synthetic collapse and accumulating vintages

Recursive generation extends slice shrinkage across an entire media ecosystem. Training models repeatedly on synthetic outputs without fresh human records triggers **model collapse**, erasing distribution tails across language models, variational autoencoders, and Gaussian mixtures (Shumailov et al. 2024).

Alemohammad et al. (2023) generalize that decay as **Model Autophagy Disorder** (`MAD`) across fully synthetic, fixed-real, and fresh-data loops. Under Gaussian resampling with bias parameter $\lambda\_{\mathrm{bias}} \in (0, 1]$, expected covariance shrinks as $\mathbb{E}[\Sigma\_t \mid \Sigma\_{t-1}] = \lambda\_{\mathrm{bias}}\Sigma\_{t-1}$ and $\Sigma\_t \to 0$ almost surely whenever each step draws a **finite** synthetic sample ($m \ge 2$). Even unbiased finite loops ($\lambda\_{\mathrm{bias}} = 1$, where Wishart sample covariance shrinks almost surely unlike $m \to \infty$) erode precision and recall (Alemohammad et al. 2023). Quality filtering ($\lambda\_{\mathrm{bias}} < 1$) speeds diversity loss, fixed real corpora only postpone decay, and fresh-data loops need enough new real draws.

Accumulating provenance-sealed vintages halts that collapse. Consider linear regression ($T \ge d+2$, noise $\sigma^2 > 0$, dimension $d \ge 1$) with isotropic Gaussian covariates ($\Sigma = I\_d$) over $n$ training rounds indexed by $i = 1, \dots, n$. Full replacement grows test error linearly ($\frac{\sigma^2 d}{T-d-1}n$) and $iT$ synthetic replacement diverges logarithmically ($\frac{\sigma^2 d}{T-d-1}\sum\_{k=1}^n k^{-1}$). By contrast, accumulating clean human and synthetic vintages weights round-$i$ noise by $1/i$, bounding accumulated test error $E\_{\mathrm{test}}^{\mathrm{Accum}}(\hat{w}\_n)$:

$$E\_{\mathrm{test}}^{\mathrm{Accum}}(\hat{w}\_n)=\frac{\sigma^2 d}{T-d-1}\sum\_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{i^2}\le\frac{\sigma^2 d}{T-d-1}\frac{\pi^2}{6}.$$

In plain words, keeping earlier human vintages alongside new data forces iteration error to fall like $1/i^2$, capping total test error within $\pi^2/6$ of the single-round baseline.

On `470M`-token TinyStories (temperature $1.0$), replacement pushed `GPT-2 (9M)` loss from $1.82$ ($t=1$) to $2.39$ ($t=4$) and $2.91$ ($t=10$), versus $1.74$ under accumulation (`Llama-2 (126M)`: $1.71$ at $t=1$ to $2.23$ replace vs. $1.59$ accumulate at $t=4$), though `200k` CelebA VAEs still shed minor details slowly (Gerstgrasser et al. 2024). Retaining **uncontaminated**, provenance-sealed human vintages ($t\_{\mathrm{ev}} \le t\_{\mathrm{disp}}$) under stationary targets—beside generative provenance from [Layer 19](69-layer-metascience-scientometrics-replication-order.md) (prompts, seeds, code, discarded branches)—anchors `T3Archive`, refuted if synthetic replacement preserved tails without fresh human records.

Two boundaries delimit reactivity. Where objects cannot observe rankings (fossils, sealed historical archives), the feedback loop drops and standard inference holds; performative distortion belongs to instruments whose outputs re-enter their inputs.

## 4. The three sites are one circuit

`{T1Framing, T3Archive, T6Performative}` forms one indivisible circuit: framing (`T1`) sets index support, deposit (`T3`) sets $\rho\_{R,Y}$, and ranking (`T6`) rewrites future support and amplifies $\rho\_{R,Y}$. Single-site fixes fail: a sharper ranker still pulls zero from outside the index, clean debiasing misreads shifted baselines, and intact custody stays invisible across mismatched chunking or tokenization.

Signature `(2, 0, 1)` leaves $S\_{\mathrm{vital}}$ locally unguarded ($k\_2 = 0$), requiring inherited `T4Formalization` and `T5Temporal` from [Layer 03](53-layer-macro-coupled-topology-order.md), `T2Severity` from [Layer 19](69-layer-metascience-scientometrics-replication-order.md), and upstream `T7Liability` ([Appendix B](appendix-b-architecture-verification.md)). Within that exposure ([Chapter 41, *The Triad and the Minimum*](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md)):

1. **Collapse Proposition 1 (*Blind Undersizing*, $\neg S\_{\mathrm{epi}}$):** Ablating `T1Framing` or `T3Archive` ($P(I)=0$, BM25 mismatch, DPR collapse, unsealed overwrites, $\rho\_{R,Y} \ne 0$, tokenizer skew) undersizes reserves to a biased corpus—*failing if* unindexed strata hold $\rho\_{R,Y} = 0$ or provenanced seals span queried populations.
2. **Collapse Proposition 2 (*Impotent Lucidity Before Ruin*, $\neg S\_{\mathrm{vital}}$):** Inside `(2, 0, 1)` without stop-work clocks, $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} < \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$ worsens as synthetic churn saturates human review ([Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md))—*failing if* queries remain reversible or `T5`/`T7` brakes halt rollout in time.
3. **Collapse Proposition 3 (*Cynical Capture and Pillaging*, $\neg S\_{\mathrm{gov}}$):** Omitting `T7Liability` beside `T6Performative` lets proxy gaming (such as Tay's symbiotic capture; Neff & Nagy 2016) offload harm onto excluded communities across external reception perimeters ($\Theta\_{\mathrm{basin}}$)—*failing if* binding liability and status-blind vetoes halt capture.

**Dual-use containment:** Because visibility gates (`T1`/`T3`) are dual-use, inherited `T7Liability` ([Chapter 40, *Institution, Incentive, and Metric*](40-institution-incentive-metric.md)) bars covert algorithmic censorship or automated disinformation, confining action to auditable custody and attested `CounterInductiveReopen`, while retrieval scores and diagnostics remain strictly *ex post*.
