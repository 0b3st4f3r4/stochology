## 1. Stopping rules, bandit bounds and option value

Picture a utility board deciding whether to build a power plant today or wait a season for fuel prices to settle, a hiring panel interviewing candidates one by one, or a medical team testing competing treatment arms. Each choice trades the risk of stopping too early against the danger of waiting until a deadline passes or the reserve drains dry. Sequential decision theory turns that judgment into explicit stopping rules, exploration budgets, and option hurdles.

### Guard T1 (Framing): Defining the stopping rule and baseline before the clock runs

In sequential decisions, `T1Framing` ($T\_1$) protects pre-committed stopping rules against opportunistic cutoffs invented after early returns appear.

On a filtered probability space, suppose an actor observes adapted rewards $Z\_t \in L^1$ across steps $0 \le t \le T$ (Snell 1952). For unbounded horizons, well-defined expected future value requires Hypothesis A ($\sup\_t Z\_t^+ \in L^1$). Working backward from $U\_T = Z\_T$, Snell's envelope sets $U\_t = \max\bigl(Z\_t, \mathbb{E}[U\_{t+1} \mid \mathcal{F}\_t]\bigr)$ at each step. The earliest optimal stopping time $\tau^\ast = \inf\lbrace{}t \in \lbrace{}0, \dots, T\rbrace{} : Z\_t = U\_t\rbrace{}$ halts once current reward matches holding out (finite for $T < \infty$; unbounded horizons require Hypothesis A and asymptotic attainment).

Search across ranked arrivals gives the cleanest exact calculation of a stopping threshold. Suppose $n \ge 2$ candidates arrive in random order. The decision-maker rejects the first $r - 1$ candidates and picks the first later arrival beating every predecessor (Lindley 1961; Ferguson 1989). This rule wins when two events align: the best overall candidate arrives at position $k \ge r$ (chance $1/n$). Meanwhile, the strongest of the first $k - 1$ must sit inside the rejected opening sample of size $r - 1$ (chance $(r-1)/(k-1)$). Summing across positions $r$ through $n$ yields winning probability $P(r)$ (with $P(1) = 1/n$):

$$P(r) = \frac{r-1}{n}\sum\_{k=r}^{n}\frac{1}{k-1}, \qquad r \ge 2.$$

Read aloud, $P(r)$ multiplies the skipped fraction by the sum of reciprocal ranks from $r$ onward. That product captures the chance that the true leader arrives after the baseline while the strongest runner-up landed inside it.

Successive differences for $2 \le r < n$, namely $P(r+1) - P(r) = \frac{1}{n}\bigl(\sum\_{j=r}^{n-1} j^{-1} - 1\bigr)$ with $P(2) - P(1) = \frac{1}{n}\sum\_{j=2}^{n-1} j^{-1} \ge 0$, fall strictly in $r$. They peak at the smallest cutoff $r^\ast \ge 2$ where $\sum\_{j=r}^{n-1} j^{-1} \le 1$. Re-indexing $j = k - 1$ and bounding $j^{-1} > \int\_j^{j+1} t^{-1}\mkern3mu dt$ gives $P(r) = \frac{r-1}{n}\sum\_{j=r-1}^{n-1} j^{-1} > -u\ln u$ for $u = (r-1)/n$, strictly above the continuum integral. Setting $x = r/n \in (0, 1)$ as $n \to \infty$, $P(r) \to -x\ln x$ peaks at $x^\ast = 1/e$ with success $1/e$. It collapses at both ends: $x \to 0^+$ leaves no baseline, while $x \to 1^-$ discards the winner during warm-up.

### Guard T2 (Severity): Testing stopping thresholds and bandit regret floors

In sequential trials and bandit allocation, `T2Severity` ($T\_2$) protects error rates and exploration budgets from premature peeking or lost variance.

Wald's (1947) sequential probability ratio test (SPRT) decides between simple hypotheses $H\_0$ and $H\_1$ with densities $f\_0, f\_1 > 0$ across i.i.d. draws. Pick error targets $\alpha\_W, \beta\_W \in (0, 1)$ ($\alpha\_W + \beta\_W < 1$) and cutoffs $0 < B < 1 < A$. The likelihood ratio $\Lambda\_t = \prod\_{s=1}^t f\_1(X\_s)/f\_0(X\_s)$ stops upon crossing $A \approx (1 - \beta\_W)/\alpha\_W$ or $B \approx \beta\_W/(1 - \alpha\_W)$, obeying overshoot bounds $A \le (1-\beta\_W)/\alpha\_W$ and $B \ge \beta\_W/(1-\alpha\_W)$. Expected sample size is minimal under $H\_0$ or $H\_1$ (neglecting boundary overshoot under Wald's 1947 matched-error idealization), swells under intermediate laws, and violates $(\alpha\_W, \beta\_W)$ under short truncation unless recalibrated or replaced by anytime *e*-processes ([Chapter 30](30-probability-as-language.md)). Pre-published error rates test a claim; post-hoc cutoffs merely describe the sample.

Multi-armed bandits extend stopping from one stream to competing options. Starting from Robbins's (1952) two-population allocation maximizing expected reward without a fixed deadline, Gittins (1979) decouples infinite-horizon bandits into single-arm indices $\nu\_i(s\_i)$. That clean decoupling holds strictly under geometric discounting in $(0, 1)$, independent *rested* Markovian arms, zero switching cost, and risk neutrality without ruin.

Lai and Robbins (1985) establish an **asymptotic** lower bound on undiscounted regret over horizon $T \ge 2$ ($\ln T > 0$). Their theorem assumes regular single-parameter families ($P\_i \ll P^\ast$, $D\_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\_i \parallel P^\ast) \in (0, \infty)$) and **uniformly good** policies ($R\_T = o(T^a)\mkern5mu\forall a > 0$). Write $\Delta\_i = \mu^\ast - \mu\_i > 0$ for arm $i$'s mean gap below the best arm. Cumulative regret then obeys $\liminf\_{T \to \infty} R\_T / \ln T \ge \sum\_{i : \Delta\_i > 0} \Delta\_i / D\_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\_i \parallel P^\ast)$. That contrasts with finite-horizon minimax scaling $\Omega(\sqrt{KT})$ when gaps shrink as $\Delta\_i \asymp \sqrt{K/T}$.

For Gaussian arms with common variance $\sigma^2 > 0$, divergence $D\_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\_i \parallel P^\ast) = \Delta\_i^2/(2\sigma^2)$ scales suboptimal pulls as $2(\sigma/\Delta\_i)^2\ln T$ and sets the asymptotic regret constant at $2\sigma^2/\Delta\_i$. Examining both ends shows where that asymptotic limit applies: as $\Delta\_i \to 0^+$, the regret constant $2\sigma^2/\Delta\_i \to +\infty$, exceeding the worst-case cap $\Delta\_i T$ unless horizon length clears $T/\ln T > 2\sigma^2/\Delta\_i^2$ ($\Delta\_i \gg \sigma\sqrt{2(\ln T)/T}$). At large gaps, the dimensionless pull factor $1/D\_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\_i \parallel P^\ast)$ drops below one once $D\_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\_i \parallel P^\ast) > 1$ ($\Delta\_i/\sigma > \sqrt{2}$), shrinking regret constants, whereas non-overlapping support ($P\_i \not\ll P^\ast$) exits regularity altogether.

### Guard T5 (Temporal): Pricing real-option delay without hitting an irreversible deadline

In real-option timing and adaptive sizing, `T5Temporal` ($T\_5$) protects survival headroom so waiting for clarity never stalls past an absorbing barrier.

Dixit and Pindyck (1994) price an irreversible sunk investment $I > 0$ when project value $V\_t = \mathbb{E}\_t[\int\_t^\infty e^{-r(s-t)}\pi\_s\mkern3mu ds] > 0$ evolves continuously. Here $V\_t$ follows $dV\_t = \mu V\_t\mkern3mu dt + \sigma V\_t\mkern3mu dW\_t$ with volatility $\sigma > 0$, drift $\mu$, and discount rate $r > \max(0, \mu)$. Their yield $\delta\_{\mathrm{yield}} = r - \mu > 0$ is a financial convenience yield (distinct from testimonial status discounting $\delta\_{\mathrm{status}}$ in [Chapter 71](71-layer-linguistic-evidential-pragmatic-order.md)). Replacing simple net-present-value ($V\_t \ge I$) with option valuation $F(V\_t)$—enforcing value-matching $F(V^\ast) = V^\ast - I$ and smooth-pasting $F'(V^\ast) = 1$—raises the investment trigger to $V^\ast = \frac{\beta\_1}{\beta\_1 - 1}I$.

Here $\beta\_1 > 1$ is the unique positive root of $Q(\beta) = \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2\beta(\beta - 1) + \mu\beta - r = 0$ (since $Q(0) = -r < 0$ and $Q(1) = -\delta\_{\mathrm{yield}} < 0$). Higher $\sigma$ lowers $\beta\_1$ and widens the option multiplier $\beta\_1/(\beta\_1 - 1)$. As $\sigma \to 0^+$, the hurdle nears $\max(1, r/\delta\_{\mathrm{yield}})$: for $0 < \mu < r$, $\beta\_1 \to r/\mu$ gives hurdle $r/\delta\_{\mathrm{yield}} > 1$, whereas for $\mu \le 0 < r$, $\beta\_1 \to +\infty$ and $\beta\_1/(\beta\_1 - 1) \to 1$. By contrast, for $\mu \ge r$, no root exceeds one ($\beta\_1 \le 1$) and finite option waiting hurdles collapse.

Real-option delay works only while waiting stays safe. For reversible acts with headroom $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} \ge \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$ ([Chapter 47](47-irreversible-time-vital-margin.md) §6.3.2, vital margin), sizing throttles to minimax regret $a^\ast\_{\mathrm{MMR}}$ ([Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md)) or epistemic Kelly shrinkage $f^\ast\_{\mathrm{epi}} = k(n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}})\hat{f}^\ast \in (0, \hat{f}^\ast)$ ([Chapter 47](47-irreversible-time-vital-margin.md) §6.1.2). That Kelly rule uses known-variance plug-in $\hat{f}^\ast = (\hat{\mu} - r)/\sigma^2 > 0$ under $\alpha > 2$ and $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} > 0$ (motivated by Baker & McHale 2013; unknown $\hat{\sigma}^2$ demands stronger scale-invariant shrinkage). Crossing an absorbing barrier before $\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$ destroys the option ([Chapter 43](43-inquiry-openness-multiverse.md)); Analytic Hierarchy Process scores (Saaty 1980; [Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md)) or impact–confidence checklists never offset breached survival floors.

## 2. The archive that is not exchangeable

Every rule in §1 assumes exchangeable arrival order, independent arm draws, or stationary log-increments $\ln(V\_{t+\Delta t}/V\_t)$ (since levels $V\_t$ themselves drift). Under **i.i.d.** continuous draws, the $k$-th observation sets an upper record with exact probability $1/k$, giving an expected record count of $\sum\_{k=1}^{n} 1/k = \ln n + \gamma + o(1)$ (Rényi 1962). Upward drift multiplies records above $\ln n + \gamma$, while downward drift pushes them toward one—making stationary rules confuse trend for candidate quality. Likewise, open-horizon SPRT breaks under short fixed truncation without recalibration or anytime-valid *e*-processes, so an honest archive must disclose the stopping rule actually executed.

A strict upward trend ($x\_1 < x\_2 < \dots < x\_n$) turns that failure extreme. Every arrival sets a new record (count $n$), so the secretary rule halts immediately at $r$ while the true maximum sits at $n$. Success plunges from its $1/e$ benchmark to zero for any $r < n$, leaving $x\_r$ as the worst post-refusal option in $\lbrace{}r, \dots, n\rbrace{}$.

Adaptive bandits (Robbins 1952; Lai & Robbins 1985) similarly destroy archive exchangeability whenever sample counts react to past averages. Let $N\_i \ge 1$ ($\mathbb{E}[N\_i] < \infty$) be the random pull count on arm $i$, $\bar{X}\_{i,N\_i}$ its logged sample mean, and $\mu\_i$ its true mean ($\mathbb{E}[|X\_{i,t}|] < \infty$). Wald's identity $\mathbb{E}[\sum\_{t=1}^{N\_i}(X\_{i,t} - \mu\_i)] = \mathbb{E}[N\_i(\bar{X}\_{i,N\_i} - \mu\_i)] = 0$ then fixes the selection bias:

$$\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}\_{i,N\_i}] - \mu\_i = -\frac{\mathrm{Cov}(N\_i, \bar{X}\_{i,N\_i})}{\mathbb{E}[N\_i]}.$$

Read aloud, the bias in an adaptive arm's recorded average equals minus the covariance between pull count and final sample mean, divided by expected pulls.

Starving early losers while extending winners creates positive covariance ($\mathrm{Cov}(N\_i, \bar{X}\_{i,N\_i}) > 0$) and biases logged arm means **downward**. Stopping early on winning streaks creates negative covariance ($\mathrm{Cov}(N\_i, \bar{X}\_{i,N\_i}) < 0$) and biases logged averages **upward**. Only stopping rules independent of realized arm averages ($\mathrm{Cov} = 0$) leave the logged archive unbiased.

## 3. Where the signature stops seeing

Because Layer `L26` carries `{T1Framing, T2Severity, T5Temporal}` with signature `(2, 1, 0)`, it leaves `T6Performative` and `T7Liability` unguarded locally. As a result, `L26` cannot see when publishing a stopping cutoff or option hurdle shifts the arrival distribution $\mathcal{D}(\theta)$ ([Chapter 48](48-reactive-worlds-performativity-goodhart.md), reactive performativity). To contain that blind spot, analysts must estimate pre- and post-publication arrival laws separately and report both intervals under the conservative bound. Whenever divergence exceeds sampling noise, they must revert to a fixed-sample rule with declared $\alpha$ alongside Manski bounds for non-random selection ($\rho\_{R,Y} \neq 0$; [Chapter 45](45-world-to-archive-big-data-paradox.md)).

Because $k\_3 = 0$, signature `(2, 1, 0)` leaves institutional governance $S\_{\mathrm{gov}}$ locally unguarded, matching its direct parents [Layer 07](57-layer-probability-credence-measurement-order.md) and [Layer 09](59-layer-experimental-sampling-causal-order.md). Upstream closure therefore depends on inherited `T7Liability` ([Layer 01](51-layer-micro-kinetic-sensorimotor-order.md)), `T6Performative` ([Layer 08](58-layer-metrological-information-geometric-order.md)), `T4Formalization`, and `T3Archive` ([Appendix B](appendix-b-architecture-verification.md)). Without that cone closure, three collapses appear ([Chapter 41, *The Triad and the Minimum*](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md)):

1. *Blind Undersizing ($\neg S\_{\mathrm{epi}}$):* Omitting `T1Framing`, `T2Severity`, or `T3Archive` ($\rho\_{R,Y} \neq 0$, adaptive non-exchangeability) stops exploration without a Cromwell floor (Lindley 1985; [Chapter 32](32-the-question-before-the-data.md)) or trusts biased adaptive means—*fails if* structural floors bound tails or selection vanishes.
2. *Impotent Lucidity Before Ruin ($\neg S\_{\mathrm{vital}}$):* Pairing $\mathcal{O}(\ln T)$ regret with over-Kelly leverage or deliberative saturation ([Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md)) forces $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} < \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$—*fails if* reversible probes preserve $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} \ge \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$.
3. *Cynical Capture and Pillaging ($\neg S\_{\mathrm{gov}}$):* Omitting `T6Performative` and `T7Liability` lets deciders pocket an option they do not own and shift ruin onto external reception perimeters ($\Theta\_{\mathrm{basin}}$; [Chapter 40, *Institution, Incentive, and Metric*](40-institution-incentive-metric.md)). That collapse *fails if* liability offsets holding cost, pre-commitments freeze cutoffs, and independent oversight bounds external spillover across reception perimeters.

Option triggers and regret bounds evaluate completed trajectories strictly *ex post* under the non-compensable minimum ([Chapter 41, *The Triad and the Minimum*](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md)), never as live control rules.
