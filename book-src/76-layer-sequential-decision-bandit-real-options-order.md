## 1. Stopping rules, bandit bounds and option value

Picture a utility board deciding whether to build a power plant today or wait a season for fuel prices to settle, a hiring panel interviewing candidates one by one, or a medical team testing competing treatment arms. Each choice trades the risk of stopping too early against the danger of waiting until a deadline passes or the reserve drains dry. Sequential decision theory turns that judgment into explicit stopping rules, exploration budgets, and option hurdles.

### Guard T1 (Framing): Defining the stopping rule and baseline before the clock runs

In sequential decisions, `T1Framing` ($T\_1$) protects pre-committed stopping rules against opportunistic cutoffs invented after early returns appear.

On a filtered probability space, suppose an actor observes adapted rewards $Z\_t \in L^1$ across steps $0 \le t \le T$ (Snell 1952). On finite horizons ($T < \infty$), backward recursion from $U\_T = Z\_T$ sets $U\_t = \max\bigl(Z\_t, \mathbb{E}[U\_{t+1} \mid \mathcal{F}\_t]\bigr)$ and yields an earliest optimal stopping time $\tau^\ast = \inf\lbrace{}t \in \lbrace{}0, \dots, T\rbrace{} : Z\_t = U\_t\rbrace{} < \infty$. On unbounded horizons ($T = \infty$), Hypothesis A ($\sup\_t Z\_t^+ \in L^1$) serves as a standard sufficient integrability condition (not strictly necessary) for defining $U\_t = \operatorname{ess\,sup}\_{\tau \ge t} \mathbb{E}[Z\_\tau \mid \mathcal{F}\_t] = \max\bigl(Z\_t, \mathbb{E}[U\_{t+1} \mid \mathcal{F}\_t]\bigr)$ almost surely; achieving a finite optimal stop $\tau^\ast < \infty$ then additionally requires $\limsup\_{t \to \infty} Z\_t = -\infty$ or attainment at $Z\_\infty := \limsup\_{t \to \infty} Z\_t$. When reward priors remain imprecise across Walley credal intervals $[\underline{P}, \overline{P}]$ or blank entries split `NOT_OBSERVED` from `INAPPLICABLE` ([Layer 07](57-layer-probability-credence-measurement-order.md)), continuation values inherit interval bounds rather than collapsing to a single expectation.

Search across ranked arrivals gives the cleanest exact calculation of a stopping threshold, resting strictly on ordinal comparisons (`T4Formalization`, Stevens scale invariance; [Layer 07](57-layer-probability-credence-measurement-order.md)) rather than cardinal reward units. Suppose $n \ge 2$ candidates arrive in random order. The decision-maker rejects the first $r - 1$ candidates and picks the first later arrival beating every predecessor (Lindley 1961; Ferguson 1989). This rule wins when two events align: the best overall candidate arrives at position $k \ge r$ (chance $1/n$). Meanwhile, the strongest of the first $k - 1$ must sit inside the rejected opening sample of size $r - 1$ (chance $(r-1)/(k-1)$). Summing across positions $r$ through $n$ yields winning probability $P(r)$ (with $P(1) = 1/n$):

$$P(r) = \frac{r-1}{n}\sum\_{k=r}^{n}\frac{1}{k-1}, \qquad r \ge 2.$$

Read aloud, $P(r)$ multiplies the skipped fraction by the sum of reciprocal ranks from $r$ onward. That product captures the chance that the true leader arrives after the baseline while the strongest runner-up landed inside it.

Successive differences for $2 \le r < n$, namely $P(r+1) - P(r) = \frac{1}{n}\bigl(\sum\_{j=r}^{n-1} j^{-1} - 1\bigr)$ with $P(2) - P(1) = \frac{1}{n}\sum\_{j=2}^{n-1} j^{-1} \ge 0$, fall strictly in $r$. They peak at the smallest cutoff $r^\ast \ge 2$ where $\sum\_{j=r}^{n-1} j^{-1} \le 1$. Re-indexing $j = k - 1$ and bounding $j^{-1} > \int\_j^{j+1} t^{-1}\mkern3mu dt$ gives $P(r) = \frac{r-1}{n}\sum\_{j=r-1}^{n-1} j^{-1} > -u\ln u$ for $u = (r-1)/n$, strictly above the continuum integral. Setting $x = r/n \in (0, 1)$ as $n \to \infty$, $P(r) \to -x\ln x$ peaks at $x^\ast = 1/e$ with success $1/e$. It collapses at both ends: $x \to 0^+$ leaves no baseline, while $x \to 1^-$ discards the winner during warm-up.

### Guard T2 (Severity): Testing stopping thresholds and bandit regret floors

In sequential trials and bandit allocation, `T2Severity` ($T\_2$) protects error rates and exploration budgets from premature peeking or lost variance.

Wald's (1947) sequential probability ratio test (SPRT) decides between distinct simple hypotheses $P\_0 \ne P\_1$ ($H\_0$ vs. $H\_1$, ensuring $\tau < \infty$ almost surely) with densities $f\_0, f\_1 > 0$ across i.i.d. draws. Pick error targets $\alpha\_W, \beta\_W \in (0, 1)$ ($\alpha\_W + \beta\_W < 1$) and cutoffs $0 < B < 1 < A$. Stopping when the likelihood ratio $\Lambda\_t = \prod\_{s=1}^t f\_1(X\_s)/f\_0(X\_s)$ crosses $A$ or $B$, Wald's overshoot bounds govern the **realized** error probabilities $(\alpha\_{\mathrm{act}}, \beta\_{\mathrm{act}})$ of the chosen cutoffs via $A \le (1 - \beta\_{\mathrm{act}})/\alpha\_{\mathrm{act}}$ and $B \ge \beta\_{\mathrm{act}}/(1 - \alpha\_{\mathrm{act}})$, never the unachieved targets $(\alpha\_W, \beta\_W)$: setting $A = (1 - \beta\_W)/\alpha\_W$ and $B = \beta\_W/(1 - \alpha\_W)$ guarantees only $\alpha\_{\mathrm{act}} + \beta\_{\mathrm{act}} \le \alpha\_W + \beta\_W$ under discrete overshoot, whereas overshoot-safe individual error control requires $A \ge 1/\alpha\_W$ and $B \le \beta\_W$. Expected sample size is minimal under $H\_0$ or $H\_1$ (neglecting boundary overshoot under Wald's 1947 matched-error idealization), swells under intermediate laws, and violates $(\alpha\_W, \beta\_W)$ under short truncation unless recalibrated or replaced by anytime *e*-processes (nonnegative test supermartingales $\mathbb{E}\_{H\_0}[E\_t \mid \mathcal{F}\_{t-1}] \le E\_{t-1}$ with $E\_0 = 1$, keeping *e*-values distinct from Euler's constant $e \approx 2.718$ in $1/e$ and $e^{-r(s-t)}$; [Chapter 30](30-probability-as-language.md)). Pre-published error rates test a claim; post-hoc cutoffs merely describe the sample.

Multi-armed bandits extend stopping from one stream to competing options. Starting from Robbins's (1952) two-population allocation maximizing expected reward without a fixed deadline, Gittins (1979) decouples infinite-horizon bandits into single-arm indices $\nu\_i(s\_i)$. That clean decoupling holds strictly under geometric discounting in $(0, 1)$, independent *rested* Markovian arms, zero switching cost, and risk neutrality without ruin. Because neither Gittins indices nor undiscounted regret bounds protect individual participants, high-variance exploratory draws ($\Delta\_i > 0$) still require per-cohort vital harm bounds (`T4`/`T5`) and informed consent ([Chapter 19](19-ethics-and-the-imposition-of-risk.md)) so trials never dump downside risk onto vulnerable cohorts across external reception perimeters ($\Theta\_{\mathrm{basin}}$).

Lai and Robbins (1985) establish an **asymptotic** lower bound on undiscounted regret over horizon $T \ge 2$ ($\ln T > 0$). Their theorem assumes regular single-parameter families ($P\_i \ll P^\ast$, $D\_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\_i \parallel P^\ast) \in (0, \infty)$) and **uniformly good** policies ($R\_T = o(T^a)\mkern5mu\forall a > 0$). Write $\Delta\_i = \mu^\ast - \mu\_i > 0$ for arm $i$'s mean gap below the best arm. Cumulative regret then obeys $\liminf\_{T \to \infty} R\_T / \ln T \ge \sum\_{i : \Delta\_i > 0} \Delta\_i / D\_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\_i \parallel P^\ast)$. That contrasts with finite-horizon minimax scaling $\Omega(\sqrt{KT})$ when gaps shrink as $\Delta\_i \asymp \sqrt{K/T}$.

For Gaussian arms with common variance $\sigma^2 > 0$, divergence $D\_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\_i \parallel P^\ast) = \Delta\_i^2/(2\sigma^2)$ scales suboptimal pulls as $2(\sigma/\Delta\_i)^2\ln T$ and sets the asymptotic regret constant at $2\sigma^2/\Delta\_i$. Examining both ends shows where that asymptotic limit applies: as $\Delta\_i \to 0^+$, the regret constant $2\sigma^2/\Delta\_i \to +\infty$, exceeding the worst-case cap $\Delta\_i T$ unless horizon length clears $T/\ln T > 2\sigma^2/\Delta\_i^2$ ($\Delta\_i \gg \sigma\sqrt{2(\ln T)/T}$). At large gaps, the dimensionless pull factor $1/D\_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\_i \parallel P^\ast)$ drops below one once $D\_{\mathrm{KL}}(P\_i \parallel P^\ast) > 1$ ($\Delta\_i/\sigma > \sqrt{2}$), shrinking regret constants, whereas non-overlapping support ($P\_i \not\ll P^\ast$) exits regularity altogether.

### Guard T5 (Temporal): Pricing real-option delay without hitting an irreversible deadline

In real-option timing and adaptive sizing, `T5Temporal` ($T\_5$) protects survival headroom so waiting for clarity never stalls past an absorbing barrier.

Dixit and Pindyck (1994) price an irreversible sunk investment $I > 0$ when project value $V\_t = \mathbb{E}\_t[\int\_t^\infty e^{-r(s-t)}\pi\_s\mkern3mu ds] > 0$ evolves continuously. Here $V\_t$ follows $dV\_t = \mu V\_t\mkern3mu dt + \sigma V\_t\mkern3mu dW\_t$ with volatility $\sigma > 0$, drift $\mu$, and discount rate $r > \max(0, \mu)$ (distinct from §1's secretary cutoff index $r$). Their yield $\delta\_{\mathrm{yield}} = r - \mu > 0$ is a financial convenience yield (distinct from testimonial status discounting $\delta\_{\mathrm{status}}$ in [Chapter 71](71-layer-linguistic-evidential-pragmatic-order.md)). Replacing simple net-present-value ($V\_t \ge I$) with option valuation $F(V\_t)$, enforcing value-matching $F(V^\ast) = V^\ast - I$ and smooth-pasting $F'(V^\ast) = 1$, raises the investment trigger to $V^\ast = \frac{\beta\_1}{\beta\_1 - 1}I$.

Here $\beta\_1 > 1$ is the unique positive root of $Q(\beta) = \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2\beta(\beta - 1) + \mu\beta - r = 0$ (since $Q(0) = -r < 0$ and $Q(1) = -\delta\_{\mathrm{yield}} < 0$). Higher $\sigma$ lowers $\beta\_1$ and widens the option multiplier $\beta\_1/(\beta\_1 - 1)$. As $\sigma \to 0^+$, the hurdle nears $\max(1, r/\delta\_{\mathrm{yield}})$: for $0 < \mu < r$, $\beta\_1 \to r/\mu$ gives hurdle $r/\delta\_{\mathrm{yield}} > 1$, whereas for $\mu \le 0 < r$, $\beta\_1 \to +\infty$ and $\beta\_1/(\beta\_1 - 1) \to 1$. By contrast, for $\mu \ge r$, no root exceeds one ($\beta\_1 \le 1$), so the option hurdle diverges ($V^\ast \to +\infty$) and no finite exercise threshold exists.

Crucially, Dixit–Pindyck's formula presupposes **proprietary waiting exclusivity** and **full-liability holding cost**. Under absent `T6Performative`, rival preemption or performative arrival shifts $\mathcal{D}(\theta)$ destroy monopoly waiting value so the decider prices an option they do not own; under absent `T7Liability` and limited liability (Innes 1990), inflating modeled $\sigma$ or asserting $\mu \ge r$ to raise $V^\ast$ while avoiding sunk safety outlays, or abandoning negative-$V\_t$ assets, turns private waiting into an unpriced put whose ongoing external damage rate $\dot{D}\_{\mathrm{basin}} > 0$ lands on external reception perimeters ($\Theta\_{\mathrm{basin}}$; [Chapter 40](40-institution-incentive-metric.md)) until draining slack forces an emergency waiver.

Real-option delay works only while waiting stays safe and external harm is escrowed. For reversible acts with headroom $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} \ge \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$ ([Chapter 47](47-irreversible-time-vital-margin.md) §6.3.2, vital margin), sizing throttles to minimax regret $a^\ast\_{\mathrm{MMR}}$ ([Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md)) or epistemic Kelly shrinkage $f^\ast\_{\mathrm{epi}} = k(n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}})\hat{f}^\ast \in (0, \hat{f}^\ast)$ ([Chapter 47](47-irreversible-time-vital-margin.md) §6.1.2; [Appendix A](appendix-a-mathematics.md) §A.2). That Kelly rule plugs $\hat{f}^\ast = (\hat{\mu} - r)/\sigma^2 > 0$ under known unit-period variance $\sigma^2 > 0$, Pareto tail exponent $\alpha\_{\mathrm{Pareto}} > 2$ ([Chapter 46](46-non-identical-asymmetry-heavy-tails.md)), $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} > 0$, and the **unit-period estimation-variance premise** $\sigma\_{\hat{\mu}}^2 := \mathrm{Var}(\hat{\mu}) = \sigma^2/n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}$ (distinct from fixed-span continuous-time sampling $\sigma^2/T$ and from Baker & McHale's 2013 SNR-dependent frequentist shrinkage, and failing whenever §2's adaptive bias or $\rho\_{R,Y} \ne 0$ contaminates $\hat{\mu}$ without [Chapter 45](45-world-to-archive-big-data-paradox.md) MSE adjustment; unknown $\hat{\sigma}^2$ demands stronger scale-invariant shrinkage). Containing deferral moral hazard additionally requires pricing external damage-rate escrow $\dot{D}\_{\mathrm{basin}}$ inside holding yield, capping volatility-driven deferral under decaying $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}}$, and locking non-abandonable remediation escrows. Crossing an absorbing barrier before $\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$ destroys the option ([Chapter 43](43-inquiry-openness-multiverse.md)); Analytic Hierarchy Process scores (Saaty 1980; [Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md)) or impact–confidence checklists never offset breached survival floors.

## 2. The archive that is not exchangeable

Every rule in §1 assumes exchangeable arrival order, independent arm draws, or stationary log-increments $\ln(V\_{t+\Delta t}/V\_t)$ (since levels $V\_t$ themselves drift). Under **i.i.d.** continuous draws, the $k$-th observation sets an upper record with exact probability $1/k$, giving an expected record count of $\sum\_{k=1}^{n} 1/k = \ln n + \gamma + o(1)$ (Rényi 1962). Upward drift multiplies records above $\ln n + \gamma$, while downward drift pushes them toward one, making stationary rules confuse trend for candidate quality. Likewise, open-horizon SPRT breaks under short fixed truncation without recalibration or anytime-valid *e*-processes (keeping *e*-values distinct from Euler's constant $e$), and *ex post* stopping disclosures fail whenever undocumented pilot draws or operational pauses go unlogged; an honest archive must seal the stopping predicate before deployment ($t\_{\mathrm{disp}}$; [Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md)), log realized assignment propensities $p\_{i,t} > 0$ for inverse-propensity or anytime-valid *e*-process reporting, and separate unpulled arms from missing records ([Layer 07](57-layer-probability-credence-measurement-order.md)).

A strict upward trend ($x\_1 < x\_2 < \dots < x\_n$) turns that failure extreme. Every arrival sets a new record (count $n$), so the secretary rule halts immediately at $r$ while the true maximum sits at $n$. Success plunges from its $1/e$ benchmark to zero for any $r < n$, leaving $x\_r$ as the worst post-refusal option in $\lbrace{}r, \dots, n\rbrace{}$.

Adaptive bandits (Robbins 1952; Lai & Robbins 1985) similarly destroy archive exchangeability whenever sample counts react to past averages. Suppose arm observations $X\_{i,t}$ are **i.i.d.** with true mean $\mu\_i$ ($\mathbb{E}[|X\_{i,t}|] < \infty$), and let $N\_i \ge 1$ ($\mathbb{E}[N\_i] < \infty$) be a **non-anticipating** random stopping pull count ($\mathbf{1}\lbrace N\_i \ge t\rbrace$ independent of future draw $X\_{i,t}$) with logged sample mean $\bar{X}\_{i,N\_i}$. Wald's identity $\mathbb{E}[\sum\_{t=1}^{N\_i}(X\_{i,t} - \mu\_i)] = \mathbb{E}[N\_i(\bar{X}\_{i,N\_i} - \mu\_i)] = 0$ then fixes the selection bias:

$$\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}\_{i,N\_i}] - \mu\_i = -\frac{\mathrm{Cov}(N\_i, \bar{X}\_{i,N\_i})}{\mathbb{E}[N\_i]}.$$

Read aloud, the bias in an adaptive arm's recorded average equals minus the covariance between pull count and final sample mean, divided by expected pulls.

Starving early losers while extending winners creates positive covariance ($\mathrm{Cov}(N\_i, \bar{X}\_{i,N\_i}) > 0$) and biases logged arm means **downward**. Stopping early on winning streaks creates negative covariance ($\mathrm{Cov}(N\_i, \bar{X}\_{i,N\_i}) < 0$) and biases logged averages **upward**. Only stopping rules uncorrelated with realized arm sample means ($\mathrm{Cov}(N\_i, \bar{X}\_{i,N\_i}) = 0$) leave the logged archive unbiased, a zero-covariance condition strictly weaker than full independence of $N\_i$ and $\bar{X}\_{i,N\_i}$.
