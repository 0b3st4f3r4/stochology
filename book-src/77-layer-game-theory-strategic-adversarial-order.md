## 1. Strategic classification and the Stackelberg game

A deployed classifier is not scored on a passive population. Hardt, Megiddo, Papadimitriou and Wootters (2016) formalised a leader-follower game: an agent with baseline profile $x\_0$ and cost $c(\cdot, \cdot)$ answers a published $\theta$ with $x^\ast(\theta, x\_0) = \arg\max\_x \bigl(f(\theta, x) - c(x, x\_0)\bigr)$, and the institution minimises its loss over the induced features. A Nash player takes the other's action as fixed; a Stackelberg learner optimises against that best-response function. With quadratic manipulation costs the displacement is collinear with the scoring gradient, so published weights bend the feature geometry before any outcome is evaluated. Schelling (1960) shows that where several responses are stable the game form settles nothing and focal points settle it, and those are institutional conventions, not parameters.

The price of gaming is arithmetic, and it belongs in the contract. With a private gain $b$ from gaming, a detection probability $\pi$ and a penalty $p$, the expected cost of gaming is $\pi p$. The agent games while $\pi p < b$ and stops as soon as penalty and detection probability together exceed the gain. Behaviour changes with no change to the model, and the whole instrument is the product of two of its parameters.

The cheapest leader-follower problem makes the surface explicit. A linear score $f(\theta, x) = w \cdot x$ with a published threshold $\tau$ and a quadratic manipulation cost $c(x, x\_0) = \frac{1}{2}\|x - x\_0\|\_2^2$ leaves a gap $g = \tau - w \cdot x\_0$, bought along $w$ at a price $g^2/(2\|w\|^2)$, so the manoeuvre pays whenever $g \le \|w\|\sqrt{2b}$ for a private benefit $b$. Every agent inside that gap of the line crosses it at a cost below the benefit, and nobody further away tries. The size of the manufactured mass is therefore set by the published distance to the line, not by anything the score measures. As $b \to 0$ the margin separates nothing, and as $\|w\| \to \infty$ the distance to the line grows at fixed effort — the formal content of score inflation, and the reason rescaling a scorecard is a governance decision.

Perdomo, Zrnić, Mendler-Dünner and Hardt (2020) generalise: the distribution $\mathcal{D}(\theta)$ is an endogenous function of the deployed parameter. If $\mathcal{D}$ is $\varepsilon$-sensitive in Wasserstein distance and the loss is $\beta$-jointly smooth and $\gamma$-strongly convex, repeated risk minimisation converges to a performatively stable point whenever $\varepsilon < \gamma/\beta$ (and in the linear-quadratic normal family, if and only if $|\varepsilon| < 1 + \lambda = \gamma/\beta$). The operator has modulus $L\_G \le \varepsilon\beta/\gamma$. At $\varepsilon = 0$ the fixed point is reached in one step; at $\varepsilon \ge \gamma/\beta$ the contraction guarantee is lost and repeated risk minimisation can fail to converge, the bound being tight; in between, the residual after $k$ rounds is at most $L\_G^{k}$ of the initial distance, so each round multiplies the error by a constant and the number of rounds to reach a tolerance $\eta$ grows only as $\ln(1/\eta)/\ln(1/L\_G)$. A stable point is not an optimal one either. Performative optimality needs the chain-rule term $\nabla\_\theta \mathcal{D}(\theta)$, which repeated risk minimisation never computes.

Robustness and invariance answer different questions. A robust classifier survives a perturbation of the input it was trained on; an invariant classifier survives a change in the world that generated the input. A strategic agent does the second. The test that separates them is stability of the coefficient across environments, and it is cheap: two environments that differ in mechanism and agree in coefficient are evidence, two that agree only under perturbation are not.

## 2. The manipulation surface

Publish the upper tail of the score as the institutional line and the surface becomes computable in advance. A perturbation budget stated as a fraction of that line reaches every reading below it whose distance to the line is inside the budget, so the reachable mass is the share of the population living just under the threshold, not the share the threshold is meant to detect. The surface an agent can manufacture without changing anything real is then larger than the class being detected, and the cost of manufacturing it is bounded by the budget — a number the institution itself published.

This is the layer's counterexample, arithmetic rather than historical. The recommended policy, retrain to the performatively stable point, is wrong in a way the fixed-point theorem cannot detect. The iteration converges quickly because convergence is a statement about the map and not about the world, and the object it converged to separates a manufactured mass from a bare one. A stable point and a dead proxy are compatible. A budget $\delta$ in score units moves the positive class from $\bar{F}(\tau)$ to $\bar{F}(\tau - \delta)$, a factor $\bar{F}(\tau - \delta)/\bar{F}(\tau)$ that for any log-concave score distribution increases as the line moves into the tail, so the more selective the line the larger the share of its positives that the budget manufactures — with every parameter inside the region where $L\_G < 1$. Manipulation-proof estimation is a mechanism-design problem over the reporting technology, not a fixed-point problem over the parameters.

## 3. Where the signature stops seeing

The thesis is that the best-response function $x^\ast(\theta, x\_0)$ is an object the principal can compute and optimise against. It expires when the response cost is unobservable and heterogeneous: then the best response is not a function, and for a published $\theta$ there is a set of induced distributions indexed by the cost distribution whose Stackelberg optimum no amount of data on $x$ identifies.

The procedure is to publish an interval instead of a point. Bound $x^\ast(\theta, x\_0)$ over the identified set $\mathcal{C}$ of cost functions, take the worst-case risk over that set, and report the interval's width beside the chosen $\theta$, in the spirit of the partial-identification margin. Choosing $\theta$ to minimise that worst case,

$$\theta\_{\mathrm{mm}} = \arg\min\_{\theta}\mkern3mu \max\_{c \in \mathcal{C}}\mkern3mu R\bigl(\theta; \mathcal{D}\_c(\theta)\bigr),$$

is Wald's (1945) minimax rule with nature replaced by the unobserved cost distribution. Its guarantee is exactly the one the situation supports, a ceiling on loss that holds whichever cost function the population has, and its price is that it is set by the least favourable member of $\mathcal{C}$, so a wide identified set buys robustness by surrendering performance on every member that is not the worst. If the width exceeds the gap between two candidate classifiers the comparison is not identified, and the honest output is that the institution cannot rank them.

The collapses follow from the missing axis. Without $S\_{\mathrm{vital}}$ an agent computes an exquisite equilibrium while playing a multiplicative game with negative time-average growth, and the account is absorbed before convergence: Impotent Lucidity Before Ruin. Ignoring the strategic response altogether collapses the proxy into noise as the population learns the line, which is blindness to the evaluated population, or Blind Undersizing. Deploying a hyper-targeted classifier against a behavioural weakness extracts private gain with no social surplus, which is Cynical Capture and Pillaging.
