## 1. Strategic classification, Stackelberg games, and performativity

Picture a lender or admissions panel publishing its scoring checklist—income stability, phone habits, or test scores. Once public, applicants do not stay passive: some earn genuine qualifications, while others alter cheap features and reshape the population.

### Guard T1 (Framing): Separating genuine merit from the published proxy

In strategic learning, `T1Framing` ($T\_1$) protects the boundary between the true qualification an institution seeks and cheap proxies contestants manipulate.

Hardt, Megiddo, Papadimitriou, and Wootters (2016) model this tension as a leader–follower game. A jury publishes binary rule $f : X \to \lbrace{}-1, +1\rbrace{}$. A contestant with baseline features $x\_0$ and manipulation cost $c(x\_0, x)$ responds with $\Delta(x\_0) = \arg\max\_{x \in X}\bigl(f(x) - c(x\_0, x)\bigr)$. Meanwhile, the jury minimizes error against true labels $h(x\_0)$, namely $\mathbb{P}\_{x\_0 \sim \mathcal{D}}[h(x\_0) \neq f(\Delta(x\_0))]$. Their solver covers separable costs $c(x\_0, x) = \max\lbrace{}0, c\_2(x) - c\_1(x\_0)\rbrace{}$, noting that squared Euclidean distance is non-separable.

Three equilibria diverge depending on how the learner anticipates response. A **Nash** player optimizes against a frozen distribution ($\theta\_{\mathrm{PS}}$). A **Stackelberg** leader commits anticipating $\Delta(\cdot)$ or $\mathcal{D}(\theta)$ ($\theta\_{\mathrm{PO}}$), differing from Nash in non-zero-sum games. A **zero-sum adversarial** learner solves $\min\_\theta \mathbb{E}\bigl[\max\_{\Vert\xi\Vert \le r} \ell(x\_0 + \xi, y; \theta)\bigr]$ ($r > 0$), where Nash and Stackelberg values match under convex–concave symmetry; ties are broken by focal conventions.

Layer `L27`'s `(2, 0, 1)` signature pairs framing ($T\_1$) and severity ($T\_2$) on $S\_{\mathrm{epi}}$ with performativity ($T\_6$) on $S\_{\mathrm{gov}}$, leaving $S\_{\mathrm{vital}}$ (`T4Formalization`, `T5Temporal`) unguarded locally. Because contestants optimize whatever objective is declared—loss, horizon, and tie-break—picking a convenient proxy over true aim compromises `T1Framing` upfront. Even so, attested counter-inductive reopening of $T\_1$ keeps framing revisable when norms fail (Canguilhem 1943/1966). With gain $b > 0$, manipulation cost $c \ge 0$, detection rate $\pi \in [0, 1]$, and fine $p \ge 0$, risk-neutral gaming pays when $\pi p + c < b$. Limited liability caps $p$ (Innes 1990), while audit flooding against finite human review drives $\pi \to 0$ ([Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md)).

Linear-threshold geometry makes that manipulation margin explicit. Consider threshold rule $\mathbf{1}\lbrace{}w \cdot x \ge \tau\rbrace{}$ ($w \neq 0$), benefit $b > 0$, and positive-definite quadratic cost $c(x\_0, x) = \frac{1}{2}(x - x\_0)^\top C (x - x\_0)$. Qualified agents ($g := \tau - w \cdot x\_0 \le 0$) pay zero cost. Unqualified agents ($g > 0$) minimize $\frac{1}{2}\delta^\top C\delta$ subject to $w \cdot \delta = g$, yielding optimal shift $\delta^\ast = \frac{g}{w^\top C^{-1}w} C^{-1}w$ at cost $\frac{g^2}{2\mkern2mu w^\top C^{-1}w}$. That shift stays collinear with fixed $w$ iff $Cw \propto w$, holding for every nonzero $w$ only when $C \propto I$.

Crossing the boundary pays iff $0 < g \le \sqrt{2b\mkern2mu w^\top C^{-1}w}$. When $C = I$, that condition simplifies to Euclidean distance $d(x\_0, H) = g/\Vert w\Vert\_2 \le \sqrt{2b}$ to hyperplane $H = \lbrace{}x : w \cdot x = \tau\rbrace{}$. Rescaling $(w, \tau) \mapsto (\alpha w, \alpha\tau)$ ($\alpha > 0$) leaves $d(x\_0, H)$ and manipulated mass unchanged. Sending $\Vert w\Vert\_2 \to \infty$ at fixed $g$ shrinks $g/\Vert w\Vert\_2 \to 0$. Under unscaled $\tau$, fixed effort $\sqrt{2b}$ generates score jump $\Vert w\Vert\_2\sqrt{2b}$ (inflating units as $\Vert w\Vert\_2$ rises), while $b \to 0^+$ shrinks $\Vert w\Vert\_2\sqrt{2b} \to 0$.

### Guard T6 (Performative): Tracking how published rules reshape populations and labels

Under strategic adaptation, `T6Performative` ($T\_6$) protects models from mistaking a reshaped population for an independent stationary baseline.

Extending Lucas's (1976) critique of policy-invariant reduced forms, Perdomo, Zrnić, Mendler-Dünner, and Hardt (2020) model endogenous maps $\mathcal{D}(\theta)$ ([Chapter 48](48-reactive-worlds-performativity-goodhart.md), reactive performativity). They write decoupled risk as $\mathcal{L}(\theta; \theta') = \mathbb{E}\_{Z \sim \mathcal{D}(\theta')}[\ell(Z; \theta)]$ and repeated retraining as $G(\theta) = \arg\min\_{\theta'} \mathcal{L}(\theta'; \theta)$. Suppose $\mathcal{D}$ is $\varepsilon$-sensitive ($W\_1(\mathcal{D}(\theta), \mathcal{D}(\theta')) \le \varepsilon\Vert\theta - \theta'\Vert\_2$), loss is $\gamma$-strongly convex, and $\nabla\_\theta\ell(z; \theta)$ is $\beta\_{\mathrm{joint}}$-jointly smooth. Whenever $\varepsilon < \gamma/\beta\_{\mathrm{joint}}$, $G$ contracts to a unique **performatively stable** fixed point $\theta\_{\mathrm{PS}}$ with Lipschitz modulus $L\_G \le \varepsilon\beta\_{\mathrm{joint}}/\gamma < 1$.

In Perdomo et al.'s (2020) one-dimensional linear-quadratic normal model ([Chapter 48](48-reactive-worlds-performativity-goodhart.md))—ridge loss $\ell(y;\theta)=\frac{1}{2}(y-\theta)^2+\frac{\lambda}{2}\theta^2$ ($\lambda > 0$) under $y \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu\_0+\varepsilon\theta,\sigma^2)$—sample-gradient sensitivity $L\_y = |\partial\_y\partial\_\theta\ell| = 1$ ($\gamma = \beta\_{\mathrm{joint}} = 1 + \lambda$) tightens that condition to exact contraction modulus $\kappa\_{\mathrm{RRM}} = |\varepsilon|/(1+\lambda) < 1$ iff $|\varepsilon| < 1 + \lambda$. Even when retraining converges, dropping the distributional gradient $\nabla\_{\theta'}\mathcal{L}(\theta;\theta')\big|\_{\theta'=\theta}$ separates $\theta\_{\mathrm{PS}}$ from the Stackelberg **performatively optimal** point $\theta\_{\mathrm{PO}}$ whenever $\varepsilon \neq 0$, $\lambda > 0$, and $\mu\_0 \neq 0$.

Full-label convergence also fails under **selective labels**. Deployment $\theta\in\Theta$ often gates outcome logging via $O=\mathbf{1}\lbrace{}f\_\theta(X)\ge\tau\rbrace{}$—defaults among approved borrowers, fraud among audited filings, rearrest among released defendants. Then $\theta$ alters both covariate behavior $\mathcal{D}(\theta)$ and observability $P(O=1\mid X,\theta)$, linking `T6Performative` into `T3Archive` censoring ($\rho\_{R,Y}\neq 0$; [Chapter 45](45-world-to-archive-big-data-paradox.md), archive paradox).

Retraining solely on accepted records $\tilde{\mathcal{D}}(\theta)$ ($O=1$) optimizes $\tilde{G}(\theta)$ rather than $G(\theta)$: Valdrighi, Valera, and Raimundo (2026) prove $\Vert G(\theta)-\tilde{G}(\theta)\Vert\_2$ can reach $\mathrm{diam}(\Theta)$ even when $\mathcal{D}$ is $\theta$-invariant, breaking standard $\theta\_{\mathrm{PS}}$ equilibria (Perdomo et al. 2020). Minimizing a $\gamma$-strongly convex worst-case log-sum-exp surrogate over label bounds $[l(x,\theta),u(x,\theta)]$ (`Robust RRM`, $\bar{G}$) holds asymptotic iterates ($\limsup\_{t\to\infty}$) within $\kappa\_{\mathrm{sel}}/(1-L\_G)$ of $\theta\_{\mathrm{PS}}$ when $G$ contracts ($L\_G<1$, $\kappa\_{\mathrm{sel}}:=\sup\_{\theta\in\Theta}\Vert\bar{G}(\theta)-G(\theta)\Vert\_2$). Yet hard cutoffs can break $\varepsilon$-sensitivity. Label sensitivity $(L\_x,L\_\theta)$ also stays unidentifiable from accepted data without domain bounds or randomized exploration ($P(O=1\mid X)>0$) (Valdrighi, Valera, & Raimundo 2026)—a selective-label trap compounding across institutions in [Chapter 98](98-when-the-target-draws-its-bow.md).

Local adversarial robustness guards perturbations around fixed $X$. By contrast, invariant causal prediction tests stability of $Y^{(e)} \mid X\_S^{(e)}$ across environments $e \in \mathcal{E}$ without intervening on $Y$ or hiding confounders (Peters, Bühlmann, & Meinshausen 2016; [Chapter 48](48-reactive-worlds-performativity-goodhart.md), causal invariance). That test still demands enough power so non-rejection of the empty set is not mistaken for safety. Strategic agents often manipulate proxies $X$ at fixed $Y$, whereas mechanism design must reward genuine causal improvement over cosmetic gaming (Kleinberg & Raghavan 2020; Manheim & Garrabrant 2018).

## 2. The manipulation surface

### Guard T2 (Severity): Auditing threshold inflation and asymmetric social burden

On the manipulation surface, `T2Severity` ($T\_2$) protects test accuracy and higher-cost applicants from error inflation around shifted cutoffs.

Consider survival function $\bar{F}(\tau) > 0$ and score perturbation budget $\delta > 0$. Gaming shifts the positive share from $\bar{F}(\tau)$ to $\bar{F}(\tau - \delta)$ by ratio $\bar{F}(\tau - \delta)/\bar{F}(\tau)$. When $\bar{F}$ is log-concave (non-decreasing hazard rate), that inflation ratio is non-decreasing in $\tau$—strictly rising for Gaussian scores and constant $e^{\kappa\delta}$ for exponential scores ($\kappa > 0$)—inflating manufactured positives as selectivity rises. Heavy-tailed power-law scores ($\tau > \delta$) reverse the slope toward unity while extreme losses dominate ([Chapter 78](78-layer-macro-finance-econometrics-actuarial-order.md), heavy-tailed risk).

A pre-registered digital-credit trial in Nairobi ($N = 1{,}557$, registry AEARCTR-0004649, cash payouts tied to smartphone logs because rules barred real loans; *arXiv:2004.03865v1*) measured this gaming directly (Björkegren, Blumenstock, & Knight 2020). Disclosing standard ML rules cut predictive accuracy by $23\\%$ on average. Naive LASSO pooled RMSE jumped from $\unicode{x24}4.00$ opaque ($\unicode{x24}3.70$ control) to $\unicode{x24}4.93$ transparent, rising from $\unicode{x24}3.24$ to $\unicode{x24}3.87$ on income and from $3.655$ to $5.762$ on activity. Static OLS squared loss leapt from $0.266$ to $3{,}961.225$, while period-by-period retraining oscillated between $11{,}611.884$ (iteration $1000$) and $565.383$ (iteration $1001$) (Björkegren, Blumenstock, & Knight 2020).

Structural GMM cost estimates in the same Nairobi experiment explain why specific habits break first (Björkegren, Blumenstock, & Knight 2020). Estimated manipulation costs spanned $\alpha\_{jj} = 0.03$ ($\mathrm{cents/action}^2$, outgoing texts; $0.04$ received texts) up to $741.46$ ($\mathrm{SE} = 16{,}612.8388$, second of average call duration), leaving the $90$th percentile $2.5$ times cheaper to game than the $10$th percentile. Under transparency, their strategy-robust estimator beat standard rules by $13\\%$ on average, cutting pooled RMSE to $\unicode{x24}4.31$ ($\unicode{x24}3.66$ income; $4.014$ activity). That capped equilibrium transparency cost at $\unicode{x24}0.31$ ($8\\%$) pooled and $\unicode{x24}0.41$ ($13\\%$) on income (Björkegren, Blumenstock, & Knight 2020).

Even strategy-aware optimization shifts costs unevenly: Milli, Miller, Dragan, and Hardt (2019) prove strategic classification still pushes asymmetric social burden $B^+(f)$ onto higher-cost disadvantaged agents across external reception perimeters ($\Theta\_{\mathrm{basin}}$).

## 3. Where the signature stops seeing

When true cost $c$ inside cost class $\mathcal{C}$ is unobserved—illustrated by the ill-conditioned duration-cost estimate above ($\hat{\alpha}\_{jj} = 741.46$, $\mathrm{SE} = 16{,}612.8388$)—realized features $x$ cannot identify a unique $\mathcal{D}\_c(\theta)$ or break ties without focal conventions (Schelling 1960). Let $R(\theta; \mathcal{D}\_c(\theta)) = \mathbb{E}\_{(X,Y) \sim \mathcal{D}\_c(\theta)}[\ell((X, Y); \theta)]$ denote expected risk under classifier $\theta$ and cost $c \in \mathcal{C}$. Wald's (1945) minimax risk rule picks $\theta\_{\mathrm{mm}}$ by guarding against the worst cost profile:

$$\theta\_{\mathrm{mm}} = \arg\min\_{\theta}\mkern3mu \sup\_{c \in \mathcal{C}}\mkern3mu R\bigl(\theta; \mathcal{D}\_c(\theta)\bigr).$$

Read aloud, the minimax rule $\theta\_{\mathrm{mm}}$ picks the classifier whose worst possible expected loss across all unknown manipulation-cost profiles $c$ in $\mathcal{C}$ is as small as possible.

That bound is attained when $\mathcal{C}$ is compact and risk upper semicontinuous, tempered by Harsanyi's (1975) critique of unrestricted maximin conservatism against paralyzing reversible probes ([Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md), triad minimum). Classifiers $\theta\_1, \theta\_2$ induce risk intervals $[\underline{R}(\theta\_i),\mkern3mu \overline{R}(\theta\_i)] = \bigl[\inf\_{c\in\mathcal{C}} R(\theta\_i; \mathcal{D}\_c(\theta\_i)),\mkern3mu \sup\_{c\in\mathcal{C}} R(\theta\_i; \mathcal{D}\_c(\theta\_i))\bigr]$, which order strictly under uniform dominance only when disjoint and remain incomparable when overlapping. This bound fails if agents invest in causal improvement outside $\mathcal{C}$ (Kleinberg & Raghavan 2020) or form coalitions that break the cost class.

Locally, `{T1Framing, T2Severity, T6Performative}` carries `(2, 0, 1)` ($k\_2 = 0$), leaving isolated traces `Unparsable` without $S\_{\mathrm{vital}}$ ([Appendix B.1](appendix-b-architecture-verification.md#b1-the-grammar-of-aim--syntax-layer-and-acceptance-predicate)).
