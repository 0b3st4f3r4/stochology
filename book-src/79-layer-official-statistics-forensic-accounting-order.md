## 1. The digit law, and the support it presupposes

Newcomb and Benford found that a first significant digit $d$ in scale-spanning data follows $P(d) = \log\_{10}(1 + 1/d)$, which puts $0.3010$ on the digit one and $0.0458$ on the digit nine. It is the unique significand measure invariant under positive scalar multiplication, delivered by a mantissa uniform in the logarithm, with $P(2) = 0.1761$ and $P(3) = 0.1249$.

The law is a statement about a mixture of scales, and a deviation from it is a statement about that mixture. Take $X$ log-uniform on a support of half a decade, $[1, \sqrt{10})$, with $\sqrt{10} \approx 3.1623$. The normalising $\log\_{10}$-length is $1/2$: the digit one occupies $[1, 2)$ of length $\log\_{10} 2 \approx 0.3010$, the digit two occupies $[2, 3)$ of length $\log\_{10}(3/2) \approx 0.1761$, and the digit three occupies $[3, \sqrt{10})$ of length $1/2 - \log\_{10} 3 \approx 0.0229$, giving $P(1) = 2\log\_{10} 2 \approx 0.6021$, $P(2) = 2\log\_{10}(3/2) \approx 0.3522$, $P(3) = 1 - 2\log\_{10} 3 \approx 0.0458$, and $P(4) = \dots = P(9) = 0$. A support narrower than a decade admits only a truncated set of leading digits and obeys a different law; calling its digits anomalous mistakes a support for a behaviour.

A pressure field bounded between two nearby values supplies such a column. Its support is a small fraction of a decade, which leaves room for two leading digits and no more, and the column shows exactly that cap: its readings begin with one or with nine and almost nothing else appears. Nigrini's mean absolute deviation (Nigrini 2000) against the Benford frequencies then sits far above the conformity threshold at which an auditor would call the population fraudulent. An auditor applying the canonical form mechanically reaches a verdict that is wrong by the distance between fraud and a physical ceiling. The rule of order is to test the scale first and the digits second. A deviation that survives the scale test is evidence; one that does not is a description of the business model.

## 2. Conservation, the dark archive, and the revision record

Pacioli's double entry makes every debit carry a credit, so the balance sheet conserves: assets equal liabilities plus equity. Aggressive engineering moves liabilities into unconsolidated vehicles, as Enron's partnerships and Lehman's Repo 105 transactions did, and the unrecorded remainder is a dark archive bounded from below by Chao's estimator $\hat{n}\_{00} \ge f\_1^2/(2f\_2)$. Preventing retrospective rewriting needs an append-only commitment backed by personal certification and clawback.

Official accounts are vintages rather than constants. On a real-time output archive the mean absolute revision is a nonzero fraction of the growth rate itself, the largest revisions approach the size of a quarterly growth figure, and a visible share of quarters changes sign between the initial release and the benchmark. An interest-rate decision taken on an initial release is a decision taken on noise of that size. The noise is not stationary either: revisions cluster in turning points rather than spreading evenly across the record.

Welfare measurement inherits the same problem. Sen (1976) showed that a headcount ratio violates monotonicity and the transfer axiom, and his distribution-sensitive index $P\_S = H[I + (1 - I)G\_{\mathrm{poor}}]$ composes the headcount, the income shortfall and the Gini among the poor, so that a transfer to the poorest reduces measured poverty more than an equal transfer near the line.

## 3. The keystone, and where the thesis stops

Layer 29 is a keystone, and each missing site reproduces a collapse. Without $T\_3$ the safety net is sized against an incomplete registry, which is the Enron omission and the uncounted settlement at once, or Blind Undersizing. Without $T\_4$ mark-to-model values and a scalar metric hide tail deprivation until the balance sheet cannot absorb it: Impotent Lucidity Before Ruin. Without $T\_7$ restatements, auditor capture and asset stripping leave the liabilities with the state: Cynical Capture and Pillaging.

The thesis is that conservation plus digit testing plus a revision margin delivers accountability. It expires where both sides of an entry are written by the same party. A transfer between two entities of one group enters the consolidated ledger as a debit and a credit that net to zero for any value of the transfer, so the identity holds exactly for a balance sheet that is entirely fiction, and a test that always passes is not evidence. The procedure moves the test to the boundary of the entity: reconcile the reported position against a ledger held by a counterparty with an opposing interest, and treat the difference as the finding. The Lehman examiner did that, since the Repo 105 entries balanced and the exposure was established from the counterparties' collateral records (Valukas 2010). An audit whose only input is the audited party's own double entry has conceded the point it exists to test. An append-only commitment earns its cost only where one link in the chain is written by a party who cannot benefit from the entry.

2. Show how hiding debt in unconsolidated vehicles breaks the conservation identity, and how a capture-recapture bound and an append-only vault together bound the omission.
3. Compute the digit-law probability of a leading two and of a leading three, and say how the two differ (answer: $0.1761$ and $0.1249$).
4. Say what second calculation an auditor owes before a deviation becomes evidence (answer: the proportion of the population near a hard ceiling, which bends the distribution).
