## 1. The digit law, and the support it presupposes

Newcomb (1881) and Benford (1938) found that a first significant digit $d$ in scale-spanning data follows $P(d) = \log\_{10}(1 + 1/d)$, which puts $0.3010$ on the digit one and $0.0458$ on the digit nine. It is the unique significand measure invariant under positive scalar multiplication (Pinkham 1961; Hill 1995), delivered by a mantissa uniform in the logarithm, with $P(2) = 0.1761$ and $P(3) = 0.1249$.

The law is a statement about a mixture of scales, and a deviation from it is a statement about that mixture. Take $X$ log-uniform on a support of half a decade, $[1, \sqrt{10})$, with $\sqrt{10} \approx 3.1623$. The normalising $\log\_{10}$-length is $1/2$: the digit one occupies $[1, 2)$ of length $\log\_{10} 2 \approx 0.3010$, the digit two occupies $[2, 3)$ of length $\log\_{10}(3/2) \approx 0.1761$, and the digit three occupies $[3, \sqrt{10})$ of length $1/2 - \log\_{10} 3 \approx 0.0229$, giving $P(1) = 2\log\_{10} 2 \approx 0.6021$, $P(2) = 2\log\_{10}(3/2) \approx 0.3522$, $P(3) = 1 - 2\log\_{10} 3 \approx 0.0458$, and $P(4) = \dots = P(9) = 0$. A support narrower than a decade admits only a truncated set of leading digits and obeys a different law; calling its digits anomalous mistakes a support for a behaviour.

A barometric column supplies such a support. Sea-level pressure recorded in hectopascals lies within a small fraction of a decade that straddles a power of ten, so its readings can begin only with nine, below the power, or with one, above it, and the digits two through eight have probability zero by construction. The distance from Benford is then fixed by the support before any reading is taken. Nigrini's mean absolute deviation is $\mathrm{MAD} = \tfrac19\sum\_{d=1}^{9}\lvert p\_d - b\_d\rvert$; the seven absent digits contribute their whole Benford mass, $1 - b\_1 - b\_9 = 1 - 0.3010 - 0.0458 = 0.6532$, and that same mass must reappear on the digits one and nine, so

$$\mathrm{MAD} \ge \frac{2 \times 0.6532}{9} \approx 0.145,$$

about ten times the $0.015$ above which Nigrini (2012) classes a first-digit distribution as nonconforming. An auditor applying the canonical form mechanically reaches a verdict that is wrong by the distance between fraud and a physical ceiling. The converse failure is as real. Deckert, Myagkov and Ordeshook (2011) found that in election returns conformity to the digit law is neither a necessary nor a sufficient sign of an honest count. The rule of order is to test the scale first and the digits second. A deviation that survives the scale test is evidence; one that does not is a description of the business model.

## 2. Conservation, the dark archive, and the revision record

Pacioli's (1494) double entry makes every debit carry a credit, so the balance sheet conserves: assets equal liabilities plus equity. Aggressive engineering moves liabilities into unconsolidated vehicles, as Enron's partnerships and Lehman's Repo 105 transactions did, and the unrecorded remainder is a dark archive bounded from below by Chao's estimator $\hat{n}\_{00} \ge f\_1^2/(2f\_2)$. Preventing retrospective rewriting needs an append-only commitment backed by personal certification and clawback.

Official accounts are vintages rather than constants. A real-time archive keeps every release of a quarter as it was first published, so the revision from first release to benchmark can be measured rather than assumed (Croushore & Stark 2001). Measured that way, the revisions to United States macroeconomic series are not well behaved: their mean is not zero, they are large relative to the series they revise, and they can be predicted from information available when the first release was made, so the first release is not an efficient forecast of the later one (Aruoba 2008). The consequence for a decision is arithmetic. Write the first release as $g\_0$ and the benchmark as $g\_0 + r$; a release closer to zero than the size of a typical revision, $\lvert g\_0\rvert < \lvert r\rvert$, can change sign, and a revision with a nonzero mean makes the sign change more likely in one direction than the other. An interest-rate decision taken on an initial release is a decision taken on noise of that size.

Before a ledger or a release there is a census, and before a census a category. Desrosières (1993) traced how the classes official statistics count, occupations, causes of death, households, were settled as conventions between administrations and the sciences, and how a class once settled makes its members countable, comparable and therefore governable. Scott (1998) gave the administrative motive: a state sees what it has first made legible, and the simplification is the price of the sight. The ontology enters the arithmetic exactly. Let a count move from $N\_1$ under one definition to $N\_2$ under a revised one; the change decomposes as

$$N\_2 - N\_1 = \underbrace{\bigl(N\_2 - N\_2'\bigr)}\_{\text{reclassification}} + \underbrace{\bigl(N\_2' - N\_1\bigr)}\_{\text{change in the world}},$$

where $N\_2'$ is the second-period population counted under the first definition. The second term is the one a reader wants and the first is the one a revision introduces, and only a bridge period in which both definitions are applied to the same population identifies $N\_2'$. A series that changes its classes without a bridge reports a sum whose parts no later arithmetic can separate.

Welfare measurement inherits the same problem. Sen (1976) showed that a headcount ratio violates monotonicity and the transfer axiom, and his distribution-sensitive index $P\_S = H[I + (1 - I)G\_{\mathrm{poor}}]$ composes the headcount, the income shortfall and the Gini among the poor, so that a transfer to the poorest reduces measured poverty more than an equal transfer near the line.

## 3. The keystone, and where the thesis stops

Layer 29 is a keystone, and each missing site reproduces a collapse. Without $T\_3$ the safety net is sized against an incomplete registry, which is the Enron omission and the uncounted settlement at once, or Blind Undersizing. Without $T\_4$ mark-to-model values and a scalar metric hide tail deprivation until the balance sheet cannot absorb it: Impotent Lucidity Before Ruin. Without $T\_7$ restatements, auditor capture and asset stripping leave the liabilities with the state: Cynical Capture and Pillaging.

The thesis is that conservation plus digit testing plus a revision margin delivers accountability. It expires where both sides of an entry are written by the same party. A transfer between two entities of one group enters the consolidated ledger as a debit and a credit that net to zero for any value of the transfer, so the identity holds exactly for a balance sheet that is entirely fiction, and a test that always passes is not evidence. The procedure moves the test to the boundary of the entity: reconcile the reported position against a ledger held by a counterparty with an opposing interest, and treat the difference as the finding. The Lehman examiner did that. The Repo 105 entries balanced on the firm's own books, and the examiner established from outside them that the transactions had been timed to quarter-ends, at $\unicode{x24}38.6$ billion at the end of the fourth quarter of 2007, $\unicode{x24}49.1$ billion at the end of the first quarter of 2008 and $\unicode{x24}50.38$ billion at the end of the second, and used to lower the net leverage the firm reported (Valukas 2010). An audit whose only input is the audited party's own double entry has conceded the point it exists to test. An append-only commitment earns its cost only where one link in the chain is written by a party who cannot benefit from the entry.
