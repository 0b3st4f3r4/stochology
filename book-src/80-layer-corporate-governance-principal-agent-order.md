## 1. Multitask contracts and the incentive that cannibalises itself

Holmström and Milgrom (1991) gave the reason high-powered pay fails organisations. An agent splits unobservable effort between a measurable proxy $y\_1 = a\_1 + \varepsilon\_1$ and an unmeasured asset such as maintenance or safety with intrinsic baseline $\bar{a}\_2$; under the strictly convex attention-substitution cost $C(a\_1, a\_2) = \frac{1}{2}c\_1 a\_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}c\_2\bigl(a\_2 - (\bar{a}\_2 - \gamma a\_1)\bigr)^2$ ($c\_1, c\_2 > 0$, $\det\nabla^2 C = c\_1 c\_2 > 0$ for every $\gamma \ge 0$), the coupled first-order conditions $\partial C/\partial a\_2 = 0$ and $\partial C/\partial a\_1 = \beta\_1$ yield $a\_1^\ast(\beta\_1) = \beta\_1/c\_1$ and $a\_2^\ast(\beta\_1) = \bar{a}\_2 - (\gamma/c\_1)\beta\_1$. Raising the piece rate on the proxy therefore removes effort linearly from the task nobody measures. The constrained optimum is muted pay, flat salary and direct behavioural constraint, not piece rates.

The loss is two lines of arithmetic, and it answers what a compensation committee actually asks. Normalise two tasks with unit costs and a target $a^\ast = (1, 1)$: the agent picks $a\_1 = \beta\_1$ and $a\_2 = 1 - \gamma\beta\_1$, so the loss is $L(\beta\_1) = (\beta\_1 - 1)^2 + (\gamma\beta\_1)^2$, minimised at $\beta\_1^\ast = 1/(1 + \gamma^2)$ with $L^\ast = \gamma^2/(1 + \gamma^2)$. At $\gamma = 0$ the first-best is attained and piece rates are exactly right; as $\gamma \to \infty$ the optimum tends to a flat salary and the principal absorbs the whole loss. At $\gamma = 1$, where substitution matches the unit curvature, the optimal piece rate already drops to half its zero-substitution level ($\beta\_1^\ast = 1/2$), and doubling $\gamma$ from $1/2$ to $1$ cuts $\beta\_1^\ast$ from $4/5$ to $1/2$ while multiplying minimum loss by $5/2$ (from $1/5$ to $1/2$), so the two instruments are not independent. With three tasks, equal costs and a weight on only the first, the whole of the effort aimed at the unweighted tasks leaves the place nobody is watching, and writing the loss as a squared distance is what makes that arguable in public rather than rhetorical. Where every task is in the contract the loss collapses onto the weights themselves, and the problem has moved from multitask to measurement design rather than been solved.

## 2. Limited liability, risk shifting, and the case that refutes the remedy

The separation of ownership from control (Berle & Means 1932) lets rent extraction pass as performance contracting (Bebchuk & Fried 2004). Innes (1990) proved that an agent choosing effort ex ante behind limited liability is optimally paid by a live-or-die contract: debt for the principal and a steep call above a threshold for the agent, provided the principal's payoff stays non-decreasing in output so that sabotage is excluded. The live-or-die profile is convex above the threshold as a property of the option payoff, not of Innes's theorem. Jensen and Meckling (1976) established what that convexity does to behaviour: the holder of a convex claim prefers a mean-preserving spread (Taleb 2018), keeps the upside of a debt-funded gamble and hands liquidation to creditors, employees and taxpayers. The prescribed instrument is deferral and clawback under a real-time and ex-post gate. The lender's side explains why the rate does not clear the market: raising it drives the safer borrowers out and pushes the rest into riskier projects, so return falls as price rises and quantity is rationed (Stiglitz & Weiss 1981).

Long-Term Capital Management is the case where that instrument was already in place. In 1998 the partners held a claim linear in the fund's equity, unprotected by limited liability against their own capital and junior to every creditor. With roughly 125 billion dollars of assets against 4.7 billion of equity (Lowenstein 2000), the equity cushion was the reciprocal of a leverage near 26 to 1, so a single ordinary adverse move of that size against total assets absorbed the equity: $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}}$ was one bad day while $\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{recovery}}$ was however long the positions took to unwind. No payoff shape survives a margin that thin, and the layer's own `(1, 0, 2)` says why. Concavity changes who is paid in which state, and the states were exhausted before the shape of the claim could matter.

The mirror case is worth naming: where the agent can shift blame onto a variable it does not control, the measured loss falls while the true one rises, and the board reads the first number as the second.

Two further mechanisms carry the same structure. Sloan (1996) documented the accrual anomaly, where high accruals relative to cash flow predict negative subsequent returns, which is revenue accelerated to hit a bonus benchmark. Lean production removes the buffer $(1 - \rho)$ as waste, and Kingman's heavy-traffic formula makes recovery queue length diverge as utilisation approaches one, so buffer and queue are one quantity read at two ends of the plant.

## 3. The collapses, and where the thesis stops

The signature omits $S\_{\mathrm{vital}}$, so the collapses are its absence. Without $S\_{\mathrm{epi}}$ the board sizes pay against unadjusted accruals and off-balance-sheet contingencies, rewarding paper profit: Blind Undersizing. Without $S\_{\mathrm{vital}}$ the inventory buffer and idle capacity are cut to lift return on invested capital and the first supply-chain shock stops the plant, with $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} < \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{shock}}$: Impotent Lucidity Before Ruin. Without $S\_{\mathrm{gov}}$ the executive buys back stock with debt to lift earnings per share, spends the research reserve, and leaves with guaranteed severance while subordinates are held in an inescapable double bind: Cynical Capture and Pillaging.

The thesis is that intensity on a measured task crowds out effort on an unmeasured one, with $\sigma\_2^2$ a property of the technology. It expires when the agent chooses the measurement technology, because then $\sigma\_2^2$ is an action and the comparative static in $\beta\_1$ is not identified: the agent paid on $a\_1$ also decides how precisely $a\_1$ and $a\_2$ are recorded. The symptom is in the archive, where an earnings column piles a large share of its records on exactly zero and another large share on a single top-coded value, with a 99th percentile a small multiple of the median. A large part of the sample therefore rests on one of two endpoints, the observed convexity of pay is set by the reporting rule, and any test of the Innes mechanism run on that column would defeat itself. The procedure is to treat the meter as a third action: estimate convexity on settlement data, state the truncation point of every series, and re-run the comparison with the meter's precision as a choice variable — the observability problem Holmström (1979) named.
