## 1. Loss ratios and the three standards

A trial decides before the past is known, so the standard of proof is a price ratio rather than a quantity of evidence. Blackstone (1765–1769) states it as ten guilty escaping for one innocent suffering. The expected-regret threshold returns $p^\ast = c/(c + l)$ for the two non-negative error terms, so a jurisdiction pricing a wrongful conviction ten times the cost of an acquittal convicts at the odds ten to one, and pricing it a hundred times convicts at a hundred to one. This is the expected-regret form, not the cost-loss form $C/(\eta L)$ of a protection budget against an efficiency-discounted loss. The two coincide only on a thin set, and legal standards are read through the first.

Both ends of the form are visible by hand. As the cost of a wrongful conviction shrinks toward zero the threshold goes to zero and a jurisdiction convicts on essentially no evidence; as the cost of an acquittal shrinks toward zero the threshold goes to one and evidence that would satisfy any juror is formally insufficient. The quantity that behaves linearly is the odds form, $p^\ast/(1 - p^\ast) = L\_{\mathrm{FP}}/L\_{\mathrm{FN}}$, which is what a likelihood ratio has to clear. With prior odds $O\_0$ and evidence of weight $\mathrm{LR}$ the posterior odds are $O\_0 \cdot \mathrm{LR}$, so conviction under a fixed odds rule needs the product to exceed that rule. A suspect drawn from a small pool needs a small likelihood ratio; the same suspect drawn from a large pool needs one scaled by the size of the pool. The standard never states how strong the evidence is in itself, only how strong it is relative to the field of suspects.

## 2. The prosecutor's fallacy and the archive a conviction rested on

The fallacy confuses $\mathbb{P}(E \mid I)$ with $\mathbb{P}(I \mid E)$, and Bayes' rule corrects it. In the 1999 conviction of Sally Clark the paediatric witness put an unexplained infant death in an affluent non-smoking household at one in 8,543 and squared it to one in 73 million, telling the court that two cot deaths had that probability (Royal Statistical Society 2001). The calculation assumed independence between consecutive deaths in one family and ignored the prior odds, under which double infant homicide is rarer than double SIDS. The Royal Statistical Society's statement called the figure baseless and named the fallacy, and the conviction it had supported was quashed on appeal (R v Clark [2003] EWCA Crim 1020). The demand that follows is a calibrated likelihood ratio $\mathrm{LR} = \mathbb{P}(E \mid H\_p)/\mathbb{P}(E \mid H\_d)$ audited in blind proficiency trials, which is where PCAST (2016) pointed when it required comparison methods to be objective and validated outside the laboratories that use them.

The same form breaks at the level of a laboratory rather than a case. Between 2003 and 2012 a chemist at the William A. Hinton State Laboratory in Massachusetts tainted and misreported samples. The state's highest court first granted every defendant whose drug certificate she had signed a conclusive presumption of egregious government misconduct, while still requiring each to show that knowledge of it would have changed the plea (Massachusetts SJC 2014). When the volume of individual motions made that route unworkable, the court declined to vacate every conviction at once and instead ordered the district attorneys to identify the cases they could not reprosecute without the tainted evidence and move to dismiss them; the dismissals entered under that order in April 2017 numbered $21{,}587$ (Massachusetts SJC 2017). The form applies without strain: once the error rate is estimated at population level the likelihood ratio of the certificates collapses toward one and every affected posterior falls back to the prior. The remedy it suggests — recompute each posterior and retry the cases below the threshold — is nonetheless wrong, and arithmetically so. The flag it produces is a screening instrument applied to a population in which the compromised share is high. The flag is sensitive and it also fires on a visible fraction of the clean records, so the predictive value of a flag stays near certainty and the clean convictions are file by file indistinguishable from the compromised ones. A threshold is a rule about one defendant; a corrupted archive is a fact about all of them.

## 3. Fairness, the collapse, and where the thesis stops

Chouldechova (2017) proved that when base re-arrest rates differ across groups ($p\_A \ne p\_B$), a score satisfying both equal positive predictive value ($\mathrm{PPV}\_A = \mathrm{PPV}\_B$) and equal false-negative rate ($\mathrm{FNR}\_A = \mathrm{FNR}\_B$, equivalently $\mathrm{TPR}\_A = \mathrm{TPR}\_B$) must produce unequal false-positive rates, with $\mathrm{FPR}\_A/\mathrm{FPR}\_B$ equal to the base-rate odds ratio $\frac{p\_A/(1-p\_A)}{p\_B/(1-p\_B)}$. The identity behind it is one line. Within each group,

$$\mathrm{FPR} = \frac{p}{1-p}\cdot\frac{1-\mathrm{PPV}}{\mathrm{PPV}}\cdot(1-\mathrm{FNR}),$$

so holding the last two factors equal across groups leaves the first, and the false-positive ratio is the odds ratio of the base rates whatever the score. The instrument that set off the debate shows the pattern in its published error rates: among Broward County defendants who did not reoffend within two years, $44.9$ percent of Black defendants against $23.5$ percent of white defendants had been labelled higher risk, while among those who did reoffend $47.7$ percent of white defendants against $28.0$ percent of Black defendants had been labelled lower risk (Larson, Mattu, Kirchner & Angwin 2016). The vendor's reply was that the score was calibrated within groups, and the identity says both parties could be right at once. The base rate entering the identity is itself a reading of an arrest archive, so stratifying the archive by employment or neighbourhood changes $p$ in each cell and changes the forced ratio with it: the disparity a court is shown depends on which partition of the defendants it is shown, and no partition is neutral.

The collapses follow the missing axis. Without $S\_{\mathrm{epi}}$ the court admits a technique with an unmeasured error rate and treats a subjective impression as proof: Blind Undersizing. Without $S\_{\mathrm{vital}}$ an under-resourced defender office and an overloaded docket run at utilisation close to one, pretrial detention outlasts the statutory sentence, and innocent defendants plead to escape the wait: Impotent Lucidity Before Ruin. Without $S\_{\mathrm{gov}}$ withheld Brady material and an unaudited laboratory are buried behind absolute immunity: Cynical Capture and Pillaging.

The thesis is that the standard is a price ratio and conviction a posterior comparison, which requires the posterior to exist and the evidence to admit a calibration. It fails when the error rate of the evidence-producing artifact is not identifiable from the record, because the proficiency trials were never run, the samples were destroyed, or the method was validated only by the examiners who use it. Then $p^\ast$ is not imprecise but undefined, and no further evidence accumulates in its favour. Move the decision up one level instead: test the artifact rather than the case, and where an artifact fails that test decide on an exclusionary rule, which requires no comparison of losses. Before any likelihood ratio is computed the artifact has to be identified, acquired and preserved in an unaltered state, and that stage is audited against an external standard rather than the laboratory's own discretion (ISO/IEC 27037:2012).
