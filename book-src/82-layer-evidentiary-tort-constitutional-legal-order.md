## By the end of this chapter you can

1. Derive the three standards of proof from the expected-regret threshold, separate that form from the cost-loss form, and resolve the Gatecrasher Paradox with Keynes's weight.
2. Show why Learned Hand's inequality fails under convexity and limited liability, and design the two circuits that replace it.
3. Map constitutional pre-commitment, veto structure and whistleblower protection onto the operators of institutional governance, and say when the ratio the threshold needs is endogenous.

## 1. Two thresholds, three standards, and the class that does not aggregate

A trial decides before the past is known, and two thresholds are in play. The cost-loss form compares a protection budget $C$ with an efficiency-discounted avoidable loss $\eta L$, requiring $p^\ast\_{\mathrm{CL}} = C/(\eta L)$. The expected-regret form compares the net cost of a false alarm with the net loss of a missed event and returns $p^\ast\_{\mathrm{PK}} = C\_{\mathrm{FP}}/(C\_{\mathrm{FP}} + L\_{\mathrm{FN}})$, reducing to one-half when the two positive error costs are equal ($C\_{\mathrm{FP}} = L\_{\mathrm{FN}} > 0$). The standards are read in the second: preponderance at parity of the two error costs ($p^\ast\_{\mathrm{PK}} = 1/2$), clear and convincing when a false sanction is priced at an intermediate multiple $k > 1$ of a false dismissal ($p^\ast\_{\mathrm{PK}} = k/(k + 1)$), and beyond a reasonable doubt at Blackstone's ten-to-one ratio ($p^\ast\_{\mathrm{PK}} = 10/11$; Blackstone 1765–1769).

A probability above the threshold is never sufficient on its own. In L. Jonathan Cohen's Gatecrasher Paradox (Cohen 1977), a rodeo shows that $1{,}000$ people sat in the stands while $499$ tickets were sold, so $501$ spectators entered without paying and a randomly picked spectator is a gatecrasher with probability $p = 0.501$, yet no court awards on naked class frequency. The reason is Keynes's weight $V(n\_{\mathrm{eff}}) = n\_{\mathrm{eff}}/(n\_{\mathrm{eff}} + n\_0)$ (Keynes 1921): without case-specific evidence the individualized count tends to zero and the weight follows it. Condemning a person on group prevalence also violates the disparity bound a fairness audit imposes. The same arithmetic generalises the paradox. A more-likely-than-not standard applied to symmetric independent defendants condemns each of them defensibly while the total award runs past the loss, so a standard correct defendant by defendant is wrong class by class; where liability is correlated the multiplication fails and the joint loss has to be allocated.

## 2. Tort liability and the two circuits

Learned Hand's negligence test in *United States v. Carroll Towing Co.* (1947) is $B < PL$, the cost-loss threshold at $\eta = 1$ with $C = B$: a duty-of-care statement about a protection budget, not a comparison of two classification errors. Its virtue is that a burden below the probability-weighted loss requires care and a burden above it does not, which makes the comparison arguable in public.

It fails in practice twice. Evaluating $PL$ at the point expectation ignores Jensen's convexity gap $\Delta\_J = \mathbb{E}[\phi(X)] - \phi(\mathbb{E}[X])$ and the heavy tail behind it. Capping collectible liability at equity $W < L$ makes the private expected cost $P\min(W, L)$, and a truncated claim is then spent on variance rather than banked — the asset substitution Jensen and Meckling (1976) derived for a limited-liability equity claim, with Innes (1990) supplying the contract half.

The remedy splits into two circuits. Harmed third parties are compensated immediately from an ex-ante capitalised reserve even when the operator's shot was adroit and unlucky, while punitive damages, bonus clawbacks and licence revocation attach only to the reckless cases that got lucky or were inapt. The split is what blocks the scapegoating inversion, in which a frontline operator is blamed, at a large multiple in damages, for the undersizing an executive chose.

## 3. Constitution and statute as circuit breakers

A constitution is Elster's (1979) pre-commitment device, and each clause has an operator. The prohibition of *ex post facto* laws and bills of attainder is pre-outcome sealing plus ex-ante distinguishability, forbidding the state from changing the acceptance rule after the trajectory is seen. Separation of powers with bicameral and judicial vetoes is a conjunctive gate, premise-rigging detection and nested tri-branch coupling. Whistleblower protection and the right to refuse unsafe work are a testimonial discount fixed at zero, carried on an uncompromised algedonic channel. Statutes on automated decisions prescribe process rather than models. A duty to inform is satisfied by a declared envelope. A right to intervention is a half-open state that is real only if the review completes inside the window where the decision remains reversible. The standard applied to the artifact is Hand's comparison run on the cost of measuring the deployed error rate against the harm of the errors it makes. A statute with a deadline and a named artifact can be failed, which is what makes it a circuit rather than a norm.

A right that may be limited needs a test for when the limit is licensed, and constitutional courts converged on proportionality: a
measure restricting a right must pursue a legitimate end, be suitable to it, be necessary in that no less restrictive means would serve as well,
and be proportionate in the strict sense, its gain to the competing principle outweighing its cost to the right. Alexy (2002) gave the last step
an explicit form, the weight formula, in which the concrete weight of principle $i$ against principle $j$ is

$$W\_{i,j} = \frac{I\_i \cdot W\_i \cdot R\_i}{I\_j \cdot W\_j \cdot R\_j},$$

with $I$ the intensity of interference, $W$ the abstract weight and $R$ the reliability of the empirical premises on each side. The reliability
term is the chapter's epistemic margin written into constitutional reasoning: a restriction justified by a premise that is merely plausible
carries less weight than one justified by a premise that is established, so a state that cannot measure the harm it claims to prevent has
weakened its own case in the formula. The first three steps are gates and the fourth is a comparison, which is the conjunctive structure of the
acceptance predicate in legal dress.

Entrenchment is the same comparison run across time. A clause that can be amended only by a supermajority $q > 1/2$ is protected by an
interval, not a point. Put the members' ideal positions on one dimension with distribution $F$ and single-peaked preferences; a status quo $x$
can be displaced to the right only by a coalition of the members whose ideal lies to the right of it, at most a share $1 - F(x)$, and to the
left only by at most $F(x)$, so $x$ survives exactly when both shares fall short of $q$:

$$F^{-1}(1 - q) < x < F^{-1}(q).$$

At $q = 1/2$ the interval shrinks to the median and the rule tracks the current majority; as $q$ rises the interval widens, every status quo
inside it is stable, and which one obtains depends on where the clause was when it was written. That dependence on history is the
hysteresis the rule buys on purpose. Buchanan and Tullock (1962) priced the trade: a more inclusive rule lowers the external cost a decision can
impose on those outvoted and raises the cost of reaching any decision at all. A constitution that sets $q$ high has chosen a wide band of
protected positions and accepted that a clause found wrong inside the band will stay wrong until a coalition of that size forms.

## 4. The form at both ends, and the case where it is applied correctly

The cost-loss form leaves the domain of a probability at one end: a budget above the discounted loss requires a probability greater than one, which is the signature that the harm cannot be expressed in this unit, while a budget below it requires a fraction of one. The two forms coincide on a thin set. Solve for the false-alarm cost, $C\_{\mathrm{FP}} = \frac{C}{\eta L - C}L\_{\mathrm{FN}}$, which is positive only while the protection budget stays below the discounted loss. Keynes's weight behaves the same way at its ends: with a fixed prior count the weight rises with the individualized count along a curve that is steep at the start and flat near one, so the first increments of evidence buy most of the certainty and the last increment is asymptotic.

*Grimshaw v. Ford Motor Co.* is remembered as the case where the form was applied correctly and the recommendation was wrong, and the record is
more instructive than the legend. The worksheet usually quoted is Grush and Saunby's (1973) submission to the federal regulator on a proposed
rollover fuel-leakage standard for the whole industry, not a Pinto rear-impact study: it priced the fix at $\unicode{x24}11$ per vehicle across $11$ million
cars and $1.5$ million light trucks, or $\unicode{x24}137$ million, against benefits of $180$ burn deaths at $\unicode{x24}200{,}000$, $180$ injuries at $\unicode{x24}67{,}000$
and $2{,}100$ burned vehicles at $\unicode{x24}700$, totalling $36 + 12.06 + 1.47 = \unicode{x24}49.53$ million (Grush & Saunby 1973; Dowie 1977). Schwartz (1991)
showed that the per-death figure was the regulator's own societal-cost estimate and not a price the manufacturer invented for its customers. That
correction makes the case harder, not softer. Since $B = 137 > PL = 49.53$, Hand's inequality, fed the most defensible ex-ante valuation then
on offer, says the precaution was not required. The jury in the Pinto case nevertheless returned a punitive award of $\unicode{x24}125$ million, some $625$
times the worksheet's figure per death, which the trial court remitted to $\unicode{x24}3.5$ million and the appellate court sustained (California Court
of Appeal 1981). The arithmetic is not the error, and neither is the provenance of the number. $L$ in $B < PL$ is the loss the law will
recognise after the harm, read by a tribunal that sees a known hazard and a cheap remedy, and an administrative price of life set before the
harm is a different quantity under the same letter. A precaution rule that imports $L$ from any party's table, the regulator's included, has
answered the question the tribunal will not ask, so $L$ must be bounded from the record of awards that the bearer of the loss can obtain.

## 5. The collapse, and where the thesis stops

The collapses are the missing sites. Without $S\_{\mathrm{epi}}$ a decision rests on group base rates at near-zero individualized weight while the selection bias of the arrest archive goes unread: Blind Undersizing. Without $S\_{\mathrm{vital}}$ docket congestion delays the preliminary injunction until the harm is irreversible: Impotent Lucidity Before Ruin. Without $S\_{\mathrm{gov}}$ limited-liability shells cap the downside while the night-shift operator is prosecuted and denied standing to halt: Cynical Capture and Pillaging.

The thesis assumes the loss ratio is exogenous, that whoever applies the threshold does not choose its inputs. It fails when the two error costs and the evidence come from one actor — an expert retained by the defendant, a regulator staffed from the industry, an insurer that both prices and audits — and then a hand-derived threshold carries no more authority than the party that set it. A partial-identification bound is the instrument already available. It returns bounds rather than a point whenever the two numbers come from one noisy source, so the procedure is to identify the producer of each input, replace the point with that interval, decide where it lies wholly on one side of the admissible set, and where it straddles take the decision by a rule requiring no comparison at all.
