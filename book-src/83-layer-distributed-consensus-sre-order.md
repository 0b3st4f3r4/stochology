## 1. What can be agreed before an action is taken

Picture a fleet of replicated database servers hit by a sudden network brownout. Packets vanish between data centers, leaving nodes unable to distinguish a crashed partner from a slow one, while impatient clients keep retrying until the traffic flood keeps the cluster pinned down even after the wire heals. Distributed systems and site reliability engineering govern that race between consensus safety, storage pauses, retry storms, and emergency shutdowns.

### Guard T5 (Temporal): Bounding quorum waits, storage stalls, and partition recovery clocks

In distributed computing, `T5Temporal` ($T\_5$) protects survival slack so waiting for consensus, disk compaction, or memory repair never exceeds recovery time.

Three core limits govern distributed systems mediating action and records across the three planes ([Aim and the Three Planes](29-aim-and-the-three-planes.md)) under clock pressure ([What Judging Is, and What It Is Not](27-what-judging-is.md)). First, Fischer, Lynch and Paterson (1985) prove that no deterministic consensus protocol in an asynchronous network with even one unannounced crash guarantees both safety and termination. Because slow nodes look identical to dead ones, only randomization or partial synchrony breaks that impasse.

Second, Castro and Liskov (1999) separate asynchronous safety from weak-synchrony liveness under Byzantine faults across $n \ge 3f + 1 > 0$ replicas ($f \in \mathbb{Z}\_{\ge 1}$). Quorums of size $n - f \ge 2f + 1$ overlap in at least $n - 2f \ge f + 1 > f$ nodes, guaranteeing at least one honest replica in every intersection to block equivocation (while honest replicas form a strict majority inside each size-$(2f + 1)$ quorum), while liveness stalls if over $f$ replicas partition. By contrast, simple crash-fault consensus requires $n = 2f + 1$ replicas (quorum $f + 1$).

Waiting for slow consensus pushes deliberation past survival slack ($\tau\_{\mathrm{bureaucratic}} > \mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}}$), violating Reflexive Brake 3 ([Chapter 50](50-methodological-families-reflexivity.md)). Normal actuation may require conjunctive consensus, whereas emergency braking must fire asymmetrically inside $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}}$, trading quorum latency against $1$-of-$N$ spurious-trip risk unless filtered sequentially ([Moral Luck, Apt Agency, and Institutional Design](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md)). Separating ex-ante commitments (*télos*) from realized outcomes (*skopós*; [Layer 01](51-layer-micro-kinetic-sensorimotor-order.md)), Lamport's happens-before relation (Lamport 1978) enforces a strict partial order for bitemporal reads without future contamination, while Merkle commitments ([Layer 10](60-layer-cryptographic-zero-knowledge-adversarial-order.md)) seal post-seal custody without preventing prior omissions.

Consensus logs also depend on physical storage latency (`T5Temporal`, `T7Liability`). Write-ahead logging (`WAL`) imposes disk flush barriers for `ACID` durability (Haerder & Reuter 1983), `B+-tree` page splits create random writes (Comer 1979), and `LSM-tree` compaction trades **read, write, and space amplification** for sudden tail stalls (O'Neil et al. 1996). Because in-place overwrites destroy history ([Chapter 34](34-sample-population-archive.md)), `T7Liability` pairs unkeyed content digests against silent bit-rot with append-only bitemporal logs (Snodgrass 2000) authenticated via keyed `RFC 2104` `HMAC-SHA256` (Krawczyk, Bellare, & Canetti 1997) or asymmetric threshold signatures (`FROST`; [Layer 10](60-layer-cryptographic-zero-knowledge-adversarial-order.md)) under external multi-party witness key custody and independent timestamp anchoring ([Moral Luck, Apt Agency, and Institutional Design](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md)), catching post-$t\_{\mathrm{disp}}$ tampering and blocking key-holder equivocation while still missing pre-seal gaps. Down in DRAM hardware, RowHammer flips occurring inside the $64\text{ ms}$ refresh window ($110$ of $129$ commercial modules; Mutlu & Kim 2019) require hardware refresh and multi-bit ECC alongside [Layer 10](60-layer-cryptographic-zero-knowledge-adversarial-order.md) attestation.

Rust's affine ownership ([Layer 06](56-layer-formal-computable-type-order.md)) protects safe code, yet consensus and C/C++ storage `FFI` engines cross `unsafe` boundaries where memory-layout and data-race bugs bypass static checks (Xu et al. 2021). Hybrid storage engines therefore pair static types with asynchronous AddressSanitizer (`ASan`) and ThreadSanitizer (`TSan`) audits outside live operational queues across `unsafe`/`FFI` seams (`T5Temporal`), strictly off the $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}}$ critical path.

### Coordinator-free 1-hop Byzantine resilience and hybrid CRDT coupling

When wide-area $n \ge 3f + 1$ consensus exceeds survival latency $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}}$, local neighborhood graphs $\mathcal{G}$ bound by cryptographic or hardware-rooted identity ([Layer 10](60-layer-cryptographic-zero-knowledge-adversarial-order.md)) against Sybil whitewashing can substitute, provided Menger vertex connectivity $\kappa\_v(\mathcal{G}) \ge f + 1$ preserves post-quarantine non-eclipse ([Layer 03](53-layer-macro-coupled-topology-order.md)). Quarantine-only coordination operates at $n \ge 2f + 2$ ($f \ge 1$, minimum degree $\delta(\mathcal{G}) \ge 2f + 1$), whereas adding asymmetric non-self rehabilitation raises the graph floor to $n \ge 2f + 3$ and $\delta(\mathcal{G}) \ge 2f + 2$ ([Appendix A](appendix-a-mathematics.md) §A.6). Both regimes rely on three primitives. First, **cross-witnessing medians** evaluate peer health solely from at least $m \ge 2f + 1$ active non-self signed neighbor receipts, guaranteeing lower-median immunity while excluding self-reports. Second, **zero-delay degree-consistency and equivocation proofs** convert verifiable conflicting epoch signatures or under-reported neighbor degrees into self-verifying fraud certificates.

Third, **topological collusion bounds** separate quarantine fraud safety from rehabilitation recovery so exiled nodes cannot deadlock. Anti-calumny safety ($q\_{\mathrm{fraud}} \ge f + 1$) and closed-neighborhood liveness ($\text{self} + \delta(\mathcal{G}) - f \ge q\_{\mathrm{fraud}}$) fix the fraud-quorum admissibility band $q\_{\mathrm{fraud}} \in [f + 1,\; \delta(\mathcal{G}) - f + 1]$. Because quarantined nodes have zero probationary voting weight and cannot self-vote, rehabilitation admits active non-self neighbor voters only, restricting recovery quorums to $q\_{\mathrm{rehab}} \in [q\_{\mathrm{fraud}} + 1,\; \delta(\mathcal{G}) - f]$. That rehabilitation band is non-empty for $q\_{\mathrm{fraud}} \le \delta(\mathcal{G}) - f - 1$ only once $\delta(\mathcal{G}) \ge 2f + 2$ and $n \ge 2f + 3$ (while preserving $\kappa\_v(\mathcal{G}) \ge f + 1$ beyond §1's $n \ge 3f + 1$ global BFT limit), and quorum thresholds must be dynamically re-evaluated after joins or decentralized peer rewiring (`HyParView`; Leitão, Pereira, & Rodrigues 2007). For large sparse graphs, $f$-local consensus preserves resilience when the topology is at least $(2f + 1)$-robust ($r \ge 2f + 1$; LeBlanc et al. 2013).

Across asynchronous partitions, continuous reserve-state trajectories $s(\xi)$ pair with a causal-conservative conflict-free replicated join-semilattice (`CRDT`; Shapiro et al. 2011) that reconciles epoch minima only under quorum-attested reserve proofs ([Layer 10](60-layer-cryptographic-zero-knowledge-adversarial-order.md)) to block zero-poisoning and tracks spent single-use linear authorization tokens and revocation tombstones in a monotone union (`G-Set`) semilattice (acknowledging that disconnected partitions still permit bounded local double-spend unless budgets are pre-sharded or escrow-capped). Under smooth piecewise-differentiable trajectories $s(\xi)$ on $[T\_0, t]$ without unlogged discrete resets, $1$-Lipschitzness of $M\_{\mathrm{Common}}$ with respect to $\|\cdot\|\_\infty$, and propagation delays uniformly bounded in $[0, D\_{\max}]$ ($D\_{\max} > 0$, post-partition stabilization origin $T\_0 \ge 0$), unit $i$'s local estimate $\widehat{M}\_i(t)$ satisfies $|\widehat{M}\_i(t) - M\_{\mathrm{Common}}(t)| \le D\_{\max}\sup\_{\xi \in [t - D\_{\max}, t]} \|\dot{s}(\xi)\|\_\infty$ for all $t \ge T\_0 + D\_{\max}$. When synchronous links stay severed longer, delay-tolerant store-carry-forward custody (`DTN`; Fall 2003; [Chapter 26](26-regulation-and-homeostasis.md)) bridges local `WAL` and `CRDT` state without erasing peaceful lineage branching.

Under asynchronous splits, conflicting readings across partitioned replicas map naturally onto Belnap–Dunn's `Both` state (Dunn 1976; Belnap 1977) and paraconsistent local containment (da Costa 1974; Carnielli & Coniglio 2016). Rather than trivializing downstream queries or silently discarding dissent through *last-write-wins*, the causal-conservative `CRDT` resolves concurrent epochs by component-wise minimum on reserves and emits self-verifying equivocation certificates until quorum reconciliation or `DTN` custody restores local consistency ($\circ A$).

## 2. Tail amplification, quorums and budgets in series

Consider a user request fanning out across $m \ge 1$ independent servers, finishing only when the slowest server responds. If each node incurs an independent tail latency spike with probability $\varepsilon \in (0, 1)$, composite spike probability $p(m)$ obeys:

$$p(m) = 1 - (1 - \varepsilon)^m \le m\varepsilon$$

by Bernoulli's inequality (Dean & Barroso 2013). Read aloud, the probability that at least one of $m$ parallel nodes hits a tail delay grows almost linearly with fan-out $m$ for small $\varepsilon$, while clean all-fast survival $(1 - \varepsilon)^m$ decays exponentially. Shared power rails, network switches, or garbage-collection pauses break independence, while hedged retries raise background load near saturation.

Queueing physics turns those spikes into lasting outages. Take stationary $GI/G/1$ interior utilisation $\rho \in (0, 1)$ ($1 - \rho > 0$) with mean service time $T\_s > 0$ and finite second moments ($0 \le c\_a^2, c\_s^2 < \infty$, $\alpha > 2$; [Layer 02](52-layer-meso-buffered-queue-order.md)). Kingman's factor $\rho/(1 - \rho)$ then governs expected queue wait $\mathbb{E}[W\_q] \approx \frac{\rho}{1 - \rho}\frac{c\_a^2 + c\_s^2}{2}T\_s$. Once delays trip client timeouts, endogenous retries make arrival rates depend on waiting time ($\lambda'(W\_q) > 0$), breaking renewal independence. Effective utilisation climbs to $\rho\_{\mathrm{eff}} \ge 1$ while $T\_s$ degrades, locking metastable overload even after normal traffic returns (Bronson et al. 2021).

Empirical outage forensics confirm how often retries sustain cascading collapses. Huang et al. (2022) examined $22$ metastable failures across $11$ organisations, including at least $4$ of $15$ major Amazon Web Services outages over the decade. Roughly $45$ percent followed engineer errors (configuration, code changes, latent bugs) and $35$ percent followed load spikes, with $45$ percent involving multiple triggers (Huang et al. 2022). Outages lasted $1.5$ to $73.53$ hours ($4$ to $10$ hours most common at $35$ percent), and retries sustained over $50$ percent of incidents (Huang et al. 2022).

Replicated safety and series reliability budgets follow tight bounds. Weak-synchrony Byzantine safety with standard authenticators needs $2f + 1$ attestations out of $n \ge 3f + 1$ replicas ($f \in \mathbb{Z}\_{\ge 1}$). That architecture adds $\Delta n / \Delta f = 3$ marginal replicas per fault (Castro & Liskov 1999). Because the ratio $3 + 1/f > 3$, fault tolerance approaches $1/3$ and quorum reachability approaches $2/3$. That requirement drops to $n = 2f + 1$ (marginal cost $2$, asymptote $1/2$) only under a crash-fault model or trusted anti-equivocation hardware restricting faults to omissions. Unauthenticated Byzantine consensus cannot drop from $3f + 1$ to $2f + 1$ via synchrony alone.

Across $k \ge 1$ serial services with individual unavailabilities $u\_i \in (0, 1)$ and availabilities $A\_i = 1 - u\_i$, independent composition gives series availability $A\_{\mathrm{series}}$ and union downtime bound:

$$A\_{\mathrm{series}} = \prod\_{i=1}^k (1 - u\_i), \qquad 1 - A\_{\mathrm{series}} \le \sum\_{i=1}^k u\_i.$$

Read aloud, total availability multiplies across $k$ independent serial links, while total downtime cannot exceed the sum of individual component downtimes. That union bound holds without independence ($1 - (1 - u)^k \approx ku$ when $ku \ll 1$). Now hold system target $A\_{\mathrm{target}} = 1 - u\_{\mathrm{target}}$ (budget $B = u\_{\mathrm{target}} T\_{\mathrm{win}}$ over window $T\_{\mathrm{win}} > 0$) across $k$ serial links: independent-link sizing caps each at $u\_i \le 1 - A\_{\mathrm{target}}^{1/k} \approx u\_{\mathrm{target}}/k$, dividing error budgets linearly by $k$. Shared zones or control planes ($\rho\_{\mathrm{cc}} > 0$) push these regimes in opposite directions: overlapping outages bring serial chains closer to $\min\_i A\_i$, whereas across parallel active-active backups shared causes collapse diversity from $1 - \prod\_{i=1}^k u\_i$ back toward $\min\_i A\_i$ ([Layer 34](84-layer-infrastructure-nuclear-hydraulic-safety-order.md)). Availability does not compose. It is spent.

Peak demand requires sizing headroom to peaks rather than averages. Under peak arrival rate $\lambda\_{\mathrm{peak}} > \bar{\lambda} > 0$, provisioning for mean utilisation $\bar{\rho} = \bar{\lambda} T\_s \in (0, 1)$ pushes peak utilisation to $\rho\_{\mathrm{peak}} = (\lambda\_{\mathrm{peak}}/\bar{\lambda})\bar{\rho} > \bar{\rho}$, driving $\rho/(1 - \rho) = (1 - \rho)^{-1} - 1$ toward divergence as $\rho\_{\mathrm{peak}} \to 1^-$ and locking non-stationary overload whenever $\rho\_{\mathrm{peak}} \ge 1$. Sizing to Kingman's unit-multiplier cutoff $\rho\_{\mathrm{peak}} \le 1/2$ ($\rho/(1 - \rho) \le 1$) limits queueing delay before fan-out or compaction spikes amplify tails ([Layer 02](52-layer-meso-buffered-queue-order.md)).

Formal continuous-time Markov chain (`CTMC`) retry-orbit models over queue occupancy $u \le K\_{\mathrm{buf}}$ and retry orbit $v$ generalize Bronson et al.'s (2021) trap to replicated storage (Alvaro et al. 2025; Habibi et al. 2024). With service rate $\mu\_0 = 1/T\_s > 0$ and timeout $\tau\_{\mathrm{to}} > 0$, uncapped retries (`MSF-Model`; Habibi et al. 2024) drive non-stationary overload once timeout probability approaches $1$. Under finite retry caps ($m\_{\mathrm{ret}} < \infty$), the chain stalls near `High` overload ($|\mathrm{Re}(\nu\_2)| \ll 1$, worsened by larger $K\_{\mathrm{buf}}$), requiring admission throttling $\lambda\_r < \mu\_0$ below the lower threshold to drain $(u, v)$ back to `Low` (Alvaro et al. 2025).

## 3. Circuit breakers, budgets, and what operations does with them

### Guard T6 (Performative): Preventing error-budget gaming, retry hysteresis, and fake canaries

In site reliability operations, `T6Performative` ($T\_6$) protects error budgets, automated rollouts, and traffic breakers from metric gaming, recovery hysteresis traps, and partial-deployment partitions.

The three-state circuit breaker maps directly onto the triadic regime table (Nygard 2007). Knight Capital illustrates how a partial rollout turns into an unmonitored network partition rather than a safe canary.
