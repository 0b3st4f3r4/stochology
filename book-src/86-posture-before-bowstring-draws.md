## 6.1.1 Pre-Draw Framing and the Type III Error

Every inquiry opens inside an inherited horizon that pre-selects what counts as normal and what counts as an anomaly (Gadamer 1960). Several traditions test that opening stance before data arrives. Meanwhile, *Kālāma* discipline resists unverified rumor (Bodhi 2012), and Aymara *Qhipnayra* places visible past losses (*nayra*) directly before one's eyes when stepping into the unseen future (*qhipa*) (Núñez & Sweetser 2006; Rivera Cusicanqui 2012)—matching how an archer inspects conditions before release.

Organizations exercise **premise control** over which questions enter the ledger, which side effects drop outside departmental borders, and when deadlines halt deliberation (Simon 1947). Compressing reality under bounded deliberate human throughput (near 10 bits/s, Zheng & Meister 2025; [Chapter 95, *Collision at Ten Bits per Second*](95-collision-at-ten-bits-per-second.md)) steers what gets noticed. Guard `T1` therefore separates transparent **communicative influence**—exposing alternatives and uncertainty—from **strategic manipulation** inside a narrowed maze (Habermas 1984).

When a framing brief trims displaced harm to flatter a narrow indicator, it commits Kimball's (1957) **Type III error**: solving the wrong problem cleanly. If spillover onto external reception perimeters ($\Theta\_{\mathrm{basin}}$) outweighs focal gains, global net gain $\Delta U\_{\mathrm{global}}$ turns negative. That displaced collapse stays strictly inside ex-post $\mathcal{D}\_{\mathrm{diag}}$ under Reflexive Brake 4 ([Chapter 32, *The Question Before the Data*](32-the-question-before-the-data.md); [Chapter 41, *The Triad and the Minimum*](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md); [Chapter 50, *Five Methodological Families, Process Tracing, and Reflexive Self-Application*](50-methodological-families-reflexivity.md)), never steering live steps.

## 6.1.3 Inter-Judge Noise vs. Epistemic Door Bias

Consider grading loan files or biopsy slides: adding reviewers shrinks disagreements between individual judges, yet a slanted rubric built into the door shifts everyone alike and goes unseen. The crossed random-effects model below proves that even an infinite panel removes personal scatter while leaving shared door bias intact.

Let $N\_{\mathrm{case}}$ cases ($i = 1, \dots, N\_{\mathrm{case}}$) be graded by $J \ge 1$ judges ($j = 1, \dots, J$) across $R \ge 1$ rating occasions ($r = 1, \dots, R$, distinct from recording indicator $R$). Decompose each rating error $\varepsilon\_{ijr}$ into five additive pieces. First comes constant door bias $b\_{\mathrm{door}}$ induced by the question itself, alongside zero-mean systematic case residual $\eta\_i$ ($\sigma\_{\mathrm{sys}}^2 \ge 0$). The remaining three pieces capture judge severity shift $\alpha\_j$ ($\sigma\_{\mathrm{level}}^2 \ge 0$, distinct from $\alpha\_{\mathrm{FDR}}$), judge-by-case interaction $\gamma\_{ij}$ ($\sigma\_{\mathrm{pattern}}^2 \ge 0$), and occasion noise $e\_{ijr}$ ($\sigma\_{\mathrm{occasion}}^2 \ge 0$, distinct from *e*-values):

$$\varepsilon\_{ijr} = b\_{\mathrm{door}} + \eta\_i + \alpha\_j + \gamma\_{ij} + e\_{ijr}.$$

With mutually uncorrelated zero-mean random effects, total centered dispersion variance sums all four variance components:

$$\sigma\_{\mathrm{total}}^2 = \sigma\_{\mathrm{sys}}^2 + \sigma\_{\mathrm{level}}^2 + \sigma\_{\mathrm{pattern}}^2 + \sigma\_{\mathrm{occasion}}^2.$$

Averaging $J$ independent judges across $R$ occasions for case $i$ yields $\bar{\varepsilon}\_{i \cdot \cdot} = b\_{\mathrm{door}} + \eta\_i + \frac{1}{J}\sum\_{j=1}^J \alpha\_j + \frac{1}{J}\sum\_{j=1}^J \gamma\_{ij} + \frac{1}{JR}\sum\_{j=1}^J \sum\_{r=1}^R e\_{ijr}$. Conditional on fixed case $i$, the squared systematic error is $(b\_{\mathrm{door}} + \eta\_i)^2$. Taking expectation across cases $i$, judges, and occasions gives the population mean-squared error ($\mathrm{MSE}$):

$$\mathrm{MSE}\bigl(\bar{\varepsilon}\_{i \cdot \cdot}\bigr) = b\_{\mathrm{door}}^2 + \sigma\_{\mathrm{sys}}^2 + \frac{\sigma\_{\mathrm{level}}^2 + \sigma\_{\mathrm{pattern}}^2}{J} + \frac{\sigma\_{\mathrm{occasion}}^2}{JR}.$$

Taking the infinite-panel limit ($J, R \to \infty$, with $J, R \ge 1$) under uncorrelated judge shifts ($\mathrm{Cov}(\alpha\_j, \alpha\_{j'}) = 0$ for $j \ne j'$) isolates what survives:

$$\lim\_{J, R \to \infty} \mathrm{MSE}\bigl(\bar{\varepsilon}\_{i \cdot \cdot}\bigr) = b\_{\mathrm{door}}^2 + \sigma\_{\mathrm{sys}}^2.$$

In plain words, multiplying committee seats and repeat scorings drives random judge and day-to-day noise to zero, yet leaves the crooked door ($b\_{\mathrm{door}}^2$) and case-level ambiguity ($\sigma\_{\mathrm{sys}}^2$) completely untouched.

Two operational failures follow immediately.

**Committee averaging cannot fix a rigged door.** Sending $J, R \to \infty$ zeroes uncorrelated scatter yet leaves $b\_{\mathrm{door}}^2 + \sigma\_{\mathrm{sys}}^2$ undiminished. Worse, if panel members share a common doctrinal tilt ($\mathrm{Cov}(\alpha\_j, \alpha\_{j'}) > 0$), even rater severity shifts fail to cancel.

**Single-rater assignments inflict arbitrary lotteries.** Suppose a frame is completely unbiased ($b\_{\mathrm{door}} = 0$ and $\sigma\_{\mathrm{sys}}^2 = 0$). Even then, any positive rater or occasion scatter ($\sigma\_{\mathrm{level}}^2 + \sigma\_{\mathrm{pattern}}^2 + \sigma\_{\mathrm{occasion}}^2 > 0$) makes indistinguishable cases draw opposite verdicts from bench assignment alone.

Open judicial and clinical archives quantify both rater scatter and diagnostic ambiguity directly across four landmark datasets.

**Pretrial bail leniency (Philadelphia and Miami-Dade).** Across 328,492 Philadelphia cases (172,407 unique defendants) and 97,538 Miami-Dade cases (66,067 unique defendants), Dobbie, Goldin, and Yang (2016) measure residualized pretrial judge leniency spanning $-0.150$ to $0.179$ (SD $0.030$). That bench-assignment spread equals a 32.9 percentage-point release gap around a 55.2 percent three-day release mean.

**Pretrial risk mismatch (New York City).** Across 758,027 New York City pretrial observations, Kleinberg et al. (2018) show judges release 48.5 percent of defendants inside the predicted riskiest 1 percent. That top risk percentile carries a 62.6 percent predicted crime risk, 56.3 percent failure-to-appear rate, and 62.7 percent rearrest rate. Individual judges thus scatter widely around the average risk profile.

**Breast biopsy and melanoma discordance.** In pathology, Elmore et al. (2015) studied 115 pathologists reading 60 slides each across 240 breast biopsy cases (6,900 individual interpretations). Overall concordance with a consensus-derived reference reached 75.3 percent (95 percent CI 73.4 to 77.0 percent), dropping to 48 percent (95 percent CI 44 to 52 percent) for atypia. Among 187 pathologists completing 4,342 round-1 interpretations across 116 invasive melanoma cases, Elmore et al. (2018) found T1a pairwise agreement moved from 41 percent (AJCC 7) to 51 percent (AJCC 8). Among the 118 re-interpreting participants, intraobserver reproducibility shifted from 59 to 64 percent.

**Universal second opinions and the unmeasured door.** Testing universal second opinions across 8,976 initial cutaneous melanocytic reads by 187 pathologists, Piepkorn et al. (2019) show single-reader misclassification falls only from 47.9 percent (95 percent CI 46.7 to 49.1 percent) to 44.8 percent (95 percent CI 42.5 to 47.1 percent). Second reads nearly halved overinterpretation (6.3 to 3.1 percent) while leaving underinterpretation unchanged (41.6 to 41.7 percent). Meanwhile, between-physician agreement rose from 54.8 to 64.3 percent ($\kappa$ $0.42$ to $0.55$).

Because all four studies benchmark readers against peer agreement or three-expert consensus inside one shared diagnostic door, they expose diagnostic penumbra ($\sigma\_{\mathrm{sys}}^2$). They also expose correlated caution ($\mathrm{Cov}(\alpha\_j, \alpha\_{j'}) > 0$), yet they cannot measure shared door bias $b\_{\mathrm{door}}$.
