## 1. Against Domesticating Formalization

At $P\_2 \to P\_3$, formalization squeezes qualitative severity into rigid cutoffs. Theodor W. Adorno called what gets left out *das Nichtidentische*, the non-identical remainder (Adorno 1966). Nicholas Georgescu-Roegen (1971) contrasted crisp sets with dialectical concepts ringed by penumbra $[c\_L, c\_U)$ ($c\_L < c\_U$). Under a binary rule $\mathbf{1}\lbrace X \ge c\rbrace$, danger concentrates inside **penumbra mass** $\mathbb{P}(c\_L \le X < c\_U)$, where nearly identical cases draw opposite verdicts.

Measurement theory (Stevens 1946; Krantz et al. 1971) explains why ordinal composites strip averages of invariant meaning ([Chapter 33, *Measuring and Erring*](33-measuring-and-erring.md), valid operations by scale type), yet empirical Likert studies routinely force metric averages onto ordinal rankings (Liddell & Kruschke 2018). Even calibrated point sums like Virginia Apgar's (1953) newborn screen stay valid only when anchored offline to bounded physiological norms (Canguilhem 1943/1966; [Chapter 46, *The Non-Identical, Scale Invariance, Convexity, and Heavy Tails*](46-non-identical-asymmetry-heavy-tails.md), scale invariance and curved harm) without $T\_6$ gaming.

## 2. Curvature, Proper Scoring, and Heavy-Tail Regimes

Even on clean ratio scales, bare averages fail at $P\_2 \to P\_3$ once losses curve upward ([Chapter 31, *Distribution and Moment*](31-distribution-and-moment.md), diverging moments; [Chapter 46](46-non-identical-asymmetry-heavy-tails.md), heavy-tail survival failure). Johan Jensen's (1906) convexity gap $\Delta\_J(\phi, X) \ge 0$ separates average loss from loss at the average, destabilizing variance whenever Pareto tail index $\alpha \le 4$ ([Chapter 12](12-derivatives-and-convexity.md), convexity and tail amplification). Likewise, the Continuous Ranked Probability Score $\mathrm{CRPS}(F, y)$ (Gneiting & Raftery 2007) is proper across all Borel laws $\mathcal{P}$, yet **strictly proper only on the finite-first-moment subclass $\mathcal{P}\_1$** ($\alpha > 1$; [Chapter 13](13-integrals-and-accumulation.md), tail integrals and proper scoring).

Across Pickands–Balkema–de Haan exceedances (Pickands 1975; Balkema & de Haan 1974), Hill's (1975) conditional tail estimator classifies archives into four Pareto moment regimes: $\alpha \le 1$, $1 < \alpha \le 2$, $2 < \alpha \le 4$, and $\alpha > 4$ (Gnedenko & Kolmogorov 1954; Embrechts, Klüppelberg, & Mikosch 1997). These estimators require offline calibration on pre-registered $k$-grids ([Chapter 31](31-distribution-and-moment.md); [Chapter 46](46-non-identical-asymmetry-heavy-tails.md), power-law diagnostics), as empirical commodity prices (Mandelbrot 1963) and fire claims (McNeil 1997) routinely enter infinite-variance territory ($\alpha \le 2$).

Because Value-at-Risk ($\mathrm{VaR}\_p$) violates subadditivity (Artzner et al. 1999), $T\_4$ relies on coherent Expected Shortfall $\mathrm{CVaR}\_p$ for $\alpha > 1$ (Acerbi & Tasche 2002), inheriting $T\_3$'s unbounded MNAR saturation $\mathrm{CVaR}\_{p,U} = +\infty$ whenever $y\_{\max} = +\infty$ and $f < 1$ ([What the Wind Leaves on Stone](88-what-the-wind-leaves-on-stone.md), missing-data tail ceiling collapse).

## 3. Chart-Conditional Jacobian, Viable-Box Invariance, and Bottleneck Identity

At each step, $T\_4$ writes only $\mathbf{m}\_t^{\mathrm{vital,tail}}$. Retrospective auditing normalizes trajectory minima $s = (s\_1, s\_2, s\_3)$ into unit coordinates $S = (S\_1, S\_2, S\_3) \in [0, 1]^3$ strictly inside ex-post $\mathcal{D}\_{\mathrm{diag}}$ under **Reflexive Brake 4** ([Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md), strict diagnostic separation; [Chapter 50](50-methodological-families-reflexivity.md), preventing dashboards from steering shifts; [Appendix A](appendix-a-mathematics.md) §A.4, unit-cube normalization, and §A.7, reception-perimeter fold). Meanwhile, **Reflexive Brake 2** forbids using infinite variance ($\alpha \le 2$ or $\Delta\_J = \infty$) to hoard buffers past $R^\ast$. Compensatory scores like Saaty's Analytic Hierarchy Process (Saaty 1980) or ICE/RICE heuristics trade shortfalls across axes, breaking informational invariance (Arrow 1950; Sen 1985).

After applying native-to-unit charts $\varphi\_a$, let smooth unit-cube recoding $\psi(S) = \bigl(\psi\_1(S\_1), \psi\_2(S\_2), \psi\_3(S\_3)\bigr)$ act coordinate by coordinate. Each map obeys $\psi\_a \in C^1([0, 1], [0, 1])$, $\psi\_a' > 0$, $\psi\_a(0) \ge 0$, and $\psi\_a(1) = 1$, giving Jacobian determinant $\det J\_\psi(S) = \prod\_{a=1}^{3} \psi\_a'(S\_a) > 0$. Three propositions separate genuine architectural invariants from display artifacts.

### Proposition 1 (Viable-Box Invariance vs. Chart-Conditional Volume)

Before reading the proof, picture three thermometer dials stretched or compressed along their glass without reversing which temperatures sit higher. Proposition 1 proves that rescaling each dial independently leaves yes/no pass thresholds intact: systems clearing every bar before still clear every bar after. Yet it warps the 3D "safe volume" on a dashboard, confusing dial curvature with real physical slack.

Fix a viability threshold $\tau \in (0, 1)$. Strict monotonicity of each $\psi\_a$ commutes with thresholding, mapping $[\tau, 1]^3$ onto $\prod\_{a=1}^{3} [\psi\_a(\tau), 1]$ with pushed-forward Lebesgue volume $\prod\_{a=1}^{3}(1 - \psi\_a(\tau))$ ([Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md), Leontief cube invariance; [Chapter 50](50-methodological-families-reflexivity.md), chart-conditional volume bounds; [Appendix A](appendix-a-mathematics.md) §A.4, monotone box preservation). Leontief orthant membership—and each per-axis boolean check at $\psi\_a(\tau)$—is therefore **invariant** under independent monotone recodings.

By contrast, Lebesgue volume remains chart-conditional. Matching $V\_{\min}(\tau) = (1 - \tau)^3 > 0$ at every $\tau$ holds only when $\prod\_{a=1}^{3}(1 - \psi\_a(\tau)) = (1 - \tau)^3$. Zero-anchored nonlinear bijections such as $\psi\_{1,2}(s) = 1 - (1-s)e^{\pm 0.2s(1-s)}, \psi\_3(s) = s$ achieve that match. Inside the top-anchored restricted affine monoid $\psi\_a(s) = \alpha\_a s + (1 - \alpha\_a)$ ($0 < \alpha\_a \le 1$), volume ratios stay invariant yet exact equality forces $\alpha\_a \equiv 1$. Dashboard area ratios therefore confound curvature with headroom.

In plain words, stretching each axis dial preserves pass/fail viability boxes identically, yet distorts visual volume unless every chart shares exact equality across scales.

### Proposition 2 (Common-Map Stability vs. Asymmetric Bottleneck Inversion)

Now ask: if we want to name which single axis is the *weakest link*, when can we trust that ranking? Proposition 2 proves that rescaling all three axes with the *same* ruler keeps the bottleneck unchanged. Stretching each axis with its *own* curve, however, lets you crown any of the three axes as the worst bottleneck on paper without moving a single physical buffer.

Under one shared strictly increasing map $\psi\_0$ ($\psi\_1 = \psi\_2 = \psi\_3 = \psi\_0$), cross-axis rank holds: $\mathrm{argmin}\_a \psi\_0(S\_a) = \mathrm{argmin}\_a S\_a$. Distinct per-axis normalizations rotate the apparent bottleneck by convention alone. Take concave $\psi\_1(s) = (1 - e^{-4s})/(1 - e^{-4})$, identity $\psi\_2(s) = s$, and convex $\psi\_3(s) = (e^{2s} - 1)/(e^2 - 1)$ on $[0, 1]$ at $S = (0.30, 0.45, 0.60)$:

1. Unscaled, axis 1 (*epi*, $0.30$) sits lowest.
2. Recoding axis 1 via $\psi\_1$ sends $0.30 \mapsto (1 - e^{-1.2})/(1 - e^{-4}) \approx 0.711844$, shifting the minimum to axis 2 (*vital*, $0.45$).
3. Recoding axis 3 via $\psi\_3$ sends $0.60 \mapsto (e^{1.2} - 1)/(e^2 - 1) \approx 0.363139$, rotating the minimum to axis 3 (*gov*).

Zero buffers moved, yet every axis rotated through the minimum. Cross-axis $\mathrm{argmin}$ requires **Ordinal Level Comparability (OLC)** on one shared scale (Sen 1985; [Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md), comparing levels across axes). It also requires simultaneous confidence bands countering finite-sample concave plug-in bias $b\_{\min}(\hat{\theta}) \le 0$ ([Appendix A](appendix-a-mathematics.md) §A.6, simultaneous uncertainty bands). Without OLC, displays report three separate booleans or unhedged ruin exposure as a threshold device.

In plain words, unless all three coordinates sit on one common OLC scale, naming the "weakest axis" reflects chart curvature rather than operational reality.

### Proposition 3 (Multiplicative Level-Set Reversal and Corner Volume Collapse)

Finally, why not multiply the three safety coordinates together into a single product score $S\_1 S\_2 S\_3 \ge \tau$? Proposition 3 proves that multiplicative products break both box invariance and safety efficiency. Different dial curves can flip which system looks safer, and near the viability boundary the product rule discards five-sixths of healthy operating states that passed every gate.

Multiplicative composites $\Omega\_{\mathrm{prod}}(\tau) = \lbrace S \in [0, 1]^3 : S\_1 S\_2 S\_3 \ge \tau \rbrace$ break Proposition 1's box invariance. Compare $S^{(A)} = (0.50, 1.00, 1.00)$ and $S^{(B)} = (0.80, 0.80, 0.80)$. Original coordinates rank $S^{(B)}$ higher ($0.80^3 = 0.512 > 0.500$), whereas $(\psi\_1, \psi\_2, \psi\_3)$ lifts $\psi(S^{(A)})$ to $(1 - e^{-2})/(1 - e^{-4}) \approx 0.880797$ above $\psi(S^{(B)}) \approx 0.483658$. Within one fixed unit chart at $\tau \in (0, 1)$ ($V\_{\min}(\tau) = (1 - \tau)^3 > 0$), the volume ratio $V\_{\mathrm{prod}}(\tau)/V\_{\min}(\tau)$ approaches $1/6$ as $\tau \to 1^-$ ([Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md) §2, multiplicative corner collapse; [Chapter 50](50-methodological-families-reflexivity.md) §9.4.1, 3D volume contraction). That discards five-sixths of strict viability and condemns balanced states clearing every gate.

Multiplying safety margins lets arbitrary dial curves invert rankings and discards five-sixths of healthy operating territory near high thresholds.

## 4. The Laminar Boundary of T4

Two limits bound laminar $T\_4$. First, pure left-truncation ($X \mid X > \delta$) preserves tail index $\alpha$, whereas net ceded excesses $X - r$ break Hill shift-invariance. Likewise, contractual liability caps $X \wedge K$ (with contract cap $K$ distinct from Hill threshold order $k$) tie top claims and flatten Hill slopes (Embrechts, Klüppelberg, & Mikosch 1997; [Chapter 31](31-distribution-and-moment.md) §7, censoring and tail shifts). That preserves physical $\alpha$ while exporting $(X - K)\_+$ onto third-party external reception perimeters (audited ex post via $\Theta\_{\mathrm{basin}}$; [Chapter 46](46-non-identical-asymmetry-heavy-tails.md) §5.6, how insurance caps hide true tail risk).

Second, $T\_4$ remains a **single-period acyclic geometry** over $O\_t$. It tracks neither multi-period depletion ($T\_5$) nor cyclic downward causation feeding $\Delta\_J$ into phase-locked collapse without decoupling breakers ([Chapter 96](96-arrows-that-bend-the-strata.md), recurrent fold collapse across strata).
