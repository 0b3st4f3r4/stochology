Static tail geometry at $T\_4$ ([Curvature of the Flight Path](89-curvature-of-the-flight-path.md), scale invariance and heavy-tail regimes) bounds single-shock severity, yet arrows fly in irreversible historical time. Knowing the tail exponent never spares a vessel whose pumps start slower than the hull fills.

## 1. Ensemble Illusion vs. Absorbing Ruin

Along a single real-world trajectory bounded by an absorbing ruin barrier $R\_{t+1} \le 0$, ensemble returns across parallel imaginary worlds diverge sharply from historical survival. Under multiplicative growth $dW\_t = W\_t((r\_{\mathrm{rf}} + f(\mu - r\_{\mathrm{rf}}))dt + f\sigma\mkern3mu dB\_t^{\mathrm{BM}})$ ($W\_0 > 0$, hurdle rate $r\_{\mathrm{rf}}$, standard Brownian motion $B\_t^{\mathrm{BM}}$ distinct from live buffer $B\_t$, leverage fraction $f \ge 0$, volatility $\sigma > 0$), ensemble averaging gives expected growth $r\_{\mathrm{rf}} + f(\mu - r\_{\mathrm{rf}})$. That linear formula pushes optimal leverage $f \to \infty$ whenever $\mu > r\_{\mathrm{rf}}$. Yet a hospital ward, power grid, or water fund cannot pool balances across unlived counterfactuals (Peters 2019; Hulme et al. 2023): hitting zero once ends compounding forever, forcing time-average growth to govern survival.

János Kornai (1986) showed how soft budget constraints (automatic bailouts, tax relief, or rescue credit) keep insolvent enterprises upright by shifting shortfalls onto an external guarantor. Erasing negative equity after $R\_t \le 0$ hides ruin by exporting deficits onto external reception perimeters (audited ex post via $\Theta\_{\mathrm{basin}}$ in $\mathcal{D}\_{\mathrm{diag}}$; [Chapter 47, *Irreversible Time, Non-Ergodicity, and the Vital Margin*](47-irreversible-time-vital-margin.md), why soft rescues displace deficits). Guard $T\_5$ tests reserves against a hard physical floor prior to rescue.

## 2. Four Clocks in Irreversible Time

Four coupled clocks measure how fast a single trajectory approaches the absorbing floor during step $t$.

### 2.1 Itô–Kelly Variance Drain and Finite-Sample Shrinkage

Even under positive average return, wild swings shrink one compounded trajectory because a 50 percent loss requires a 100 percent gain to break even.

Applying Itô's (1951) Lemma to continuous Brownian diffusion (Gaussian increments with $W\_0 > 0$ and **known** unit-period return variance $\sigma^2 > 0$, while finite-variance jump or discrete returns obey tail index $\alpha > 2$) separates ensemble drift from pathwise logarithmic growth $g\_{\mathrm{time}}(f) = r\_{\mathrm{rf}} + f(\mu - r\_{\mathrm{rf}}) - \frac{1}{2}f^2\sigma^2$. For positive excess drift ($\mu, \hat{\mu} > r\_{\mathrm{rf}}$) and effective sample size $n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} > 0$, this fixes John Kelly's (1956) growth optimum $f^\ast = (\mu - r\_{\mathrm{rf}})/\sigma^2$, finite-sample epistemic shrinkage $k(n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}) = 1/(1 + 1/n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}) \in (0, 1)$ (motivated by Baker & McHale 2013), and shrunk fraction $f^\ast\_{\mathrm{epistemic}} = k(n\_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}})(\hat{\mu} - r\_{\mathrm{rf}})/\sigma^2$ (estimating unknown $\hat{\sigma}^2$ requires stronger scale-invariant shrinkage; [Chapter 38, *Time and Dependence*](38-time-and-dependence.md), geometric growth drag; [Chapter 47](47-irreversible-time-vital-margin.md), irreversible variance-shrunk leverage; [Appendix A](appendix-a-mathematics.md) §A.2, non-ergodic reserve corridor).

Because utility bounds (Samuelson 1971), finite-horizon violations (Ford & Kay 2023, 2024), and Paretian jumps (Mandelbrot 1963) defeat stop-loss triggers ([Chapter 47](47-irreversible-time-vital-margin.md), stop-loss failure under jumps), Itô–Kelly marks a **survival corridor**, not a scalar preference law. Noisy data compress safe leverage sharply below textbook averages, and jumping markets skip past stop-losses.

### 2.2 Kingman's Heavy-Traffic Queue Saturation

The second clock sits in congestion: as a service desk or emergency ward fills near 100 percent capacity, arrival jitter causes waiting queues to explode long before capacity hits the ceiling.

Consider a stationary single-server $GI/G/1$ renewal queue with utilization $\rho\_{\mathrm{queue}} = \lambda/\mu\_s \in (0, 1)$ (distinct from lag-1 autocorrelation $\rho\_1$ and Meng's $\rho\_{R,Y}$), finite second moments ($\alpha > 2$), and arrival/service variance sum $c\_a^2 + c\_s^2 > 0$. Kingman's (1961, 1962) heavy-traffic asymptote drives expected wait $\mathbb{E}[W\_q]$ toward $+\infty$ as $\rho\_{\mathrm{queue}} \to 1^-$ ([Chapter 38, *Time and Dependence*](38-time-and-dependence.md), queue divergence near full load; [Chapter 47, *Irreversible Time and the Vital Margin*](47-irreversible-time-vital-margin.md), lean-operating trap). That wait-time explosion drains effective recovery headroom $\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$ before shocks even land.

In plain words, running an institution "lean" near full utilization turns minor delays into unbounded recovery backlogs.

### 2.3 Allostatic Erosion of Canguilhem–Holling Basins and Frontline Human Reserves

The third clock tracks slow structural wear on living systems and frontline workers. Under chronic strain, an organization or caregiver adapts on the surface while silently shrinking the invisible safety margin left before sudden breakdown.

Georges Canguilhem (1943/1966) named **vital normativity** the power to absorb *infidélité du milieu* and institute new operating norms. Combining this with C. S. Holling's (1973) basin width, consider a logistic stock with intrinsic growth $r > 0$ (distinct from hurdle rate $r\_{\mathrm{rf}}$ in §1 and MGF argument in §2.4), carrying capacity $K > 0$, and harvest rate $0 \le H \le rK/4$. Its stability basin narrows as $D(H) = K\sqrt{1 - 4H/(rK)}$, where $dD/dH \to -\infty$ as harvest approaches maximum yield $H \to (rK/4)^-$.

Peter Sterling and Joseph Eyer (1988) formalized predictive physiological recalibration as *allostasis*, while Bruce S. McEwen (1998) named cumulative wear **allostatic load**. Chronic load shrinks normalized tolerance $D\_t / D\_0$ ($D\_0 > 0$) invisibly. Rising lag-1 autocorrelation $\rho\_1 = \exp(-\lambda\_{\mathrm{recovery}}\Delta t) \to 1^-$ ($\lambda\_{\mathrm{recovery}} \to 0^+$, $\Delta t > 0$; Scheffer et al. 2009) warns of diffusive folds asymmetrically. Inverting that conditional commits the prosecutor's fallacy (Boettiger & Hastings 2012), while slow variance misses $0 < \alpha < 2$ Lévy jumps exceeding $D\_t$ (within the broader $\alpha \le 2$ infinite-variance boundary; Gnedenko & Kolmogorov 1954).

Chronic harvest or workload hollows out recovery depth steeply near the edge, while sudden jumps can shatter a weakened basin without slow warning signs.

Caregiver archives reveal this basin wear long before financial insolvency appears on an institutional ledger.

**Workplace epistemic inequality.** Screening 152 papers (34 retained, 20 definitions) into three necessary and sufficient conditions for moral distress, Morley et al. (2019) trace workplace epistemic inequality (citing Fricker 2007). Distress intensifies whenever frontline clinicians are excluded from institutional decisions.

**Missing longitudinal tracking against global shortages.** Walters, Murray, and Young (2026) review 12 emergency-department studies (2015–2025), including 7 quantitative cross-sectional studies spanning 169 to 390 participants and zero longitudinal cohorts. Set against a World Health Organization 2025 projection of a 4.1 million nurse shortfall by 2030, this gap shows missing longitudinal tracking of cumulative moral injury.

**Defensive collapse inside rigid units.** In a 28-bed pediatric unit ($n = 11$ of 13), da Silva et al. (2026) trace how collective defensive strategies sustain care under an inflexible organization. Once those shared defenses deplete, workers suffer psychological decompensation.

**Cross-national care penalties and public buffers.** Across 20,046 respondents (22 European countries, EQLS) and 13,304 respondents (20 countries, ESS; 18.3% in paid care), Lightman and Kevins (2025/2026) measure systematic wellbeing penalties. Unpaid adult caring carries a penalty ($\beta = -0.00191$, $p < 0.01$) that vanishes near 27% of GDP social spending. Paid paraprofessional care carries a steeper penalty ($\beta = -0.171$, $p < 0.05$), dropping 0.505 scale units (57% of a standard deviation) at the 10th expenditure percentile before vanishing near 26% of GDP. Starving collective care buffers silently exports strain onto staff and household external reception perimeters (audited ex post via $\Theta\_{\mathrm{basin}}$).

### 2.4 Selection-Deflated Cramér–Lundberg Ruin Exponent

The fourth clock prices sudden shock accumulation against a finite buffer, showing how positive mean surplus turns brittle under extreme losses or biased records.

Suppose net replenishment $c > \lambda\_{\mathrm{shock}}\mu\_X > 0$ clears mean shock load (using Poisson shock arrival rate $\lambda\_{\mathrm{shock}} > 0$, mean shock $\mu\_X > 0$, and replenishment $c$, kept distinct from §2.1–§2.3). Classical finite-buffer ruin bounds $\psi(B) \le e^{-R\_{\mathrm{CL}}B}$ (with Cramér–Lundberg adjustment exponent $R\_{\mathrm{CL}} \in (0, r\_{\max})$ solving $\lambda\_{\mathrm{shock}}(M\_X(R\_{\mathrm{CL}}) - 1) = cR\_{\mathrm{CL}}$, distinct from reserve $R\_t$, Brake 2 ceiling $R^\ast$, and recording indicator $R$ in $\rho\_{R,Y}$; Cramér 1930; Lundberg 1903) hold for light-tailed non-negative shocks $X \in [0, \infty)$ satisfying Cramér's condition ($M\_X(r) = \mathbb{E}[e^{rX}] < \infty$ on $(0, r\_{\max})$ with $\lim\_{r \uparrow r\_{\max}} M\_X(r) = +\infty$, where MGF argument $r > 0$ is distinct from $r\_{\mathrm{rf}}$), whereas heavy-tailed Pareto losses drive $R\_{\mathrm{CL}} = 0$. Now impose bounded shock support $X \in [0, x\_{\max}]$ ($x\_{\max} > 0$), interior sampling fraction $f \in (0, 1)$, non-zero severity spread $\sigma\_Y > 0$, reserve interval $0 \le B\_L \le B \le B\_U$, and $T\_3$ missingness $\bar{\rho} \ge |\rho\_{R,Y}|$ with $\bar{\rho} < \rho^\star\_{R,Y}$ so post-defect surplus holds ($c > \lambda\_{\mathrm{shock}}\bar{E}\_{-\bar{\rho}}[X]$). Worst-case severity depresses the adjustment exponent to $\bar{R}\_{\mathrm{CL}}(-\bar{\rho}) \le R\_{\mathrm{CL}}$ ([Chapter 47](47-irreversible-time-vital-margin.md) §6.3.4, reserve-interval ruin ceiling), raising the ruin bound exponentially to $\psi(B) \le e^{-\bar{R}\_{\mathrm{CL}}(-\bar{\rho}) B\_L}$.

## 3. Master Survival Inequality, Lexicographic Regimes, and Survival Pre-Emption

The four clocks meet in one same-unit race between **Time-to-Survival** $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} = B\_t / \dot{D}\_{\mathrm{crisis}}$ (live buffer $B\_t \ge 0$, crisis drawdown rate $\dot{D}\_{\mathrm{crisis}} > 0$) and **Effective Time-to-Recovery** $\mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t) = \tau\_{\mathrm{detect}} + \tau\_{\mathrm{delib}}(\mathcal{I}\_t) + \tau\_{\mathrm{actuate}}$ ($\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} \ge \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$; Simchi-Levi, Schmidt, & Wei 2014). This race extends earlier survival clocks ([Chapter 38, *Time and Dependence*](38-time-and-dependence.md); [Chapter 47, *Irreversible Time, Non-Ergodicity, and the Vital Margin*](47-irreversible-time-vital-margin.md)).

Under algedonic bypass (Beer 1972; $\delta\_{\mathrm{status}} = 0$) and unatrophied skill ($a\_{\mathrm{skill}, t} = 1$), $\tau\_{\mathrm{detect}}(0, 1)$ sets baseline lag; discounting $\delta\_{\mathrm{status}} \in (0, 1)$ and deskilling $a\_{\mathrm{skill}, t} \in [0, 1]$ ($(0, 1]$ finite) stretch pre-ceiling lag by $((1 - \delta\_{\mathrm{status}})a\_{\mathrm{skill}, t})^{-1}$ ([Chapter 39](39-cause-intervention-counterfactual.md); [Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md) §§8.2.2, 8.6.1); $\tau\_{\mathrm{delib}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$ prices bounded review ([Chapter 95](95-collision-at-ten-bits-per-second.md)) and $\tau\_{\mathrm{actuate}}$ absorbs queue wait.

Avoiding Harsanyi's (1975) paralysis critique, Wald's (1945) and Sniedovich's (2007, 2012) global maximin spans Filter I's admissible set only; infeasible sets route to Regime III and $T\_1$ reopening ([Chapter 47](47-irreversible-time-vital-margin.md)). Strictly **ex post**, **Reflexive Brake 2** audits over-precaution via covariant ceiling $R^\ast = \max\bigl(R\_{\mathrm{floor}},\mkern3mu R\_{\mathrm{ref}}(\kappa\_{\mathrm{prec}}/c\_{\mathrm{urgent}} - 1)\bigr)$ (pre-anchored physical scale $R\_{\mathrm{ref}} > 0$, survival-and-impairment floor $R\_{\mathrm{floor}} \ge 0$, weights $\kappa\_{\mathrm{prec}}, c\_{\mathrm{urgent}} > 0$; Scarf 1960; Samuelson 1971; [Chapter 50](50-methodological-families-reflexivity.md) §9.3.2) alongside $\mathcal{D}\_{\mathrm{diag}}$, never rationing live buffers ([Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md)).

To defeat compliance denial-of-service, the per-step operator evaluates guards in **strict lexicographic precedence** ([The Triad and the Minimum](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md), strict ordering across regimes).

**1. Survival Pre-Emption and Finite-Clock Gate.** When declared clocks yield $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} < \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$, **Regime III-B (Algedonic Halt)** fires **ahead** of slot completeness, co-reporting simultaneous governance corruption. Otherwise, missing or uncalibrated `T5` clocks enter **Regime III-A (Quarantine)** (or `Regime III-B` if governance channels are corrupted), never passing $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} \ge \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$ unchecked. Missing forms therefore never trap an expiring vessel.

**2. Channel Integrity, Completeness, Reopenings, and Floors.** With finite $\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} \ge \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathcal{I}\_t)$, governance-alarm corruption triggers **Regime III-B** (`Algedonic Halt`, latches, replenishment lock) **ahead** of slots or $T\_1$ reopenings (co-reporting `CounterInductiveReopen`/`Unparsable`; [Appendix B](appendix-b-architecture-verification.md) §B.1). Intact channels route unsealed slots or $T\_1$ reopenings to `Regime III-A` quarantine ahead of floor restoration.

**3. Avoidable-Loss Regimes and Safe Abstention ($L\_t \le U\_t$).** Given finite clocks, sealed avoidable-loss bounds $[L\_t, U\_t]$ ($L\_t \le U\_t$), clean channel, and cleared floors against cost $C > 0$, action partitions into `Regime I` (`Buffered Action` or `Safe Abstention`), `Regime II` (reversible probing), and `Regime III` (irreversible refusal or binary lethal-omission impasse):

*Regime I, Buffered Action ($L\_t > C$, or boundary $L\_t = C < U\_t$):* full buffered rollout ($a = 1$).

*Regime II ($L\_t < C < U\_t$):* reversible acts with linear payoff over unpooled stakes execute deterministic fractional share $a^\ast\_{\mathrm{MMR}} = (U\_t - C)/(U\_t - L\_t) \in (0, 1)$. Under wide ambiguity or binary/set-identified stakes, minimax regret randomizes while maximin favors inaction (Montiel Olea, Qiu, & Stoye 2025; [Chapter 86](86-posture-before-bowstring-draws.md); [Chapter 87](87-aiming-against-counterfactual-winds.md)). Aradillas Fernández et al. (2025) prove ex-ante and ex-post robust Bayes diverge, while randomization can remain optimal both ex-ante (weak scalar identification) and ex-post (ex-post robust Bayes $\Gamma$-PER under sign ambiguity).

Conversely, **irreversible** straddles never probe: survivable abstention ($a = 0$) triggers **Regime III unilateral probe refusal**, whereas a binary choice ($a \in \lbrace 0, 1\rbrace$) with lethal omission flags a **binary lethal-omission impasse**, escalating to standpoint-threshold governance ([Chapter 99](99-unsealing-the-vault-after-impact.md), resolving lethal-omission forks after unsealing the vault).

*Regime I, Safe Abstention ($U\_t \le C$, including $L\_t < C = U\_t$ and $L\_t = U\_t = C$):* benign non-action ($a = 0$), distinct from **Regime III (*Lowering the Bow*)**.

## 4. The Laminar Boundary of T5

Two physical couplings and two laminar idealizations bound $T\_5$. First, common-mode shocks can impair fraction $c\_{\mathrm{imp}} \in [0, 1)$ ($1 - c\_{\mathrm{imp}} > 0$) of the buffer built against them, cutting survival time to $(1 - c\_{\mathrm{imp}})B\_t / \dot{D}\_{\mathrm{crisis}}$ and scaling required reserves by $1/(1 - c\_{\mathrm{imp}}) \to +\infty$ as $c\_{\mathrm{imp}} \to 1^-$. Correlated power-grid fuel freezes illustrate this breach (Federal Energy Regulatory Commission and North American Electric Reliability Corporation 2021; [Chapter 47](47-irreversible-time-vital-margin.md), correlated reserve impairment in irreversible time), spilling deficits onto external reception perimeters (audited ex post via $\Theta\_{\mathrm{basin}}$).

Second, for baseline recovery $\mathrm{TTR}\_0 > 0$, market depth $M > 0$ (specializing [Chapter 47](47-irreversible-time-vital-margin.md)'s absorption $M(B, \mathrm{TTS})$, distinct from $M\_{\mathrm{Leontief}}$ and [Chapter 83](83-layer-distributed-consensus-sre-order.md)'s multiplier), and impact sensitivity $\kappa > 0$ ($\kappa$ distinct from $\kappa\_{\mathrm{prec}}$), forced liquidation $A \ge 0$ lengthens recovery via $\mathrm{TTR}(A) = \mathrm{TTR}\_0(1 + \kappa A/M)$. That lengthening drives fire-sale spirals (Danielsson & Shin 2003; Brunnermeier & Pedersen 2009; [Chapter 47](47-irreversible-time-vital-margin.md), endogenous liquidity spirals under irreversible time).

Finally, laminar $T\_5$ assumes uncorrupted telemetry against alert-flooding or engineered queue saturation $\rho\_{\mathrm{queue}} \to 1^-$. Those saturation traps return under superposition ([Chapter 95](95-collision-at-ten-bits-per-second.md), compliance collision at ten bits per second; [Chapter 97](97-the-hijacked-kairos-and-exception.md), hijacked emergency exception capture), alongside an unreactive target handed to $T\_6$.

Yet $T\_5$ treats external responses as indifferent to deployed rules. [When the Target Reads the Archer](91-when-the-target-reads-the-archer.md) (Chapter 91, `T6`) crosses from $P\_3$ to $P\_1$ to audit calibration, causal invariance, performative shift, and perimeter reception.
