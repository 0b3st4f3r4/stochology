This appendix carries the consolidated bibliography of the treatise, and the map from each layer of the argument to the chapters that carry it. Every entry is the source the book cites; the reading rules at the end of the appendix say what an entry does and does not assert.

## C.1 The Six Layers of the Argument and the Chapters That Carry Them

The treatise is argued in six layers, and they do not map onto the parts one to one: the ethical refusals precede the framework, the syntax layer sits over the mediation circuit across Parts I–IV, and the combinatorial layer carries the whole of Part V. The table records which chapters carry which layer, so that a reader following one strand can read it without reading the other five.

| Layer of the argument | Conceptual role | Chapters |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ethical and interpersonal refusals | Three refusals ($R\_1$: renouncing control over reception; $R\_2$: exposing pact betrayal, scapegoating and double binds; $R\_3$: refusing to turn organizational cognitive influence into strategic manipulation). | Ch. `00`, `01`, `04`, `06`, `07`, `19`, `20`, `26`, `27`, `29`, `40`, `43`, `48`, `49`, `84`, `85` |
| Three-plane circuit and collapse propositions | The mediation circuit over world, archive and institution, the Nuclear Triad, the three collapse propositions, and the three canonical operators. | Ch. `00`–`08`, `29`, `41`, `42`–`50` |
| Trajectory syntax | The syntax over $3 + 3 + 1 = 7$ circuit transits and guard sites (`T1`–`T7`), four verdicts (`Interior`, `Boundary`, `Collapse`, `Unparsable`), the two-tape separation of *télos* and *skopós*, and non-commutation. | Ch. `06`, `29`, `41`, `42`, `50`, `51`, `56`, Appendix A.5, Appendix B.1 |
| Reflexive core | Termination of the infinite regress of higher-order uncertainty at the agent's own *télos*, as a declared stopping decision of the instrument with its cost in width (Chapter 41), the $3 \to 7$ inventory of reflexive invariants across three partitions (`3/2/2`, `3+3+1`, `2/2/2+1`), and the three regimes of action. | Ch. `17`, `36`, `41`, `44`, `45`, `47` |
| Combinatorial graph | The equivalence between the $\binom{7}{3} = 12 + 9 + 9 + 4 + 1 = 35$ guard triples and the $7 \times 5 = 35$ orders of knowledge across strata `S1`–`S7`, with the cumulative Leontief homomorphism. | Ch. `00`, `09`, `51`–`85`, Appendix A.6, Appendix B.2 |
| Political ontology | *Potentia*, *potestas*, *multitudo*, and the immanent institutions of polycentric commons under non-compensable Leontief margins. | Ch. `00`, `20`, `29`, `40`, `41`, `42`, `49`, `50`, `85` |

## C.3 How to Read This List

An entry in this appendix asserts three things and no more: that the work exists, that it appeared in the year given, and that it supports the specific claim the book attaches to it. The class belongs to the use and not to the source: the same author can be authoritative for a finding the book quotes and silent for a method the book argues on other grounds, so a name in this list is never by itself a warrant for everything a paragraph does with it.

That distinction is what keeps the list from becoming an appeal to authority. Where a number appears in the book, it is the source's number, quoted with the source's own units and qualifiers, and the sentence that carries it names the source in the same breath; where a claim is an argument rather than a measurement, the citation supports the argument and the book carries the reasoning. No entry in this list asks the reader to accept a conclusion the cited work does not contain.

Two consequences follow for a reader checking a claim. First, a source listed here without a page or a section is still a source, but the claim it supports is the one the book wrote down, not the one the source contains in full, and where the two differ, the book is what has to be corrected. Second, an entry is never evidence that a formal derivation is right: a derivation is checked against its premises and its proof, and no citation can substitute for that check.

Two maintenance rules complete the reading. An entry is never silently corrected: if a year, a journal or a page turns out to be wrong, the correction is made visibly in the revision record rather than overwritten without notice, because a bibliography that changes without a trace is a bibliography nobody can audit. And an entry is never kept alive by being hedged: a source that could not be verified is removed rather than annotated, and the claim it supported is either re-argued or withdrawn. The list is therefore a record of what this book can stand behind, in the only form in which standing behind something can be checked, and its incompleteness is part of the record too, since a claim that lost its source is a claim the next edition has to earn again.
