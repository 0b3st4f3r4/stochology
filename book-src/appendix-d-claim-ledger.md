This appendix lists the claims this book borrows rather than establishes. A claim is in the ledger only when its warrant is a source a reader can open: a named author, a year, and a published finding. Nothing here rests on a computation performed for this book, on a simulated trajectory, or on a parameter chosen so that an argument runs.

Two consequences follow. A claim whose source reports a figure is carried with that figure exactly as the source states it, in the source's own unit, and no figure is extended, recomputed, or rounded from it. A claim whose source argues rather than measures is carried as the argument, with no numeral attached, because filling the cell with a number nobody published would be inventing the claim. Where an argument needs a quantity that the literature does not report, this book reasons with the form of the relation and leaves the instantiation to the reader's own case.

| A deployed sepsis alert whose warnings are mostly false produces alert fatigue as an arithmetic consequence, not as a failure of clinical attention. | Wong et al. | High-stakes use of a social indicator corrupts the very process the indicator was built to monitor. | Campbell (1979) | Campbell's law: the more a quantitative indicator is used for social decision-making, the more it distorts and corrupts the social processes it is meant to record. |

| Clustered observation costs effective sample size, so a raw row count is not a measure of information. | Kish (1965) | Design effect and effective sample size: unequal weights reduce the effective count to $n\_{\mathrm{eff}} = \left(\sum\_i w\_i\right)^2 / \sum\_i w\_i^2$, and the effective sample should be reported beside the nominal one. |
| Clustering inflates the variance of a mean, and the inflation is a known function of cluster size and intra-cluster correlation. | Moulton (1986) | The one-way cluster design effect $\mathrm{DEFF} = 1 + (\bar{m} - 1)\rho\_I$ under equal cluster sizes ($m\_c \equiv \bar{m}$, replaced by effective cluster size $\tilde{m} = \bar{m}(1 + \mathrm{CV}^2(m))$ when cluster sizes vary), so clustering must be priced before a count is read as information. |
| A large $n$ cannot repair a recording mechanism correlated with the outcome. | Meng (2018) | The data defect correlation: the error of a sample mean factorises into the recording-outcome correlation $\rho\_{R,Y}$, the coverage drop-out ratio $\sqrt{(1 - f)/f}$, and the population standard deviation $\sigma\_Y$, so the first factor sets the non-vanishing selection floor regardless of total population size $N$; and for weighted estimators $\tilde{G}\_n - G\_N$, replaces $\sqrt{(1 - f)/f}$ by $\sqrt{(1 - f + \mathrm{CV}\_W^2)/f}$ (equivalently $\sqrt{(1 - f\_w)/f\_w}$ with weighted sampling fraction $f\_w = f / \mathrm{DEFF}\_{\mathrm{Kish}}$, Eqs. 3.12–3.13). |
| Queueing delay grows without bound as utilisation approaches saturation. | Kingman (1961) | The heavy-traffic limit for stationary $GI/G/1$ renewal queues with finite interarrival and service second moments ($c\_a^2, c\_s^2 < \infty$, Pareto tail exponent $\alpha > 2$), in which waiting time scales with $\rho/(1 - \rho)$ as $\rho \to 1^-$: a system run near full utilisation holds no spare time to absorb a shock. |
| A convex penalty makes a quantity read at the mean look better than the truth. | Jensen (1906) | Jensen's inequality: for $\phi$ convex on a convex interval $I$ with $\mathbb{P}(X \in I) = 1$, $\mathbb{E}[\lvert X\rvert] < \infty$, and $\mathbb{E}[\lvert\phi(X)\rvert] < \infty$, $\phi(\mathbb{E}[X]) \le \mathbb{E}[\phi(X)]$, so cost read at the mean understates cost averaged over states. |
| Inputs that cannot substitute for one another cannot be compensated by surplus elsewhere. | Leontief (1941) | Fixed-proportion technology: at an elasticity of substitution of zero, additional quantity of one input does not make up a shortage of another. |
| A regulator must command at least the variety of the disturbance it absorbs. | Ashby (1956) | The Law of Requisite Variety: only variety can destroy variety, so a controller facing a high-variety environment needs matching internal variety rather than a longer rulebook. |
| Whether the moments of a heavy tail exist is read from the tail index. | Hill (1975) | An estimator of the tail exponent from the largest order statistics, with the mean and the variance finite only above the corresponding exponent thresholds; sequential log-spacings yield unbiased $\mathbb{E}[\hat{\xi}\_{\mathrm{Hill}}(k)] = 1/\alpha$ (eq. 2.6, p. 1166; §5, pp. 1171–1172) while the reciprocal estimator carries finite-$k$ moments $\mathbb{E}[\hat{\alpha}\_{k,n}] = k\alpha/(k-1)$ ($k > 1$) and $\mathrm{Var}(\hat{\alpha}\_{k,n}) = (k\alpha)^2/[(k-1)^2(k-2)]$ ($k > 2$). |
| The unseen part of a population is bounded from below by the rarest observed classes. | Chao (1984); Chao (1989); Chao & Colwell (2017) | Distinguishes the nonparametric lower bound on unseen classes from single-stream multinomial singleton and doubleton counts (`Chao1`, Chao 1984) from multi-list incidence capture–recapture under heterogeneity model $M\_h$ with finite-sample factor $(t - 1)/t$ and $f\_2 = 0$ regularisation (`Chao2`, Chao 1989; Chao & Colwell 2017). |
| A plug-in Kelly fraction computed from estimated parameters over-bets. | Baker & McHale (2013) | Shows that the plug-in Kelly fraction is too aggressive when the win probability and the payoff are estimated rather than known, and derives a shrunken fraction for that parameter uncertainty. |
| A forecast should be scored by a strictly proper rule, so that reporting honestly is the optimal strategy. | Gneiting & Raftery (2007) | Characterises proper and strictly proper scoring rules: the expected score is optimised by reporting the true predictive distribution, and the continuous ranked probability score is proper on Borel probability measures $\mathcal{P}$ and strictly proper on the finite-first-moment subclass $\mathcal{P}\_1$ where $\mathbb{E}\lvert X\rvert < \infty$ (§4.2, p. 367). |
| A reliability diagram built by arbitrary binning is not reproducible, and a decomposition that is not reproducible cannot be audited. | Dimitriadis, Gneiting & Jordan (2021) | The CORP decomposition: a consistent, optimally binned, and reproducible split of the Brier score into reliability, resolution, and uncertainty. |
| Conditional probabilities are handled better as natural frequencies than as percentages. | Gigerenzer & Hoffrage (1995) | Bayesian reasoning improves sharply when the same information is presented as counts over a natural reference tree instead of as conditional percentages, lifting standard Bayesian accuracy from 16% to 46% without instruction (*Psychological Review*, 102(4), 684–704). |
| A fixed-sample error guarantee does not survive repeated looks at the accumulating data. | Ville (1939) | Ville's maximal inequality for nonnegative supermartingales bounds the barrier-crossing probability across stopping times, supplying the stopping-time-invariant foundation on which anytime-valid tests and *e*-processes are built—a guarantee a fixed-sample interval lacks when recomputed after every observation. |
| Evidence can be weighed on a logarithmic scale, and that scale is what makes sequential accumulation additive. | Good (1950) | The weight of evidence in decibans: ten times the base-10 logarithm of the likelihood ratio, the unit in which sequential evidence accumulates by addition rather than by multiplication. |
| The units that return are the wrong sample for deciding where the vulnerability lies. | Wald (1943) | The survivorship argument: damage concentrates where returning units are hit and is absent where hits are lethal, so the missing units carry the information and the protection belongs where the survivors show no damage. |
| An organisation can normalise a deviation until the warning inside its own data has already been priced as acceptable. | Vaughan (1996) | The Challenger launch decision: each earlier episode of joint erosion had been reclassified as within an acceptable flight rationale, so the signal was present and already discounted when the fatal launch was approved. |
| The February 2021 failure of the Texas interconnection was a fuel and unit-availability failure rather than a shortage of installed capacity. | Federal Energy Regulatory Commission & North American Electric Reliability Corporation (2021) | The staff reports on the cold-weather outages in Texas and the South Central United States document generating-unit trips and fuel-supply failures across the fleet during the event. |
| Output treated as dependable evidence can convict the people who reported its faults. | Christie (2020) | The Post Office Horizon litigation (pp. 49–70, esp. pp. 49, 51–52): documents judicial fact-finding rejecting the "two in a million" error-rate claim advanced by the Post Office's IT expert once the presumption of computer-evidence dependability broke down, culminating in a £57.75 million civil settlement. |
| A composite score that sums ordinal codes onto a fixed integer scale performs an operation the ordinal scale does not license. | Apgar (1953) | The neonatal scoring rule: five signs each coded 0, 1, or 2, summed onto an integer scale of 0 to 10 and compared against a threshold. |
| Under low pre-study odds and small samples, most statistically significant findings are expected to be false. | Ioannidis (2005) | The argument that the probability a published finding is true depends on pre-study odds, on power, and on the number of competing hypotheses, so a nominal significance threshold is not a guarantee of truth. |
| A case-control association can survive every confounder the study measured and still not license causal language. | Doll & Hill (1950) | A case-control series reporting an association between heavy smoking and lung carcinoma that the measured confounders in its own tables could not carry. |
| A probability of exactly zero or one is permanent under Bayes' rule. | Lindley (1985) | Cromwell's rule: dogmatic priors and posteriors are excluded because no finite likelihood ratio revises them, so a probability that must remain revisable is kept in the open interval. |
| A decision to test or to treat has a threshold in the ratio of the two errors. | Pauker & Kassirer (1975); Pauker & Kassirer (1980) | Separates the single therapeutic decision threshold where treatment and no treatment are indifferent ($p^\ast$ from the ratio of false-positive cost to net error loss; Pauker & Kassirer 1975) from the three-zone clinical partition with testing and treatment thresholds requiring $\mathrm{Sens} + \mathrm{Spec} > 1$ (Pauker & Kassirer 1980). |
| A real-time archive can be compared against its own later revisions, and the revisions measure what the first release did not know. | Croushore & Stark (2001) | A real-time macroeconomic dataset of successive vintages, built so that an initial release can be set against the later record of the same quarter (in the precursor Working Paper 99-4, documenting four-quarter average growth revisions exceeding 1 percentage point and single-quarter revisions up to nearly 5 percentage points across successive vintages). |
| Replacing a randomised control group with a non-experimental comparison archive can reverse the sign and magnitude of a treatment-effect estimate. | Dehejia & Wahba (1999) | Re-evaluation of the National Supported Work experimental benchmark ($\unicode{x24}1{,}794$ across 185 treated and 260 control observations; NBER WP 6586 / *J. Am. Stat. Assoc.* 94(448)) against non-experimental full CPS-1 comparison estimates (15,992 controls: naive difference in means $-\unicode{x24}8{,}498$ and $\unicode{x24}972$ under regression adjustment with all pre-treatment covariates, Table 2 Panel B/C col. 5, p. 20; Table 3 col. 2, p. 21), showing selection bias under non-random comparison arms. |
| Improving a physiological surrogate endpoint does not guarantee lower mortality and can conceal net clinical harm. | Cardiac Arrhythmia Suppression Trial (CAST) Investigators (1989) | Across 2,309 patients entering titration and 1,727 (75%) randomized despite arrhythmia suppression on Holter monitoring, arrhythmic death or non-fatal cardiac arrest ran at 33/730 (4.5%) on the active encainide and flecainide arms versus 9/725 (1.2%; relative risk 3.6, 95% CI 1.7–8.5) on placebo, with total mortality at 56/730 (7.7%) versus 22/725 (3.0%; relative risk 2.5, 95% CI 1.6–4.5) over 10 months of mean follow-up. |
| Pooling underpowered trials designed for other endpoints can surface a safety signal that sits close to the null boundary and depends on inclusion sensitivity. | Nissen & Wolski (2007) | Meta-analysis of published and unpublished rosiglitazone trials reporting a myocardial infarction odds ratio of 1.43 (95% confidence interval [1.03, 1.98]). |
