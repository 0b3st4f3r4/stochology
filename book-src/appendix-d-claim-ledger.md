This appendix lists the claims this book borrows rather than establishes. A claim is in the ledger only when its warrant is a source a reader can open: a named author, a year, and a published finding. Nothing here rests on a computation performed for this book, on a simulated trajectory, or on a parameter chosen so that an argument runs.

Two consequences follow. A claim whose source reports a figure is carried with that figure exactly as the source states it, in the source's own unit, and no figure is extended, recomputed, or rounded from it. A claim whose source argues rather than measures is carried as the argument, with no numeral attached, because filling the cell with a number nobody published would be inventing the claim. Where an argument needs a quantity that the literature does not report, this book reasons with the form of the relation and leaves the instantiation to the reader's own case.

## Literature-Warranted Claims

| Claim | Source | What the source reports |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| A deployed sepsis alert whose warnings are mostly false produces alert fatigue as an arithmetic consequence, not as a failure of clinical attention. | Wong et al. (2021) | External validation of the proprietary Epic Sepsis Model across 27,697 hospitalised patients: under real clinical prevalence the model reported a sensitivity of 33% and a positive predictive value of 12%. |
| Conscious behavioural throughput has a biological ceiling, which caps how much an institution can route through one person. | Zheng & Meister (2025) | Human behavioural and sensory-motor information throughput is bounded near $10\text{ bits/s}$, so a hierarchy cannot raise its own processing rate by adding documentation. |
| An imperfect binary risk classification cannot simultaneously achieve predictive parity (equal positive predictive value across groups), equal false-positive rates, and equal false-negative rates when the groups' base rates differ. | Chouldechova (2017) | With unequal prevalence across groups and equal positive predictive value, equal false-positive and equal false-negative rates cannot both hold; the incompatibility is a theorem about the instrument, not a defect of one classifier. |
| Solving the wrong problem precisely is a distinct failure from being wrong about the answer. | Kimball (1957) | Names the error of the third kind: an exact answer to the question that was not the one worth asking. |
| Evaluators grade a decision by its outcome even when the information available at decision time is held fixed. | Baron & Hershey (1988) | Experimental demonstration of outcome bias: the judged quality of a decision tracks the result obtained rather than the ex ante case for making it. |
| A body can endorse every premise of a conjunctive policy by majority and still reject the conjunction. | List & Pettit (2011) | The discursive dilemma: premise-wise and conclusion-wise aggregation of one set of votes diverge, so a collective verdict is not the sum of individual judgements. |
| A warning from a speaker in a subordinate position can be discounted for reasons that have nothing to do with the evidence. | Fricker (2007) | Testimonial injustice: credibility is deflated by prejudice about the speaker's identity or standing, so the deficit sits in the hearer's economy of credibility rather than in the claim. |
| A reporting hierarchy needs a channel that bypasses itself when ordinary escalation is too slow. | Beer (1972) | The Viable System Model's algedonic signal: a pain or distress channel that reaches the regulator ahead of the ordinary reporting path. |
| Any statistical regularity used for purposes of control tends to collapse. | Goodhart (1975) | The monetary-policy observation that a regularity measured under one regime ceases to hold once it is used to steer that regime. |
| A measure that becomes a target ceases to be a good measure. | Strathern (1997) | States the maxim in the short form the institutional literature quotes, as the audit's own summary of what control does to a regularity. |
| High-stakes use of a social indicator corrupts the very process the indicator was built to monitor. | Campbell (1979) | Campbell's law: the more a quantitative indicator is used for social decision-making, the more it distorts and corrupts the social processes it is meant to record. |
| Rankings do not merely describe the ranked world; the ranked reorganise around the ranking. | Espeland & Sauder (2007) | Reactivity: a measure feeds back into the behaviour of those measured, so the distribution after publication is not the distribution that was measured. |
| When outcomes are missing within a bounded support, the data identify an interval rather than a point. | Manski (1990) | Worst-case bounds for a mean with missing outcomes: the recorded mean re-weighted at the support extrema, with the width set by the missing fraction and the support width. |
| A monotone-selection assumption narrows the worst-case interval. | Lee (2009) | Trimming bounds under monotone selection: once it is assumed which units can be missing, the unidentified mass shrinks and the bounds tighten. |
| A partially identified parameter needs a confidence region that covers the interval, not a point estimate with a standard error. | Imbens & Manski (2004) | Confidence intervals for partially identified parameters that carry identification width as well as sampling error. |
| Clustered observation costs effective sample size, so a raw row count is not a measure of information. | Kish (1965) | Design effect and effective sample size: unequal weights reduce the effective count to $n\_{\mathrm{eff}} = \left(\sum\_i w\_i\right)^2 / \sum\_i w\_i^2$, and the effective sample should be reported beside the nominal one. |
| Clustering inflates the variance of a mean, and the inflation is a known function of cluster size and intra-cluster correlation. | Moulton (1986) | The one-way cluster design effect $\mathrm{DEFF} = 1 + (\bar{m} - 1)\rho\_{\mathrm{intra}}$, so clustering must be priced before a count is read as information. |
| A large $n$ cannot repair a recording mechanism correlated with the outcome. | Meng (2018) | The data defect correlation: the error of a sample mean factorises into the recording-outcome correlation $\rho\_{R,Y}$, the coverage drop-out ratio $\sqrt{(1 - f)/f}$, and the population standard deviation $\sigma\_Y$, so the first factor sets the non-vanishing selection floor regardless of total population size $N$. |
| Queueing delay grows without bound as utilisation approaches saturation. | Kingman (1961) | The heavy-traffic limit, in which waiting time scales with $\rho/(1 - \rho)$: a system run near full utilisation holds no spare time to absorb a shock. |
| A convex penalty makes a quantity read at the mean look better than the truth. | Jensen (1906) | Jensen's inequality: for convex $\phi$, $\phi(\mathbb{E}[X]) \le \mathbb{E}[\phi(X)]$, so cost read at the mean understates cost averaged over states. |
| Inputs that cannot substitute for one another cannot be compensated by surplus elsewhere. | Leontief (1941) | Fixed-proportion technology: at an elasticity of substitution of zero, additional quantity of one input does not make up a shortage of another. |
| A regulator must command at least the variety of the disturbance it absorbs. | Ashby (1956) | The Law of Requisite Variety: only variety can destroy variety, so a controller facing a high-variety environment needs matching internal variety rather than a longer rulebook. |
| Whether the moments of a heavy tail exist is read from the tail index. | Hill (1975) | An estimator of the tail exponent from the largest order statistics, with the mean and the variance finite only above the corresponding exponent thresholds. |
| The unseen part of a population is bounded from below by the rarest observed classes. | Chao (1984) | A nonparametric lower bound on the number of unseen classes, built from the singleton and doubleton counts of a capture–recapture sample. |
| A plug-in Kelly fraction computed from estimated parameters over-bets. | Baker & McHale (2013) | Shows that the plug-in Kelly fraction is too aggressive when the win probability and the payoff are estimated rather than known, and derives a shrunken fraction for that parameter uncertainty. |
| A forecast should be scored by a strictly proper rule, so that reporting honestly is the optimal strategy. | Gneiting & Raftery (2007) | Characterises proper and strictly proper scoring rules: the expected score is optimised by reporting the true predictive distribution, and the continuous ranked probability score is strictly proper. |
| A reliability diagram built by arbitrary binning is not reproducible, and a decomposition that is not reproducible cannot be audited. | Dimitriadis, Gneiting & Jordan (2021) | The CORP decomposition: a consistent, optimally binned, and reproducible split of the Brier score into reliability, resolution, and uncertainty. |
| Conditional probabilities are handled better as natural frequencies than as percentages. | Gigerenzer & Hoffrage (1995) | Bayesian reasoning improves sharply when the same information is presented as counts over a natural reference class instead of as conditional percentages. |
| A fixed-sample error guarantee does not survive repeated looks at the accumulating data. | Ville (1939) | Ville's inequality for nonnegative martingales: it bounds the probability that a test statistic ever crosses a barrier, which is exactly the guarantee a fixed-sample interval lacks when it is recomputed after every observation. |
| Evidence can be weighed on a logarithmic scale, and that scale is what makes sequential accumulation additive. | Good (1950) | The weight of evidence in decibans: ten times the base-10 logarithm of the likelihood ratio, the unit in which sequential evidence accumulates by addition rather than by multiplication. |
| The units that return are the wrong sample for deciding where the vulnerability lies. | Wald (1943) | The survivorship argument: damage concentrates where returning units are hit and is absent where hits are lethal, so the missing units carry the information and the protection belongs where the survivors show no damage. |
| An organisation can normalise a deviation until the warning inside its own data has already been priced as acceptable. | Vaughan (1996) | The Challenger launch decision: each earlier episode of joint erosion had been reclassified as within an acceptable flight rationale, so the signal was present and already discounted when the fatal launch was approved. |
| The February 2021 failure of the Texas interconnection was a fuel and unit-availability failure rather than a shortage of installed capacity. | Federal Energy Regulatory Commission & North American Electric Reliability Corporation (2021) | The staff reports on the cold-weather outages in Texas and the South Central United States document generating-unit trips and fuel-supply failures across the fleet during the event. |
| Output treated as dependable evidence can convict the people who reported its faults. | Christie (2020) | The Post Office Horizon litigation: a legal presumption that computer evidence is dependable, and what that presumption cost once the system was shown to be wrong. |
| A composite score that sums ordinal codes onto a fixed integer scale performs an operation the ordinal scale does not license. | Apgar (1953) | The neonatal scoring rule: five signs each coded 0, 1, or 2, summed onto an integer scale of 0 to 10 and compared against a threshold. |
| Under low pre-study odds and small samples, most statistically significant findings are expected to be false. | Ioannidis (2005) | The argument that the probability a published finding is true depends on pre-study odds, on power, and on the number of competing hypotheses, so a nominal significance threshold is not a guarantee of truth. |
| A case-control association can survive every confounder the study measured and still not license causal language. | Doll & Hill (1950) | A case-control series reporting an association between heavy smoking and lung carcinoma that the measured confounders in its own tables could not carry. |
| A probability of exactly zero or one is permanent under Bayes' rule. | Lindley (1985) | Cromwell's rule: dogmatic priors and posteriors are excluded because no finite likelihood ratio revises them, so a probability that must remain revisable is kept in the open interval. |
| A decision to test or to treat has a threshold in the ratio of the two errors. | Pauker & Kassirer (1975) | The threshold approach to clinical decision-making: the probability at which treatment and no treatment are indifferent is the false-positive cost over the sum of the two error costs. |
| A real-time archive can be compared against its own later revisions, and the revisions measure what the first release did not know. | Croushore & Stark (2001) | A real-time macroeconomic dataset of successive vintages, built so that an initial release can be set against the later record of the same quarter. |
| Replacing a randomised control group with a non-experimental comparison archive can reverse the sign and magnitude of a treatment-effect estimate. | Dehejia & Wahba (1999) | Re-evaluation of the National Supported Work experimental benchmark (\$1,794.34) against non-experimental CPS comparison estimates (naive difference -\$8,497.52), showing selection bias under non-random comparison arms. |
| Improving a physiological surrogate endpoint does not guarantee lower mortality and can conceal net clinical harm. | Cardiac Arrhythmia Suppression Trial (CAST) Investigators (1989) | Despite arrhythmia suppression on Holter monitoring, arrhythmic death or non-fatal cardiac arrest ran at 4.5% on the active encainide and flecainide arms versus 1.2% on placebo, with higher total mortality on active therapy. |
| Pooling underpowered trials designed for other endpoints can surface a safety signal that sits close to the null boundary and depends on inclusion sensitivity. | Nissen & Wolski (2007) | Meta-analysis of published and unpublished rosiglitazone trials reporting a myocardial infarction odds ratio of 1.43 (95% confidence interval [1.03, 1.98]). |
| An unbroken calibration and certification chain does not prevent a systematic instrumental bias when the primary reference is mis-set. | NASA (1990) | Optical systems investigation reporting that the Hubble Space Telescope primary mirror was figured against a reflective null corrector whose field lens sat 1.3 millimetres from its specified spacing. |
| High class-level relative frequency alone does not establish individualised civil liability when case-specific weight is absent. | Cohen (1977) | The Gatecrasher Paradox: with 1,000 spectators in the stands and 499 tickets sold, naked class prevalence exceeds one-half while individualised proof against any picked spectator is zero. |
| Cost-benefit precaution rules fail when the loss valuation inserted into the inequality is an ex-ante administrative price rather than the loss a tribunal recognises after the harm. | Grush & Saunby (1973); Dowie (1977); Schwartz (1991); California Court of Appeal (1981) | Grush and Saunby's submission on a proposed industry-wide rollover fuel-leakage standard priced the fix at \$11 per vehicle across 11 million cars and 1.5 million light trucks (\$137 million total) against \$200,000 per death, \$67,000 per injury, and \$700 per vehicle (\$49.53 million across 180 deaths, 180 injuries, and 2,100 vehicles); Schwartz identifies the per-death figure as the federal regulator's societal-cost estimate; the \$125 million punitive verdict in *Grimshaw v. Ford Motor Co.* was remitted to \$3.5 million. |
| Ignoring independence failures and prior odds in forensic probability testimony produces misleadingly extreme joint likelihood claims. | Royal Statistical Society (2001) | Formal statement on the Sally Clark trial warning that squaring an estimated 1 in 8,543 single SIDS probability to 1 in 73 million assumed sibling independence and omitted prior odds. |
| Linear managerial skin in the game does not prevent rapid insolvency when leverage compresses survival time below position-unwinding time. | Lowenstein (2000) | Documents Long-Term Capital Management entering the 1998 crisis with roughly \$125 billion in assets against \$4.7 billion in equity, wiping out capital during stressed market illiquidity. |
| Partial software rollout across a live serving fleet acts as a heterogeneous partition rather than a controlled fractional canary probe. | U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) (2013) | Enforcement order documenting the 1 August 2012 Knight Capital deployment failure across eight servers, losing more than \$460 million within about forty-five minutes. |
| Redundant monitoring and scheduled stability audits fail together when shared constitutive assumptions induce common-cause collapse. | Robertson et al. (2019) | Expert panel investigation of the 25 January 2019 Feijão Dam I failure at Brumadinho (270 fatalities), attributing the rapid collapse to static liquefaction of loose, saturated tailings rather than overtopping. |
| Catch-per-unit-effort series targeted on dense aggregations can mask stock depletion until the collapse of a commons. | Hutchings & Myers (1994); Task Force on Incomes and Adjustment in the Atlantic Fishery (1993) | Documents the collapse of northern Atlantic cod (*Gadus morhua*) spawning biomass by more than 98% since the early 1960s prior to the July 1992 moratorium, affecting some 35,000 workers across Atlantic coastal communities. |
| Locally congruent flood-control rules can amplify aggregate damage across a hydrologically coupled river basin. | World Bank (2012) | Rapid assessment of the 2011 Chao Phraya floods reporting total damages and losses of THB 1.425 trillion (approximately US\$46.5 billion). |
| An administrative grading algorithm applied without individual consent and with irreversible allocation consequences can lose public legitimacy rapidly enough to compel complete revocation. | Office of Qualifications and Examinations Regulation (Ofqual) (2020) | Interim report (13 August 2020) and Chair's statement (17 August 2020) revoking the summer 2020 A-level and GCSE calculated-grade standardisation algorithm in favour of centre assessment grades. |
| A significance-filtered literature overstates the effects a replication will find. | Open Science Collaboration (2015) | Of 100 replications of studies from three psychology journals, 97% of the original studies had reported significant results against 36% of the replications, and the mean effect size fell from $r = 0.403$ in the originals to $r = 0.197$ in the replications. |
| A risk score calibrated within groups can show unequal error rates across groups whose base rates differ. | Larson, Mattu, Kirchner & Angwin (2016) | Among Broward County defendants who did not reoffend within two years, 44.9% of Black and 23.5% of white defendants had been labelled higher risk by COMPAS; among those who did reoffend, 47.7% of white and 28.0% of Black defendants had been labelled lower risk. |
