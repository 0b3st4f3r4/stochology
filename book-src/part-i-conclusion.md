# Part I — Conclusion

*This page states what Part I has established, reads the eight chapters as one argument, and names what it leaves open. It adds no claim of its own.*

## What the eight chapters established

Four linked steps summarize what Part I established, moving from a single choice under irreversible time to the non-tradeable triad.

A decision cannot wait for certainty, and split ledgers divide reasonable people looking at the same forecast. [Chapter 01](01-deciding-without-knowing.md) shows that acting and waiting both carry costs, fixing a cost–loss cutoff $p^\ast = \min\bigl(1, C/(\eta L)\bigr)$ where the agency paying $C$ with capped liability $L\_{\mathrm{inst}} < L\_{\mathrm{affected}}$ holds back across forecasts sitting between their two thresholds while the household downstream begs for protection.

An average is not a map of risk, and outcomes do not grade judgment. [Chapter 02](02-the-average-is-not-the-story.md) and [Chapter 03](03-the-biggest-number-in-the-room.md) show that danger lives in spread, curved harm, extreme shocks, and survival clocks ($\mathrm{TTS}\_{\mathrm{survival}} \ge \mathrm{TTR}\_{\mathrm{recovery}}$), while [Chapter 04](04-it-worked-so-i-was-right.md) separates pre-outcome aim (*télos*) from lucky hits (*skopós*), showing that outcome-only grading tracks skill safely only when reckless shortcuts almost never pass, careful stewards almost never lose, and harms are neither gamed nor shifted onto outsiders.

A table is a selection before it is a count, and a published measure is an intervention. [Chapter 05](05-what-a-number-doesnt-say.md) and [Chapter 06](06-who-is-missing-from-the-table.md) show how calibration, rare-event denominators, and skewed intake doors shape every figure (where piling up biased rows shrinks error bars around the wrong answer), while [Chapter 07](07-when-the-measure-becomes-the-goal.md) shows actors adapting around high-stakes metrics until proxies break.

The one positive claim is the thesis in its simplest form: three protections must hold together, and they do not trade. [Chapter 08](08-three-things-you-cant-trade.md) shows that honest knowledge, survival slack, and accountable authority combine as the Leontief minimum $M\_{\mathrm{Leontief}}$ rather than a weighted sum or product, paired strictly after the fact with the remaining tolerance of **external reception perimeters** ($\Theta\_{\mathrm{basin}} \in [0, 1]$, distinct from internal survival basins) in the co-diagnostic pair $\mathcal{D}\_{\mathrm{diag}} = (M\_{\mathrm{Leontief}}, \Theta\_{\mathrm{basin}})$ without adding a fourth axis or turning the verdict into a live target.

## What remains open

Three questions remain open at the close of Part I:

- **Formal bases and calibrated measures across planes:** Part I uses rough illustrative scales, leaving formal mathematical, philosophical, and natural-science bases to Part II and calibrated cross-plane measures to Parts III and IV.
- **The closed three-arena map:** [Chapter 29](29-aim-and-the-three-planes.md) formalizes the World–Archive–Institution loop, and [Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md) derives its full guard-site architecture and non-tradeable minimum.
- **Protecting external reception perimeters:** Binding authority across multi-step choices to the downstream risk burden borne by affected outsiders, an ethical refusal opened in [the Prologue](00-preface.md) and audited across **external reception perimeters** ($\Theta\_{\mathrm{basin}}$), belongs to Parts III and IV.

## What the next part does with it

[Part II — Introduction](part-ii-introduction.md) maps out the toolkit ahead, opening in [Chapter 09](09-sets-relations-functions.md), and sits here rather than earlier because you now see what the tools are for. Sets, relations, bounds, limits, curvature, accumulation, linear maps, and probability arrive not as abstract exercises, but as the building blocks of the three safeguards. Bounds show what an honest knowledge margin looks like; upward curvature shows why uneven shocks hurt more than steady averages; stability explains how feedback loops settle or run away. The philosophical chapters ask what justifies a claim and limits imposing risk on others, while the natural-science chapters supply mechanisms (entropy, buffers, selection, and regulation) that every real system has to obey.

Nothing in Part II decides anything on its own. That is the whole point of putting it second.
