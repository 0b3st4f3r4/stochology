# Part I — Conclusion

*This page states what Part I has established, reads the eight chapters as one argument, and names what it leaves open. It adds no claim of its own.*

## What the eight chapters established

Four results survive the part, and every one of them is negative in form.

A record of outcomes is not a record of judgment. [Chapter 04](04-it-worked-so-i-was-right.md) shows the outcome and the warrant coming apart in both directions: no accumulation of results can certify a procedure, and no single failure can condemn one.

A mean is not a description of the exposure. [Chapter 02](02-the-average-is-not-the-story.md) and [Chapter 03](03-the-biggest-number-in-the-room.md) together show that the decision is exposed to the distribution and to its tail, and that the two summaries a reader reaches for first — the average and the largest value seen — each answer a question the decision did not ask.

A table is a selection before it is a count. [Chapter 05](05-what-a-number-doesnt-say.md) and [Chapter 06](06-who-is-missing-from-the-table.md) show that the denominator, the population and the rule by which units entered the record are part of the number, and that a record selected on the outcome carries a bias no care in the arithmetic removes.

And a published measure is an intervention. [Chapter 07](07-when-the-measure-becomes-the-goal.md) shows the world reading the measure and responding, so the measure's meaning is not fixed the moment it is defined.

The one positive result is the thesis in its minimal form: three things must be carried, and they do not trade. [Chapter 08](08-three-things-you-cant-trade.md) does not prove that there are three. It shows that the three it names are not substitutes, which is enough to rule out the aggregate.

## The argument read as one

In order, the chapters form a single movement. [Chapter 01](01-deciding-without-knowing.md) closes the loop and puts the decision before the observation. Chapters [02](02-the-average-is-not-the-story.md), [03](03-the-biggest-number-in-the-room.md) and [04](04-it-worked-so-i-was-right.md) show that the record circulating inside that loop cannot carry the decision alone: not the mean, not the maximum, not the outcome. Chapters [05](05-what-a-number-doesnt-say.md) and [06](06-who-is-missing-from-the-table.md) show why. The record is a selection, and the selection sits upstream of every number computed from it. [Chapter 07](07-when-the-measure-becomes-the-goal.md) closes the circle: the record is also downstream of the decision, because the world reacts to what the record publishes. Then [Chapter 08](08-three-things-you-cant-trade.md) draws the consequence. If the record is upstream and downstream both, no single quantity stands for the whole, and the combination that remains is a minimum.

The order matters. Read the conclusion of [Chapter 08](08-three-things-you-cant-trade.md) without the loop of [Chapter 01](01-deciding-without-knowing.md) and "minimum" sounds like a stylistic preference for caution. It is not. It is what is left once the record has been shown unable to carry the decision alone.

## What remains open

Three questions are open at the end of the part, and the book answers them in order.

What quantity measures each axis? Part I names knowledge, survival and authorization as things that must be carried, and gives no measure. The measures arrive in Part III, after the bases supply the objects they are built from.

Why three? The part uses the number without defending it. The defense — that the three are the planes of a circuit, and that the circuit's transits are what a guard site guards — is in [Chapter 29](29-aim-and-the-three-planes.md); the arithmetic that fixes the count is in [Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md).

And what happens when the three are combined across many decisions rather than one? Part I treats a single decision. The sequential case, where each decision changes the archive the next one reads, belongs to Part IV.

## What the next part does with it

Part II is a toolkit, and it sits here rather than earlier because the reader now knows what the tools are for. Sets and relations, orders and bounds, limits, convexity, accumulation, linear maps and measure arrive not as mathematics for its own sake but as the objects the three axes will be built from: a bound is what an epistemic margin is made of, a convexity is what makes a tail asymmetric, a contraction is what makes a feedback loop stable. The philosophical chapters then ask what it is for a claim to be warranted, and the scientific ones supply the mechanisms — entropy, buffers, selection, regulation — that the vital axis will borrow.

Nothing in Part II decides anything. That is the point of putting it second.
