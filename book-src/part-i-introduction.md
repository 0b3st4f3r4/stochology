# Part I — Introduction

*This page states once what the eight chapters of Part I attempt, and in what order. It argues nothing the chapters do not argue; it says where to look.*

## The case the part takes seriously

The part begins with a decision already taken. Judgment under structural uncertainty is not the case where the distribution is known and only the arithmetic is left. It is the case where the act must come before the observation, and where the observation, when it finally arrives, arrives changed by the act. Everything the book later formalizes follows from taking that sentence literally.

[Chapter 01](01-deciding-without-knowing.md) sets the loop: a state, an archive that records part of it, an institution that acts on the record, an outcome that returns to the archive. The loop is not complicated. It is closed, and a closed loop cannot be evaluated one step at a time without losing what makes it a loop.

## Three comforts the part removes

The next three chapters take away beliefs that make the loop look easier than it is.

[Chapter 02](02-the-average-is-not-the-story.md) separates the average from the story it is asked to tell. A mean summarises a distribution; a decision is exposed to the distribution, not to its first moment. [Chapter 03](03-the-biggest-number-in-the-room.md) separates the largest value observed from the value that matters. The maximum of a sample is a statement about the sample, and the decision is exposed to what the sample has not yet shown. [Chapter 04](04-it-worked-so-i-was-right.md) separates the outcome from the warrant. A decision taken well can end badly and a decision taken badly can end well, so a good result is no evidence that the reasoning was sound, and a bad result is no evidence that it was not.

Each of the three is a claim about what a record can carry. Together they say that a record of outcomes is not yet a record of judgment.

## From belief to table

The following three chapters move from the reader's beliefs to the tables the reader is shown.

[Chapter 05](05-what-a-number-doesnt-say.md) asks what a number leaves out: the denominator, the population it was computed over, the comparison it presupposes, the alternative it was selected against. [Chapter 06](06-who-is-missing-from-the-table.md) asks who is missing from the table and what their absence does to the number that survives. A record selected on the outcome is not a random sample of the world; the bias it carries belongs to the selection, not to the arithmetic. [Chapter 07](07-when-the-measure-becomes-the-goal.md) asks what happens when the measure is published and acted upon. The world reacts to being measured, and the reaction becomes part of the measurement.

Three positions, one argument: a number is a claim about a population, taken through a record, inside a world that reads the record.

## The claim the part establishes

[Chapter 08](08-three-things-you-cant-trade.md) states the thesis in its minimal form. A decision under uncertainty must carry three things — what it knows, what it can survive, who authorized it — and they do not substitute for one another. A great deal of knowledge does not buy survival at the ruin barrier; a large reserve does not buy a warranted claim; a legitimate procedure buys neither. Because they do not substitute, the aggregate that combines them is a minimum rather than a weighted sum, and the minimum is diagnostic: it names the axis that failed.

That is all Part I establishes, and it manages it without algebra. The reader who wants the derivation is sent forward on purpose: Part II supplies the formal objects, Part III assembles them into the circuit.

## What the part defers

Three deferrals, and the deferral is part of the design.

The axes are named, not derived. Part I says that knowledge, survival and authorization do not trade; it does not say what quantity measures each, nor why there are three rather than some other number. The derivation is in [Chapter 29](29-aim-and-the-three-planes.md), and the count is fixed in [Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md).

Nothing here is quantified. Part I carries no formula a reader could apply to a case, and that is deliberate: a formula introduced before the reader has seen what it is for turns into a recipe, and a recipe is what [Chapter 07](07-when-the-measure-becomes-the-goal.md) warns about.

And nothing here is decided. The part tells the reader what to stop believing. What to do instead comes later, and it requires knowing what the record can support — which is the subject of the bases.
