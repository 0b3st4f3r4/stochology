# Part II — Introduction

*This page states once what the eighteen chapters of Part II supply, and why their order runs in three groups. It argues nothing the chapters do not argue.*

## Why a part of bases at all

A framework that says knowledge, survival and authorization do not trade has to say what each of them is made of. Part I said only that the three do not substitute. Part II supplies the materials. The formal objects from which a measure of each can be built. The epistemological vocabulary in which a claim to know can be assessed. The scientific mechanisms in which survival and regulation actually occur.

The part is a toolkit. Its chapters are stocks of material, not steps in a proof, and the reader is expected to return to them.

## The first group: the formal objects

[Chapter 09](09-sets-relations-functions.md) fixes the language: sets, relations, functions, and the difference between a relation that holds and a function that assigns. [Chapter 10](10-numbers-order-bounds.md) supplies order and bounds, the material of any statement about a margin. [Chapter 11](11-sequences-limits-continuity.md) supplies limits and continuity; without them "the tail" and "the limit" are metaphors. From [Chapter 12](12-derivatives-and-convexity.md) comes convexity, which is why a tail can be asymmetric and why a minimum can be preferred to an average. [Chapter 13](13-integrals-and-accumulation.md) supplies accumulation. [Chapter 14](14-linear-algebra.md) supplies linear maps and the invariance that later becomes the question of what a recoding may change. [Chapter 15](15-probability-as-a-measure.md) supplies measure: the difference between a probability as a statement about sets and a probability as a feeling.

## The second group: warrant and legitimacy

The formal group can say what follows from what. It cannot say when a claim is warranted, and it cannot say when the imposition of a risk is authorized. [Chapter 16](16-what-knowledge-is.md), [Chapter 17](17-justification-and-induction.md) and [Chapter 18](18-philosophy-of-science.md) take the first question: what knowledge is, what justifies it, how a scientific claim is tested and revised. [Chapter 19](19-ethics-and-the-imposition-of-risk.md) takes the second. An imposed risk is an act on someone who did not choose it, and consent, compensation and precaution are not interchangeable. [Chapter 20](20-legitimacy-and-institutions.md) takes the third: legitimacy is not popularity, and an institution's authority to decide is a separate question from the quality of its decisions.

The placement does work. The ethical and institutional chapters sit among the bases, not in a later applied part. The governance axis is one of the three coordinates, not an application of the framework, and a coordinate cannot be added after the geometry is fixed.

## The third group: the mechanisms

[Chapter 21](21-mechanics-determinism-chaos.md) through [Chapter 26](26-regulation-and-homeostasis.md) supply the physical and biological mechanisms the vital axis borrows. Determinism with sensitive dependence is why a finite horizon is a real limit and not an excuse. Entropy and the statistical arrow of time are why irreversibility is not a psychological illusion. Rates, equilibrium and buffers say what a reserve is and why it is finite. Variation and selection say how a population adapts, and how it fails to. Regulation and homeostasis say what feedback can hold and what it cannot.

Each is a mechanism with its own literature, and none of them is the framework. What the framework takes from each is one object: a horizon, an arrow, a buffer, a selection differential, a feedback loop.

## How to read the part

The chapters are written to be read in order but not to be read once. A reader who wants the derivation of the triad can take the formal group and skip to Part III. A reader who wants the ethics can take [Chapter 19](19-ethics-and-the-imposition-of-risk.md) and [Chapter 20](20-legitimacy-and-institutions.md) on their own. What the part asks of every reader is one thing: that no object be asked to do work it was not built for. A bound is not a warrant. A mechanism is not a justification. A proof of stability is not a proof that the stable arrangement is legitimate.
