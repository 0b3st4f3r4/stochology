*This page maps out what the eighteen chapters of Part II supply, and why they appear in three groups. It offers a readable guide rather than an argument of its own.*

## Why a part of bases at all

Part I showed that knowledge, survival, and accountability cannot replace one another. To build on that insight, we need to see what each safeguard rests on. We need mathematical tools for honest records and margins, philosophical standards for when a claim or risk is justified, and natural-science mechanisms showing how real systems endure or break.

Think of Part II as a workshop rather than a single linear proof. Each chapter starts from everyday examples before building formal tools, so you can read them in sequence and return to any shelf when Part III puts it to work.

## The first group: the formal objects

[Chapter 09](09-sets-relations-functions.md) sets the stage with collections, groupings, rankings, and recording maps, showing how coarse forms erase distinctions. [Chapter 10](10-numbers-order-bounds.md) moves along the number line from counting to gapless real numbers, interval bounds, computer rounding quirks, and extreme tails. [Chapter 11](11-sequences-limits-continuity.md) follows step-by-step sequences into limits, sudden jumps, and why one lived timeline can shrink even when an average across many worlds grows.

[Chapter 12](12-derivatives-and-convexity.md) introduces slopes and curvature, explaining why curving-upward costs turn average-based plans into underfunded traps. [Chapter 13](13-integrals-and-accumulation.md) turns rates into accumulated totals, links averages to weighted area, and brackets missing data inside honest ranges. [Chapter 14](14-linear-algebra.md) builds from two-dimensional vectors and tiny grids up to hidden blind spots, high-dimensional surprises, and stability. [Chapter 15](15-probability-as-a-measure.md) places probability on clearly listed outcomes, walking through updating rules, safe stopping criteria, and worlds that shift when measured.

## The second group: warrant and legitimacy

Mathematics tells us what follows from a premise, but not when a claim is trustworthy, when a risk may be imposed on others, or when an authority is legitimate. [Chapter 16](16-what-knowledge-is.md), [Chapter 17](17-justification-and-induction.md), and [Chapter 18](18-philosophy-of-science.md) ask what divides genuine knowledge from lucky guesses, how far past patterns carry inference, and how scientific claims survive severe tests. [Chapter 19](19-ethics-and-the-imposition-of-risk.md) separates irreversible harms subject to refusal from exposures where consent, voice, and precaution cannot be traded away. [Chapter 20](20-legitimacy-and-institutions.md) shows why answerable authority—open to frontline alarms and accountable for harm pushed onto outside communities and habitats—differs from mere popularity or forecast accuracy.

The placement of these chapters does real work. Ethics and institutional rules sit right beside mathematics among the foundations, not as an afterthought. The governance coordinate is not one reading among three—it fixes the sign of the competitive differential on which the other two margins are priced—and it cannot be added after the geometry is fixed.

## The third group: the mechanisms

[Chapter 21](21-mechanics-determinism-chaos.md) through [Chapter 26](26-regulation-and-homeostasis.md) bring in the physical, chemical, and biological mechanisms behind survival and regulation. [Chapter 21](21-mechanics-determinism-chaos.md) shows why deterministic chaos puts a sharp clock on prediction horizons. [Chapter 22](22-thermodynamics-and-entropy.md) traces order to continuous energy use bounded by physical dissipation and natural renewal. [Chapter 23](23-statistical-physics-and-time.md) explains why a single irreversible path cannot rely on averages across many imagined worlds.

[Chapter 24](24-chemical-rates-equilibrium-buffers.md) models physical reserves as finite chemical buffers that absorb shocks smoothly right up until they run dry. [Chapter 25](25-evolution-variation-selection.md) tracks variation and selection, showing why competition rewards short-term reserve stripping unless shared rules bind everyone. [Chapter 26](26-regulation-and-homeostasis.md) examines living feedback loops, showing why delayed signals and mounting wear require survival slack to outlast recovery time.

Each chapter stays faithful to its own science while handing Part III clear physical limits: finite forecast horizons, energy budgets, single-path bounds, finite buffers, selection pressures, and delay-limited survival clocks.

## How to read the part

Read these chapters in order, though you do not need to master every formula on one pass. Every chapter opens with intuition and small examples you can check by hand before introducing heavier tools.

Part III will assemble all three groups into one judgment framework: [Chapter 27](27-what-judging-is.md) and [Chapter 29](29-aim-and-the-three-planes.md) draw on aim and epistemology, while [Chapter 40](40-institution-incentive-metric.md) and [Chapter 41](41-the-triad-and-the-minimum.md) formalize incentives and the non-substitutable minimum. Skipping philosophy or science leaves that assembly incomplete, and readers starting from ethics can pair [Chapter 19](19-ethics-and-the-imposition-of-risk.md) and [Chapter 20](20-legitimacy-and-institutions.md) first. Ask no single tool to do work it was not built for: a bound is not a warrant, a mechanism is not a justification, and mathematical stability is not institutional legitimacy.
