# Part IV — Introduction

*This page states once what the nine chapters of Part IV do, and why the part is organised as a loop and three filters. It argues nothing the chapters do not argue.*

## From the framework to a case

Part III stated the circuit and derived the axes. A framework that stops there is a description; what it has not yet shown is that it can be run. Part IV runs it.

The part takes one case and follows it through the loop. The case is not an illustration added at the end. It is the object the filters are applied to, and each filter is stated in the form the case requires.

## The loop

[Chapter 42](42-sequential-loop-naive-agent.md) states the sequential loop and the optimizer that ignores it. The naive optimizer treats each step as if the distribution were fixed and the archive were a sample; the loop shows why that treatment is not merely optimistic but incoherent, because each action changes the archive the next action reads.

The chapter's contribution is the object the rest of the part operates on: a trajectory, not a decision.

## Filter I: from world to archive

The epistemic axis becomes an operation in three chapters. [Chapter 43](43-inquiry-openness-multiverse.md) states what an inquiry must keep open: the specifications not yet excluded, the specifications the archive cannot distinguish. [Chapter 44](44-underdetermination-partial-identification.md) states what follows when the archive underdetermines the answer: a bound, not a point, and a test that has to be severe as well as positive. [Chapter 45](45-world-to-archive-big-data-paradox.md) states what happens to an estimate when the archive grows without the population growing with it, and why more data can make an estimate worse.

The filter's output is not a number. It is a region, together with a statement of what the region cannot separate.

## Filter II: asymmetry and irreversible time

The vital axis becomes an operation in two chapters. [Chapter 46](46-non-identical-asymmetry-heavy-tails.md) states why units that are not identical produce tails, and why a tail is not a large variance. [Chapter 47](47-irreversible-time-vital-margin.md) states the vital margin: the time to survive against the time to recover, the reserve as a barrier, and the reason a probability of ruin is positive at every finite reserve.

The filter's output is a duration and a barrier, not a probability of a good outcome.

## Filter III: reactive worlds and institutions

The governance axis becomes an operation in two chapters. [Chapter 48](48-reactive-worlds-performativity-goodhart.md) states reactivity: a measure that is acted upon changes the world it measures, and the change is not noise. [Chapter 49](49-moral-luck-apt-agency-institutions.md) states the institutional question that reactivity raises — who is answerable for an outcome that depends on luck, and what design makes answerability apt instead of scapegoating.

[Chapter 50](50-methodological-families-reflexivity.md) closes the part by turning the framework on itself: five methodological families, the process-tracing question of how a mechanism is evidenced, and the reflexive question of what the book's own procedure would have to satisfy.

## How the filters compose

The filters are stated in sequence but they are not a pipeline that can be run once. Each filter's output is an input to the next, and the next filter's action changes the archive the first one reads. That is what the loop of [Chapter 42](42-sequential-loop-naive-agent.md) established, and it is why the composition is a per-step operator rather than an aggregate.

The reader should expect the case to get worse before it gets better. The epistemic filter widens the answer into a region; the vital filter shows that the region's width does not buy time; the governance filter shows that the time bought can be spent by a party who did not pay for it. What the operator does is act on all three at each step, and what the diagnosis does is say afterwards which one failed.
