# Stratum II — The Instrument and Its Commitment

*An opening page for Stratum II, stated once at the level its five chapters share. It names where each
instance is argued and does not restate them.*

---

Stratum II is argued in five numbered chapters of its own — [Chapter 56](56-layer-formal-computable-type-order.md), [Chapter 57](57-layer-probability-credence-measurement-order.md), [Chapter 58](58-layer-metrological-information-geometric-order.md), [Chapter 59](59-layer-experimental-sampling-causal-order.md) and [Chapter 60](60-layer-cryptographic-zero-knowledge-adversarial-order.md), one order each. The stratum's own
argument — that every claim about the world is made through an instrument that has already decided what
can be said — is stated once, at the level the five orders share, on this page.

The five orders look like the most technical part of the book, and they are the most consequential,
because they are where a commitment is made before any evidence arrives. A logic decides which audit is
possible; a credence decides what a sentence can mean before it is a number; a scale decides which
comparison survives; a design decides which population the estimate is about; a protocol decides what
counts as soundness against an adversary who knows the method.

## The audit that has to be syntactic

The first order is the one that gives the stratum its floor. A check that requires understanding cannot
be run on a system that is too large to understand, so the audit that scales is the one that reads
form, not meaning ([Chapter 56](56-layer-formal-computable-type-order.md)). Linear and affine
resource-types make this concrete: a resource used twice is not a resource used well, it is a different
claim, and the type system refuses the second use without knowing anything about the domain. The
practical value of the refusal is time: a wrong write licensed by a declaration is caught
before the write, not after the loss.

The stratum's first warning is already here and recurs in the other four: the syntactic audit is sound
to the extent that the types are faithful. Where the type is narrower than the obligation, the
audit passes and the obligation fails, and no amount of running the audit recovers it. The unit
mismatch that lost a spacecraft is the canonical case, and it is a type failure and not a
computational one (NASA 1999; and the failure of the launcher's inertial reference, Lions 1996).

## Credence before number, and the scale as commitment

The second order asks what a probability is before it is a quantity. A credence is a sentence in a
language of evidence, and the sentence has a grammar — the same content asserted with different
warrants is not the same credence ([Chapter 57](57-layer-probability-credence-measurement-order.md)).
The width of a credal set is then not decoration: it is an evidential distance, and two agents with the
same point estimate and different widths are not in the same state of knowledge.

The third element of the order is the one that outlives the analysis. A scale is a commitment to which
comparisons are meaningful, and the commitment is not undone by later processing: a transformation that
preserves order does not preserve the arithmetic performed on the original numbers (Stevens 1946), and
the use of a number as though the commitment had not been made is where measurement becomes a
governance act and not a technical one (Espeland & Stevens 1998). The order therefore carries a
signature with no third coordinate, and the stratum reads that absence as its own governance axial exposure.

## Two instruments that must agree

The third order is the metrological one, and it is the keystone of the stratum. Uncertainty of the
first kind is what repeated observation scatters; uncertainty of the second kind is what no repetition
removes, because it is a judgment about the instrument and not a sample from it (NASA 1990). The
information-geometric reading makes the distinction structural: the space of distributions carries a
metric, the projection onto a model is a projection in that metric, and the residual is a distance,
not a tolerance.

The fourth order is where the instrument meets a population it did not choose. A design fixes which
contrast is identified before any data arrive, and the same benchmark evaluated under two designs can
report opposite conclusions without either being wrong, because they are answers to different questions
(Dehejia & Wahba 1999). The stratum therefore treats the pure-methodologist signature, every reading on
one axis and none on the others, as its own warning: a method that is entirely epistemic is a method
that has not yet met the liability question.

The fifth order closes the stratum with the adversary inside the instrument. Soundness is a property of
a protocol against an opponent who knows the protocol, and the security claim is an inequality over
strategies and not a fact about a run (Fiat & Shamir 1986). The side-channel literature is the proof
that the claim is about the implementation as much as about the mathematics: a proof that ignores the
timing of the machine is a proof about an idealisation (Kocher 1996). The order carries a vital axial
exposure on its local triple for a reason worth stating plainly: a cryptographic guarantee protects a secret, and a secret is
not a reserve.

## What this stratum does not settle

It does not say which instrument is fundamental. The five orders are one constraint stated five times,
not a hierarchy: the logic does not ground the scale, the scale does not ground the design, and the
design does not ground the protocol.

Nor does it convert its commitments into one another. A type-check, a credal width, a Fisher–Rao
distance, an identified contrast and a soundness bound are not exchangeable quantities, and the stratum
deliberately keeps them apart. What it adds to the chapters is the claim that binds them: an instrument
is a promise about what will be counted as evidence, and the promise is made before the evidence. It
cannot be renegotiated once the result is known.
