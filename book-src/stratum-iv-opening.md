# Stratum IV — The Interval Between the Decision and the Evidence

*An opening page for Stratum IV, stated once at the level its five chapters share. It names where each
instance is argued and does not restate them.*

---

Stratum IV is argued in five numbered chapters of its own — [Chapter 66](66-layer-pharmacological-clinical-diagnostic-order.md), [Chapter 67](67-layer-epidemiology-outbreak-surveillance-order.md), [Chapter 68](68-layer-pharmacovigilance-regulatory-toxicology-order.md), [Chapter 69](69-layer-metascience-scientometrics-replication-order.md) and [Chapter 70](70-layer-acoustic-aesthetic-tragic-pedagogical-order.md), one order each. Its argument belongs to the five together rather than to any one of them, so it is stated once, here: in practice the decision is taken before the evidence exists, and the interval between the two is the object being governed.

Each chapter makes a claim about a body, a population, a literature or a student, and in each the record that would settle the claim is written afterwards, by the party that took the decision. A dose is chosen before the response. A case definition is fixed before the outbreak is counted. A drug is released before the harm appears. A result is published before the field's correction, a grade issued before the learning it certifies. Read separately they are five professions. Read together they are one problem stated five times: **the evidence arrives late, and the party that must act early is the party that writes the record.**

## The dose, and the window in time

The first order stakes the stratum's claim in the smallest unit available. A drug is not a substance but an interval: below the therapeutic band it does nothing, above it, it harms, and the width of the band is what a protocol is for ([Chapter 66](66-layer-pharmacological-clinical-diagnostic-order.md)). The decision threshold is no more a matter of taste: it is a ratio of costs, and the ratio is where the two classifications are priced against each other (Pauker & Kassirer 1975). The chapter's hardest case has the correct drug harming anyway, and it is included because a framework that only reads failures of knowledge cannot explain a failure of administration (Mitchell et al. 1973).

The order's second contribution: the window is a margin in time. The vital axis is not a metaphor here, since a therapy given outside its window is not a weaker therapy but a different intervention.

## Two clocks, and the edge of the archive

The second order is the epidemiological one, and it supplies the stratum's central ratio. One clock runs on the transmission, another on the reporting, and the decision is taken against the second while the first is still running. What fixes the meaning of a published count is the ratio between them, not either clock alone ([Chapter 67](67-layer-epidemiology-outbreak-surveillance-order.md)). So a threshold cannot be a property of a pathogen: the same reproduction number is manageable under one reporting delay and unmanageable under another.

The chapter's structural claim is sharper. A case definition is the archive's edge. What the definition does not admit is not under-counted, it is absent, and no better sampling after the fact repairs a series built on it. The stratum reads that as the general form of the problem shared with the orders above and below: the record is produced by the same act that produces the phenomenon.

## The trial that cannot see the harm, and the field that selects its own result

The third order is where the stratum becomes uncomfortable, because the instrument is not broken and still cannot answer. A trial sized to detect a benefit has almost no power against a rare harm, and the rule of three states the limit exactly: absence of events in a finite sample bounds the rate from above without establishing zero (Hazell & Shakir 2006). The report that changed practice accumulated what the trials could not show, and that it took a meta-analysis is the point rather than an anecdote (Nissen & Wolski 2007).

The fourth order moves the same structure from the clinic to the literature. The value of a result is a property of the field, not of the study: selection decides which effect sizes are published, so the published effect is a biased estimator of the true one by construction, and the bias is a function of the selection pressure rather than of anyone's conduct (Smaldino & McElreath 2016). The chapter's warning about the correction is the one most often skipped. A correction applied to a literature selected on significance can overcorrect, so the remedy has to be read against the same selection it repairs.

## The measure that absorbs the effort

The fifth order is the keystone and the strangest of the five, because its object is formation rather than information. A tuning system cannot be made to close: the comma is a residue of the arithmetic, not a defect of the tuning, and every temperament is a decision about where to put it (the derivation is in [Chapter 70](70-layer-acoustic-aesthetic-tragic-pedagogical-order.md)). Attic tragedy is read as a circuit for the same reason. The form is a device for holding a contradiction the audience is asked to accept rather than resolve.

The pedagogical half states the stratum's most practical claim. A measure that becomes the goal absorbs the effort it was meant to direct: teaching to the test is not a corruption of the measure but its normal operation once the measure is used for judgement (Campbell 1979; Messick 1995). And the didactic transposition that turns a body of practice into a curriculum changes the object it transmits (Chevallard 1985). The order closes the stratum because it shows the interval in its purest form: the grade is issued before the learning it certifies, and the record of the learning is written by the institution that issued the grade.

## What this stratum does not settle

It does not price the interval. A day of reporting delay, a rare adverse event, a selected effect size and a grade are not convertible, and nothing here converts them. The common structure is a lag between decision and evidence, not a unit.

Nor does it claim that every late decision is a failure. The lag is structural, and the party acting early writes the record, which is why the same institution appears on both sides of the interval. What the page adds to the five chapters is the sentence none of them can say from inside itself: the evidence will arrive, and the question is whether the record was written in a form that can still correct the decision that preceded it.
