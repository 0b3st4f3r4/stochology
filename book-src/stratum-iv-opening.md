# Stratum IV — The Interval Between the Decision and the Evidence

*An opening page for Stratum IV, stated once at the level its five chapters share. It names where each instance is argued and does not restate them.*

---

Stratum IV is argued in five numbered chapters of its own — [Chapter 66](66-layer-pharmacological-clinical-diagnostic-order.md), [Chapter 67](67-layer-epidemiology-outbreak-surveillance-order.md), [Chapter 68](68-layer-pharmacovigilance-regulatory-toxicology-order.md), [Chapter 69](69-layer-metascience-scientometrics-replication-order.md) and [Chapter 70](70-layer-acoustic-aesthetic-tragic-pedagogical-order.md), one order each. Its argument belongs to the five together rather than to any one of them, so it is stated once, here: in practice the decision is taken before the evidence exists, and the interval between the two is the object being governed.

Each chapter examines a body, population, literature, or student where the party acting early also authors the record read later: dose precedes response, case definition precedes counts, approval precedes rare harm, publication precedes replication, and grades precede long-term mastery. Five professions confront one problem: **evidence arrives late under incumbent authorship.**

## What the five orders share

Across bedside dosing, epidemic surveillance, post-market safety, selective publication, and schooling, action cannot wait for completed proof. Stratum IV is anchored at guard site `T2Severity`, which demands tests strong enough to rule out harm rather than mistaking noisy null studies for safety.

Left unchecked, the party that acted early writes the later record in its own favor. Under-powered trials, shifting case definitions, voluntary under-reporting, significance filters, and exam drills all bend late evidence back toward the incumbent rule.

To keep early choices corrigible, live action cannot lean on the retrospective co-diagnostic pair $\mathcal{D}\_{\mathrm{diag}}$, which evaluates finished trajectories strictly *ex post*. Instead, commitments are structured *ex ante*—before outcomes arrive—using pre-sealed thresholds, reversible fractional probes, and unannounced low-stakes audits kept off ranking tables.

## How the five orders build step by step

The five chapters walk outward from one patient at the bedside to an entire scientific field and the classroom:

1. **Bedside dosing ([Chapter 66](66-layer-pharmacological-clinical-diagnostic-order.md), `L16`):** Starts with one patient and one drug, where clearance lags and split hospital-versus-patient error costs make fixed doses unsafe.
2. **Outbreak surveillance ([Chapter 67](67-layer-epidemiology-outbreak-surveillance-order.md), `L17`):** Scales that timing lag to a population, where contagion doubles faster than reporting lines and shifting case definitions hide spread.
3. **Post-market safety ([Chapter 68](68-layer-pharmacovigilance-regulatory-toxicology-order.md), `L18`):** Follows approved therapies into millions of prescriptions, where rare injuries surface inside biased voluntary filings and delayed recalls.
4. **Scientific literature ([Chapter 69](69-layer-metascience-scientometrics-replication-order.md), `L19`):** Steps back to research communities, where file-drawer omissions and career metrics distort what gets published and replicated.
5. **Tragic and formative order ([Chapter 70](70-layer-acoustic-aesthetic-tragic-pedagogical-order.md), `L20`):** Completes the stratum as a balanced keystone, tracing fixed-grid mismatches from acoustic tuning and Attic tragedy to classroom assessment.

## What this stratum does not settle

It does not price the interval. A day of reporting delay, a rare adverse event, a selected effect size, and a grade are not convertible, and nothing here converts them. The common structure is a lag between decision and evidence, not a shared unit.

Nor does every delayed verdict fail: the interval is structural. This opening poses the question no single chapter settles alone: when late evidence arrives, can the sealed record still overturn the early decision?
