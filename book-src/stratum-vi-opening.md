# Stratum VI — The Adversary Inside the Measurement

*An opening page for Stratum VI, stated once at the level its five chapters share. It names where each
instance is argued and does not restate them.*

---

Stratum VI is argued in five numbered chapters of its own — [Chapter 76](76-layer-sequential-decision-bandit-real-options-order.md), [Chapter 77](77-layer-game-theory-strategic-adversarial-order.md), [Chapter 78](78-layer-macro-finance-econometrics-actuarial-order.md), [Chapter 79](79-layer-official-statistics-forensic-accounting-order.md) and [Chapter 80](80-layer-corporate-governance-principal-agent-order.md), one order each. The argument they share, that in these orders every number is produced by a party whose payoff depends on the number, is stated once, at the level the five orders share, on this page.

These are the orders in which the object being measured responds to the measurement. A stopping rule faces an environment that does not wait. A classifier faces a population that adapts to it. A risk model faces a balance sheet that moves when the model is believed. A ledger faces a management that chooses what the ledger records. A contract faces an agent who is paid on the proxy rather than on the thing. Read separately they are five industries; read together they are one problem stated five times: **the estimate is an input to the process it estimates, so the process's response belongs inside the model rather than outside it.**

## The archive that is not exchangeable

The first order is where the stratum's technical content begins. Stopping rules, bandit bounds and option value all assume that the future resembles the past in a specific way: that the observations arriving next are drawn from the same distribution as the observations already seen. Where that fails the bound is not violated; it is a statement about a different problem ([Chapter 76](76-layer-sequential-decision-bandit-real-options-order.md)). The practical form of the failure is that the optimal policy computed on the archive is optimal for the archive, and the archive was written by the policy.

The signature is a governance axial exposure, and the chapter is explicit about why. The machinery of sequential decision is genuinely powerful, and its power is the reason the assumption is not questioned where it matters most.

## The manipulation surface

The second order makes the adversary explicit. In strategic classification the agent's response to the classifier is not a nuisance but the object: the classifier is a Stackelberg commitment, and the population's best response is what the classifier actually faces ([Chapter 77](77-layer-game-theory-strategic-adversarial-order.md)). The manipulation surface is therefore a property of the model rather than of the population, and a model that is accurate on the historical distribution can be arbitrarily wrong against a population that has learned it.

This is not a story about bad actors. A response to an incentive does not require intent, and the institutions that treat adaptation as cheating have usually already lost the argument they thought they were having.

## Ruin, and the risk that grows when it is hedged

The third order is the keystone, and it carries the stratum's sharpest result. Ruin under light and heavy tails is not the same phenomenon. With a heavy tail the probability of an extreme event is not small enough for the buffer that a variance-based argument would size, and the reserve that "should" be adequate is inadequate by construction ([Chapter 78](78-layer-macro-finance-econometrics-actuarial-order.md)). Coherent risk measures fix an inconsistency among measures; they do not fix the tail.

The endogenous part is what connects this order to the rest of the stratum. Funding liquidity and market liquidity reinforce each other, so a shock that forces sales moves the price that determines the collateral that forces the sales (Brunnermeier & Pedersen 2009). The model did not fail to predict the shock. It participated in it.

## The book that must balance, and the dark archive

The fourth order is the accounting one, and its discipline is the oldest in the stratum. Double entry is a conservation law: an entry that does not balance is not a small error but a category error, and the ledger's authority comes from that closure rather than from any single figure ([Chapter 79](79-layer-official-statistics-forensic-accounting-order.md)). The forensic reading adds the part the closure cannot supply. A ledger can balance perfectly and still be about the wrong entity, and the dark archive — the transactions that never entered — is invisible to the arithmetic. The chapter's case is the one where the balance was true and the disclosure was not (Valukas 2010). The digit law is used there in its correct form, as a test of the support a fabricated series presupposes rather than as a proof of fraud (Nigrini 2000).

## The incentive that cannibalises itself, and the liability that licenses it

The fifth order closes the stratum with the mechanism the whole book has been pointing at. A multitask contract rewards what can be measured, so the agent reallocates effort away from what cannot, and the reallocation is rational rather than devious ([Chapter 80](80-layer-corporate-governance-principal-agent-order.md); Berle & Means 1932; Bebchuk & Fried 2004). Limited liability then separates the downside from the decision. The party that chooses the risk does not carry the tail, so the private differential selects the exposure even where the social one does not.

The chapter's honesty is worth naming here. It reports the case that refutes the obvious remedy: tightening the contract does not restore the missing dimension, because the dimension was not in the contract's vocabulary. Credit rationing at equilibrium is the same structure from the lender's side (Stiglitz & Weiss 1981), and the principal-agent relation is not a malfunction to be repaired but a form whose cost has to be carried somewhere (Lowenstein 2000).

## What this stratum does not settle

It does not price reputation, and it does not claim that every principal-agent relation is a trap. The claim is conditional: where the payoff depends on the proxy rather than on the thing, the proxy becomes the thing, and the stratum's five orders are five ways that substitution shows up.

Nor does it supply a design that removes the problem. What it supplies is a reading. When a number in these orders looks too good, the question is not whether it was computed correctly but who benefits from its being believed, and that question is answerable from the artifact, which is what makes the stratum auditable at all.
