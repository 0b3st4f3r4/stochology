## **Abstract**

This essay presents the theoretical, methodological, and normative architecture of **Stochology**, an integrative framework and transversal research program that investigates the validity, formal limits, and institutional responsibility of human judgment under structural uncertainty. Addressed to methodologists, applied statisticians, operations researchers, and philosophers and sociologists of science and risk, the essay argues that knowing and deliberating when action must precede the outcome requires analyzing a single coupled circuit across three planes: the material-historical world, the documentary archive and formal model, and institutional deliberation. Combining an immanent critique of methodological inversions with abductive triangulation across statistical, physical-ecological, and ethical-sociological lineages, we map three mediating filters and seven constitutive tensions where analytical instruments generate specific pathologies when treated as self-sufficient. From this traversal emerge three safeguards that are non-substitutable near their critical survival boundaries—the **epistemic margin** ($S_{\mathrm{epi}}$), the **vital margin** ($S_{\mathrm{vital}}$), and **fully apt agency under institutional governance** ($S_{\mathrm{gov}}$)—whose joint threshold viability is tracked by a Liebig–Leontief bottleneck index,

$$M_{\mathrm{Leontief}} = \min\bigl(S_{\mathrm{epi}},\, S_{\mathrm{vital}},\, S_{\mathrm{gov}}\bigr),$$

articulated through three structural collapse propositions, illustrated and stress-tested via a $2\times 2\times 2$ factorial ablation and $100$-seed Monte Carlo suite in a reproducible sequential reference environment (`CommunityReserveEnv`), and subjected to internal Goodhart brakes and pre-registered severe empirical falsifiers.

**Keywords:** Stochology; ethics of aim; immanent critique; partial identification; non-ergodicity; performativity; institutional responsibility; Liebig–Leontief complementarity.

## **I. Introduction: The Genealogy of the Shot and the Program of Stochology**

Technical, linguistic, and institutional mediators carve up reality long before it reaches human deliberation. Economic indicators, forensic reports, medical records, demographic censuses, historical chronicles, and digital databases never offer events in the raw. As the history and sociology of quantification have shown (Porter 1995; Desrosières 1998; Bowker and Star 1999), such instruments deliver a world already formatted by administrative conventions of equivalence (*conventions d'équivalence*) and classification infrastructures designed to make certain ways of seeing, comparing, and governing possible. Knowing and deciding are therefore never equivalent to the serene contemplation of a motionless landscape. They unfold as a permanent friction between the administrative effort to order experience into concepts and the material resistance of historical and biological life. Whenever an agent must act before the course of events is fully revealed—as happens to the clinician facing an acute syndrome, the magistrate weighing incomplete evidence, the planner managing critical infrastructure, or the historian confronting a mutilated archive—the decisive question ceases to be the raw volume of accumulated records. The central problem becomes the cognitive and moral integrity of judgment while the outcome of action remains open.

The history of Western thought preserved the trace of this problem in the etymology of the word *stochastic*. When Jacob Bernoulli (1713/2006), in Part IV, Chapter 2 of his posthumous *Ars Conjectandi*, sought a name for the art of measuring the probability of uncertain things, he defined it as *ars conjectandi sive stochastice*—the art of conjecturing, or stochastics. In doing so, Bernoulli drew upon a Greek semantic family bound to the practical experience of aiming at a mark through opacity. In classical Greek lexicography (Liddell, Scott, and Jones 1940, s.v. *στόχος*), the noun **στόχος (*stókhos*)** originally denoted a physical upright post or pillar set up as a mark for archers to shoot at, and by direct extension the act of aiming, the shot, or a conjectural guess—a cognitive projection that Latin rendered as *coniectura*. From *stókhos* derive the verb **στοχάζεσθαι (*stokházesthai*)**—to aim at a mark, to endeavor to hit or divine—and the agent noun **στοχαστής (*stokhastēs*)**, the conjecturer or interpreter of signs. Philological precision requires distinguishing this root both from the noun for the archer (**τοξότης, *toxótēs***), which derives from a separate root meaning the bow (**τόξον, *tóxon***), and from the noun **σκοπός (*skopós*)**, the watched target or lookout (connected to *skopeîn*, "to behold," borrowed into Latin as *scopus* alongside the native Latin equivalents ***meta*** and ***propositum***, whereas native Latin *signum* corresponds instead to Greek **σημεῖον, *sēmeîon***, "sign"). What bridges the lexical field of *stókhos* to the classical figure of the archer (*toxótēs*) is the compound **εὐστοχία (*eustokhía*)** and its verb **εὐστοχεῖν (*eustokheîn*)**: "good aim" or skill in hitting the mark across an uncertain medium. Whereas *skopós* names the external object under watch, *stókhos* and *eustokhía* name the disciplined orientation of judgment across opacity.

Centuries before the mathematical calculus of probabilities, Plato granted *stokházesthai* a central place in the classification of human knowledge. In the *Philebus* (55e–56c), examining the structure of the arts and crafts (**τέχναι, *tékhnai***), Plato divides them by an epistemic criterion: the degree of exactness (**ἀκρίβεια, *akríbeia***) afforded by measurement, number, and weighing (*métron*, *arithmós*, *stathmós*). On one side stand the arts supported by the rule, the lathe, the compass, the plumb line, and the scale, whose paradigm is carpentry and building construction. On the other side stand the arts that cannot rely on a mechanical gauge and must operate through practiced conjecture and aim (**στοχασμός, *stokhasmós***)—beginning with music, which attunes consonance "not by measure, but by the conjectural aim of practiced study and exercise" (*ou métrōi allà melétēs stokhasmōi*, 56a, where **μελέτη, *melétē*** denotes disciplined practice), followed by clinical medicine, agriculture, maritime navigation, and military command. Crucially, Plato's own evaluative valence in the *Philebus* ranked the conjectural arts *below* the measuring arts in scientific purity (*akríbeia*). Stochology consciously inverts that Platonic hierarchy for open historical and biological systems: wherever contingency, non-ergodic time, and strategic reactivity render mechanical gauges incomplete, forcing false *akríbeia* onto a turbulent medium produces dogmatic blindness, making self-critical *stokhasmós* the higher form of practical rationality.

Whether such conjectural practices truly deserved the status of a rational art (*tékhnē*) rather than mere knack or routine (*empeiría*, as Plato pressed against rhetoric in the *Gorgias* 463a–465a) became one of the defining controversies of ancient epistemology and medicine. In the fifth century BCE, treatises of the *Corpus Hippocraticum* approached this terrain from three angles: *On Ancient Medicine* (*De prisca medicina*, ch. 9; Hippocrates 1923) maintained that the physician, confronting the individual constitution of each sick body, holds no exact arithmetic measure of diet beyond the "sensation of the body" (*toû sṓmatos tḕn aísthēsin*) and must aim (*stokhásasthai*) without fixed weights; the opening sentence of the *Aphorisms* (*Aphorismi* I.1; Hippocrates 1923) anchored clinical action in the fleeting critical instant (**καιρός, *kairós***: *ho bíos brakhýs, hē dè tékhnē makrē, ho dè kairòs oxýs*—"life is short, the art long, the critical instant acute"); and the semiotic treatises *Prognostic* (*Prognosticon*) and *On the Art* (*De arte*) codified how the clinician reads observable symptoms and diagnostic signs (**σημεῖα, *sēmeîa***; **τεκμήρια, *tekmēria***). In the fourth century BCE, Aristotle (*Nicomachean Ethics* II.6, 1106b15–28; VI.5, 1140a25–b30) placed this same conjectural aim at the heart of ethical and political deliberation: moral virtue is "capable of aiming at the intermediate" (*stokhastikos tou mesou*), and practical wisdom (**φρόνησις, *phrónēsis***) is the reasoned capacity to act well with regard to human goods across contingent particulars that admit of being otherwise. In the Hellenistic and Roman periods, debates among medical and philosophical schools—systematized in the Aristotelian commentator tradition by Alexander of Aphrodisias (*In Aristotelis Topicorum libros octo commentaria*) and in medicine by Galen of Pergamon (1985, 2000; *De sectis*; *Ars medica*)—crystallized the technical category of **στοχαστικὴ τέχνη (*stokhastikē tékhnē*)**: a "conjectural art" in which method is fully rational and accountable to criticism even though material success remains exposed to external contingencies beyond the practitioner's mastery. Alongside this scholastic medical tradition, modern classical scholarship led by Marcel Detienne and Jean-Pierre Vernant (1974) reconstructed the broader archaic and classical figure of **μῆτις (*mētis*)**—the plastic, vigilant intelligence of the pilot and clinician who navigate shifting currents at the acute *kairós*.

The status of this classical genealogy must be stated without Whiggish teleology. As historians of science and philosophy have emphasized (Sambursky 1956; Hacking 1975), Plato, the Hippocratic authors, Antipater of Tarsus, Alexander of Aphrodisias, and Galen were theorizing qualitative diagnostic and ethical prudence rather than a quantitative calculus of relative frequencies, which emerged only in the seventeenth century. Connecting ancient *stokhastikē tékhnē* and Antipater's Stoic archer (Section 6.1) to Bernoulli's *stochastice* is therefore an **explicit structural analogy across distinct epistemic epochs**, not a claim of unbroken doctrinal filiation, and etymology licenses no mathematical theorem. Rather, this genealogy performs a precise conceptual task: it recovers the structural separation between the internal discipline of the aim (*télos* / method) and the external contingency of the hit (*skopós* / realization), asking what it means to aim justly when the map is incomplete and another person will bear the impact of the arrow.

### **1.1. Definition of Stochology, the Three-Plane Circuit, and Theoretical Demarcation**

To address this problem in the age of formal models and bureaucratic institutions, **Stochology** (from *stókhos*, aim or conjecture, and *lógos*, reasoned account) is formulated here as an integrative framework that investigates the conditions of validity, formal limits, and institutional responsibility of judgment under structural uncertainty.

What justifies organizing this inquiry as a distinct integrative framework rather than leaving its parts dispersed across statistics, decision theory, and moral philosophy? A transversal program is defined not by a monopoly over a single technical tool, but by the delimitation of a unit of analysis that specialized fields habitually sever into non-communicating fragments. Whereas statistical methodology examines the mapping from phenomena to data, decision theory analyzes the mapping from probability distributions to rational choice, and sociology or law studies the impact of rules on society, Stochology takes as its indivisible unit of analysis the complete circuit of mediation across three planes:

1. *The plane of the material and historical world:* the terrain where events unfold in irreversible time, marked by extreme shocks, interdependencies among individuals, unequal social visibility, and human agents who react strategically when observed, classified, or governed;
2. *The plane of representation, model, and archive:* the documentary and symbolic space—whether a judicial file, a historical archive, a sample survey, or an algorithmic model—in which the world is compressed into classifications, tables, and equations subject to structural discrepancy and selective omissions;
3. *The plane of deliberation and institutions:* the practical sphere in which individuals, committees, or organizations act upon those imperfect representations, allocating scarce reserves, imposing norms, and distributing across social groups the benefits of success and the burdens of error.

The central thesis of Stochology is that grave failures of judgment under uncertainty rarely originate inside a single isolated plane; they originate in the unguarded passages from one plane to another. Three direct transits drive institutional action. First, in the passage from the world to the archive, vulnerable populations and structural dependencies are lost through selective non-recording and cluster echoing. Second, in the passage from the archive to the decision, simplifying point summaries (such as the lone arithmetic mean) conceal convex tail hazards and overwhelm human deliberative capacity. Third, in the passage from the decision back to the world, the intervention itself alters strategic behavior and consumes finite physical and temporal buffers. Watching over these three direct transits requires three corresponding reflexive locks: requiring the model to declare its causal and structural premises before fitting numbers; requiring the institution to seal its hypotheses, failed attempts, and error-liability rules before consulting the archive; and requiring the temporal scale of evidence and auditing to respect the physical survival clock of the system under governance.

Crucially, this three-plane mediation circuit does not begin only when a state bureaucracy or corporate board compiles a spreadsheet; it operates across a continuous scale stretching from the **micro-hermeneutic ecology of interpersonal communication** to macro-institutional governance. Long before an organization reifies a proxy metric into an audit target (Goodhart 1975; Strathern 1997), the pathology of turning mediations into instruments of control takes root in the intersubjective anxiety to master how one is received by the other—attempting to annex the external reception (**σκοπός, *skopós***) through impression management and mirror-surveillance rather than caring for the internal integrity of the aim (**τέλος, *télos***: clarity, presence, and vulnerability). Just as a statistical model must declare its partial-identification interval $[L_t, U_t]$ so that formal concepts do not amputate the singular remainder of the **Non-Identical** (Adorno 1966/1973), a human subject in dialogue must preserve what Georges Canguilhem (1943/2009) called a "margin of tolerance for the inconsistencies of the environment" (*une marge de tolérance des infidélités du milieu*), refusing to reduce the interlocutor to an extension of one's own image. The Nuclear Triad $(S_{\mathrm{epi}}, S_{\mathrm{vital}}, S_{\mathrm{gov}})$ thus rests on a three-fold ethical and phenomenological refusal that unites intimate life and public institutions: renouncing narcissistic control over intimate reception ($S_{\mathrm{epi}}$ and $S_{\mathrm{vital}}$ as dialogical openness and tolerance for contingency), exposing the psychosocial architecture of bad faith and scapegoating when pacts are betrayed, and preserving the communicative transparency of decision premises in the governance of institutions ($S_{\mathrm{gov}}$).

Situating Stochology with precision requires demarcating how it connects and restructures three major twentieth- and twenty-first-century traditions on uncertainty:

- *Ambiguity, partial identification, and the separation of description from prescription:* In 1921, Frank Knight (1921) and John Maynard Keynes (1921) independently warned against reducing all epistemic doubt to a single numerical probability, distinguishing measurable *risk* from unquantifiable *uncertainty* and separating the balance of probability from the *weight of argument* ($V$). Four decades later, Daniel Ellsberg's (1961) two-urn thought experiments showed that reasonable decision-makers distinguish known proportions (risk) from unknown proportions (ambiguity) in ways that systematically violate Leonard J. Savage's (1954) *Sure-Thing Principle*—a system of subjective expected utility (SEU) that Savage himself restricted to logically closed "small worlds" (a logical completeness assumption distinct from physical *ergodicity*, examined in Section 5.2). To operate when a unique prior is untenable, Peter Walley (1991) developed imprecise probabilities, Peter J. Huber (1964, 1981) founded robust statistics, and Charles Manski (2003, 2013) established *partial identification* in econometrics. Crucially, Stochology enforces a strict boundary that applied work often blurs: partial identification is a *descriptive* epistemic discipline (determining the sharp identification region $\Theta_I$ compatible with the observed data and maintained assumptions), whereas choosing an action given $\Theta_I$ requires an explicit *prescriptive* decision rule.
- *Taxonomy of decision rules and lexicographic survival constraints:* In decision theory and engineering, distinct prescriptive rules under ambiguity are frequently conflated under the generic label of "robustness." When Yakov Ben-Haim (2006) proposed *Info-Gap* theory—maximizing local immunity to error around a nominal reference model—Moshe Sniedovich (2007, 2012) proved that if the reference model is misspecified, local robustness creates a "No-Man's Land" of policies that collapse under global deviations. Replacing local robustness requires distinguishing four non-equivalent global criteria:
  1. Savage's (1954) *Subjective Expected Utility* (SEU), which averages over $\Theta_I$ with a single subjective prior;
  2. Itzhak Gilboa and David Schmeidler's (1989) *Maxmin Expected Utility* (MEU), axiomatized over Anscombe–Aumann acts via six axioms—*weak order*, *certainty-independence (C-independence)*, *Archimedean continuity*, *monotonicity*, *uncertainty aversion* (preference for randomization/hedging between indifferent acts), and *non-degeneracy*—which maximizes minimum expected utility $\min_{P \in \mathcal{P}} \mathbb{E}_P[u(a, \theta)]$ over a closed convex credal set of priors $\mathcal{P}$;
  3. Abraham Wald's (1945) *Minimax Risk* (or *Maximin Utility*, $\max_a \min_{\theta \in \Theta_I} u(a, \theta)$), which evaluates the worst *point state* $\theta \in \Theta_I$ (coinciding with MEU when $\mathcal{P}$ contains all distributions supported on $\Theta_I$, but differing whenever $\mathcal{P}$ restricts prior shapes or state-space dependencies); and
  4. Savage's (1951) and Manski's (2003, 2013) *Minimax Regret*, which minimizes maximum opportunity loss $\max_{\theta \in \Theta_I}[\max_{a'} u(a', \theta) - u(a, \theta)]$ relative to the ex-post optimal act.

  Neither Wald's maximin nor Manski's minimax regret guarantees a physical survival floor by itself: if every action leads to ruin in the worst state, Wald's criterion merely selects the least bad ruin, while in a binary treatment choice where the identified interval $[L_M, U_M]$ straddles the per-unit treatment cost $C$ ($L_M < C < U_M$), maximin dictates universal abstention whereas minimax regret recommends fractional treatment $a^* = (U_M - C)/(U_M - L_M)$ `[Assumed]`. Whereas Arthur D. Roy's (1952) classic *safety-first* criterion minimized the Chebyshev upper bound on disaster probability $\mathbb{P}(R \le d) \le \sigma^2 / (\mu - d)^2$ within a single-stage mean-variance portfolio, Stochology formulates a *two-stage lexicographic survival-first and minimax-regret rule* coupling Roy's disaster-avoidance principle with David Simchi-Levi, William Schmidt, and Yehua Wei's (2014) dynamic recovery physics: actions that violate the physical survival condition ($\mathrm{TTS}_t \ge \mathrm{TTR}_t$, or ruin probability above a hard floor $\epsilon$) are filtered out first; within the survival-admissible set, residual ambiguity is adjudicated by minimax regret for reversible allocations and by worst-case bounds when irreversible thresholds are at stake (detailed with comparative statics in Section 4.2, Table 3).
- *The integrative contribution of Stochology:* Each of these traditions solved a decisive local problem: Manski addressed archival missingness; Wald, Savage, and Gilboa–Schmeidler formalized choice under ambiguity; Roy, Kelly, and Simchi-Levi addressed survival over time; Callon, MacKenzie, and Goodhart analyzed performative reactivity; Sosa, Scanlon, and Hansson theorized epistemic virtue and the ethics of risk. Applied in isolation, however, each safeguard remains vulnerable to failure on the adjacent planes. Computing an unimpeachable partial-identification interval on the plane of the archive does not prevent ruin if operations run at $96\text{--}100\%$ `[Assumed]` capacity on the temporal plane, or if limited-liability contracts on the institutional plane reward executives for liquidating safety buffers. The specific object of Stochology is the structural interdependence of these three planes.

### **1.2. The Nuclear Triad, Liebig–Leontief Complementarity, and the Three Collapse Propositions**

To keep the mediation circuit intact across world, archive, and institution, Stochology maintains that responsible judgment under structural uncertainty requires the simultaneous presence of three safeguards:

1. **The epistemic margin (on the plane of knowledge and the archive):** the methodological and ethical duty to refuse false point exactness. Whenever archives suffer from selective non-recording, clustering, or structural model discrepancy, the analyst must declare the sharp partial-identification interval $[L_t, U_t]$ compatible with the evidence (Manski 2003; Imbens and Manski 2004; Lee 2009), the structural discrepancy between simulator and reality (Kennedy and O'Hagan 2001), the variance-adjusted effective sample size $n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}$ alongside Meng's selection-bias defect (Kish 1965; Moulton 1986; Meng 2018), and the antidogmatic reserve required to learn from refutation (Lindley 1985);
2. **The vital margin (on the material and temporal plane):** the operational duty to preserve physical, biological, financial, and temporal slack—across both *operational buffer stocks* and *ecological attraction-basin width*—against absorbing barriers and hysteretic tipping points. It requires maintaining capacity headroom ($1 - \rho_t$) against heavy-traffic queue explosions (Kingman 1961; Scarf 1960), shrinking multiplicative exposure under sampling estimation variance so that volatility drain does not trigger bankruptcy (Itô 1951; Kelly 1956; Baker and McHale 2013), ensuring that local Time-to-Survive exceeds Time-to-Recover ($\mathrm{TTS}_t \ge \mathrm{TTR}_t$, Simchi-Levi, Schmidt, and Wei 2014), and preserving biological normativity and ecological basin width $D_t$ against allostatic contraction (Canguilhem 1943/2009; Holling 1973; McEwen 1998; Scheffer et al. 2009);
3. **Fully apt agency under institutional governance (on the ethical and political plane):** the constitutional duty to bind the act of judging to symmetry of risk and accountability. It requires translating the reflective competence to withhold an unsafe shot (Sosa 2015) into collective constitutional vetoes and independent audit channels with affected-citizen participation (Beer 1972; List and Pettit 2011), eliminating testimonial credibility discounts against frontline alarms (Fricker 2007, 2010), protecting deliberative attention from alert flooding (Gigerenzer and Hoffrage 1995; Zheng and Meister 2025), and structuring contracts with downside clawbacks at the decision-making top, limited-liability protection at the vulnerable base, and public disclosure of error trade-offs (Jensen and Meckling 1976; Holmström 1979; Holmström and Milgrom 1991; Innes 1990; Chouldechova 2017).

*Upfront Normative License and Division of Normative Registers:* Because Sections III through V employ prescriptive language ("must," "duty," "obligation"), the normative architecture of the essay must be stated at the outset (and constructed in full in Section 6.1). Taken in isolation for a solitary individual whose actions affect no one else, the epistemic margin is an *epistemic norm of truth-tracking* (Clifford 1877; Adler 2002; Fantl and McGrath 2009) and the vital margin is a *prudential norm of self-preservation* (Roy 1952; Kelly 1956). In institutional governance, however, where decision-makers impose unbuffered tail risks and classification errors onto third parties who did not consent to bear them (Hansson 2013), an explicitly defended contractualist premise—Thomas M. Scanlon's (1998) principle of *non-rejectability* and *due care* grounded in the asymmetric agency-destroying finality of irreversible ruin—transforms all three safeguards into **strict moral obligations of interpersonal justice**.

Why are these three safeguards aggregated near their critical boundary via a **Liebig–Leontief minimum** rather than an additive arithmetic mean or a smooth Cobb–Douglas product—and what is the exact epistemic status of this aggregator? Three methodological clarifications are essential:

1. *Unified commensuration principle (normalized critical-headroom distance):* Raw interval coverage, physical queue headroom, ecological basin radius, and constitutional veto rules do not share a common physical unit. To avoid arbitrary ad hoc scaling, Stochology maps every component $c_{i,k,t}$ across all three planes onto $[0, 1]$ using a **single unifying measurement principle**: *normalized critical-headroom distance*,
   $$c_{i,k,t} = \operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}\!\left(\frac{x_{i,k,t} - x_{i,k,\mathrm{crit}}}{x_{i,k,\mathrm{safe}} - x_{i,k,\mathrm{crit}}}\right) \in [0, 1],$$
   where $0$ marks the physical or institutional collapse boundary ($x = x_{\mathrm{crit}}$: trivial full-support interval width $y_{\max} - y_{\min}$, heavy-traffic queue knee $\rho_{\mathrm{crit}}$, Kelly overbetting ceiling $f_{\mathrm{ceil}}$, collapsed ecological basin $D_{\mathrm{crit}} = 0$, Critical Slowing Down bifurcation $\rho_{1,\mathrm{crit}}$, or disabled $S3^*$ veto) and $1$ marks the full precautionary design target ($x = x_{\mathrm{safe}}$). All three indices $S_{\mathrm{epi}}, S_{\mathrm{vital}}, S_{\mathrm{gov}} \in [0, 1]$ thus share a common dimensionless interpretation: the fraction of safe operating headroom remaining above that plane's failure threshold $\tau \in (0, 1)$ (set to $\tau = 0.50$ `[Assumed]` in the reference benchmark, and tested across $\tau \in [0.35, 0.65]$).
2. *Epistemic status of $M_{\mathrm{Leontief}}$ (threshold bottleneck index vs. interior CES trade-offs):* Stochology does *not* advance $M_{\mathrm{Leontief}}$ as a cardinal social welfare function over interior consumption bundles. Well above the critical threshold ($S_{\mathrm{epi}}, S_{\mathrm{vital}}, S_{\mathrm{gov}} \gg \tau$), partial trade-offs exist at the margin and can be represented by a Constant Elasticity of Substitution (CES) function $M_{\mathrm{CES}}(\sigma) = \bigl(\frac{1}{3}\sum_{i} S_i^{(\sigma-1)/\sigma}\bigr)^{\sigma/(\sigma-1)}$ with moderate elasticity $\sigma > 0$ (governed by Reflexive Brake 2 in Section 7.2). Near or below the critical survival boundary ($S_i < \tau$), however, the three planes form a **serial causal chain of viability** in which elasticity of substitution collapses ($\sigma \to 0^+$): an uncalibrated, downward-biased archive ($S_{\mathrm{epi}} \to 0$) causes the institution to miscalculate the baseline around which physical buffers are sized; zero physical or basin slack ($S_{\mathrm{vital}} \to 0$) causes immediate ruin under a heavy-tailed shock even when the shock was accurately bounded; and a captured governance regime ($S_{\mathrm{gov}} \to 0$) actively liquidates the buffer and weaponizes epistemic uncertainty as an alibi for inaction. Taking the $\sigma \to 0^+$ limit of $M_{\mathrm{CES}}(\sigma)$ yields the **Liebig–Leontief bottleneck index** (`TriadicIntegrity` in `crates/stochology/src/synthesis.rs`):
   $$M_{\mathrm{Leontief}}\bigl(S_{\mathrm{epi}},\, S_{\mathrm{vital}},\, S_{\mathrm{gov}}\bigr) = \lim_{\sigma \to 0^+} M_{\mathrm{CES}}(\sigma) = \min\bigl(S_{\mathrm{epi}},\, S_{\mathrm{vital}},\, S_{\mathrm{gov}}\bigr) \in [0, 1] \qquad \text{\texttt{[Assumed bottleneck index]}}$$
3. *Empirical discriminating condition between $M_{\mathrm{Leontief}}$ and compensatory aggregators (and its regime boundary):* Because $M_{\mathrm{Leontief}}$ is formulated as a bottleneck viability index, it admits a direct discriminating test against compensatory aggregators such as the arithmetic mean $M_{\mathrm{arith}} = \frac{1}{3}(S_{\mathrm{epi}} + S_{\mathrm{vital}} + S_{\mathrm{gov}})$ ($\sigma \to \infty$): when a single safeguard is suppressed ($S_i \approx 0$) while the other two are held near $0.90\text{--}1.00$, $M_{\mathrm{arith}} \approx 0.60 > \tau = 0.50$ classifies the institution as "above threshold" (safe), whereas $M_{\mathrm{Leontief}} \approx 0.00 < \tau$ predicts structural collapse. In both the reference simulation (Table 1, where $M_{\mathrm{arith}} = 0.503$ and $0.612$ commit a $100\%$ false-safe error on `NoVitalMargin` and `NoGovernance` across $100/100$ seeds despite $100\%$ ruin) and empirical Falsifiers 1–4 (Section 7.2), non-substitutability is corroborated when an absorbing survival barrier or severe archival/institutional defect is active so that single-axis deficits trigger ruin or substantive excess harm that high scores on the remaining two axes fail to prevent ($F_1\text{--}F_3$ corroborated with $d_{\mathrm{obs}} \in \{0.321, 2.514, 0.385\} > d_{\mathrm{crit}}$ and $\mathrm{SEV} \in [0.972, 0.995]$ `[Simulated]`); conversely, in interior regimes with moderate shocks and a doubled physical reserve buffer (`CompensatoryBufferAgent`), partial buffer compensation is possible and unconditional non-compensability is genuinely refuted ($F_4$: $d_{\mathrm{obs}} = 0.077 \le d_{\mathrm{crit}} = 0.1104, \mathrm{SEV} = 0.996, \text{refuted} = \text{true}$ `[Simulated]`).

To illustrate and stress-test this architecture transparently—distinguishing internal mechanism verification from external empirical validation—the companion pure-Rust verification suite implements a sequential agent–world–institution reference environment (`CommunityReserveEnv` in `crates/stochology/src/engine.rs`, executed via `cargo run --release --bin stochology`, deterministic `SplitMix64` stream with pre-registered `DEFAULT_SEED = 2026` and $100$-seed Monte Carlo suite `seeds = 2000..2099`, horizon $T = 24$ shifts, initial reserve $R_0 = 45.0$, demand support $[y_{\min}, y_{\max}] = [5.0, 65.0]$, and threshold $\tau = 0.50$ `[Assumed]`). To maintain **aggregation consistency within and across axes** (avoiding the inconsistency of taking a multiplicative product inside an index while taking a minimum at the top), every multi-component index in `CommunityReserveEnv` aggregates its normalized critical-headroom components via the non-compensatory bottleneck minimum $\min(\cdot)$:

- **Epistemic Margin Index ($S_{\mathrm{epi}}$, `[Assumed]` operational specification):** empirical coverage of true latent community demand $S_t$ by the declared interval $[L_t, U_t]$, penalized quadratically by normalized interval width so that lazy $[y_{\min}, y_{\max}]$ inflation yields zero score (Reflexive Brake 1):
  $$S_{\mathrm{epi}} = \left(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} \mathbf{1}\bigl\{S_t \in [L_t, U_t]\bigr\}\right) \cdot \left[1 - \left(\frac{\overline{U - L}}{y_{\max} - y_{\min}}\right)^2\right]$$
- **Vital Margin Index ($S_{\mathrm{vital}}$, `[Assumed]` operational specification uniting operational stock slack $S_{\mathrm{stock}, t}$ and dynamical ecological-engineering basin resilience $S_{\mathrm{basin}, t}$):** mean over shifts of $\min(S_{\mathrm{stock}, t},\, S_{\mathrm{basin}, t})$, where operational stock slack $S_{\mathrm{stock}, t}$ takes the bottleneck minimum across (i) Kingman utilization headroom relative to the heavy-traffic knee $\rho_{\mathrm{crit}} = 0.88$ `[Assumed]`, (ii) Baker–McHale epistemic Kelly leverage safety relative to the overbetting ceiling $f_{\mathrm{ceil}} = 1.60$ `[Assumed]` (computed strictly from the **variance-effective sample size** $n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} = \min(n_{\mathrm{cluster}}, n_{\mathrm{Kish}})$ rather than Meng's bias scalar), and (iii) reserve buffer commitment relative to $B_{\min} = 10.0$ `[Assumed]`; and **attraction-basin resilience** $S_{\mathrm{basin}, t}$ couples **Holling's ecological basin width ratio** $D_t / D_0 = (x_{\mathrm{high}}^*(A_t) - x_{\mathrm{tip}}^*(A_t))/D_0$ (contracted by cumulative McEwen allostatic wear $A_t$ whenever $\rho_{\mathrm{base}, t} > 0.85$) with **Scheffer's engineering recovery-rate / Critical Slowing Down margin** $(1 - \hat{\rho}_{1,t})/(1 - \rho_{1,\mathrm{crit}})$ at $\rho_{1,\mathrm{crit}} = 0.85$ `[Assumed]`:
  $$S_{\mathrm{vital}} = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} \min\Biggl(\underbrace{\min\!\left(\operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}\!\left(\frac{1 - \rho_{\mathrm{base}, t}}{1 - \rho_{\mathrm{crit}}}\right),\, \operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}(f_{\mathrm{ceil}} - f_t),\, \operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}\!\left(\frac{B_t}{B_{\min}}\right)\right)}_{S_{\mathrm{stock}, t}\text{ (operational stock bottleneck)}},\; \underbrace{\min\!\left(\operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}\!\left(\frac{D_t}{D_0}\right),\, \operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}\!\left(\frac{1 - \hat{\rho}_{1, t}}{1 - \rho_{1, \mathrm{crit}}}\right)\right)}_{S_{\mathrm{basin}, t}\text{ (ecological width }D_t/D_0\text{ \& CSD bottleneck)}}\Biggr)$$
- **Institutional Governance Index ($S_{\mathrm{gov}}$, `[Assumed]` operational specification):** mean over shifts of the bottleneck minimum across active constitutional circuit-breaker authority ($\mathbf{1}\{\text{brake}_t\}$), undiscounted reception of frontline algedonic signals ($1 - \delta_{\mathrm{cred}, t}$), and restraint of single-metric proxy pressure ($1 - \beta_{\mathrm{proxy}, t}$):
  $$S_{\mathrm{gov}} = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} \min\Bigl(\mathbf{1}\bigl\{\text{brake}_t\bigr\},\; 1 - \delta_{\mathrm{cred}, t},\; 1 - \beta_{\mathrm{proxy}, t}\Bigr)$$

## **II. The Method: Immanent Critique, Abductive Triangulation, and the Heuristic Topology**

### **2.1. Immanent Critique, Reification, and the Operational Incidence of the Non-Identical**

Why do scientific and administrative institutions fall repeatedly into these three collapse modes? Stochology advances the hypothesis that systematic failures of judgment under uncertainty arise not merely from technical incompetence or individual malice, but from the internal dynamic of rationalization whenever a methodological instrument forgets the conditions that bound its validity. To analyze this dynamic, our method combines three distinct operations that must be named with transparency rather than conflated under a single label:

1. **Immanent critique** of four recurring methodological inversions (tracking how each analytical instrument defeats its own declared aim when absolutized);
2. **Abductive triangulation** across three convergent disciplinary lineages (Section 2.2); and
3. **Explicit normative reconstruction** via contractualist risk ethics (Section 6.1).

The first operation draws on the tradition of critical theory—specifically Georg Lukács's (1923/1971) analysis of **reification (*Verdinglichung*)**, Max Horkheimer's (1937/2002) demarcation between **traditional theory** (which treats the division of scientific labor and institutional categories as natural facts) and **critical theory** (which reflects on their historical production and social function), Max Horkheimer and Theodor W. Adorno's (1944/2002) diagnosis in *Dialectic of Enlightenment* of how instrumental formalization inverts into domination, and Adorno's (1966/1973) epistemology of the **Non-Identical (*das Nichtidentische*)** in *Negative Dialectics*.

In the history of inquiry and governance, reason repeatedly constructs a disciplining instrument to cure a real defect of unreflective practice; yet when that instrument is reified as self-sufficient, it generates a higher-order blindness by its own standards:

- To overcome arbitrary guesswork, inquiry demands *structured theory*; yet theory, insulated by auxiliary assumptions and institutional interests, begins to select confirming evidence and patch over anomalies;
- To overcome verbal vagueness, thought turns to *mathematical formalization*; yet formal models, seduced by analytical tractability, amputate qualitative transition zones, convex asymmetries, and heavy tails in favor of a docile average;
- To overcome the fragility of small samples, states and corporations build *monumental archives and digital registries*; yet archival volume fosters an illusion of omniscience while driving nominal error bands toward zero around populations whose most vulnerable members were systematically omitted;
- To become practically effective, knowledge translates itself into *rules of intervention and performance targets*; yet imposing a static indicator upon a reactive social and material world induces strategic gaming, erodes causal mechanisms, and consumes finite buffers in irreversible time.

Clarifying the philosophical status of this movement is essential to avoid a performative contradiction. Whereas Hegelian dialectics treats *determinate negation* (*Aufhebung*) as a synthetic engine culminating in a closed system, Adorno's (1966/1973) *Negative Dialectics* insists that conceptual identification is constitutive of thought and that the **Non-Identical**—the singular, historical, and material excess of the object—resists exhaustion by any concept, parameter, or closed table of categories. For Adorno, respecting the Non-Identical is not an anti-intellectual refusal of construction; it requires assembling **constellations** of concepts that expose where each isolated concept falls short of its object. If Stochology claimed that declaring a partial-identification interval $[L, U]$ "captures" the Non-Identical, or presented its three filters and seven tensions as a closed transcendental deduction, it would commit the very reification Lukács (1923/1971) and Adorno (1966/1973) diagnosed.

At the same time, if the Non-Identical were merely quarantined inside a separate "ontological register" with zero incidence on formal practice, it would be reduced to an inert Kantian *thing-in-itself* (*Ding an sich*). In Stochology, the Non-Identical is never domesticated or exhausted by formalism, but **its resistance leaves three negative operational traces inside the formal apparatus**—while an explicit **Clause of Non-Closure** states what each index $S_i$ necessarily excludes:

1. First, in Manski's partial-identification interval $[L_M, U_M]$, the width $U_M - L_M$ marks the quantitative trace of what the archive failed to record—yet **$S_{\mathrm{epi}}$ excludes qualitative hermeneutical silences**: if an administrative archive lacks the conceptual category to register a form of suffering (Fricker 2007; Bowker and Star 1999), interval arithmetic over existing columns cannot invent the missing category, requiring ethnographic and historical source criticism (Section 7.1);
2. Second, in Marc Kennedy and Anthony O'Hagan's (2001) structural discrepancy function $\delta(x) = y(x) - f(x, \theta)$, predictive uncertainty widens as action moves outside historical support—yet **$S_{\mathrm{vital}}$ excludes unmodeled ontological novelties**: tracking $D_t / D_0$ and $\hat{\rho}_{1,t}$ along known state variables does not eliminate radical qualitative regime creation beyond the state space; and
3. Third, in Bengt Holmström and Paul Milgrom's (1991) multitask incentive theorem and Stafford Beer's (1972) algedonic channel, formal contracts restrain proxy pressure—yet **$S_{\mathrm{gov}}$ excludes the living political praxis of the governed**: a constitutional veto rule is an institutional shell that remains dead letter without active contestation by frontline workers and affected communities.

The seven-tension topology summarized in Table 2 is therefore an open, revisable **institutional constellation** mapped onto the seven chronological junctures of the action loop—framing a question (Tension 1), testing a hypothesis over time (Tension 2), formalizing a summary measure (Tension 3), aggregating an archive (Tension 4), intervening in a reactive social field (Tension 5), compounding exposure across irreversible time (Tension 6), and assigning moral and contractual liability for the outcome (Tension 7). Note that while Tensions 1 through 6 each occupy a single subsection, Tension 7 is examined across two subsections (Sections 6.1 and 6.2) to separate the individual ethics of aim and moral luck (*First Face*) from collective corporate and constitutional design (*Second Face*).

### **2.2. Abductive Triangulation Across Three Convergent Lineages**

Across these seven junctures, Stochology braids together three distinct intellectual lineages whose convergence provides abductive support for the Nuclear Triad:

1. *The epistemological and statistical lineage (partial identification, severe testing, and model discrepancy):* articulating Adorno's (1966/1973) critique of identificatory closure with Deborah Mayo's (2018) severe-testing error statistics, Charles Manski's (2003, 2013) partial identification, Marc Kennedy and Anthony O'Hagan's (2001) Bayesian model discrepancy, and Xiao-Li Meng's (2018) analysis of archival selection defects, it establishes that the gap between representation and reality must remain visible as an *epistemic margin*;
2. *The material, thermodynamic, and ecological-sociotechnical lineage (irreversible time, resilience, and performativity):* uniting Nicholas Georgescu-Roegen's (1971) critique of mechanistic reversibility, Georges Canguilhem's (1943/2009) biological normativity, Crawford S. Holling's (1973) ecological resilience, John F. C. Kingman's (1961) heavy-traffic queueing theory, and the sociology of quantification and performativity (Porter 1995; Callon 1998; Desrosières 1998; Bowker and Star 1999; MacKenzie 2006), it establishes that every intervention acts upon a reactive, entropic medium, demanding a *vital margin*;
3. *The ethical, contractual, and political-institutional lineage (apt agency, risk imposition, and organizational constitution):* uniting Antipater of Tarsus's Stoic archer (Stobaeus, *Eclogae* II.76.9–15; Cicero, *De Finibus* III.22), Bernard Williams's (1981) and Thomas Nagel's (1979) analyses of moral luck, Ernest Sosa's (2007, 2015) virtue epistemology, Thomas M. Scanlon's (1998) contractualism, Sven Ove Hansson's (2013) ethics of risk, Miranda Fricker's (2007, 2010, 2020) epistemic injustice, Stafford Beer's (1972) organizational cybernetics, Christian List and Philip Pettit's (2011) group agency, and the economics of moral hazard and classification fairness (Jensen and Meckling 1976; Holmström 1979; Holmström and Milgrom 1991; Innes 1990; Chouldechova 2017), it establishes that methodological honesty requires *fully apt agency under symmetric institutional governance*.

## **III. The First Filter: When Interest Conceals and Theory Insulates Itself**

### **3.1. Tension 1 — Situational Interest versus Intellectual Rectitude**

The first mediation occurs before any data are gathered or any equation is written. It resides in the social, economic, and institutional position occupied by the questioner: interest selects what deserves to be seen and conceals what is convenient to ignore. No researcher, magistrate, physician, public administrator, or corporate board deliberates outside society; all operate embedded in structures of funding, career incentives, bureaucratic prestige, and state or corporate power. When project approval, budget renewal, a firm's market valuation, or an administration's political survival depends on a given conclusion, a gravitational field inclines attention. Clues favoring the desired result are received with benevolence, while contrary signs are subjected to asymmetric skepticism or left outside the frame of inquiry.

The most pervasive form of this distortion rarely requires explicit data fabrication. Altering a primary document is a crude offense; far safer and more routine is shaping the prior architecture of inquiry. An institution adopts a narrow definition of the problem that excludes collateral externalities; slices a time series at a convenient base year; designs a safety study with a sample size too small to detect real harm; or runs twenty exploratory models behind closed doors and publishes only the single specification that crossed a nominal threshold.

This pathology must be analyzed across two distinct registers that should not be conflated: the macro-institutional register of **structural agnotology** (Proctor and Schiebinger 2008) and the micro-organizational register of **epistemic vices** and bureaucratic ritual (Frankfurt 2005; Cassam 2019). As Robert Proctor demonstrated in coining *agnotology*, ignorance is frequently not a passive vacuum awaiting scientific light, but an actively manufactured political-economic product—exemplified by tobacco, asbestos, and fossil-fuel campaigns that funded deliberately underpowered or narrowly framed studies to sustain the regulatory alibi that "the evidence remains inconclusive." Distinct from this organized macro-political manufacture of doubt is the dispositional and procedural pathology that Quassim Cassam (2019) terms **epistemic insouciance**—a routinized bureaucratic indifference to truth that generalizes Harry Frankfurt's (2005) diagnosis in *On Bullshit*. Whereas the liar and the industrial agnotologist track the truth strategically in order to conceal or delay its regulatory impact, the producer of bureaucratic *bullshit* severs commitment to factual reality altogether, caring only that the technical report satisfies formal ritual and projects institutional authority.

To prevent inquiry from being corrupted at its origin by structural agnotology, selective reporting, or framing asymmetry, Stochology establishes three methodological locks:

1. *Antidogmatic reserve (Cromwell's Rule):* In 1650, Oliver Cromwell addressed to the General Assembly of the Kirk of Scotland the plea: *"I beseech you, in the bowels of Christ, think it possible you may be mistaken."* Dennis Lindley (1985) formalized this warning as **Cromwell's Rule**: in probabilistic updating, assigning a prior probability of strictly $p = 0$ or $p = 1$ to any contingent empirical proposition destroys the capacity to learn from experience, since Bayes's rule leaves a zero or unit prior invariant regardless of how much contrary evidence accumulates. Intellectual rectitude requires clamping prior beliefs to the interior interval $[\epsilon, 1 - \epsilon]$ with $\epsilon > 0$ (`\epsilon = 10^{-4}` `[Assumed]` in the reference suite);
2. *Bitemporal pre-registration and the separation of invariant principle from contingent parameter:* Because institutional memory readily reinterprets the past to justify the present, the only reliable defense against post-hoc target painting (*Texas sharpshooter* bias) is bitemporal separation: sealing the hypothesis, the loss function, and the evaluation protocol at time $t_1$ before observing the outcome realization at time $t_2$. This temporal discipline rests on a distinction between *invariant methodological principles*—such as sealing rules before observing outcomes and disclosing all tested specifications—and *contingent domain parameters* (such as choosing a significance level $\alpha = 0.05$ or $\alpha = 0.01$ `[Assumed]`), which remain adjustable to context so long as they are fixed ex ante;
3. *Multiverse transparency, e-BH discovery control, and standpoint/contextual objectivity against Type III framing error:* When an analyst searches across $K$ plausible data-cleaning and modeling choices, reporting only the most favorable path inflates false positives dramatically. Sara Steegen et al. (2016) counter this via the **specification curve (*multiverse analysis*)**, evaluating stability across the entire combinatorics of defensible specifications, while Ruodu Wang and Aaditya Ramdas (2022) provide the **e-BH procedure**, which controls the false discovery rate under arbitrary dependence among tests. Yet even a reproducible calculation fails if it commits what Allyn Kimball (1957), Howard Raiffa (1968), and Ian Mitroff and Tom Featheringham (1974) termed **Type III Error**: giving a mathematically precise answer to the wrong framing of the problem. Two standard decision parameterizations must be distinguished here:
   - In the canonical operational **cost–loss model** (Murphy 1973; Richardson 2000), where preventive action incurs a certain protection cost $C > 0$ and mitigates a fraction $\eta \in (0, 1]$ of an avoidable loss $L > C$, expected expense is minimized by acting whenever event probability exceeds $p_{\mathrm{CL}}^* = C / (\eta L)$ `[Assumed]` (in Chapter 2's `EpistemicMarginState::from_inquiry_framing` benchmark with $C = 100$, efficacy $\eta = 0.85$, and institutional loss $L_{\mathrm{inst}} = 200$, $p_{\mathrm{CL, inst}}^* = 0.588$ with $\mathrm{EVPI} = 12.60$, whereas under affected-citizen loss $L_{\mathrm{affected}} = 800$, $p_{\mathrm{CL, affected}}^* = 0.147$ with $\mathrm{EVPI} = 82.00$, yielding a framing gap of $0.441$ `[Assumed]`);
   - In the clinical and classification **expected-regret model** (`pauker_kassirer_threshold` in `epistemic.rs`, Pauker and Kassirer 1975), where $C_{\mathrm{FP}} > 0$ denotes the net cost of a false alarm (acting when no event occurs) and $L_{\mathrm{FN}} > 0$ denotes the net loss of a missed event (failing to act when the event occurs), expected regret is minimized by acting whenever $p_{\mathrm{PK}}^* = C_{\mathrm{FP}} / (C_{\mathrm{FP}} + L_{\mathrm{FN}}) = 1 / (1 + L_{\mathrm{FN}}/C_{\mathrm{FP}})$ `[Assumed]`.

   Under *either* parameterization, when an institution evaluates the threshold using only its own reputational or budgetary loss ($L_{\mathrm{inst}} \ll L_{\mathrm{affected}}$), it inflates the action threshold ($p_{\mathrm{inst}}^* \gg p_{\mathrm{affected}}^*$) and systematically under-protects exposed citizens. Overcoming Type III Error therefore requires integrating both **Sandra Harding's (1991) strong objectivity**—which demands initiating inquiry from the standpoint of marginalized populations who bear the material downside of institutional error—and **Helen Longino's (1990) contextual empiricism and transformative criticism**, which subjects background assumptions and loss parameters to public contestation across diverse epistemic perspectives.

   At the same time, **Herbert Simon's (1947/1997 [1979])** theory of bounded rationality in *Administrative Behavior* reveals why Type III framing error is the primary vehicle of intra-organizational domination (`EpistemicMarginState::from_inquiry_framing` in `crates/stochology/src/epistemic.rs`). Because human behavioral and sensory-motor information throughput is biologically constrained by a ceiling of roughly $10\text{ bits/s}$ (Zheng and Meister 2025; Section 6.2), creating a structural compression bottleneck at the organizational interface, no operator at the base of a complex organization can recompute global state spaces from scratch during a shift; consequently, whoever directs an organization inevitably acts upon the **decision premises** of subordinates, selecting which variables, uncertainty intervals, and loss weights enter the team's visual field. Every manager or institutional architect is therefore a **cognitive influencer by obligation, but becomes a cognitive manipulator by choice**. When leadership exercises **legitimate cognitive influence**—what **Jürgen Habermas (1984 [1981/2012])** terms *communicative action*—it structures attention by sharing partial-identification bounds $[L_t, U_t]$, decision criteria, and affected-citizen spillover losses ($L_{\mathrm{affected}}$) in the open (`CognitiveFramingRegime::CommunicativeInfluence`, with premise-rigging index $\mathrm{PRI} = 0.00$ `[Simulated]`). When leadership instead practices **cognitive manipulation**—Habermas's *concealed strategic action*—it uses its Filter I framing power to manufacture artificial blind spots, concealing epistemic width and collateral spillover ($\mathrm{PRI} > 0.95$ `[Simulated]`) so that frontline operators are forced to choose inside a **decision labyrinth pre-rigged at the top** (`CognitiveFramingRegime::StrategicPremiseRigging`). Preserving the formal illusion of frontline autonomy inside a rigged labyrinth allows executives to shift not only financial risk (Innes 1990) but moral and legal blame onto the operational base—a direct violation of **Immanuel Kant's (1996 [1785/2007])** principle of the autonomy of reason, which forbids instrumentalizing another rational agent's judgment as a mere gear in an institutional alibi.

### **3.2. Tension 2 — Accommodating Confirmation versus Fruitful Refutation and the Mayo–Manski Bridge**

Even when immediate situational interests are constrained, a second tension arises from the logic of empirical testing itself: when observations contradict an established theory or policy model, the path of least resistance is to introduce *ad hoc* adjustments that rescue the model from refutation.

Since David Hume showed that no finite sequence of past observations deductively entails future regularities, Karl Popper (1934/2002) located scientific demarcation in *falsifiability*—the willingness to state in advance which observable outcomes would refute a conjecture. Yet Pierre Duhem (1906/1954) and Willard Van Orman Quine (1951) exposed the structural vulnerability of naive falsificationism (**the Duhem–Quine thesis**): because a core hypothesis $H$ generates testable predictions only when conjoined with a bundle of auxiliary assumptions $A_1 \wedge A_2 \wedge \dots \wedge A_m$ (instrument calibration, survey comprehension, absence of external confounders), an empirical failure $\neg O$ entails only $\neg(H \wedge A_1 \wedge \dots \wedge A_m)$ `[Assumed]`, leaving the analyst free to deflect blame onto an auxiliary clause. Imre Lakatos (1970) resolved this ambiguity dynamically by evaluating *research programs* over time: a modification is **progressive** when the revised auxiliary assumptions predict novel, independently testable facts (as Le Verrier's postulation of Neptune did in 1846), and **degenerating** when exceptions are tailored solely to absorb counterexamples post hoc, shrinking the empirical content of the theory.

In contemporary data-intensive science, the temptation toward accommodating adjustment is amplified by optional stopping and post-hoc threshold selection. Deborah Mayo (2018) formulated the error-statistical antidote in the **severity principle (*severe testing*)**: data $x_0$ that agree with a claim $H$ count as genuine evidence for $H$ if and only if the test procedure had a high probability of detecting the falsity of $H$ had $H$ been false. Examining drinking water with the naked eye in a clear glass yields an outcome ("clear water") that agrees with the hypothesis of purity, yet has zero severity against a colorless toxin. In classical error statistics, peeking at a sequential trial day after day and stopping as soon as a fixed-sample $p$-value dips below $0.05$ without pre-specified group-sequential alpha-spending inflates actual Type I error far above its nominal level. To provide anytime-valid sequential inference without requiring a pre-fixed sample size, a distinct martingale paradigm has matured: Glenn Shafer's (2021) *testing by betting*, Peter Grünwald, Rianne de Heide, and Wouter Koolen's (2024) *safe testing* via growth-rate optimal (*GROW*) **e-values** (nonnegative variables $E \ge 0$ satisfying $\mathbb{E}_{H_0}[E] \le 1$), and Aaditya Ramdas et al.'s (2023) **e-processes**—sequential nonnegative supermartingales $(E_t)_{t \ge 1}$ satisfying $\mathbb{E}_{H_0}[E_\tau] \le 1$ at any arbitrary data-dependent stopping time $\tau$, which by Jean Ville's (1939) maximal inequality guarantees $\mathbb{P}_{H_0}(\exists t \ge 1 : E_t \ge 1/\alpha) \le \alpha$ `[Assumed]`.

At this point arises a central methodological question that Stochology addresses: how can Deborah Mayo's (2018) demand for severe, refutable testing be coordinated with Charles Manski's (2003, 2013) partial identification, which demonstrates that observational and attrition-ridden data rarely identify a single point parameter, yielding instead an interval $\Theta_I = [\theta_L, \theta_U]$?

Rather than claiming a new probability theorem, the **Mayo–Manski bridge** is an operational synthesis that enforces a strict three-part division of labor—and a sharp temporal separation between **pre-data design parameters** ($\alpha$, power $1 - \beta$) and **post-data severity evaluation** ($\mathrm{SEV}$)—conditional on explicitly declared domain assumptions:

1. *Manski's descriptive moment and declared conditioning assumptions (delimiting the sharp identification region $\Theta_I$):* In evaluating a clinical treatment or social policy (as in Robert LaLonde's (1986) classic comparison of experimental National Supported Work data against observational CPS controls, where in `nsw_experimental.csv` vs. `cps_observational.csv` the experimental ATE is $+\$1{,}794$ whereas the unadjusted observational difference is $-\$8{,}498$ `[Measured]`), counterfactual outcomes are unobserved and a fraction $p_{\mathrm{miss}}$ of outcomes is missing or selectively censored. Manski (2003) shows that producing a single point estimate $\hat{\theta}$ requires imposing untestable assumptions (such as missing-at-random). Partial identification refuses unverifiable extrapolation and computes the sharp **identification region** $\Theta_I = [\theta_L, \theta_U]$ conditional on explicitly stated domain premises—such as known bounded support $[y_{\min}, y_{\max}]$ (Manski 2003), monotone selection in sample attrition (Lee 2009), or arbitrary dependence in competing risks (Peterson 1976);
2. *The Imbens–Manski confidence interval and Mayo's post-data severity on the boundary of $\Theta_I$:* Let $\theta_{\mathrm{crit}}$ denote the minimum substantively worthwhile effect (or safety threshold) required to justify an intervention. To account simultaneously for identification ambiguity ($\hat{\Delta} = \hat{\theta}_U - \hat{\theta}_L \ge 0$) and finite-sample sampling variability ($\hat{\sigma}_L, \hat{\sigma}_U$ over the **variance-effective sample size** $n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} = \min(n_{\mathrm{cluster}}, n_{\mathrm{Kish}})$), Guido Imbens and Charles Manski (2004) constructed the two-sided $(1-\alpha)$ asymptotic confidence interval for the partially identified parameter $\theta \in [\theta_L, \theta_U]$:
   $$\mathrm{CI}_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{IM}} = \left[\hat{L}_{1-\alpha},\; \hat{U}_{1-\alpha}\right] = \left[\hat{\theta}_L - c_\alpha\frac{\hat{\sigma}_L}{\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}}},\; \hat{\theta}_U + c_\alpha\frac{\hat{\sigma}_U}{\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}}}\right] \qquad \text{\texttt{[Assumed]}}$$
   Crucially, the critical factor $c_\alpha \in [z_{1-\alpha},\, z_{1-\alpha/2}]$ solves the adaptive coverage equation
   $$\Phi\!\left(c_\alpha + \sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}}\,\frac{\hat{\theta}_U - \hat{\theta}_L}{\max(\hat{\sigma}_L, \hat{\sigma}_U)}\right) - \Phi(-c_\alpha) = 1 - \alpha \qquad \text{\texttt{[Assumed]}},$$
   interpolating smoothly between the two-sided quantile $z_{1-\alpha/2}$ when $\hat{\theta}_U = \hat{\theta}_L$ and the one-sided quantile $z_{1-\alpha}$ when the identification width $\hat{\theta}_U - \hat{\theta}_L$ dominates sampling noise. Here statistical precision requires separating **pre-data design properties** ($\alpha$ and power $1-\beta$, which can and must be pre-registered before data collection) from **Mayo's (2018) post-data severity function**, which is a function of the realized data $x_0$ and can only be computed *after* observing $T(x_0) = \hat{\theta}_L(x_0)$. Under asymptotic normality of the boundary estimators $\hat{\theta}_L, \hat{\theta}_U$ (or via studentized bootstrap resampling when finite-sample trimming bounds in Lee 2009 or competing-risks minimums in Peterson 1976 depart from Gaussianity), post-data severity for the claim $H: \theta_L > \theta_{\mathrm{crit}}$ against the composite null $H_0: \theta_L \le \theta_{\mathrm{crit}}$ is:
   $$\mathrm{SEV}\bigl(\theta_L > \theta_{\mathrm{crit}};\, x_0\bigr) = \mathbb{P}\!\left(T(X) \le T(x_0) \;\middle|\; \theta_L = \theta_{\mathrm{crit}}\right) \approx \Phi\!\left(\frac{\hat{\theta}_L(x_0) - \theta_{\mathrm{crit}}}{\hat{\sigma}_L / \sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}}}\right);$$
3. *Separating the diagnostic outcome from the prescriptive choice rule:* Combining $\mathrm{CI}_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{IM}}$ and post-data $\mathrm{SEV}(\cdot)$ yields three distinct epistemic states:
   - **Severe worst-case adequacy ($\hat{L}_{1-\alpha} > \theta_{\mathrm{crit}}$ and post-data $\mathrm{SEV}(\theta_L > \theta_{\mathrm{crit}}; x_0) \ge 1 - \alpha$):** Even under the most adverse completion of missing records compatible with the maintained support assumptions, the effect exceeds $\theta_{\mathrm{crit}}$ with high post-data severity;
   - **Severe refutation ($\hat{U}_{1-\alpha} \le \theta_{\mathrm{crit}}$ and post-data $\mathrm{SEV}(\theta_U \le \theta_{\mathrm{crit}}; x_0) \ge 1 - \alpha$):** Even under the most optimistic completion of the archive, the policy fails to reach $\theta_{\mathrm{crit}}$;
   - **Underdetermined ambiguity ($\hat{L}_{1-\alpha} \le \theta_{\mathrm{crit}} < \hat{U}_{1-\alpha}$) or non-informative bounds ($\Theta_I = [y_{\min}, y_{\max}]$):** Here, descriptive identification alone does not select an action. Note also why severe worst-case adequacy is structurally harder to attain than severe refutation when missingness $p_{\mathrm{miss}}$ is large: because missingness widens $[\theta_L, \theta_U]$, requiring $\hat{L}_{1-\alpha} > \theta_{\mathrm{crit}}$ is a sufficient condition across all completions of the archive, whereas $\hat{L}_{1-\alpha} \le \theta_{\mathrm{crit}} < \hat{U}_{1-\alpha}$ does *not* mandate universal paralysis. Claiming that one must always abstain whenever $\hat{L}_{1-\alpha} \le \theta_{\mathrm{crit}}$ is Wald's (1945) prescriptive *maximin* rule, not a theorem of Manski. As detailed in Section 4.2 (Table 3), when $\hat{L}_{1-\alpha} \le \theta_{\mathrm{crit}} < \hat{U}_{1-\alpha}$, Stochology first checks the physical survival constraint ($\mathrm{TTS} \ge \mathrm{TTR}$): if the intervention threatens an absorbing ruin barrier, Sosa's meta-aptness mandates withholding the shot; if the intervention is reversible and survival-safe, Manski's (2003, 2013) *minimax regret* rule allocates fractional treatment $a_{\mathrm{MMR}}^* = (U_M - C)/(U_M - L_M)$ while targeted data collection narrows $\Theta_I$.

Recognizing Manski's identification region is thus the precondition for genuine severity in Mayo's sense: applying a conventional point-null $t$-test to an archive with unadjusted non-random missingness ($\rho_{R,Y} \ne 0$) yields near-zero post-data severity $\mathrm{SEV}(\theta > \theta_{\mathrm{crit}}; x_0) \to 0$ for the substantive claim that the true population parameter exceeds $\theta_{\mathrm{crit}}$, because the unadjusted test procedure had virtually no capability to detect that the apparent point effect was generated by selection bias.

## **IV. The Second Filter: When the Model's Norm Narrates and the Archive Omits**

### **4.1. Tension 3 — Domesticating Formalization versus Reflexive Formalization**

Whereas the first filter governs problem framing and hypothesis testing, the second filter operates in the symbolic instruments that translate phenomena into measures: formal models and documentary archives. Historically, mathematical and classificatory formalization made it possible to expose hidden premises, compare distant regimes, and subject arguments to public verification. Yet this achievement harbors the third pathological inversion: the internal grammar of the formal model begins to legislate over what counts as real.

While Theodor Adorno (1966/1973) critiqued the tendency of identificatory concepts to dismiss qualitative singularity as "noise" and called for conceptual constellations to expose that remainder, Nicholas Georgescu-Roegen (1971) analyzed the parallel methodological mechanism inside mathematical economics by contrasting **arithmomorphic concepts** with **dialectical concepts**. Arithmomorphic concepts possess discrete, motionless boundaries like integers ($2 \ne 3$, with no intermediate zone). Dialectical concepts—such as "health," "justice," "clinical severity," or "ecological stability"—are surrounded by a constitutive **dialectical penumbra** ($[a, b]$) within which qualitative transitions unfold (for example, in `danish_fire.csv`, $39.6\%$ `[Measured]` of insurance losses fall inside the contested $[1.5, 4.0]\text{ MDKK}$ penumbra, and in the deterministic $3{,}000$-flight benchmark sample of `nyc_flights_2013.csv`, column `arr_delay`, $8.2\%$ `[Measured]` of arrival delays fall inside the $10\text{--}20$ minute penumbra around the FAA's sharp $15$-minute on-time cutoff). The error of uncritical formalism lies not in employing mathematics, but in erasing the penumbra by imposing a knife-edge cut where a transition zone exists.

Stochology therefore distinguishes **domesticating formalization**—which adopts Gaussian curves, linear additive models, and lone arithmetic means simply for analytical convenience—from **reflexive formalization**, which uses formal tools to measure the cost of simplification across four dimensions:

1. *Convex harm asymmetry and Jensen's Gap (Jensen 1906):* Let $g(X)$ be a twice-differentiable convex harm or congestion function ($g''(x) > 0$) evaluated over a random demand or environmental stress $X$ with mean $\mu = \mathbb{E}[X]$ and variance $\sigma_X^2$. By Johan Jensen's (1906) inequality, expected harm strictly exceeds the harm evaluated at the average scenario:
   $$\Delta_J = \mathbb{E}\bigl[g(X)\bigr] - g\bigl(\mathbb{E}[X]\bigr) \ge 0, \qquad \Delta_J \approx \frac{1}{2} g''(\mu)\,\sigma_X^2 \qquad \text{\texttt{[Assumed]}}$$
   Exposed to one hour in a $27^\circ\mathrm{C}$ environment and one hour in a $47^\circ\mathrm{C}$ environment `[Assumed]`, a human organism faces an arithmetic mean ambient temperature of $37^\circ\mathrm{C}$ yet dies of hyperthermia; a hospital ward whose utilization oscillates between $60\%$ and $99\%$ `[Assumed]` suffers explosive queueing delays that a plan sized for the $80\%$ mean never anticipates. In the Danish fire insurance archive (`danish_fire.csv`, $n = 2{,}167$ claims, column `loss_mdkk` in millions of DKK), the **raw loss** $X$ has a sample mean of $\bar{X} = 3.39$, a median of $1.78$, and a $95\text{th}$ percentile of $q_{95} = 9.97$ `[Measured]`; under the Chapter 5 reinsurance stress function $\phi(X) = 0.08 X^{1.65}$, evaluation at the mean is $\phi(\bar{X}) = 0.598$ whereas expected stress is $\mathbb{E}[\phi(X)] = 1.613$, yielding a **Jensen convexity gap of $\Delta_J = 1.015$** (`+170%` `[Measured]`; likewise, under quadratic harm $g(X) = X^2$, $g(\bar{X}) = 11.46$ vs. $\mathbb{E}[X^2] = 83.80$, $\Delta_J = 72.34$ `[Measured]`). Planning for the lone mean systematically underfunds convex reality by $\Delta_J$;
2. *Heavy-tailed extremes, Pareto moment regimes, and threshold selection (Hill 1975):* For variables governed by power-law right tails $\mathbb{P}(X > x) \sim C x^{-\alpha}$ (such as financial losses, wildfire areas, epidemic superspreading, or infrastructure delays), Bruce M. Hill (1975) derived the order-statistic tail-index estimator over the top $k$ extremes $X_{(n)} \ge \dots \ge X_{(n-k)}$:
   $$\hat{\alpha}_k = \left[\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k} \ln\!\left(\frac{X_{(n-i+1)}}{X_{(n-k)}}\right)\right]^{-1} \qquad \text{\texttt{[Assumed]}}$$
   The tail exponent $\alpha$ partitions reality into three distinct moment regimes: (i) $\alpha > 2$, where variance $\sigma^2 < \infty$ is finite but higher moments and kurtosis can still destabilize Gaussian intervals; (ii) $1 < \alpha \le 2$, where the population mean $\mu < \infty$ remains finite but the **population variance is infinite ($\sigma^2 = \infty$)** (as in `danish_fire.csv`, where at $k = 150$ $\hat{\alpha}_{150} = 1.39 \in (1, 2]$, at $k = 120$ $\hat{\alpha}_{120} = 1.446$, and across the entire stability grid $k \in \{50, 100, 150, 200, 250\}$ in `hill_stability_across_k` $\hat{\alpha}_k \in [1.36, 1.87] \subset (1, 2]$ `[Measured]`), rendering sample standard deviations erratic downward-biased mirages; and (iii) $\alpha \le 1$, where even the population mean ceases to exist ($\mu = \infty$). In practice, applying $\hat{\alpha}_k$ requires confronting the **threshold-selection bias–variance trade-off**: choosing $k$ too small inflates estimation variance, whereas choosing $k$ too large contaminates the tail with central-body observations, requiring stability audits across a plateau of $k$ values (`hill_stability_across_k`) before feeding $\hat{\alpha}_k$ into reserve sizing;
3. *Structural model discrepancy (Kennedy and O'Hagan 2001):* Every mechanistic or econometric model $f(x, \theta)$ omits real-world processes. Marc Kennedy and Anthony O'Hagan (2001) demonstrated that calibrating $\theta$ under the fiction of zero structural error forces parameter estimates to absorb omitted physics or social dynamics, producing overconfident out-of-sample extrapolations. Reflexive formalization decomposes reality as $y(x) = f(x, \theta) + \delta(x) + \varepsilon$, modeling structural discrepancy $\delta(x)$ explicitly via a Gaussian-process prior so that predictive uncertainty widens automatically as $x$ moves outside historical support (in Chapter 7's `GovernanceState::from_ex_ante_gate` benchmark anchored on empirical forecast terciles, posterior discrepancy mean and standard deviation shift from $(-0.0283, 0.044)$ in-sample at $x = 0.55$ to $(-0.0000, 0.500)$ out-of-sample at $x = 5.0$ `[Measured]`);
4. *Measurement scale invariance and CORP calibration decompositions for Brier and CRPS (Stevens 1946; Krantz et al. 1971; Murphy 1973; Gneiting, Balabdaoui, and Raftery 2007; Dimitriadis, Gneiting, and Jordan 2021):* In representational measurement theory (Stevens 1946; Krantz et al. 1971), computing arithmetic means on ordinal scales is ill-defined because strictly increasing monotone transformations $\phi(\cdot)$ can reverse mean rankings while leaving medians, quantiles, and Mann–Whitney dominance $\mathbb{P}(A > B) + \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{P}(A = B)$ invariant. In probabilistic forecasting, Allan H. Murphy (1973) and Tilmann Gneiting, Fadoua Balabdaoui, and Adrian E. Raftery (2007) established that forecast adequacy requires **maximizing sharpness (resolution) subject to calibration (reliability)**. Timo Dimitriadis, Tilmann Gneiting, and Alexander I. Jordan (2021) placed this decomposition on a parameter-free footing via the isotonic Pool-Adjacent-Violators (**CORP**) identity for binary Brier scores:
   $$\mathrm{BS} = \underbrace{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(p_i - \hat{\pi}_i)^2}_{\mathrm{REL}_{\mathrm{CORP}} \text{ (miscalibration)}} - \underbrace{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\hat{\pi}_i - \bar{y})^2}_{\mathrm{RES}_{\mathrm{CORP}} \text{ (discriminative resolution)}} + \underbrace{\bar{y}(1 - \bar{y})}_{\mathrm{UNC}} \qquad \text{\texttt{[Assumed]}}$$
   where $\hat{\pi}_i$ are the PAV-recalibrated probabilities (verified across $n = 19{,}025$ consecutive +6-hour lead-time synoptic transitions $(t \to t+1)$ from $20{,}778$ total rows, excluding $1{,}753$ track-terminal rows, along the storm tracks of `atlantic_storms.csv` predicting next-step hurricane-force wind $\ge 64\text{ kt}$ from current barometric pressure, where $\mathrm{BS} = 0.0731 = 0.0028 - 0.1171 + 0.1873$ to machine precision $4.62 \times 10^{-15}$ `[Measured]`). For continuous heavy-tailed variables (such as flood stages, electricity load, or financial drawdowns), the same isotonic CORP decomposition extends directly to the **Continuous Ranked Probability Score** ($\mathrm{CRPS}(F, y) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} [F(z) - \mathbf{1}\{y \le z\}]^2\,\mathrm{d}z = \mathrm{REL}_{\mathrm{CRPS}} - \mathrm{RES}_{\mathrm{CRPS}} + \mathrm{UNC}_{\mathrm{CRPS}}$, Gneiting and Raftery 2007; Dimitriadis, Gneiting, and Jordan 2021). This exact identity provides the mathematical engine for Stochology's first reflexive Goodhart brake (Section 7.2).

### **4.2. Tension 4 — Sample Volume versus Evidential Weight, the Silence of the Archive, and the Epistemic Margin**

When the fragility of simplified models becomes undeniable, institutions frequently turn to the accumulation of massive digital registries (*Big Data*), assuming that millions of rows ($n \to N$) automatically wash away measurement bias. Here the fourth pathological inversion takes hold: archival volume is mistaken for evidential quality, converting selective omission into high-precision error.

In Chapter 6 of *A Treatise on Probability*, John Maynard Keynes (1921) distinguished the **probability** $p$ of an argument from its **weight of evidence** $V(E, H)$, which can be represented operationally by the normalized knowledge ratio:

$$V = \frac{K}{K + I} \in (0, 1) \qquad \text{\texttt{[Assumed functional representation]}}$$

where $K$ measures the balance of relevant knowledge and $I$ the remaining relevant ignorance. Two clinical or judicial assessments may both yield $p = 0.50$, yet one rests on a two-line administrative note ($V \ll 1$) while the other rests on exhaustive diagnostic testing and cross-examination ($V \to 1$). Probability measures the tilt of the scale; evidential weight measures how much of the relevant reality has actually been placed upon the pans.

Nearly a century later, Xiao-Li Meng (2018) proved an exact finite-population identity that explains why ignoring evidential weight and collection design produces the **Big Data paradox**. Let $R_i \in \{0, 1\}$ indicate whether individual $i$ in a population of size $N$ is recorded in an archive of size $n = \sum_{i=1}^N R_i$, with sampling fraction $f = n / N$. Since $\mathrm{Cov}(R, Y) = f(\bar{Y}_n - \bar{Y}_N)$ and $\sigma_R = \sqrt{f(1 - f)}$, the error between the archive sample mean $\bar{Y}_n$ and the true population mean $\bar{Y}_N$ is identically:

$$\bar{Y}_n - \bar{Y}_N = \underbrace{\rho_{R, Y}}_{\text{data defect correlation}} \cdot \underbrace{\sqrt{\frac{1 - f}{f}}}_{\text{dropout odds factor}} \cdot \underbrace{\sigma_Y}_{\text{population standard deviation}} \qquad \text{\texttt{[Assumed]}}$$

Equating the mean squared error of $\bar{Y}_n$ to that of a simple random sample of size $n_{\mathrm{Meng}}$ (where $\mathrm{MSE}_{\mathrm{SRS}} = (1 - n_{\mathrm{Meng}}/N)\sigma_Y^2 / n_{\mathrm{Meng}} \approx \sigma_Y^2 / n_{\mathrm{Meng}}$ for $N \gg n_{\mathrm{Meng}}$) yields the leading-order MSE-equivalent effective sample size $n_{\mathrm{Meng}} \approx \frac{f}{1 - f}\frac{1}{\rho_{R,Y}^2}$ `[Assumed]`. Three consequences follow immediately:

1. *Relative coverage $f = n/N$, not raw count $n$, governs the dropout factor:* a registry of one million records in a nation of two hundred million ($f = 0.005$ `[Assumed]`) retains a large dropout multiplier $\sqrt{(1-f)/f} \approx 14.1$ `[Assumed]`;
2. *Selection correlation $\rho_{R,Y}$ does not vanish as $n$ grows under homogeneous collection:* if a digital platform or administrative registry systematically under-records poorer or sicker strata ($\rho_{R,Y} \ne 0$), scaling $n$ upward without altering the inclusion mechanism leaves $\rho_{R,Y}$ untouched while shrinking the nominal standard error $\sigma_Y / \sqrt{n}$ toward zero;
3. *Maximum confidence in the wrong answer:* In Meng's (2018) empirical calibration of the 2016 U.S. presidential election polls, a non-random sample of $n = 2.3 \times 10^6$ respondents with a tiny recording correlation of $\rho_{R,Y} \approx -0.005$ possessed the same effective information content as a genuinely randomized sample of roughly $n_{\mathrm{Meng}} \approx 400$ respondents `[Literature]` (and in Chapter 5's benchmark with $n = 2.3 \times 10^6$, $N = 10^7$, and $\rho_{R,Y} = -0.05$ `[Assumed]`, $n_{\mathrm{Meng}}$ collapses to $119.5$ `[Assumed]`). The larger the unadjusted archive, the closer to zero falls the probability that its nominal confidence interval covers the true parameter $\bar{Y}_N$.

Two further mechanisms compound this contraction of information in the transit from world to archive:

First, observations in social, clinical, and financial archives arrive in correlated clusters (hospitals, schools, neighborhoods, trading days). In regression and survey sampling with equal cluster sizes $\bar{m}$ and intra-cluster correlation $\rho_{\mathrm{intra}} > 0$, Brent Moulton (1986) and Cathy Campbell (1977) highlighted the variance inflation factor $1 + (\bar{m} - 1)\rho_{\mathrm{intra}}$, which Leslie Kish (1965) generalized to unequal cluster sizes $m_g$ with coefficient of variation $\mathrm{CV}_m$:

$$\mathrm{DEFF} = 1 + \bigl[\bar{m}\bigl(1 + \mathrm{CV}_m^2\bigr) - 1\bigr]\rho_{\mathrm{intra}}, \qquad n_{\mathrm{cluster}} = \frac{n}{\mathrm{DEFF}} \qquad \text{\texttt{[Assumed]}}$$

(In `cps_observational.csv`, grouping $n = 15{,}992$ individuals directly by `(age, educ)` demographic-educational cohorts on continuous 1978 earnings `re78` yields empirical one-way ANOVA intra-cluster correlation and $\mathrm{DEFF} = 10.37$, reducing the variance-effective sample size to $n_{\mathrm{cluster}} = 1{,}542.0$ with Keynes weight $V = 0.984$ at $n_0 = 25.565$ `[Assumed]` / `[Measured]`; in `bank_calls.csv`, grouping $n = 27{,}716$ five-minute call windows by operational day yields $\mathrm{DEFF} = 8.19$ and reduces $n$ to $n_{\mathrm{cluster}} = 3{,}384.5$ with $V = 0.971$ at $n_0 = 100.0$ `[Assumed]` / `[Measured]`.)

Second, archival omission is a socio-material and institutional process that requires deploying the analytical concepts of Science and Technology Studies (STS) rather than treating "missing data" as a purely technical accident. In Donald Rubin's (1976; Little and Rubin 1987/2002) statistical taxonomy, missingness is **Missing Not At Random (MNAR)** whenever the probability of non-recording depends on the unobserved outcome itself. Sociologically, however, three concrete institutional mechanisms produce MNAR silences:

- *Conventions of equivalence and mechanical objectivity (Desrosières 1998; Porter 1995):* Before any phenomenon can be counted, state and corporate bureaucracies must establish **conventions of equivalence** (Desrosières 1998) that strip away local context to treat heterogeneous lives as commensurable units, relying on rigid rules of **mechanical objectivity** (Porter 1995) to shield vulnerable officials from political distrust;
- *Classification infrastructure, residual categories, and biographical torque (Bowker and Star 1999; Hacking 1995):* Every classification system (such as the International Classification of Diseases or a municipal welfare registry) privileges certain institutional workflows while relegating atypical or marginalized trajectories to mute **residual categories** ("other / not elsewhere classified"). When individuals whose lived reality does not fit the administrative grid are forced to contort their biographies to obtain care or legal recognition, they experience what Geoffrey Bowker and Susan Leigh Star (1999) term **torque**, while the categories themselves reshape social identities through Ian Hacking's (1995) **looping effects** (*making up people*);
- *Hermeneutical injustice and structural agnotology (Fricker 2007; Proctor and Schiebinger 2008):* When an archive lacks the conceptual category needed to register a group's harm, it inflicts **hermeneutical injustice** (Fricker 2007); when authorities preserve that blind spot to avoid regulatory or fiscal liability—or revise historical macroeconomic series across release vintages (in the Philadelphia Fed real-output archive `rtdsm_routput_vintages.csv`, annualized quarterly growth exhibits a mean absolute revision of $1.47$ percentage points, a maximum revision of $8.02$ percentage points, and a $6.6\%$ sign-flip rate across $Q = 241$ quarters `[Measured]`)—omission operates as structural agnotology.

Where selection defects, cluster correlations, and socio-institutional omissions operate together, Stochology's **epistemic margin** replaces point extrapolation with four formal audit tools and a strict separation between **sampling variance** and **selection bias**:

- *Separating sampling-variance effective sample size ($n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}$) from selection-bias defect ($n_{\mathrm{Meng}}$):* Statistical rigor forbids plugging a bias metric into a variance formula. We must distinguish (a) **Kish's (1965) weight-concentration sample size** $n_{\mathrm{Kish}} = (\sum w_i)^2 / \sum w_i^2$ `[Assumed]`, which measures sampling variance inflation when inverse-probability weights (Horvitz and Thompson 1952) concentrate on underserved strata; (b) **cluster-adjusted sample size** $n_{\mathrm{cluster}} = n / \mathrm{DEFF}$ `[Assumed]`, which measures sampling variance inflation from intra-cluster correlation; and (c) **Meng's (2018) selection-defect sample size** $n_{\mathrm{Meng}} \approx \frac{f}{1-f}\rho_{R,Y}^{-2}$ `[Assumed]`, which summarizes non-random *location bias* $\Delta_{\mathrm{Meng}} = \rho_{R,Y}\sqrt{(1-f)/f}\,\sigma_Y$. In Stochology (and in `StochologicalAgent::act` in `crates/stochology/src/engine.rs`), **location bias** ($\Delta_{\mathrm{Meng}}$ and missingness fraction $p_{\mathrm{miss}}$) enters *exclusively* by widening the partial-identification location interval $[L_t, U_t]$, whereas **sampling-variance formulas**—both the Imbens–Manski standard-error denominator $\hat{\sigma}/\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}}$ and Baker–McHale epistemic Kelly shrinkage $f_{\mathrm{epi}}^* = \frac{n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}}{n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} + 1}\frac{\hat{\mu}-r}{\hat{\sigma}^2}$—use *strictly* the variance-effective sample size:
  $$n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} = \min\bigl(n_{\mathrm{cluster}},\, n_{\mathrm{Kish}}\bigr) \qquad \text{\texttt{[Assumed conservative variance floor]}}$$
- *Unseen population lower bounds (Chao 1984, 1987):* When incomplete registries or capture–recapture lists record $f_1$ singletons (individuals seen once) and $f_2$ doubletons (individuals seen twice), **Anne Chao's (1984, 1987) nonparametric lower bound** $\hat{N}_{\mathrm{unseen}} = f_1^2 / (2 f_2)$ `[Assumed]` estimates the minimum size of the uncounted population under heterogeneous catchability without assuming equal inclusion probabilities;
- *Dependent censoring and attrition bounds (Peterson 1976; Lee 2009):* When subjects exit follow-up under dependent competing risks, **Peterson's (1976) sharp competing-risks bounds** bracket the joint survival distribution without assuming independent censoring, while **Lee's (2009) trimming bounds** bracket treatment effects under selective sample attrition;
- *Worst-case partial identification (Manski 2003):* Most generally, for any bounded outcome $Y \in [y_{\min}, y_{\max}]$ with missingness fraction $p_{\mathrm{miss}}$ and observed mean $\bar{Y}_{\mathrm{obs}}$, **Manski's (2003) sharp worst-case bounds** delimit the ambiguity interval:
  $$\bigl[L_M,\, U_M\bigr] = \Bigl[(1 - p_{\mathrm{miss}})\bar{Y}_{\mathrm{obs}} + p_{\mathrm{miss}} y_{\min},\; (1 - p_{\mathrm{miss}})\bar{Y}_{\mathrm{obs}} + p_{\mathrm{miss}} y_{\max}\Bigr] \qquad \text{\texttt{[Assumed]}}$$

How does an institution pass from this descriptive interval $[L_M, U_M]$ to a concrete action without conflating identification with prescription—and how does it address standard decision-theoretic objections to lexicographic rules? In classical utility theory, unconstrained lexicographic preferences violate Archimedean continuity and cannot be represented by a single real-valued utility function. In engineering, clinical triage, and institutional risk management, however, **discontinuity at an absorbing ruin barrier is a physical feature of reality, not a mathematical defect**: no finite marginal efficiency gain compensates for crossing an irreversible threshold of death or systemic insolvency. Moreover, coupling the hard survival floor with minimax regret alters the unconstrained comparative statics whenever the survival constraint is active: if the unconstrained minimax-regret fractional allocation $a_{\mathrm{MMR}}^* = (U_M - C)/(U_M - L_M)$ `[Assumed]` would consume reserves beyond the survival ceiling $a_{\max}(\mathrm{TTS} \ge \mathrm{TTR})$, the constrained optimal allocation is clipped to $a_{\mathrm{safe}}^* = \min\bigl(a_{\mathrm{MMR}}^*,\, a_{\max}(\mathrm{TTS} \ge \mathrm{TTR})\bigr)$, shifting comparative statics from the upside boundary $U_M$ to the physical recovery rate $1/\mathrm{TTR}$. ## **V. The Third Filter: When the Truth of the World Tears Under Action and Time**

### **5.1. Tension 5 — Passive Observation versus Performative Intervention**

Suppose an investigator has constrained situational framing biases, subjected hypotheses to severe sequential tests, respected convex tail asymmetries, and declared partial-identification bounds over archival omissions. Even then, the third mediating filter opens as soon as that representation is deployed to govern society: the instant a model or indicator is used to intervene in the world, the social and material field reacts to the intervention and alters the regularities the model was built upon.

The first ground of this rupture is causal and structural, formalized by Judea Pearl (2009; Pearl and Mackenzie 2018) in the three-rung **Ladder of Causation**:

1. *Rung 1 — Association ($\mathbb{P}(Y \mid X)$, passive observation):* recording empirical correlations in an undisturbed regime, which cannot distinguish structural causes from confounding (such as higher mortality in tertiary hospitals that receive the sickest patients);
2. *Rung 2 — Intervention ($\mathbb{P}(Y \mid \mathrm{do}(X = x))$, material action):* actively setting $X$ to $x$, which severs incoming confounding arrows and alters the data-generating distribution—requiring predictors whose structural coefficients remain invariant across distinct environments (**Invariant Causal Prediction**, Peters, Bühlmann, and Meinshausen 2016; verified in `atlantic_storms.csv` across $n = 20{,}778$ observations, where the physical barometric-pressure slope range across three seasonal cyclogenesis regimes is $0.009$ versus $0.076$ for the non-invariant latitude proxy, alongside a +6h lead-time CORP-PAV Brier decomposition across $n = 19{,}025$ storm transitions $\mathrm{BS} = 0.0731 = \mathrm{REL}(0.0028) - \mathrm{RES}(0.1171) + \mathrm{UNC}(0.1873)$ closing to $4.62 \times 10^{-15}$ `[Measured]`) and accounting for spillover and interference when scaling from a pilot trial to a population (Hudgens and Halloran 2008);
3. *Rung 3 — Counterfactuals ($\mathbb{P}(Y_x = y' \mid X = x, Y = y)$, retrospective attribution):* evaluating what would have occurred in a singular historical case under an alternative action, where Jin Tian and Judea Pearl (2000) derived sharp partial-identification bounds for the **Probability of Necessity ($\mathrm{PN}$)** and **Probability of Necessity and Sufficiency ($\mathrm{PNS}$)** `[Assumed]` combining experimental and observational evidence.

In social, economic, and administrative life, climbing from passive observation to intervention encounters a second complication: the agents being modeled interpret the rule and adapt strategically. In macroeconomics, Robert Lucas (1976) showed that historical correlations dissolve when agents adjust expectations to a new policy regime, while Charles Goodhart (1975) formulated the law that any observed statistical regularity tends to collapse once control pressure is placed upon it—a mechanism generalized in the anthropology of audit culture by Marilyn Strathern (1997) ("when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure").

Going beyond the proverb requires engaging the distinct sociological mechanisms analyzed by Wendy Espeland and Michael Sauder (2007), David Manheim and Scott Garrabrant (2018), Donald MacKenzie (2006), and Juan Perdomo et al. (2020):

First, in the sociology of institutional rankings, Espeland and Sauder (2007) demonstrate that **reactivity** operates through two distinct mechanisms prior to overt cheating: **commensuration** (reducing qualitative institutional diversity to a single ordinal rank, which transforms cognitive attention and redistributes internal resources toward ranked items) and **self-fulfilling prophecy** (where external audiences allocate funding and prestige according to the rank, confirming the ranking's initial tiering). Complementing this sociological analysis, Manheim and Garrabrant (2018) classify four formal modes of Goodhart target collapse when an institution optimizes a proxy $M$ in place of a latent value $V$: *regressional collapse* (selecting noise tails where $M - V$ is largest), *extremal collapse* (pushing the state outside the historical domain where $M$ and $V$ co-varied), *causal collapse* (intervening on a downstream symptom $M$ rather than the upstream cause $V$, as analyzed in strategic classification by Milli et al. 2019; Kleinberg and Raghavan 2020; Miller, Milli, and Hardt 2020), and *adversarial collapse* (direct gaming of $M$ by strategic actors).

Second, in Science and Technology Studies, the program of **performativity**—grounded in the reflexive sociology of Barry Barnes (1983), formulated for economic sociology by Michel Callon (1998), and developed historically by Donald MacKenzie (2006)—demonstrates that formal models are not passive *cameras* recording a pre-existing market or bureaucracy, but *engines* that actively format socio-material practices. MacKenzie (2006) distinguishes four levels of performativity: (i) *generic performativity*, where a model is used by practitioners; (ii) *effective performativity*, where its use measurably alters processes; (iii) *Barnesian performativity* (named after Barry Barnes), where practical adoption of a model reshapes institutional arrangements so that reality conforms more closely to the model's postulates; and (iv) **counterperformativity**, where widespread adoption of the model alters material practices in a way that undermines the model's own empirical premises (as, in MacKenzie's 2006 historical reconstruction, the automated hedging of Black–Scholes portfolio insurance interacted with market illiquidity during the October 1987 crash, after which options markets developed the persistent volatility skew that violated the flat-volatility premise of the classic Black–Scholes formula `[Literature]`). Whereas Goodhart's Law describes the statistical decoupling of a target proxy under incentive pressure, MacKenzie's counterperformativity describes the material undoing of a model's truth conditions through its own infrastructural enactment.

Third, Juan Perdomo, Tijana Zrnic, Celestine Mendler-Dünner, and Moritz Hardt (2020) formalized **performative prediction**, where deploying a model parameter $\theta$ induces a distribution shift $\mathcal{D}(\theta)$. A parameter $\theta_{\mathrm{PS}}$ achieves **performative stability** when it is a fixed point of repeated retraining, $\theta_{\mathrm{PS}} = \arg\min_{\theta} \mathbb{E}_{Z \sim \mathcal{D}(\theta_{\mathrm{PS}})}[\ell(Z; \theta)]$ `[Assumed]`: the model appears well-calibrated on the distribution its own deployment generated (for example, a credit-scoring rule that denies loans to a neighborhood, induces business failures, and then cites those failures as confirmation of its risk score). **Performative optimality** ($\theta_{\mathrm{PO}} = \arg\min_{\theta} \mathbb{E}_{Z \sim \mathcal{D}(\theta)}[\ell(Z; \theta)]$ `[Assumed]`) minimizes expected loss accounting explicitly for how $\theta$ shifts $\mathcal{D}(\theta)$. Because in Perdomo et al.'s (2020) formal definition $\ell(Z; \theta)$ is still the designer's chosen loss function, Stochology adds an explicit **normative requirement**: the loss function $\ell$ evaluated in $\theta_{\mathrm{PO}}$ must include the externalized social harm borne by affected citizens rather than narrow institutional default loss alone.

### **5.2. Tension 6 — Ensemble Averages versus Trajectories in Irreversible Time**

The second rupture of the third filter concerns the arrow of time. As Nicholas Georgescu-Roegen (1971) argued in *The Entropy Law and the Economic Process*, standard formal models routinely adopt the reversible mechanical time of classical kinematics, in which trajectories can be run backward without loss. Living organisms, ecosystems, households, and public infrastructures, however, inhabit **irreversible thermodynamic and historical time**: dissipated reserves, cumulative physiological wear, and crossings of ruin barriers admit no time-reversal symmetry.

Ignoring temporal irreversibility produces the sixth constitutive tension: conflating the **ensemble average** across parallel realizations at a fixed instant with the **time average** along a single historical trajectory. Consider a sequence of river crossings over thin ice where each crossing carries a $90\%$ probability of multiplying wealth by $1.5$ and a $10\%$ probability of drowning (an absorbing barrier where wealth drops permanently to $0$) `[Assumed]`. Across an ensemble of $100$ parallel travelers crossing once on a single afternoon, $90$ survive with $1.5 W_0$, yielding a positive ensemble mean return of $+35\%$ `[Assumed]`. For a single historical traveler—a person, a firm, or a municipal reserve—who must cross the river $100$ days in succession carrying prior accumulations forward, survival probability after $100$ crossings is $0.9^{100} \approx 2.66 \times 10^{-5}$ `[Assumed]`: the time-trajectory growth rate collapses to ruin with near certainty.

To analyze this divergence without historical or mathematical conflation, Stochology distinguishes three levels:

1. *Discrete multiplicative compounding versus Itô's continuous-time stochastic lemma (Itô 1944, 1951):* Already in elementary discrete arithmetic, multiplicative processes obey the geometric–arithmetic mean inequality: losing $50\%$ of a reserve and then gaining $50\%$ yields $(1 - 0.50)(1 + 0.50) = 0.75$ `[Assumed]`, a net loss of $25\%$ despite a zero arithmetic mean return. In continuous time, Kiyosi Itô's (1944, 1951) stochastic calculus establishes the exact differential rule. For a resource $W_t$ governed by geometric Brownian motion $\mathrm{d}W_t / W_t = \mu\,\mathrm{d}t + \sigma\,\mathrm{d}B_t$ with ensemble drift $\mu$ and volatility $\sigma$, Itô's lemma proves that logarithmic wealth evolves as
   $$\mathrm{d}\ln W_t = \left(\mu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2\right)\mathrm{d}t + \sigma\,\mathrm{d}B_t \qquad \text{\texttt{[Assumed]}}$$
   The term $-\frac{1}{2}\sigma^2$ is the continuous **Itô volatility drain**: variance pulls the long-run time-average growth rate $g = \mu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2$ strictly below the ensemble drift $\mu$ (in `djia_1885_2023.csv`, across $37{,}931$ daily closing rows yielding $n = 37{,}930$ daily arithmetic returns $r_t = (P_t - P_{t-1})/P_{t-1}$, ensemble drift is $\hat{\mu} = 0.000243$ whereas time-average logarithmic growth is $g = 0.000186$, a $23.6\%$ volatility drain `[Measured]`);
2. *Kelly–Breiman growth, Merton's CRRA portfolio theory, Samuelson's critique, and Baker–McHale epistemic shrinkage (Kelly 1956; Breiman 1961; Merton 1969, 1971; Samuelson 1971, 1979; Baker and McHale 2013; Peters 2019):* For a leveraged exposure fraction $f$ in an excess-return process with risk-free rate $r$, the time-average logarithmic growth rate is
   $$g(f) = r + f(\mu - r) - \frac{1}{2}f^2\sigma^2 \qquad \text{\texttt{[Assumed]}}$$
   Its **excess growth rate over the risk-free rate**, $g(f) - r = f(\mu - r) - \frac{1}{2}f^2\sigma^2$, is maximized at the **Kelly fraction** $f_{\mathrm{plug\text{-}in}}^* = (\mu - r)/\sigma^2$ (Kelly 1956; Breiman 1961). At double the Kelly fraction ($f = 2 f_{\mathrm{plug\text{-}in}}^*$), excess growth vanishes so that $g(2 f_{\mathrm{plug\text{-}in}}^*) = r$ `[Assumed]`; for $f > 2 f_{\mathrm{plug\text{-}in}}^*$, the portfolio underperforms the risk-free rate ($g(f) < r$), and total growth $g(f)$ becomes **strictly negative ($g(f) < 0$)** whenever $f$ exceeds the zero-crossing threshold $f_0 = f_{\mathrm{plug\text{-}in}}^* + \sqrt{(f_{\mathrm{plug\text{-}in}}^*)^2 + 2r/\sigma^2}$ `[Assumed]` (which reduces to $2 f_{\mathrm{plug\text{-}in}}^*$ when $r = 0$). Precision requires noting two critical caveats. First, Kelly's rule avoids *asymptotic* ruin in idealized continuous processes, but over finite horizons it still permits severe interim drawdowns; furthermore, when drift $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$ are estimated from a finite **sampling-variance effective sample size** $n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} = \min(n_{\mathrm{cluster}}, n_{\mathrm{Kish}})$, Rose Baker and Ian McHale (2013) show under Gaussian estimation uncertainty that the optimal fraction requires **epistemic Kelly shrinkage**:
   $$f_{\mathrm{epi}}^* = \frac{n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}}{n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} + 1}\,\frac{\hat{\mu} - r}{\hat{\sigma}^2} \qquad \text{\texttt{[Assumed]}}$$
   (Note the strict categorical boundary enforced in Section 4.2: $n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}$ shrinks for *sampling variance*, whereas systematic selection bias $\rho_{R,Y} \ne 0$ shifts location and enters exclusively via the pessimistic lower bound $L_t$ of the drift.) Second, historical and analytical precision requires separating the distinct contributions of **Robert C. Merton (1969, 1971)** and **Paul A. Samuelson (1971, 1979)**. Merton (1969, 1971) derived the continuous-time optimal portfolio rule $f^*(\gamma) = (\mu - r)/(\gamma\sigma^2)$ for general Constant Relative Risk Aversion (CRRA) utility $U(W) = W^{1-\gamma}/(1-\gamma)$, demonstrating that Kelly's logarithmic growth rule corresponds exactly to the special case $\gamma = 1$. Samuelson (1971, 1979) then deployed that equivalence to critique Kelly proponents who elevated the $\gamma = 1$ rule into a universal dominance theorem, proving that maximizing expected log-wealth does not dominate other risk-averse preferences ($\gamma > 1$) over finite horizons when survival is not at stake. Consequently, when Ole Peters (2019) synthesized this literature under the banner of *ergodicity economics*, its genuine methodological contribution lay not in refuting expected-utility axiomatics, but in foregrounding how unbuffered multiplicative exposure in the presence of absorbing barriers renders ensemble averages a treacherous guide for policy;
3. *Conditions for non-ergodic criticality and the distinction between absorbing ruin and ecological hysteresis:* Not every decision problem is non-ergodic. When losses are small, additive, and bounded far away from ruin thresholds, ensemble expectations remain serviceable approximations. Non-ergodic divergence becomes decisive when **(a) sequential multiplicative or state-dependent compounding** interacts with **(b) critical boundary crossings**. Here physical precision requires distinguishing two boundary regimes:
   - **Strictly absorbing ruin barriers** (clinical death, legal bankruptcy/insolvency, species extinction), from which recovery is physically or legally impossible ($\mathrm{TTR} = \infty$); and
   - **Hysteretic regime shifts** in ecological and socio-ecological systems (Holling 1973; Scheffer et al. 2009), where crossing an unstable fold bifurcation flips a lake, fishery, or climate subsystem into a degraded alternative stable state. Unlike clinical death, a hysteretic regime shift is not strictly absorbing—restoration remains physically possible, but only by driving environmental pressure far below the original tipping threshold at a drastically higher social and material recovery cost ($\mathrm{TTR}_{\mathrm{hysteretic}} \gg \mathrm{TTS}$).

### **5.3. Survivorship Bias, Ecological Resilience, and the Operational Mechanics of the Vital Margin**

Invoking survival barriers requires dispelling two symmetrical fallacies surrounding the concept of "survival":

- *Statistical survivorship bias (from world to archive, Wald 1943):* Analyzing Allied bombers returning from European sorties during the Second World War, Abraham Wald (1943) demonstrated that fuselage zones riddled with bullet holes on returning aircraft (wings and tail) indicated where hits were *survivable*, whereas the scarcity of hits on returning engines and cockpits indicated that aircraft struck there were lost at sea ($\mathbb{P}(\text{lost} \mid \text{engine hit}) \gg \mathbb{P}(\text{lost} \mid \text{wing hit})$, reconstructed in Chapter 7's `WaldSurvivorshipAudit` as $84.0\%$ engine lethality vs. $7.2\%$ wing lethality `[Assumed]`). Conditioning an archive on survivors inverts the true vulnerability map;
- *The normative fallacy of survival (from history to value):* In a non-ergodic world with unequal power, the mere fact that an institution or speculator has survived to date is neither proof of epistemic rationality nor a certificate of moral legitimacy, since tail risks can remain latent for years and powerful actors routinely externalize ruin onto unbuffered populations.

Stochology therefore separates (1) **material survival** as a *lexicographic precondition* (the physical floor required for agents and communities to continue existing and deliberating), (2) **epistemic survival** of conjectures under severe testing, and (3) **legitimate institutional survival**, which is bound by the duty not to cannibalize the vital margins of others.

The positive grounding of the **vital margin** draws on Georges Canguilhem's (1943/2009) philosophy of medicine and Crawford S. Holling's (1973) systems ecology, while preserving the full depth of both concepts. In *The Normal and the Pathological*, Canguilhem (1943/2009) rejected the positivist identification of health with conformity to a statistical mean: an organism capable of surviving only at a fixed average temperature or workload is already pathological. Crucially, Canguilhem's **biological normativity** is not merely a static buffer stock within a fixed regime; it is the *generative capacity to institute new physiological and practical norms* when the environment proves unfaithful, supported operationally by "a margin of tolerance for the inconsistencies of the environment" (*une marge de tolérance des infidélités du milieu*). In systems ecology, Holling (1973) similarly separated **engineering resilience** (local return speed $\lambda > 0$ near an equilibrium point) from **ecological resilience** (the width $D_t = x_{\mathrm{high}}^* - x_{\mathrm{tip}}^*$ of the attraction basin measuring how large a shock the system can absorb before crossing an unstable separatrix into a hysteretic degraded regime).

Here a crucial theoretical tension must be resolved: in Section 4.1, Jensen's inequality treated environmental fluctuation as a convex cost ($\Delta_J \ge 0$), whereas Holling (1973) and Crawford Holling and Gary Meffe (1996), in their classic diagnosis of the **"Pathology of Natural Resource Management" (*command-and-control*)**, showed that policies obsessed with suppressing all natural fluctuations (such as extinguishing every minor brushfire or stabilizing river flows artificially) destroy ecological resilience. How can fluctuation be simultaneously a convex cost and a condition of resilience? The synthesis lies in separating **scale and regime**: rigid command-and-control suppression of low-amplitude variability causes hidden stress or combustible fuel to accumulate silently, shrinking the basin radius $D_t$ until an inevitable shock triggers a catastrophic convex jump across the separatrix. A well-designed vital margin therefore *permits and absorbs low-amplitude natural variability* (which exercises adaptive normativity and preserves basin width $D_t$) while *maintaining hard buffers against crossing the tipping or ruin boundary*.

To operationalize both dimensions without collapsing ecological basin width into a purely local return-rate heuristic, Stochology links the **operational stock dimension** ($S_{\mathrm{stock}}$) and the **dynamical basin dimension** ($S_{\mathrm{basin}}$) across three coupled mechanisms:

1. *Heavy-traffic queueing congestion (Kingman 1961; Scarf 1960):* On the operational stock plane, when a service facility (hospital, court, electrical grid) is pushed toward $100\%$ utilization ($\rho = \lambda_{\mathrm{arr}} / \mu_{\mathrm{serv}} \to 1^-$) to eliminate "idle capacity," John F. C. Kingman's (1961) heavy-traffic approximation proves that expected waiting time explodes hyperbolically with arrival and service coefficients of variation ($c_a^2, c_s^2$):
   $$\mathbb{E}[W_q] \approx \left(\frac{\rho}{1 - \rho}\right)\left(\frac{c_a^2 + c_s^2}{2}\right)\tau_{\mathrm{service}} \qquad \text{\texttt{[Assumed]}}$$
   (In `vic_elec.csv`, across $n = 52{,}608$ half-hourly electricity demand readings, mean demand is $4{,}665\text{ MW}$ while the $95\text{th}$-percentile surge reaches $6{,}106\text{ MW}$, a $+30.9\%$ jump `[Measured]`; evaluating Kingman's formula as utilization shifts from $\rho = 0.78$ to $\rho = 0.97$ inflates the queueing delay multiplier from $3.55\times$ to $32.33\times$ `[Assumed]`.) Preserving operational headroom and Herbert Scarf's (1960) $(s, S)$ inventory buffers is the direct stock-level defense against queue strangulation;
2. *Allostatic load, basin contraction, and the heavy-tail basin-breach probability (McEwen 1998; Hill 1975; Holling 1973):* On the physiological and ecological plane, chronic operation near the ceiling accumulates **allostatic load** $A_t$ (McEwen 1998), lowering carrying capacity $K(A_t) = K_0 - \gamma A_t$ and contracting the attraction-basin radius $D_t = x_{\mathrm{high}}^*(A_t) - x_{\mathrm{tip}}^*(A_t)$ (in Chapter 7's bistable May–Holling model `CanguilhemHollingBasin`, allostatic wear of $\Delta K = 2.5$ shrinks the attraction-basin radius by $53.8\%$, from $D_0 = 6.40$ to $D_{\mathrm{worn}} = 2.96$, while driving lag-1 autocorrelation from $\rho_1 = 0.578$ to $0.791$ `[Simulated]`). Connecting Section 4.1's heavy-tailed shocks $X_t \sim \mathrm{Pareto}(x_m, \alpha)$ directly to Holling's basin radius $D_t > x_m$ yields the exact **single-shock basin-breach probability**:
   $$\mathbb{P}\bigl(X_t > D_t\bigr) = \left(\frac{x_m}{D_t}\right)^{\alpha} \qquad \text{\texttt{[Assumed]}}$$
   This equation formalizes the exact synergy between Tension 3 and Tension 6: a heavier tail (smaller $\alpha \le 2$) and an allostatically narrowed basin (smaller $D_t$) interact as a power law to accelerate regime collapse;
3. *Coupling ecological basin width ($D_t/D_0$), Critical Slowing Down, and the $\mathrm{TTS}_t \ge \mathrm{TTR}_t$ bridge (Holling 1973; Dakos et al. 2008; Scheffer et al. 2009; Boettiger and Hastings 2012; Simchi-Levi, Schmidt, and Wei 2014):* Because Holling (1973) demonstrated that local engineering return speed $\lambda_t$ and global ecological basin width $D_t$ are distinct dimensions—and because Carl Boettiger and Alan Hastings (2012) proved that rising lag-1 autocorrelation $\hat{\rho}_{1,t} = e^{-\lambda_t \Delta t}$ in finite windows can suffer from false alarms or depend on the sampling interval $\Delta t$—Stochology refuses to reduce $S_{\mathrm{basin}, t}$ to $\hat{\rho}_{1,t}$ alone. Instead, in `CommunityReserveEnv`, $S_{\mathrm{basin}, t}$ takes the non-compensatory minimum of the **ecological basin-width ratio** $D_t(A_t)/D_0$ (shrunk by cumulative McEwen allostatic wear $A_t$) and the **Critical Slowing Down margin** $\operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}\!\bigl(\frac{1 - \hat{\rho}_{1,t}}{1 - \rho_{1,\mathrm{crit}}(\Delta t)}\bigr)$, where the critical threshold $\rho_{1,\mathrm{crit}}(\Delta t) = e^{-\lambda_{\min}\Delta t}$ is calibrated to the sampling interval $\Delta t$ against detrended AR(1)/Fourier surrogate envelopes (Dakos et al. 2008; Boettiger and Hastings 2012). This dynamical state then feeds directly into David Simchi-Levi, William Schmidt, and Yehua Wei's (2014) physical **breathing-time inequality**, linking operational recovery time $\mathrm{TTR}_t$ to the inverse recovery rate $1/\lambda_t$ with explicit dimensional consistency (both measured in shifts $\Delta t$, with log-attenuation factor $\kappa_{\mathrm{rec}} = \ln(d_{\mathrm{crisis},0}/d_{\mathrm{tol}})$):
   $$\mathrm{TTS}_t = \frac{B_t}{d_{\mathrm{crisis}, t}} \;\ge\; \mathrm{TTR}_t = \frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{rec}}}{\lambda_t} = \frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{rec}}\,\Delta t}{-\ln \rho_{1, t}} \qquad \text{\texttt{[Assumed]}}$$
   As allostatic wear contracts $D_t$ or the basin shallows ($\lambda_t \to 0^+, \rho_{1,t} \to 1$), recovery time $\mathrm{TTR}_t \to \infty$ diverges, triggering the lexicographic safety brake before the separatrix is crossed.

## **VI. The Architecture of Judgment Under Turbulence: *Télos*, Moral Luck, Apt Agency, and Institutional Contract**

### **6.1. Tension 7 (First Face) — Antipater's Archer (*Télos* and *Skopós*), Moral Luck, and the Normative Bridge**

Even when an agent respects all six preceding locks, no human precaution eliminates contingency: a scrupulous clinician may lose a patient to an unpredictable complication, a civil-defense evacuation may be followed by a sudden shift in a cyclone's track, and a reckless executive may survive several quarters unharmed by sheer luck. This generates the seventh constitutive tension: how can we evaluate the responsibility of decision-makers under uncertainty without either worshiping fortuitous outcomes or granting impunity to reckless governance?

Hellenistic philosophy confronted this problem through a technical distinction formulated within the Stoa, whose philological transmission requires precision. In Book III of *De Finibus Bonorum et Malorum* (III.20–22; Cicero 1914), Cicero presents in Latin the analogy of the archer (*toxótēs*) or spear-thrower: the archer must do everything within his craft to aim straight (which Cicero terms the *ultimum* or *extremum bonorum*, the supreme good internal to right conduct—a quality of conjectural skill that classical Greek elsewhere associates with the *stókhos* root as **εὐστοχία, *eustokhía***, "good aim"), even though striking the external target (*propositum* or *scopus*) remains exposed to crosswinds after the shaft leaves the bow. While Cicero leaves the Greek scholarch unnamed in that passage, the anthology of **Joannes Stobaeus** (*Eclogae* II.7.6a, II.76.9–15 Wachsmuth = Long and Sedley 1987, 64C; Stobaeus 1884) explicitly preserves the authorship and exact Greek formula: the distinction belongs to **Antipater of Tarsus** (head of the Stoa in the second century BCE, formulating the archer analogy in response to the Academic skepticism and *Carneadea divisio* of **Carneades of Cyrene**, before Panaetius of Rhodes shifted Stoic ethics toward the four *personae* and practical appropriateness, **καθῆκον, *kathēkon***, in Cicero's *De Officiis* I.107–121; Cicero 1913). Antipater separated the **τέλος (*télos*, the internal end—defined technically in Stobaeus II.76.9–15 as $\tau\grave{o}\;\pi\tilde{\alpha}\nu\;\tau\grave{o}\;\kappa\alpha\theta\text{'}\;\alpha\dot{\upsilon}\tau\grave{o}\nu\;\pi\omicron\iota\varepsilon\tilde{\iota}\nu\;\delta\iota\eta\nu\varepsilon\kappa\tilde{\omega}\varsigma\;\kappa\alpha\grave{\iota}\;\dot{\alpha}\pi\alpha\rho\alpha\beta\alpha\tau\omega\varsigma$, "doing everything in one's power continuously and undeviatingly to achieve the primary natural things")** from the **σκοπός (*skopós*, the external target set before the agent)**. Hitting the *skopós* is a **preferred indifferent** (**προηγμένον, *proēgmenon***; rendered in Cicero, *De Finibus* III.20–22 as ***sumendum*** or ***eligendum***, "to be taken/selected," rather than *expetendum*, "to be sought as an ultimate end in itself"). At the same time, historical honesty requires noting that our fullest narrative witness—**Plutarch** (*De communibus notitiis adversus Stoicos* 1070f–1071e = Long and Sedley 1987, 64C, with Carneadean context across 64B–E; Plutarch 1976)—is a hostile polemicist drawing on Carneades' Academic critique of the Stoic archer analogy, arguing that no archer could value the act of aiming (*télos*) higher than hitting the target (*skopós*) unless aiming is ordered to hitting.

In twentieth-century ethics, **Bernard Williams (1981 [1976])** and **Thomas Nagel (1979 [1976])** reopened this problem in their landmark symposium on **moral luck**: Williams coined the concept of *moral luck* and introduced the category of *agent-regret* (through the cases of Gauguin and the blameless lorry driver), while Nagel formulated the analytical **fourfold taxonomy** distinguishing *constitutive*, *circumstantial*, *causal*, and *resultant* luck. Shortly thereafter, **Jonathan Baron and John C. Hershey (1988)** demonstrated experimentally how **outcome bias** leads observers to rate the very same decision as competent when the realization is favorable and negligent when it fails.

How does Stochology answer both Plutarch's ancient objection to the Stoic archer and the modern challenge of moral luck? First, to answer Plutarch without retreating into a purely inward ethics of good intentions, Stochology draws on **Ernest Sosa's (2007, 2015)** virtue epistemology (*AAA structure*): a performance is **accurate** if it hits the target (even by luck); **adroit** if it exercises genuine methodological competence (incorporating epistemic and vital margins); and **apt** if it succeeds *because* it is adroit. Aiming (*télos*) is therefore not severed from hitting (*skopós*): adroitness is defined by objective reliability across counterfactually close worlds. At the second order, Sosa (2015) defines **fully apt performance** (*meta-aptness*) as the reflective competence to assess the environmental risk of the field and determine whether one is in a position to take an apt shot at all. Extending Sosa's epistemic concept to practical institutional action—an explicit normative bridge constructed below—yields the duty to **lower the bow and withhold irreversible intervention** when identification bounds are wide and absorbing barriers are close, while simultaneously weighing the **cost of the unshot arrow (Type II / failure-to-rescue error)** whenever status-quo inaction itself inflicts irreversible harm within the $\mathrm{TTS} \ge \mathrm{TTR}$ window.

Second, Stochology does not claim that *aptness* dissolves resultant moral luck. Here Bernard Williams's (1981) distinction between *blame* and **agent-regret** is decisive—provided we enforce a strict boundary between **natural crosswinds** and **adulterated air before the shot**. Even when an adroit lorry driver—or a scrupulous clinician or infrastructure planner—took every due precaution and is rightly exempted from blame for negligence (correcting Baron–Hershey outcome bias), an adverse tail realization leaves an irreducible moral residue: *agent-regret*, the recognition that it was *one's own action or policy* that stood in the causal chain of another's loss. While Williams himself remained skeptical of reducing ethical life to a formal "morality system," Stochology translates the institutional analogue of agent-regret into a concrete public design requirement: **no-fault reparative compensation**, solidaristic insurance for citizens struck by residual tail events, and mandatory recording of the adverse outcome in the public registry. Crucially, however, resultant moral luck (`SosaJudgmentGrade::AdroitExAnteUnluckyOutcome`) and no-fault reparative *agent-regret* apply **exclusively when the deviation of the arrow results from the natural crosswinds of stochastic contingency after an adroit shot**. When the informational and communicative medium was deliberately corrupted prior to the decision—when leadership rigged the decision premises at the top (Simon 1947/1997 [1979]), suppressed partial-identification bounds, or silenced frontline warnings—**the air itself was adulterated before the shot**. In that regime (`GovernanceState::from_real_time_and_ex_post_gates` in `crates/stochology/src/governance.rs`), invoking "stochastic misfortune" or "unforeseeable crosswinds" is an agnotological fraud that masks deliberate betrayal of the institutional pact.

We are now in a position to state transparently the **normative bridge** that licenses the prescriptive duties of this essay. Rather than claiming to deduce moral duties from bare statistical facts alone, Stochology derives **ought from ought applied to institutional facts**—combining descriptive facts about archival omission and non-ergodic ruin with explicitly imported normative premises across three steps:

1. *The ethics of belief and pragmatic encroachment (Clifford 1877/1879; Adler 2002; Fantl and McGrath 2009):* In epistemology, William Kingdon Clifford (1877/1879) and Jonathan Adler (2002) established that belief and public assertion are governed by an evidentialist norm of proportioning assent to evidence, while Jeremy Fantl and Matthew McGrath's (2009) principle of **pragmatic encroachment** shows that whether a body of evidence $E$ suffices to warrant asserting "policy $a$ is safe" is not invariant to practical stakes: as the severity of potential harm increases, the evidential threshold required for warrant rises. By itself, however, this first step establishes an *epistemic* norm of warrant, not yet an interpersonal moral duty;
2. *The structural asymmetry of institutional risk imposition (Hansson 2013):* For a solitary decision-maker who exposes only their own wealth or body to risk, preserving a vital margin is a *prudential* requirement, not a duty of justice. Sven Ove Hansson's (2013) ethics of risk supplies the empirical-institutional premise that transforms prudence into interpersonal morality: in bureaucratic and corporate governance, **the decision-maker who selects the risk exposure is systematically distinct from the third parties who bear the physical downside if the gamble fails**. Moreover, even when an ex-ante individual ruin probability appears small ($p_i = 10^{-3}$ `[Assumed]`), imposing that exposure across an unbuffered population of $N = 10^5$ `[Assumed]` citizens yields an expected toll of $\mathbb{E}[\text{ruin}] = N p_i = 100$ `[Assumed]` ruined lives—stripping the institution of the excuse that resultant catastrophe was an unforeseeable fluke;
3. *Scanlonian contractualism, the statistical-victim problem, and reconciliation of the individualist restriction (Scanlon 1998; Hansson 2013; Frick 2015):* Under Thomas M. Scanlon's (1998) contractualism, an act is wrong if its performance under the circumstances would be disallowed by any principle for the general regulation of behavior that no affected person could reasonably reject. Crucially, Scanlon's **individualist restriction** forbids justifying a principle by summing aggregate conveniences across a group to outweigh a grave burden imposed on a single person. To respect Scanlon's individualist restriction strictly without illicitly aggregating "a hundred people" into a collective super-claimant, the contractualist comparison must be conducted **person by person** using Scanlon's (1998, ch. 5) requirement of **due care**, Hansson's (2013, ch. 4) **reciprocal risk-acceptance criterion**, and Johann Frick's (2015) analysis of **ex-ante contractualism for statistical versus identified lives**. Consider the **Candidate Principle of Unbuffered Point Optimization** ($P_{\mathrm{naive}}$): *"An institution may size reserves and deploy interventions based on point estimates and high-utilization averages whenever doing so maximizes expected net efficiency."* Compare the generic reasons of two individual standpoints:
   - *Standpoint A (any single uncounted or unbuffered citizen exposed to an absorbing barrier):* Under $P_{\mathrm{naive}}$, this individual citizen is exposed without warning or consent to preventable irreversible ruin (death, insolvency, permanent loss of livelihood) because the institution declined to bound archival missingness or maintain survival slack;
   - *Standpoint B (any single taxpayer or institutional beneficiary of lean efficiency):* Under the rival **Stochological Principle of Bounded Exposure** ($P_{\mathrm{stoch}}$), this individual bears a bounded, reversible opportunity cost to fund capacity headroom ($1 - \rho \ge 0.20$ `[Assumed]`) and partial-identification audits.

   Under the explicitly imported normative premise that **an individual's generic objection to unconsented, preventable exposure to an irreversible absorbing barrier is weightier than another individual's generic objection to a bounded, reversible efficiency cost**—unless the risk exposure is integral to a reciprocal system of protection that benefits and safeguards that very individual (Scanlon 1998, ch. 5; Hansson 2013; Frick 2015)—any single citizen in Standpoint A can reasonably reject $P_{\mathrm{naive}}$. Two classic objections from welfare economics clarify why this premise is non-compensatory at the ruin boundary: (i) against the *Kaldor–Hicks / ex-post compensation objection*, crossing a strictly absorbing barrier ($\mathrm{TTR} = \infty$) extinguishes the subject capable of receiving compensation; and (ii) against the *infinite-precaution paralysis objection* (that any societal activity carries non-zero background risk), $P_{\mathrm{stoch}}$ does not demand zero risk, but rather forbids *liquidating known epistemic and vital margins* ($\mathrm{TTS}_t < \mathrm{TTR}_t$) for asymmetric efficiency gains. Concealing the width of $[L_t, U_t]$ or liquidating the vital margin when another person will bear the impact of the arrow violates reciprocal justification.

When the three margins come into practical conflict under scarcity or emergency, Stochology proposes an explicit **Normative Priority Rule** (formulated as a revisable constitutional working order):

- **First Priority (Lexicographic Survival Floor and Symmetric Barrier Adjudication):** Protecting immediate human life and preventing the crossing of an absorbing ruin barrier ($\mathrm{TTS}_t \ge \mathrm{TTR}_t$, Roy 1952)—including preventing life-threatening classification errors in acute clinical or emergency triage—takes lexical priority over procedural delay (governing Reflexive Brake 3) and over point-efficiency gains. When an extreme crisis presents a **symmetric life-versus-life trade-off** where *every* available action exposes some citizens to an absorbing barrier (so that the zero-ruin set $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{safe}}$ is empty), First Priority adjudicates between the competing absorbing barriers by minimizing maximum expected mortality regret across the partial-identification interval $[L_t, U_t]$ under public $S3^*$ audit;
- **Second Priority (Epistemic Non-Concealment, Even Under Emergency Fast-Path):** Both within the survival-admissible set and during an acute emergency fast-path (Reflexive Brake 3), the duty to declare honest partial-identification bounds $[L_t, U_t]$ without fabrication or agnotological inflation remains binding: when $\tau_{\mathrm{bureaucratic}} > \mathrm{TTS}_t$, Reflexive Brake 3 suspends *slow collegiate deliberation*, never epistemic honesty—the emergency release is executed immediately while simultaneously logging the wide interval $[L_t, U_t]$ in `TemporalVault` so that emergency action never fabricates point certainty;
- **Third Priority (Equitable Allocation of Opportunity Cost and Liability):** The trade-off between present relief and future reserve stock (Reflexive Brake 2) and the distribution of non-lethal classification errors must be publicly justified under constitutional governance with downside liability at the top.

### **6.2. Tension 7 (Second Face) — Group Agency, Cybernetic Veto, Contractual Asymmetry, and the Fairness Trilemma**

In modern society, the agent who sets interest rates, hospital bed ratios, environmental quotas, or judicial risk scores is not a solitary Stoic archer, but a corporate or state institution. Relying on individual moral heroism inside a perversely structured bureaucracy is sociologically naive. Translating apt agency into institutional architecture requires combining **Christian List and Philip Pettit's (2011) theory of group agency**, **Miranda Fricker's (2007, 2010, 2020) critique of epistemic injustice and institutional epistemic vices**, **Stafford Beer's (1972) organizational cybernetics**, and the **economics and sociology of contracts and classification**:

1. *Constitutionalizing the conjunctive safety brake and eliminating testimonial injustice (List and Pettit 2011; Beer 1972; Fricker 2007, 2010, 2020):* List and Pettit (2011) proved via the **discursive dilemma** that a committee voting by simple majority on a conclusion ($C \leftrightarrow P_1 \wedge P_2 \wedge P_3$, where $P_1, P_2, P_3$ are the three safeguards) can vote to approve an unsafe deployment even when a majority of specialists judges at least one safeguard violated `[Assumed]`. Rational group agency therefore requires a **premise-based conjunctive constitution** in which any violated safety premise exercises veto power. In Stafford Beer's (1972) Viable System Model, this requires separating operational management from an independent **System $3^*$ ($S3^*$) sporadic audit channel** and an **algedonic emergency channel**—explicitly incorporating **representation from affected communities and frontline workers** alongside technical auditors so that the boundary between model assumptions and lived harm is not monopolized by technocrats—which bypasses hierarchical filtering to trigger automatic suspension when errors breach declared bounds. Here Miranda Fricker's (2007, 2010, 2020) work operates on two distinct levels that must both be preserved:
   - First, on the **intrinsic deontological plane**, **testimonial injustice** (discounting a speaker's credibility due to identity prejudice, Fricker 2007) dishonors frontline workers, nurses, or marginalized residents in their fundamental capacity as knowers—an intrinsic epistemic and moral wrong even before its downstream consequences materialize;
   - Second, on the **cybernetic-institutional plane** (Fricker 2010, 2020), when an organization exhibits *inferential inertia* or applies a credibility discount $\delta_{\mathrm{cred}} \in [0, 1)$ (whether rooted in identity prejudice or hierarchical status devaluation) to frontline algedonic distress signals, it attenuates the effective per-shift log-evidence increment by $(1 - \delta_{\mathrm{cred}})$, multiplying the shifts required to reach an emergency circuit-breaker threshold by $1 / (1 - \delta_{\mathrm{cred}})$ `[Assumed model mechanism]` (in Chapter 8's `AlgedonicChannel` benchmark, $\delta_{\mathrm{cred}} = 0.55$ `[Assumed]` stretches recognition from $5$ shifts to $12$ shifts, breaching $\mathrm{TTS} = 8$ `[Assumed]`);
2. *Ending asymmetric risk-shifting, premise-rigging, and scapegoat blame transfer at the top while protecting the operational base (Simon 1947/1997 [1979]; Jensen and Meckling 1976; Holmström 1979; Stiglitz and Weiss 1981; Girard 1986 [1982/2004]; Innes 1990; Taleb 2018):* While Nassim Nicholas Taleb (2018) popularized the demand for symmetry of exposure ("skin in the game"), applying raw downside penalties to frontline operators (on-call clinicians, teachers, maintenance crews) who lack authority over system capacity or leverage is both unjust and inefficient. Bengt Holmström's (1979) **informativeness principle** proves that an additional performance signal should enter an agent's incentive contract if and only if it is **statistically informative about the agent's hidden action (effort)** conditional on already contracted signals (that is, the existing signals are not sufficient statistics for the pair) `[Assumed]`. Because frontline operators do not control macro-level reserve sizing or exogenous tail surges, penalizing them for systemic overload injects uninsurable noise without effort informativeness. Conversely, at the executive top, three distinct economic mechanisms illuminate the pathology of asymmetric upside: **Michael C. Jensen and William H. Meckling (1976)** showed how limited-liability equity incentives induce **asset substitution (risk-shifting)** toward high-variance projects; **Joseph E. Stiglitz and Andrew Weiss (1981)** demonstrated in credit markets how debt contracts under imperfect information induce borrowers to select riskier, lower-success-probability projects while rationing credit; and **Robert D. Innes (1990)** proved in principal–agent theory that when an agent subject to **limited liability** (downside bounded at zero) chooses hidden **effort ex ante**, the constrained-optimal monotonic contract takes a *"live-or-die"* (debt/option-like) form. When executive remuneration pairs limited downside liability with uncapped upside bonuses, these mechanisms create a direct incentive to liquidate vital buffers and shift probability mass into extreme tails. Furthermore, combining **Herbert Simon's (1947/1997 [1979])** analysis of premise control with **René Girard's (1986 [1982/2004])** anthropology of the **scapegoat mechanism** explains how executives transfer not only financial risk, but moral and cognitive guilt onto the operational base (`GovernanceState::from_real_time_and_ex_post_gates` in `crates/stochology/src/governance.rs`). By preserving the formal illusion of frontline autonomy inside a decision labyrinth whose premises and capacity buffers were pre-rigged at the top, leadership prepares a sacrificial target in advance: when the depleted reserve collapses, attributing $b_{\mathrm{frontline}} = 85.0\%$ `[Assumed]` of institutional blame to frontline operators—whereas in Chapter 8's variance-decomposition audit (`ContractAndLiabilityAudit`), $L_{\mathrm{struct}} = 83.3\%$ `[Simulated]` of tail deficit variance originates in executive structural sizing versus only $L_{\mathrm{oper}} = 6.3\%$ `[Simulated]` in frontline operational noise (with $10.4\%$ exogenous/interaction residual)—produces a **scapegoat blame transfer** of $\Delta_{\mathrm{scapegoat}} = \max(0, b_{\mathrm{frontline}} - L_{\mathrm{oper}}) = +78.7\text{ percentage points}$ and a **$13.6\times$ blame inversion ratio** ($b_{\mathrm{frontline}} / L_{\mathrm{oper}}$) `[Simulated]`. Stochology therefore mandates **solidaristic limited-liability protection at the operational base** paired with **bitemporal clawback escrows (`TemporalVault`) and patrimonial restitution at the decision-making top**;
3. *Protecting unmeasured care and breaking the pragmatic double-bind trap in multitask environments (Bateson 1972/2000; Holmström and Milgrom 1991; Strathern 1997; Espeland and Sauder 2007):* When professional work comprises both an easily counted proxy task (consultations per hour, arrest quotas, publication counts) and an unmeasured high-value dimension (diagnostic listening, de-escalation, replicable rigor), Bengt Holmström and Paul Milgrom (1991) proved that high-powered incentives on the counted metric rationally drive effort on unmeasured care toward zero—a formal mechanism reinforced sociologically by audit and ranking reactivity (Strathern 1997; Espeland and Sauder 2007). When high-powered proxy coercion ($\beta_{\mathrm{proxy}, t} \to 1$) is combined with Fricker's testimonial discount on frontline alarms ($\delta_{\mathrm{cred}, t} \to 1$) and the disabling of the constitutional $S3^*$ brake ($\mathbf{1}\{\text{brake}_t\} = 0$), the frontline practitioner is locked into **Gregory Bateson's (1972/2000) double-bind trap** (`BatesonDoubleBindAudit`, $\mathrm{DB}_t = \beta_{\mathrm{proxy}, t}\cdot\delta_{\mathrm{cred}, t}\cdot(1 - \mathbf{1}\{\text{brake}_t\}) = 0.510$ in `NoGovernance` vs. $0.000$ in `FullTriad` `[Simulated]`): condemned as underperforming if they pause throughput to protect unmeasured safety, and condemned as negligent scapegoats if they obey the metric and a tail crisis erupts;
4. *Public justification under Chouldechova's Fairness Trilemma and the duty of the embedded practitioner (Chouldechova 2017):* When an institution deploys a risk instrument across groups $g \in \{A, B\}$ marked by unequal historical base rates $p_A \ne p_B \in (0, 1)$, Alexandra Chouldechova (2017) proved via Bayes's rule the exact algebraic identity linking the False Positive Rate ($\mathrm{FPR}_g$, the proportion of negative-outcome individuals subjected to false alarms), the False Negative Rate ($\mathrm{FNR}_g$, the proportion of positive-need or positive-risk individuals missed by the rule), and Positive Predictive Value ($\mathrm{PPV}_g$, calibration parity):
   $$\mathrm{FPR}_g = \frac{p_g}{1 - p_g} \cdot \frac{1 - \mathrm{PPV}_g}{\mathrm{PPV}_g} \cdot \bigl(1 - \mathrm{FNR}_g\bigr) \qquad \text{\texttt{[Assumed]}}$$
   Whenever $p_A \ne p_B$ and $\mathrm{PPV}_A = \mathrm{PPV}_B$, it is algebraically impossible to equalize both $\mathrm{FPR}$ and $\mathrm{FNR}$ across groups (in `toronto_arrests.csv` across $n_{\mathrm{all}} = 5{,}226$ arrests, stratifying across the $n_{\mathrm{stratum}} = 3{,}577$ records in the intersectional contrast `colour == Black && !employed` versus `colour == White && employed` yields non-release base rates $p_A = 0.393$ and $p_B = 0.112$, so enforcing equal $\mathrm{PPV} = 0.65$ and equal $\mathrm{TPR} = 0.80$ ($\mathrm{FNR} = 0.20$) inflicts $\mathrm{FPR}_A = 0.278$ vs. $\mathrm{FPR}_B = 0.054$—a $5.13\times$ false-positive disparity ratio $\mathrm{FPR}_A / \mathrm{FPR}_B$, and a $2.11\times$ disparity ratio across all $5{,}226$ records by race marginal alone where $p_{\mathrm{Black}} = 0.259$ vs. $p_{\mathrm{White}} = 0.142$ `[Measured]`). Connecting Chouldechova's mathematical theorem to Scanlon's contractualist principle of non-rejectability yields a direct **normative consequence**: because no value-neutral technical optimum exists under unequal base rates, presenting an algorithm as "objectively fair" conceals a political choice about which group will bear excess false alarms or excess unmet need. The institution is morally obligated to disclose its chosen error trade-off publicly, and the individual statistician or engineer embedded inside an institution that refuses disclosure has the professional duty to record the disparity in the independent $S3^*$ audit log and refuse to sign off on a false claim of neutrality;
5. *Cognitive ergonomics and natural frequencies against alert fatigue (Gigerenzer and Hoffrage 1995; Wong et al. 2021; Zheng and Meister 2025):* Finally, institutional accountability collapses if frontline human operators are cognitively overwhelmed. Jieyu Zheng and Markus Meister (2025) demonstrated that human behavioral and sensory-motor information throughput operates under a biological bottleneck of roughly $10\text{ bits/s}$ `[Literature]`. When commercial clinical algorithms flood wards with low-specificity alarms—as Andrew Wong et al. (2021) documented in showing that the widely deployed Epic Sepsis Model generated an $88\%$ false-alarm rate ($1 - \mathrm{PPV} = 88\%$, $\mathrm{PPV} = 12\%$) `[Literature]`—operators suffer alert fatigue and disable or ignore the channel. Stochology requires filtering automated alerts to respect the $10\text{ bits/s}$ deliberative ceiling and communicating residual uncertainty in **natural frequencies** (Gigerenzer and Hoffrage 1995; e.g., in Chapter 8's `CognitiveAlertFilter` clinical benchmark on $N = 1{,}000$ patients with $2\%$ sepsis prevalence: *"out of $1{,}000$ patients, $20$ develop sepsis; the unfiltered score triggers at $93.9\%$ FDR ($\mathrm{PPV} = 6.1\%$), whereas the throughput-filtered alert triggers on $31$ patients, of whom $16$ are true cases and $15$ are false alarms, raising $\mathrm{PPV}$ to $52.7\%$" `[Assumed]`*);
6. *The political ontology of the promise, the suffocation of fraud, and the refusal of the automaton (Spinoza 2007 [1670/2003]; Kant 1996 [1785/2007]; Weil 1952 [1949/2001]; Arendt 1958 [2016]; Habermas 1984 [1981/2012]):* At its deepest normative level, the institutional apparatus of $S_{\mathrm{gov}}$ is not a merely technical set of incentive patches, but a defense of democratic and communicative agency under uncertainty. In *The Human Condition*, **Hannah Arendt (1958 [2016])** showed that because human action unfolds in an irreversible and unpredictable web of plural relationships, the faculty of making and keeping **promises** is the only moral force capable of erecting "islands of security" in an ocean of future uncertainty without destroying freedom. The cryptographic bitemporal commitment of `TemporalVault`—sealing hypotheses, partial-identification bounds, and structural liability shares at $t_1$ before outcomes materialize at $t_2$—is the institutional translation of Arendt's promise: retroactively rewriting those pacts to scapegoat subordinates when a crisis hits is a direct assault on the public realm. ### **7.2. Reflexive Self-Application: Goodhart Brakes, Operationalized Falsifiers, and Meta-Theoretical Limits**

An essay that diagnoses how theories insulate themselves and how institutional indicators degenerate under control pressure would fail its own standard if it presented Stochology as immune to perversion. Two reflexive questions must therefore be answered explicitly: what happens if an organization turns the three safeguards of Stochology itself into isolated bureaucratic targets under Goodhart's Law? And what pre-registered observable outcomes would empirically falsify the core claims of this program?

First, each axis of the Nuclear Triad degenerates if maximized as a raw one-sided quantity detached from the other two, and each possesses an internal mathematical and institutional brake embedded in $(S_{\mathrm{epi}}, S_{\mathrm{vital}}, S_{\mathrm{gov}})$:

1. *Degeneration of the Epistemic Margin — Skeptical Inflation and the Alibi of Indecision:* If an agency rewards analysts solely for nominal interval coverage ($\mathbf{1}\{S_t \in [L_t, U_t]\}$), analysts can game the target by widening $[L_t, U_t]$ to the trivial support $[y_{\min}, y_{\max}]$. Such an interval is never empirically falsified, yet conveys zero information and shields officials from action. **Reflexive Brake 1:** Under the Murphy (1973), Gneiting, Balabdaoui, and Raftery (2007), and Dimitriadis, Gneiting, and Jordan (2021) CORP decomposition, calibration ($\mathrm{REL}_{\mathrm{CORP}} \to 0$) is inseparable from **discriminative resolution** ($\mathrm{RES}_{\mathrm{CORP}} > 0$), and $S_{\mathrm{epi}}$ penalizes uninformative width via $1 - ((U - L)/(y_{\max} - y_{\min}))^2$. Lazy skeptical inflation drives $\mathrm{RES}_{\mathrm{CORP}} = 0.029$ (versus $0.117$ for the sharp calibrated forecast in Chapter 9's audit `[Measured]`) and collapses $S_{\mathrm{epi}} \to 0$;
2. *Degeneration of the Vital Margin — Regressive Hoarding and Bureaucratic Self-Preservation:* If reserve accumulation is turned into an unconditional target blind to present deprivation, a ministry or corporation may hoard liquidity or withhold urgent relief from vulnerable households under the pretext of "protecting the future buffer." **Reflexive Brake 2:** In Scarf's (1960) inventory theory and Samuelson's (1971, 1979) intertemporal welfare analysis, every precautionary buffer $R$ is bounded above by **marginal social opportunity cost** $c_{\mathrm{urgent}}$. Modeling net social welfare as $W(R) = \kappa \ln(1 + R) - c_{\mathrm{urgent}} R$ `[Assumed]`, welfare peaks at the finite optimum $R^* = \max(0, \kappa / c_{\mathrm{urgent}} - 1)$ `[Assumed]` and turns strictly negative under regressive hoarding ($W(R^*) = +2.55$ at $R^* = 3.0$ vs. $W(R_{\mathrm{hoard}}) = -11.97$ at $R_{\mathrm{hoard}} = 25.0$ for $\kappa = 4.0, c_{\mathrm{urgent}} = 1.0$ `[Assumed]`);
3. *Degeneration of Institutional Governance — Procedural Theater and Paralysis in Emergency:* If pre-registration, collegiate deliberation, and documentary auditing become a slow ritual ($\tau_{\mathrm{bureaucratic}} > \mathrm{TTS}$), an institution facing an acute flood, sepsis shock, or grid collapse will miss the critical instant of intervention (*kairós*) while completing paperwork. **Reflexive Brake 3:** Governance is lexicographically subordinated to the physical survival clock ($\mathrm{TTS} \ge \mathrm{TTR}$, Simchi-Levi, Schmidt, and Wei 2014). Whenever $\tau_{\mathrm{bureaucratic}} > \mathrm{TTS}$ (e.g., $\tau_{\mathrm{bureaucratic}} = 12.0 > \mathrm{TTS} = 4.0$ `[Assumed]`), Sosa's (2015) meta-aptness mandates triggering a pre-committed emergency fast-path ($\tau_{\mathrm{fast}} = 1.5 \le \mathrm{TTS} = 4.0$ `[Assumed]`) that releases vital buffers immediately while simultaneously recording the wide partial-identification interval $[L_t, U_t]$ in `TemporalVault` (preserving Priority 2 of Section 6.1) and deferring full collegiate forensic review until survival is secured;
4. *Degeneration of the Composite Index Itself — Self-Performativity and Checklist Compliance (Espeland and Stevens 1998; Hacking 1995; Espeland and Sauder 2007):* What if an institution turns the Liebig–Leontief index $M_{\mathrm{Leontief}} = \min(S_{\mathrm{epi}}, S_{\mathrm{vital}}, S_{\mathrm{gov}})$ itself into a bureaucratic certification badge or executive performance target? By Wendy Espeland and Mitchell Stevens's (1998) sociology of **commensuration** and Ian Hacking's (1995) **looping effects**, any composite safety score turned into a target invites cosmetic compliance—such as staging nominal pre-registrations, inflating stated capacity denominators so utilization $\hat{\rho}_t$ appears below $0.80$, or classifying active structural failures just above $\tau = 0.50$. **Reflexive Brake 4 (Anti-Checklist Constitutional Rule):** $M_{\mathrm{Leontief}}$ is constitutionally prohibited from serving as a scalar reward target or compensation multiplier; it operates exclusively as a *diagnostic circuit-breaker* whose component premises ($[L_t, U_t]$, physical buffer stock $B_t$, basin recovery rate $\lambda_t$, and unfiltered frontline algedonic signals) are audited directly through sporadic $S3^*$ field inspections co-governed by affected citizens.

## **VIII. Conclusion: The Seven Tensions and the Integrity of Aim**

Throughout this programmatic essay, we have articulated **Stochology** as an integrative framework and transversal grammar open to specialists across the humanities, law, social sciences, medicine, ecology, engineering, and statistical sciences. Starting from the classical Greek distinction between exact mechanical measurement (*métron*) and conjectural aiming through opacity (*stókhos*, *eustokhía*, *stokhastikē tékhnē*), we argued that responsible judgment when action must precede the outcome requires analyzing, as a single coupled loop, the passages across the **material-historical world**, the **archive and formal model**, and **institutional deliberation**.

Across the **three mediating filters and seven constitutive tensions**, we traced how each achievement of rationalization turns into a pathology when severed from its limits, and how the three safeguards of the Nuclear Triad—the **epistemic margin** ($S_{\mathrm{epi}}$), the **vital margin** ($S_{\mathrm{vital}}$), and **fully apt agency under institutional governance** ($S_{\mathrm{gov}}$)—cohere at their critical threshold under the Liebig–Leontief minimum $M_{\mathrm{Leontief}} = \min(S_{\mathrm{epi}}, S_{\mathrm{vital}}, S_{\mathrm{gov}})$. Where the epistemic margin is suppressed, physical buffers are sized blindly around a downward-biased point mirage (Collapse Proposition 1); where the vital margin is eliminated in the name of lean efficiency, complete diagnostic lucidity watches helplessly as heavy-tailed shocks breach the absorbing or hysteretic boundary (Collapse Proposition 2); where symmetric governance, algedonic listening, and downside clawbacks are absent, epistemic doubt is weaponized as an agnotological alibi while common reserves are pillaged under moral hazard and metric gaming (Collapse Proposition 3). To declare without fabrication the width of what the archive leaves unknown, to preserve the material and temporal slack that living systems require to institute norms in an unfaithful environment, and to answer in institutional solidarity for the impact of the arrow once released: such is the task of Stochology and the substantive meaning of the ethics of aim.

## **Glossary of Classical and Technical Terms**

### **Part A — Classical Greek, Latin, and Philosophical Terms**

- **Ἀκρίβεια (*akríbeia*):** Exactness or precision grounded in fixed mechanical measurement, number, and weighing (*métron*, *arithmós*, *stathmós*), contrasted by Plato (*Philebus* 55e–56c) with the conjectural rigor of *stokhasmós*.
- **Εὐστοχία (*eustokhía*) and εὐστοχεῖν (*eustokheîn*):** "Good aim" or skill in hitting the mark across an uncertain medium; the lexical bridge uniting the root **στόχος (*stókhos*)** with the classical figure of the archer (**τοξότης, *toxótēs***).
- **Καιρός (*kairós*):** The fleeting, critical instant of acute crisis or opportunity in clinical medicine (*Aphorismi* I.1: *ho dè kairòs oxýs*), navigation, and strategy when decisive action cannot be postponed beyond the physical survival window ($\mathrm{TTS}$).
- **Καθῆκον (*kathēkon*):** In Stoic ethics (developed especially by Panaetius of Rhodes; Cicero, *De Officiis* I.107–121), an "appropriate action" or incumbency suited to one's roles (*personae*) and circumstances.
- **Μελέτη (*melétē*):** Disciplined practice, study, or exercise, by whose conjectural aim (*melétēs stokhasmōi*, Plato, *Philebus* 56a) the conjectural arts attune judgment without a mechanical gauge.
- **Μῆτις (*mētis*):** Plastic, vigilant, and adaptive practical intelligence capable of navigating shifting and ambiguous terrains (Detienne and Vernant 1974).
- **Non-Identical (*das Nichtidentische*) and Reification (*Verdinglichung*):** In Georg Lukács (1923/1971) and Theodor W. Adorno (1966/1973), *reification* treats historically produced abstractions as natural things, whereas the *Non-Identical* names the singular, material excess of the object that resists exhaustion by any concept or statistical summary—leaving operational traces in partial-identification width $[L_M, U_M]$, model discrepancy $\delta(x)$, and unmeasured care.
- **Προηγμένα (*proēgmena*):** In Stoic ethics, "preferred indifferents" (rendered as *producta*, *promota*, or *praeposita* in Cicero, *De Finibus* III.20–22, corresponding to things that are *sumenda* or *eligenda*, "to be taken/selected," rather than *expetenda*)—external outcomes such as health or hitting the target (*skopós*) that a rational agent selects without equating them with moral virtue (*télos*).
- **Σημεῖα (*sēmeîa*):** Observable clinical symptoms, clues, and diagnostic traces (rendered in Latin as *signa*) theorized in the Hippocratic *Prognostic* and *On the Art* and in Galenic medicine (alongside conclusive signs, *tekmēria*).
- **Σκοπός (*skopós*):** The watched external target or mark (connected to *skopeîn*, "to look at," rendered in Latin as *scopus*, *meta*, or *propositum*), distinguished in Antipater of Tarsus's Stoic ethics from the internal end (*télos*).
- **Στόχος (*stókhos*), στοχάζεσθαι (*stokházesthai*), στοχαστικὴ τέχνη (*stokhastikē tékhnē*):** In its literal primary sense attested in Liddell, Scott, and Jones (1940, *LSJ* s.v. στόχος), *stókhos* denotes a pillar or post set up as a mark for archers, and by extension an aim, shot, or conjecture (Latin *coniectura*); from it derive the verb *stokházesthai* (to aim at a mark, to conjecture), the noun *stokhastēs* (conjecturer), and the category *stokhastikē tékhnē* (conjectural art, systematized by Alexander of Aphrodisias and Galen), which Jacob Bernoulli (1713/2006) revived as *ars conjectandi sive stochastice*.
- **Τέλος (*télos*):** In Antipater of Tarsus's archer analogy (Stobaeus, *Eclogae* II.76.9–15 = Long and Sedley 1987, 64C), the internal consummate end of doing everything within one's power continuously and undeviatingly to achieve the primary natural things, as distinct from the external outcome of striking the *skopós*.
- **Τόξον (*tóxon*) and τοξότης (*toxótēs*):** The bow (*tóxon*) and the archer (*toxótēs*), an etymological family distinct from *stókhos* that supplies the classical figure of the archer aiming under crosswinds.

### **Part B — Core Technical and Methodological Terms**

- **Cognitive Influence vs. Cognitive Manipulation (*Premise-Rigging*, $\mathrm{PRI}$):** In Herbert Simon's (1947/1997 [1979]) bounded-rationality theory and Jürgen Habermas's (1984 [1981/2012]) theory of communicative action (`EpistemicMarginState::from_inquiry_framing` in `crates/stochology/src/epistemic.rs`), *legitimate cognitive influence* structures attention by sharing partial-identification intervals $[L_t, U_t]$ and spillover losses ($L_{\mathrm{affected}}$) transparently ($\mathrm{PRI} \le 0.20$), whereas *cognitive manipulation* (*concealed strategic action*) rigs the decision labyrinth at the top by concealing epistemic width or collateral harm while preserving the formal illusion of frontline autonomy.
- **CORP and CRPS Decomposition:** The Consistent, Optimally Binned, Reproducible Pool-Adjacent-Violators isotonic decomposition of the Brier score ($\mathrm{BS} = \mathrm{REL}_{\mathrm{CORP}} - \mathrm{RES}_{\mathrm{CORP}} + \mathrm{UNC}$) and Continuous Ranked Probability Score ($\mathrm{CRPS}$) into miscalibration ($\mathrm{REL}$) and discriminative resolution ($\mathrm{RES}$) (Gneiting and Raftery 2007; Dimitriadis, Gneiting, and Jordan 2021).
- **Design Effect ($\mathrm{DEFF}$) and Effective Sample Size ($n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}$):** Variance inflation from intra-cluster correlation, $\mathrm{DEFF} = 1 + [\bar{m}(1 + \mathrm{CV}_m^2) - 1]\rho_{\mathrm{intra}}$ (Kish 1965; Campbell 1977; Moulton 1986), combined with Kish's weight-concentration sample size $n_{\mathrm{Kish}} = (\sum w_i)^2/\sum w_i^2$ into the sampling-variance effective sample size $n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} = \min(n/\mathrm{DEFF}, n_{\mathrm{Kish}})$, and strictly separated from Meng's (2018) selection-bias MSE sample size $n_{\mathrm{Meng}} \approx \frac{f}{1-f}\rho_{R,Y}^{-2}$.
- **Double-Bind Trap ($\mathrm{DB}_t$):** In Gregory Bateson's (1972/2000) pragmatics of communication (`GovernanceState::from_real_time_and_ex_post_gates` in `crates/stochology/src/governance.rs`), the synergistic organizational trap $\mathrm{DB}_t = \beta_{\mathrm{proxy}, t}\cdot\delta_{\mathrm{cred}, t}\cdot(1 - \mathbf{1}\{\text{brake}_t\}) \in [0, 1]$ inflicted in Collapse Proposition 3 when coercive single-metric proxy pressure ($\beta_{\mathrm{proxy}, t} \to 1$), testimonial silencing of emergency alarms ($\delta_{\mathrm{cred}, t} \to 1$), and a disabled constitutional veto ($\mathbf{1}\{\text{brake}_t\} = 0$) simultaneously close every legitimate path of action for frontline operators.
- **Epistemic Margin Index ($S_{\mathrm{epi}} \in [0, 1]$):** The normalized operational diagnostic of Filter I–II integrity, combining empirical interval coverage indicator $\mathbf{1}\{S_t \in [L_t, U_t]\}$, CORP resolution-to-uncertainty ratio, and an anti-inflation sharpness penalty $1 - ((U_t - L_t)/(y_{\max} - y_{\min}))^2$ (Reflexive Brake 1).
- **e-value and e-process:** A single-stage *e*-value is a nonnegative random variable $E \ge 0$ with $\mathbb{E}_{H_0}[E] \le 1$; a sequential *e*-process $(E_t)_{t \ge 1}$ is a nonnegative supermartingale satisfying $\mathbb{E}_{H_0}[E_\tau] \le 1$ at any arbitrary stopping time $\tau$ (Ville 1939; Shafer 2021; Grünwald, de Heide, and Koolen 2024; Ramdas et al. 2023).
- **Imbens–Manski Confidence Interval ($\mathrm{CI}_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{IM}}$):** A two-sided confidence interval $[\hat{\theta}_L - c_\alpha\hat{\sigma}_L/\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}},\, \hat{\theta}_U + c_\alpha\hat{\sigma}_U/\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}}]$ for a partially identified parameter $\theta \in [\theta_L, \theta_U]$, where $c_\alpha \in [z_{1-\alpha}, z_{1-\alpha/2}]$ adapts to the ratio of identification width to sampling error (Imbens and Manski 2004).
- **Institutional Governance Index ($S_{\mathrm{gov}} \in [0, 1]$):** The normalized operational diagnostic of Filter III–IV institutional integrity, taking the non-compensatory minimum of bitemporal pre-registration (`TemporalVault`), executive downside clawback coverage $s_{\mathrm{clawback}, t}$, and frontline algedonic listening weight $1 - \delta_{\mathrm{cred}, t}$ (subject to Reflexive Brake 3).
- **Kelly Fraction and Epistemic Shrinkage ($f_{\mathrm{plug\text{-}in}}^*, f_{\mathrm{epi}}^*$):** The exposure fraction $f_{\mathrm{plug\text{-}in}}^* = (\mu - r)/\sigma^2$ maximizing excess logarithmic growth $g(f) - r$ (Kelly 1956), shrunk by $n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}/(n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} + 1)$ under finite-sample parameter variance (Baker and McHale 2013).
- **Liebig–Leontief Minimum ($M_{\mathrm{Leontief}}$):** The bottleneck aggregation rule $M_{\mathrm{Leontief}} = \min(S_{\mathrm{epi}}, S_{\mathrm{vital}}, S_{\mathrm{gov}}) \in [0, 1]$ (limit as $\sigma \to 0^+$ of a Constant Elasticity of Substitution aggregator) capturing serial non-compensability of the three safeguards at their critical survival threshold $\tau$.
- **Post-Data Severity ($\mathrm{SEV}$):** In Deborah Mayo's (2018) error statistics, the post-data probability $\mathrm{SEV}(\theta > \theta_{\mathrm{crit}}; x_0) = \mathbb{P}(T(X) \le T(x_0) \mid \theta = \theta_{\mathrm{crit}})$ evaluated after observing $x_0$, distinguished from pre-registered design parameters $(\alpha, 1 - \beta)$.
- **Scapegoat Mechanism in Risk-Shifting ($\Delta_{\mathrm{scapegoat}}$):** Combining René Girard's (1986 [1982/2004]) anthropology of sacrificial substitution with Herbert Simon's (1947/1997 [1979]) premise-rigging and Bengt Holmström's (1979) informativeness principle (`GovernanceState::from_real_time_and_ex_post_gates` in `crates/stochology/src/governance.rs`), the excess blame $\Delta_{\mathrm{scapegoat}} = \max(0, b_{\mathrm{frontline}} - L_{\mathrm{oper}})$ shifted onto frontline operators when leadership breaks its bitemporal commitment (`TemporalVault`) to disguise "adulterated air before the shot" ($L_{\mathrm{struct}} \gg L_{\mathrm{oper}}$) as frontline failure.
- **Time-to-Survive ($\mathrm{TTS}_{\mathrm{survival}}$) and Time-to-Recover ($\mathrm{TTR}_{\mathrm{recovery}}$):** The physical breathing-time inequality $\mathrm{TTS}_{\mathrm{survival}, t} = B_t / d_{\mathrm{crisis}, t} \ge \mathrm{TTR}_{\mathrm{recovery}, t} = \kappa_{\mathrm{rec}}/\lambda_t = -\kappa_{\mathrm{rec}}\Delta t/\ln \rho_{1,t}$ (Simchi-Levi, Schmidt, and Wei 2014), linked to the inverse recovery rate $1/\lambda_t$ of Critical Slowing Down (Scheffer et al. 2009).
- **Vital Margin Index ($S_{\mathrm{vital}} \in [0, 1]$):** The normalized operational diagnostic of physical and ecological survival slack, taking the non-compensatory minimum of the stock survival ratio $S_{\mathrm{stock}, t} = \operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}(\mathrm{TTS}_t/\mathrm{TTR}_t - 1)$ and the ecological attraction-basin integrity $S_{\mathrm{basin}, t} = \min(D_t/D_0, \mathrm{CSD\ margin}_t)$ (bounded above by Reflexive Brake 2).
