# **The Ethics of Aim: Conjecture, Construction, and Rupture in the Knowledge of the Real**

*(Foundations of a Stochology)*

---

## **Abstract**

This programmatic essay presents the theoretical and methodological foundations of **Stochology**, conceived as an integrative framework and transversal disciplinary program—simultaneously epistemological, operational, and ethical-institutional—that investigates the conditions of validity, formal limits, and practical responsibility of human judgment under structural uncertainty. It argues that knowing and deliberating when outcomes remain opaque requires analyzing, as a single coupled circuit, the passages across three planes of experience: the material and historical world in which phenomena unfold, the archive and formal model that represent them, and the institutional deliberation that intervenes in collective life. Rather than claiming a closed deductive system, the essay organizes its method as an immanent critique and heuristic topology across three mediating filters and seven constitutive tensions, showing how each achievement of analytic reason—theoretical framing, mathematical formalization, documentary accumulation, and target-based governance—generates a specific pathology when treated as self-sufficient. From this traversal emerge three non-substitutable safeguards—the **epistemic margin**, the **vital margin**, and **fully apt agency under institutional governance**—whose joint viability obeys a strict Liebig–Leontief minimum, $M_{\mathrm{Leontief}} = \min(S_{\mathrm{epi}}, S_{\mathrm{vital}}, S_{\mathrm{gov}})$, formalized through three structural collapse propositions and operationalized in a sequential agent–world–institution loop. Finally, the essay traces the practical resonances of this grammar across five methodological families—survey and administrative archives, experimental and policy evaluation, operations and ecological resilience, algorithmic governance, and qualitative, historical, clinical, and ethnographic inquiry—while subjecting Stochology itself to Goodhart's Law and to explicit empirical falsifiers and normative commitments.

**Keywords:** Stochology; ethics of aim; immanent critique; partial identification; non-ergodicity; performativity; institutional responsibility; Leontief complementarity.

## **I. Introduction: The Genealogy of the Shot and the Program of Stochology**

Technical, linguistic, and institutional mediators carve up reality long before it reaches human deliberation. Economic indicators, forensic reports, medical records, demographic censuses, historical chronicles, and digital databases never offer events in the raw. As the history and sociology of quantification have shown (Porter 1995; Desrosières 1998; Bowker and Star 1999), such instruments deliver a world already filtered and arranged according to institutional categories designed to make certain ways of seeing, comparing, calculating, and governing possible. Knowing and deciding are therefore never equivalent to the serene contemplation of a motionless landscape; they constitute a permanent friction between the administrative effort to order experience into concepts and the material resistance of historical and natural life. When an agent is forced to act before the course of events is fully revealed—as happens to the clinician facing an acute syndrome, the magistrate weighing incomplete evidence, the planner managing critical infrastructure, or the historian confronting a mutilated archive—the decisive question ceases to be the raw volume of accumulated records. The central problem becomes the cognitive and moral integrity of judgment while the outcome of action remains open.

The history of Western thought preserved the trace of this problem in the etymology of the word *stochastic*. When Jacob Bernoulli (1713/2006), in Part IV, Chapter 2 of his posthumous *Ars Conjectandi*, sought a name for the art of measuring the probability of uncertain things, he defined it as *ars conjectandi sive stochastice*—the art of conjecturing, or stochastics. In doing so, Bernoulli drew upon a Greek semantic family bound to the practical experience of aiming at a mark through opacity. In classical Greek, the noun **στόχος (*stókhos*)** designated the narrow upright stake or pillar set up as a mark for shooting and, by extension, conjecture or the act of aiming—a cognitive movement that Latin rendered as *coniectura*. From *stókhos* derive the verb **στοχάζεσθαι (*stokházesthai*)**—to aim at a mark, to endeavor to hit or divine—and the agent noun **στοχαστής (*stokhastēs*)**, the conjecturer or diviner of signs. Philological rigor requires distinguishing this root both from the noun for the archer (**τοξότης, *toxótēs***), which derives from an entirely separate root meaning the bow (**τόξον, *tóxon***), and from the noun **σκοπός (*skopós*)**, the watched target or lookout (connected to *skopeîn*, "to behold" or "watch," borrowed into Latin as *scopus* alongside the native Latin *signum*). Whereas *skopós* names the external object under watch, *stókhos* names the oriented projection of judgment across an uncertain medium—which is why this program takes the name *Stochology* while later engaging the Stoic ethical distinction between *télos* and *skopós* (Section 6.1).

Centuries before the mathematical calculus of probabilities, Plato granted *stokházesthai* a central place in the classification of human knowledge. In the *Philebus* (55e–56c), examining the structure of the arts and crafts (*tékhnai*), Plato divides them not by the ontological turbulence of their matter, but by an epistemic criterion: the degree of exactness (*akríbeia*) afforded by measurement, number, and weighing (*métron*, *arithmós*, *stathmós*). On one side stand the arts supported by the rule, the lathe, the compass, the plumb line, and the scale, whose paradigm is carpentry and building construction. On the other side stand the arts that cannot rely on a mechanical gauge and must operate through practiced conjecture and aim (*stokhasmós*). The first example invoked by Plato (56a) is not a game of chance, but music, which attunes consonance "not by measure, but by the conjectural aim of practiced perception" (*ou métrōi allà melétēs stokhasmōi*); it is followed by clinical medicine, agriculture, maritime navigation, and military command.

The recognition that these crafts possess a rational discipline of their own—distinct from geometric demonstration, yet reducible neither to blind luck nor to arbitrary routine—matured across Antiquity. In the fifth century BCE, treatises of the *Corpus Hippocraticum* approached this terrain from two complementary angles: *On Ancient Medicine* (*De prisca medicina*, ch. 9) maintained that the physician, confronting the individual constitution of each sick body, holds no exact arithmetic measure of diet beyond the "sensation of the body" (*toû sṓmatos tḕn aísthēsin*), while the semiotic treatises *Prognostic* (*Prognosticon*) and *On the Art* (*De arte*) codified how the clinician reads and weighs observable clues, symptoms, and diagnostic signs (**σημεῖα, *sēmeîa***; **τεκμήρια, *tekmēria***). In the Hellenistic and Roman periods, controversies among medical and philosophical schools—systematized by Alexander of Aphrodisias and by Galen of Pergamon (*De sectis*; *Ars medica*)—consecrated the technical category of **στοχαστικὴ τέχνη (*stokhastikē tékhnē*)**: a "conjectural art" or "art of aim," in which method is fully rational and accountable to criticism even though material success remains exposed to external contingencies beyond the practitioner's mastery. Alongside this scholastic medical tradition, modern classical scholarship led by Marcel Detienne and Jean-Pierre Vernant (1974) reconstructed the broader archaic and classical figure of **μῆτις (*mētis*)**—the plastic, vigilant intelligence of the pilot and clinician who act at the fleeting critical instant (**καιρός, *kairós***).

The scope of this genealogy must be stated without equivocation. As historians of science and philosophy have emphasized (Sambursky 1956; Hacking 1975), Plato, the Hippocratic authors, and Galen were theorizing qualitative diagnostic prudence rather than a quantitative calculus of relative frequencies, which emerged only in the seventeenth century. Evoking *stokhastikē tékhnē*, *sēmeîa*, and *mētis* here is neither an anachronistic projection of modern statistics onto Antiquity nor an appeal to classical authority. It is a heuristic and revisable genealogy whose purpose is to recall that the dilemma of judging methodically without possessing certainty precedes modern mathematization and was not dissolved by it. When modernity narrowed the art of aim (*stókhos*) into the formal calculus of stochastic processes, it gained extraordinary analytical power, yet frequently bracketed the epistemological and ethical question that originally animated the conjectural arts: what does it mean to aim justly when the map is incomplete and another person will bear the impact of the arrow?

### **1.1. Definition of Stochology, the Three-Plane Circuit, and Theoretical Demarcation**

To address this problem in the age of formal models and bureaucratic institutions, **Stochology** (from *stókhos*, aim or conjecture, and *lógos*, reasoned account or critical study) is formulated here as an integrative framework and transversal disciplinary program that investigates the conditions of validity, formal limits, and institutional responsibility of judgment under structural uncertainty.

What justifies organizing this inquiry as a distinct program rather than leaving its parts dispersed across statistics, decision theory, and moral philosophy? A research program is defined not by a monopoly over a technical tool, but by the delimitation of a unit of analysis that specialized fields habitually sever into non-communicating fragments. Whereas statistical methodology examines the mapping from phenomena to data, decision theory analyzes the mapping from probability distributions to rational choice, and sociology or law studies the impact of rules on society, Stochology takes as its indivisible unit of analysis the complete circuit of mediation across three planes:

1. *The plane of the material and historical world:* the terrain where events unfold in irreversible time, marked by extreme shocks, interdependencies among individuals, unequal social visibility, and human agents who react strategically when observed, classified, or governed;
2. *The plane of representation, model, and archive:* the documentary and symbolic space—whether a judicial file, a historical archive, a sample survey, or an algorithmic model—in which the world is compressed into classifications, tables, and equations subject to structural discrepancy and selective omissions;
3. *The plane of deliberation and institutions:* the practical sphere in which individuals, committees, or organizations act upon those imperfect representations, allocating scarce reserves, imposing norms, and distributing across social groups the benefits of success and the burdens of error.

The central thesis of Stochology is that grave failures of judgment under uncertainty rarely originate inside a single isolated plane; they originate in the unguarded passages from one plane to another. Three direct transits drive institutional action. First, in the passage from the world to the archive, vulnerable populations and structural dependencies are lost through selective non-recording and cluster echoing. Second, in the passage from the archive to the decision, simplifying point summaries (such as the lone arithmetic mean) conceal convex tail hazards and overwhelm human deliberative capacity. Third, in the passage from the decision back to the world, the intervention itself alters strategic behavior and consumes finite physical and temporal buffers. Watching over these three direct transits requires three corresponding reflexive movements: requiring the model to declare its causal and structural premises before fitting numbers; requiring the institution to seal its hypotheses, failed attempts, and error-liability rules before consulting the archive; and requiring the temporal scale of evidence and auditing to respect the physical survival clock of the system under governance.

Situating Stochology with precision requires demarcating how it connects and restructures three major twentieth- and twenty-first-century traditions on uncertainty:

- *Ambiguity, partial identification, and the separation of description from prescription:* In 1921, Frank Knight (1921) and John Maynard Keynes (1921) independently warned against reducing all epistemic doubt to a single numerical probability, distinguishing measurable *risk* from unquantifiable *uncertainty* and separating the balance of probability from the *weight of argument* ($V$). Four decades later, Daniel Ellsberg's (1961) two-urn thought experiments and informal elicitations showed that reasonable decision-makers distinguish known proportions (risk) from unknown proportions (ambiguity) in ways that systematically violate Leonard J. Savage's (1954) *Sure-Thing Principle*—a system of subjective expected utility (SEU) that Savage himself restricted to logically closed "small worlds" (a logical completeness assumption distinct from physical *ergodicity*, examined in Section 5.2). To operate when a unique prior is untenable, Peter Walley (1991) developed imprecise probabilities, Peter J. Huber (1964, 1981) founded robust statistics, and Charles Manski (2003, 2013) established *partial identification* in econometrics. Crucially, Stochology enforces a strict boundary that applied work often blurs: partial identification is a *descriptive* epistemic discipline (determining the sharp identification region $\Theta_I$ compatible with the observed data and maintained assumptions), whereas choosing an action given $\Theta_I$ requires an explicit *prescriptive* decision rule;
- *Taxonomy of decision rules and lexicographic survival constraints:* In decision theory and engineering, distinct prescriptive rules under ambiguity are frequently conflated under the generic label of "robustness." When Yakov Ben-Haim (2006) proposed *Info-Gap* theory—maximizing local immunity to error around a nominal reference model—Moshe Sniedovich (2007, 2012) proved that if the reference model is misspecified, local robustness creates a "No-Man's Land" of policies that collapse under global deviations. Yet replacing local robustness requires distinguishing four non-equivalent global criteria: (i) Savage's (1954) *Subjective Expected Utility*, which averages over $\Theta_I$ with a single prior; (ii) Itzhak Gilboa and David Schmeidler's (1989) *Maxmin Expected Utility* (MEU), which maximizes minimum expected utility $\min_{P \in \mathcal{P}} \mathbb{E}_P[u]$ over a credal set of priors $\mathcal{P}$; (iii) Abraham Wald's (1945) *Minimax Risk* (or *Maximin Utility*, $\max_a \min_{\theta \in \Theta_I} u(a, \theta)$), which optimizes against the worst state in $\Theta_I$; and (iv) Savage's (1951) and Manski's (2003, 2013) *Minimax Regret*, which minimizes maximum opportunity loss $\max_{\theta \in \Theta_I}[\max_{a'} u(a', \theta) - u(a, \theta)]$ relative to the ex-post optimal act. Neither Wald's maximin nor Manski's minimax regret guarantees a survival floor by itself: if every action leads to ruin in the worst state, Wald's criterion merely selects the least bad ruin, while in a binary treatment choice where the identified interval $[L, U]$ straddles the cost threshold $C$ ($L < C < U$), maximin dictates universal abstention whereas minimax regret recommends positive or fractional treatment whenever $U - C$ is substantial. Stochology resolves this by subordinating optimization to a *lexicographic safety-first constraint* (Roy 1952; Simchi-Levi, Schmidt, and Wei 2014): actions that violate the physical survival condition ($\text{Time-to-Survive} \ge \text{Time-to-Recover}$, or ruin probability above a hard floor) are filtered out first; within the survival-admissible set, residual ambiguity is adjudicated by minimax regret for reversible allocations and by worst-case bounds when irreversible thresholds are at stake. In the temporal domain, this constraint is coupled with the multiplicative growth theory of John L. Kelly Jr. (1956) and Leo Breiman (1961), tempered by the finite-horizon caveats of Paul Samuelson (1971, 1979) and Robert C. Merton (1969, 1971) and the non-ergodic dynamics foregrounded by Ole Peters (2019);
- *The integrative contribution of Stochology:* Each of these traditions solved a decisive local problem: Manski addressed archival missingness; Wald, Savage, and Gilboa–Schmeidler formalized choice under ambiguity; Roy, Kelly, and Simchi-Levi addressed survival over time; Callon, MacKenzie, and Goodhart analyzed performative reactivity; Sosa, Scanlon, and Hansson theorized epistemic virtue and the ethics of risk. Applied in isolation, however, each safeguard remains vulnerable to failure on the adjacent planes. Computing an unimpeachable partial-identification interval on the plane of the archive does not prevent ruin if operations run at $96\text{--}100\%$ capacity on the temporal plane, or if limited-liability contracts on the institutional plane reward executives for liquidating safety buffers. The specific object of Stochology is the structural interdependence of these three planes.

### **1.2. The Nuclear Triad, Leontief Complementarity, and the Three Collapse Propositions**

To keep the mediation circuit intact across world, archive, and institution, Stochology maintains that responsible judgment under structural uncertainty requires the simultaneous presence of three safeguards:

1. **The epistemic margin (on the plane of knowledge and the archive):** the methodological and ethical duty to refuse false point exactness. Whenever archives suffer from selective non-recording, clustering, or structural model discrepancy, the analyst must declare the sharp partial-identification interval $[L_t, U_t]$ compatible with the evidence (Manski 2003; Imbens and Manski 2004; Lee 2009), the structural discrepancy between simulator and reality (Kennedy and O'Hagan 2001), the discounted effective sample size $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (Moulton 1986; Meng 2018), and the antidogmatic reserve required to learn from refutation (Lindley 1985);
2. **The vital margin (on the material and temporal plane):** the operational duty to preserve physical, biological, financial, and temporal slack against absorbing barriers and irreversible wear. It requires maintaining capacity headroom ($1 - \rho_t$) against heavy-traffic queue explosions (Kingman 1961; Scarf 1960), shrinking multiplicative exposure under parameter uncertainty so that volatility drain does not trigger bankruptcy (Itô 1951; Kelly 1956; Baker and McHale 2013), ensuring that local Time-to-Survive exceeds Time-to-Recover ($\mathrm{TTS}_t \ge \mathrm{TTR}_t$, Simchi-Levi, Schmidt, and Wei 2014), and moderating interventions to preserve ecological and social resilience (Canguilhem 1943/2009; Holling 1973; Perdomo et al. 2020);
3. **Fully apt agency under institutional governance (on the ethical and political plane):** the constitutional duty to bind the act of judging to symmetry of risk and accountability. It requires translating the reflective wisdom to withhold an unsafe shot (Sosa 2015) into collective constitutional vetoes and independent audit channels (Beer 1972; List and Pettit 2011), eliminating testimonial credibility discounts against frontline alarms (Fricker 2007), protecting deliberative attention from alert flooding (Gigerenzer and Hoffrage 1995; Zheng and Meister 2025), and structuring contracts with downside clawbacks at the decision-making top, limited-liability protection at the vulnerable base, and public disclosure of error trade-offs (Holmström 1979; Holmström and Milgrom 1991; Innes 1990; Chouldechova 2017).

These three safeguards do not aggregate as substitutable inputs, nor as an arbitrary product. Like the voussoirs of a single arch—or, in formal production and ecological theory, like Justus von Liebig's Law of the Minimum and Wassily Leontief's fixed-proportions technology—systemic integrity is governed by the **Leontief minimum**:

$$M_{\mathrm{Leontief}}\bigl(S_{\mathrm{epi}},\, S_{\mathrm{vital}},\, S_{\mathrm{gov}}\bigr) = \min\bigl(S_{\mathrm{epi}},\, S_{\mathrm{vital}},\, S_{\mathrm{gov}}\bigr) \in [0, 1]$$

In the sequential agent–world–institution loop (`CommunityReserveEnv`, Chapters 1 and 9), each safeguard score $S_i \in [0, 1]$ is defined operationally from trajectory observables across horizon $T$ with support $[y_{\min}, y_{\max}]$ and survival threshold $\tau \in (0, 1)$:
- **Epistemic Margin Index ($S_{\mathrm{epi}}$):** empirical coverage of the true latent demand $S_t$ by the declared interval $[L_t, U_t]$, penalized quadratically by uninformative interval width so that lazy $[y_{\min}, y_{\max}]$ inflation yields zero credit:
  $$S_{\mathrm{epi}} = \left(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} \mathbf{1}\bigl\{S_t \in [L_t, U_t]\bigr\}\right) \cdot \left[1 - \left(\frac{\overline{U - L}}{y_{\max} - y_{\min}}\right)^2\right]$$
- **Vital Margin Index ($S_{\mathrm{vital}}$):** mean normalized capacity headroom relative to the heavy-traffic knee ($\rho_{\mathrm{crit}} = 0.88$), weighted by epistemic Kelly leverage safety and reserve buffer commitment $B_t$:
  $$S_{\mathrm{vital}} = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} \operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}\!\left(\frac{1 - \rho_{\mathrm{base}, t}}{1 - \rho_{\mathrm{crit}}}\right) \cdot \operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}(1.60 - f_t) \cdot \operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}\!\left(\frac{B_t}{B_{\min}}\right)$$
- **Institutional Governance Index ($S_{\mathrm{gov}}$):** conjunction of active constitutional circuit-breaker authority ($S3^*$), undiscounted reception of frontline algedonic signals ($1 - \delta_{\mathrm{cred}, t}$), and restraint of single-metric proxy pressure ($1 - \beta_{\mathrm{proxy}, t}$):
  $$S_{\mathrm{gov}} = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} \mathbf{1}\bigl\{\text{brake}_t\bigr\} \cdot \bigl(1 - \delta_{\mathrm{cred}, t}\bigr) \cdot \bigl(1 - \beta_{\mathrm{proxy}, t}\bigr)$$

Because each index $S_i$ measures net functional adequacy—peaking when intervals are sharp yet calibrated, when buffers respect opportunity costs, and when audits act within $\mathrm{TTS}$—pushing any raw variable to an extreme degrades its own index (Section 7.2), while suppressing any single axis ($S_i < \tau \implies M_{\mathrm{Leontief}} < \tau$) triggers one of three structural collapse modes:

- **Collapse Proposition 1 ($\neg\text{Epistemic Margin}: S_{\mathrm{epi}} < \tau \le \min(S_{\mathrm{vital}}, S_{\mathrm{gov}}) \implies \text{Blind Undersizing}$):** Suppose an institution enforces constitutional governance ($S_{\mathrm{gov}} \ge \tau$) and commits to maintaining physical capacity headroom and reserve rules ($S_{\mathrm{vital}} \ge \tau$), but suppresses the epistemic margin ($S_{\mathrm{epi}} < \tau$) by sizing capacity around a naive point mean $\bar{O}_t$ from a large archive with non-random exclusion of vulnerable clusters ($\rho_{R,Y} \ne 0$) and unadjusted intra-cluster correlation ($\mathrm{DEFF} > 1$). Because nominal standard errors shrink as $O(n^{-1/2})$ while selection bias $\bar{Y}_n - \bar{Y}_N = \rho_{R,Y}\sqrt{(1-f)/f}\,\sigma_Y$ remains invariant to sample size, the declared interval $[L_t, U_t]$ misses true latent demand during tail surges ($\text{coverage} < 25\%$). Consequently, the vital buffer is anchored to a downward-biased baseline, causing realized utilization $\rho_t = S_t / C_t$ and convex social harm $H_t$ to spike despite intact governance and benign intentions;
- **Collapse Proposition 2 ($\neg\text{Vital Margin}: S_{\mathrm{vital}} < \tau \le \min(S_{\mathrm{epi}}, S_{\mathrm{gov}}) \implies \text{Impotent Lucidity Before Ruin}$):** Suppose the technical staff computes honest Manski–Lee partial-identification intervals ($S_{\mathrm{epi}} \ge \tau$, achieving $>85\%$ coverage of true demand) and the organization respects constitutional auditing ($S_{\mathrm{gov}} \ge \tau$), but in the name of "lean efficiency" eliminates the vital margin ($S_{\mathrm{vital}} < \tau$), operating at $\rho_{\mathrm{base}} \approx 0.96$ utilization with minimal buffer allocation ($B_t = 0.02 R_t$) and over-leveraged exposure ($f_t > f_{\mathrm{Kelly}}^*$). When a Pareto tail surge ($\alpha < 2$) strikes—an event explicitly contained within the analysts' own announced interval $[L_t, U_t]$—Kingman waiting times explode as $\rho_t / (1 - \rho_t)$, overload drain exhausts the reserve ($R_{t+1} \le 0$), and the trajectory is absorbed at the ruin barrier ($\tau_{\mathrm{ruin}} \le T$) before any course correction can occur. Without a vital margin, epistemic honesty and procedural rectitude are reduced to diagnostic lucidity over one's own collapse;
- **Collapse Proposition 3 ($\neg\text{Institutional Governance}: S_{\mathrm{gov}} < \tau \le \min(S_{\mathrm{epi}}, S_{\mathrm{vital}}) \implies \text{Cynical Capture and Pillaging of the Reserve}$):** Suppose an organization possesses both analysts who compute valid identification bounds ($S_{\mathrm{epi}} \ge \tau$) and engineers who provision nominal buffer stocks ($S_{\mathrm{vital}} \ge \tau$), but lacks symmetric institutional governance ($S_{\mathrm{gov}} < \tau$): executives operate under limited-liability payoffs without downside clawbacks (Innes 1990), impose high-powered single-metric proxy pressure ($\beta_{\mathrm{proxy}} = 0.85$, Holmström and Milgrom 1991), discount frontline algedonic distress signals ($\delta_{\mathrm{cred}} = 0.60$, Fricker 2007), and disable the conjunctive $S3^*$ circuit breaker. Through both contractual moral hazard and the organizational sociology of audit and ranking regimes (Strathern 1997; Espeland and Sauder 2007), the first two safeguards are rapidly subverted: epistemic uncertainty is weaponized as an agnotological alibi to delay protective action (Proctor and Schiebinger 2008), while proxy gaming erodes baseline capacity and unbraked overload liquidates the common reserve until the system breaches the ruin barrier with more than triple the cumulative social harm of the intact triad.

## **II. The Method: Immanent Critique, Convergent Lineages, and the Heuristic Topology**

### **2.1. Immanent Critique and the Limits of Negative Dialectics**

Why do scientific and administrative institutions fall repeatedly into these three collapse modes? Stochology advances the hypothesis that systematic failures of judgment under uncertainty arise not merely from technical incompetence or individual malice, but from the internal dynamic of rationalization whenever a methodological instrument forgets the conditions that bound its validity. To analyze this dynamic without claiming a closed metaphysical system, our method draws on **immanent critique** and engages the warning formulated by Theodor W. Adorno (1966/2009) in *Negative Dialectics*.

In the history of inquiry and governance, reason repeatedly constructs a disciplining instrument to cure a real defect of unreflective practice; yet when that instrument is absolutized as self-sufficient, it generates a higher-order blindness:
- To overcome arbitrary guesswork, inquiry demands *structured theory*; yet theory, insulated by auxiliary assumptions and institutional interests, begins to select confirming evidence and patch over anomalies;
- To overcome verbal vagueness, thought turns to *mathematical formalization*; yet formal models, seduced by analytical tractability, amputate qualitative transition zones, convex asymmetries, and heavy tails in favor of a docile average;
- To overcome the fragility of small samples, states and corporations build *monumental archives and digital registries*; yet archival volume fosters an illusion of omniscience while driving nominal error bands toward zero around populations whose most vulnerable members were systematically omitted;
- To become practically effective, knowledge translates itself into *rules of intervention and performance targets*; yet imposing a static indicator upon a reactive social and material world induces strategic gaming, erodes causal mechanisms, and consumes finite buffers in irreversible time.

Clarifying the philosophical status of this movement is essential to avoid a performative contradiction. Whereas Hegelian dialectics treats *determinate negation* (*Aufhebung*) as a synthetic engine that culminates in a positive, closed totality, Adorno's (1966/2009) *negative dialectics* insists that conceptual identification is constitutive of thought and that what he terms the **Non-Identical**—the singular, historical, and material excess of the object—can never be captured inside a concept, parameter, or closed table of categories. If Stochology claimed that declaring a partial-identification interval $[L, U]$ "captures" the Non-Identical, or presented its three filters and seven tensions as a definitive transcendental deduction of human reason, it would commit the very reification Adorno diagnosed.

Accordingly, Stochology distinguishes strictly between the *ontological register* of the Non-Identical (the inexhaustible singularity of historical and biological reality, accessible only through ongoing immanent critique and qualitative listening) and the *epistemic-operational register* of partial identification and vital slack (the formal refusal to pretend that a point number exhausts what the archive omits). Why, then, does this essay organize its exposition around three filters and seven tensions? Not because reality is ontologically triadic or septenary, but because the sequential loop of institutional action passes chronologically through seven practical junctures—framing a question (Tension 1), testing a hypothesis over time (Tension 2), formalizing a summary measure (Tension 3), aggregating an archive (Tension 4), intervening in a reactive social field (Tension 5), compounding exposure across irreversible time (Tension 6), and assigning moral and contractual liability for the outcome (Tension 7). The topology summarized in Table 1 is therefore an open, revisable **institutional heuristic**—a series of methodological self-limiting locks designed to prevent models and bureaucracies from mistaking their abstractions for the whole.

### **2.2. The Three Convergent Lineages**

Across these seven junctures, Stochology braids together three distinct intellectual lineages:

1. *The epistemological and statistical lineage (partial identification, severe testing, and model discrepancy):* articulating Adorno's (1966/2009) critique of identificatory closure with Deborah Mayo's (2018) severe-testing error statistics, Charles Manski's (2003, 2013) partial identification, Marc Kennedy and Anthony O'Hagan's (2001) Bayesian model discrepancy, and Xiao-Li Meng's (2018) analysis of archival selection defects, it establishes that the gap between representation and reality must remain visible as an *epistemic margin*;
2. *The material, thermodynamic, and ecological-sociotechnical lineage (irreversible time, resilience, and performativity):* uniting Nicholas Georgescu-Roegen's (1971) critique of mechanistic reversibility, Georges Canguilhem's (1943/2009) biological normativity, Crawford S. Holling's (1973) ecological resilience, John F. C. Kingman's (1961) heavy-traffic queueing theory, and the sociology of quantification and performativity (Callon 1998; Desrosières 1998; Bowker and Star 1999; MacKenzie 2006), it establishes that every intervention acts upon a reactive, entropic medium, demanding a *vital margin*;
3. *The ethical, contractual, and political-institutional lineage (apt agency, risk imposition, and organizational constitution):* uniting Antipater of Tarsus's Stoic archer (Stobaeus, *Eclogae* II.76; Cicero, *De Finibus* III.22), Bernard Williams's (1981) and Thomas Nagel's (1979) analyses of moral luck, Ernest Sosa's (2007, 2015) virtue epistemology, Thomas M. Scanlon's (1998) contractualism, Sven Ove Hansson's (2013) ethics of risk, Miranda Fricker's (2007) epistemic injustice, Stafford Beer's (1972) organizational cybernetics, Christian List and Philip Pettit's (2011) group agency, and the economics of moral hazard and classification fairness (Holmström 1979; Holmström and Milgrom 1991; Innes 1990; Chouldechova 2017), it establishes that methodological honesty requires *fully apt agency under symmetric institutional governance*.

## **III. The First Filter: When Interest Conceals and Theory Insulates Itself**

### **3.1. Tension 1 — Situational Interest versus Intellectual Rectitude**

The first mediation occurs before any data are gathered or any equation is written. It resides in the social, economic, and institutional position occupied by the questioner: interest selects what deserves to be seen and conceals what is convenient to ignore. No researcher, magistrate, physician, public administrator, or corporate board deliberates outside society; all operate embedded in structures of funding, career incentives, bureaucratic prestige, and state or corporate power. When project approval, budget renewal, a firm's market valuation, or an administration's political survival depends on a given conclusion, a gravitational field inclines attention. Clues favoring the desired result are received with benevolence, while contrary signs are subjected to asymmetric skepticism or left outside the frame of inquiry.

The most pervasive form of this distortion rarely requires explicit data fabrication. Altering a primary document is a crude offense; far safer and more routine is shaping the prior architecture of inquiry. An institution adopts a narrow definition of the problem that excludes collateral externalities; slices a time series at a convenient base year; designs a safety study with a sample size too small to detect real harm; or runs twenty exploratory models behind closed doors and publishes only the single specification that crossed a nominal threshold.

This pathology operates simultaneously on two planes analyzed by science studies and epistemology: the structural plane of **agnotology** (Proctor and Schiebinger 2008) and the dispositional plane of **epistemic vices** (Cassam 2019). As Robert Proctor demonstrated in coining *agnotology*, ignorance is frequently not a passive vacuum awaiting scientific light, but an actively manufactured political and industrial product—exemplified by tobacco, asbestos, and fossil-fuel campaigns that funded deliberately underpowered or narrowly framed studies to sustain the regulatory alibi that "the evidence remains inconclusive." Within organizations, Quassim Cassam (2019) pairs this organized **epistemic malevolence** with **epistemic insouciance**: a routinized bureaucratic indifference to truth that generalizes Harry Frankfurt's (2005) diagnosis in *On Bullshit*. Whereas the liar must still track the truth in order to conceal it, the producer of *bullshit* severs commitment to factual reality altogether, caring only that the technical report satisfies formal ritual and projects institutional authority.

To prevent inquiry from being corrupted at its origin by structural agnotology, selective reporting, or framing asymmetry, Stochology establishes three methodological locks:

1. *Antidogmatic reserve (Cromwell's Rule):* In 1650, Oliver Cromwell addressed to the General Assembly of the Kirk of Scotland the plea: *"I beseech you, in the bowels of Christ, think it possible you may be mistaken."* Dennis Lindley (1985) formalized this warning as **Cromwell's Rule**: in probabilistic updating, assigning a prior probability of strictly $p = 0$ or $p = 1$ to any contingent empirical proposition destroys the capacity to learn from experience, since Bayes's rule leaves a zero or unit prior invariant regardless of how much contrary evidence accumulates. Intellectual rectitude requires clamping prior beliefs to the interior interval $[\epsilon, 1 - \epsilon]$ with $\epsilon > 0$;
2. *Bitemporal pre-registration and the separation of invariant principle from contingent parameter:* Because institutional memory readily reinterprets the past to justify the present, the only reliable defense against post-hoc target painting (*Texas sharpshooter* bias) is bitemporal separation: sealing the hypothesis, the loss function, and the evaluation protocol at time $t_1$ before observing the outcome realization at time $t_2$. This temporal discipline rests on a distinction between *invariant methodological principles*—such as sealing rules before observing outcomes and disclosing all tested specifications—and *contingent domain parameters* (such as choosing a significance level $\alpha = 0.05$ or $\alpha = 0.01$), which remain adjustable to context so long as they are fixed ex ante;
3. *Multiverse transparency, e-BH discovery control, and strong objectivity against Type III framing error:* When an analyst searches across $K$ plausible data-cleaning and modeling choices, reporting only the most favorable path inflates false positives dramatically. Sara Steegen et al. (2016) counter this via the **specification curve (*multiverse analysis*)**, evaluating stability across the entire combinatorics of defensible specifications, while Ruodu Wang and Aaditya Ramdas (2022) provide the **e-BH procedure**, which controls the false discovery rate under arbitrary dependence among tests. Yet even a reproducible calculation fails if it commits what Allyn Kimball (1957), Howard Raiffa (1968), and Ian Mitroff and Tom Featheringham (1974) termed **Type III Error**: giving a mathematically precise answer to the wrong framing of the problem. In cost–loss decision framing with protection cost $C$ and avoidable loss $L$, the optimal probability threshold for preventive action is $p^* = C / (C + L)$. When an institution evaluates $p_{\mathrm{inst}}^* = C / (C + L_{\mathrm{inst}})$ using only its own reputational or budgetary loss $L_{\mathrm{inst}}$ while omitting the catastrophic loss $L_{\mathrm{affected}} \gg L_{\mathrm{inst}}$ borne by exposed citizens, it manufactures a structural framing gap. Overcoming Type III Error therefore requires integrating the **strong objectivity** of Helen Longino (1990) and Sandra Harding (1991) directly into the method: abandoning the bureaucratic "view from nowhere" by auditing problem definitions, sample power, and loss parameters from the standpoint of the populations who bear the downside of error.

### **3.2. Tension 2 — Accommodating Confirmation versus Fruitful Refutation and the Mayo–Manski Bridge**

Even when immediate situational interests are constrained, a second tension arises from the logic of empirical testing itself: when observations contradict an established theory or policy model, the path of least resistance is to introduce *ad hoc* adjustments that rescue the model from refutation.

Since David Hume showed that no finite sequence of past observations deductively entails future regularities, Karl Popper (1934/2002) located scientific demarcation in *falsifiability*—the willingness to state in advance which observable outcomes would refute a conjecture. Yet Pierre Duhem (1906/1954) and Willard Van Orman Quine (1951) exposed the structural vulnerability of naive falsificationism (**the Duhem–Quine thesis**): because a core hypothesis $H$ generates testable predictions only when conjoined with a bundle of auxiliary assumptions $A_1 \wedge A_2 \wedge \dots \wedge A_m$ (instrument calibration, survey comprehension, absence of external confounders), an empirical failure $\neg O$ entails only $\neg(H \wedge A_1 \wedge \dots \wedge A_m)$, leaving the analyst free to deflect blame onto an auxiliary clause. Imre Lakatos (1970) resolved this ambiguity dynamically by evaluating *research programs* over time: a modification is **progressive** when the revised auxiliary assumptions predict novel, independently testable facts (as Le Verrier's postulation of Neptune did in 1846), and **degenerating** when exceptions are tailored solely to absorb counterexamples post hoc, shrinking the empirical content of the theory.

In contemporary data-intensive science, the temptation toward accommodating adjustment is amplified by optional stopping and post-hoc threshold selection. Deborah Mayo (2018) formulated the antidote in the **severity principle (*severe testing*)**: data $x_0$ that agree with a claim $H$ count as genuine evidence for $H$ if and only if the test procedure had a high probability of detecting the falsity of $H$ had $H$ been false. Examining drinking water with the naked eye in a clear glass yields an outcome ("clear water") that agrees with the hypothesis of purity, yet has zero severity against a colorless toxin. Likewise, peeking at a sequential trial day after day and stopping as soon as a conventional $p$-value dips below $0.05$ destroys severity. To preserve severity under continuous monitoring, three complementary sequential paradigms have recently matured: Glenn Shafer's (2021) *testing by betting* (where evidence is measured by the wealth of a nonnegative martingale bet against the null via Ville's inequality), Peter Grünwald, Rianne de Heide, and Wouter Koolen's (2024) *safe testing* via growth-rate optimal (*GROW*) **e-values** ($E_t \ge 0$ with $\mathbb{E}_{H_0}[E_t] \le 1$ at any stopping time), and Aaditya Ramdas et al.'s (2023) unified game-theoretic confidence sequences.

At this point arises a central methodological question that Stochology seeks to resolve: how can Deborah Mayo's (2018) demand for severe, refutable testing be reconciled with Charles Manski's (2003, 2013) partial identification, which demonstrates that observational and attrition-ridden data rarely identify a single point parameter, yielding instead an interval $\Theta_I = [\theta_L, \theta_U]$?

Apparent tension between the two dissolves once we formalize their division of labor and explicitly separate **descriptive identification** from **prescriptive choice**:

1. *Manski's descriptive moment (delimiting the identification region $\Theta_I$):* In evaluating a clinical treatment or social policy (as in Robert LaLonde's, 1986, classic comparison of experimental National Supported Work data against observational CPS controls), counterfactual outcomes are unobserved and a fraction $p_{\mathrm{miss}}$ of outcomes is missing or selectively censored. Manski (2003) shows that producing a single point estimate $\hat{\theta}$ requires imposing untestable assumptions (such as missing-at-random). Partial identification refuses unverifiable extrapolation and computes the sharp **identification region** $\Theta_I = [\theta_L, \theta_U]$ compatible with the empirical distribution and bounded support $[y_{\min}, y_{\max}]$ (or monotone trimming under selective attrition, Lee 2009);
2. *Mayo's error-statistical moment (severe testing on the boundary of $\Theta_I$):* Let $\theta_{\mathrm{crit}}$ denote the minimum substantively worthwhile effect (or safety threshold) required to justify an intervention. Rather than applying a test to a point-identified mirage, the **Mayo–Manski bridge** tests the composite null hypothesis
   $$H_0: \inf_{\theta \in \Theta_I} \theta \le \theta_{\mathrm{crit}} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad H_0: \theta_L \le \theta_{\mathrm{crit}}$$
   using the Imbens and Manski (2004) lower confidence bound $\hat{L}_{1-\alpha} = \hat{\theta}_L - c_{1-\alpha}\hat{\sigma}_L / \sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}$, which accounts simultaneously for identification ambiguity ($\theta_U - \theta_L$) and finite-sample variance ($\hat{\sigma}_L / \sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}$);
3. *Separating the diagnostic outcome from the prescriptive choice rule:* The Mayo–Manski bridge yields three distinct epistemic states:
   - **Severe worst-case adequacy ($\hat{L}_{1-\alpha} > \theta_{\mathrm{crit}}$):** Even if every missing record in the archive took its most adverse value, the effect exceeds $\theta_{\mathrm{crit}}$ with severity $1 - \alpha$;
   - **Severe refutation ($\hat{U}_{1-\alpha} \le \theta_{\mathrm{crit}}$):** Even under the most optimistic completion of the archive, the policy fails to reach $\theta_{\mathrm{crit}}$;
   - **Underdetermined ambiguity ($\hat{L}_{1-\alpha} \le \theta_{\mathrm{crit}} < \hat{U}_{1-\alpha}$) or non-informative bounds ($\Theta_I = [y_{\min}, y_{\max}]$):** Here, descriptive identification alone does not select an action. Claiming that one must always decide solely by $\hat{L}_{1-\alpha}$ is not a theorem of Manski—it is Wald's (1945) prescriptive *maximin* rule, which can induce paralysis when $\Theta_I$ is wide. As detailed in Section 4.2 (Table 2), when $\hat{L}_{1-\alpha} \le \theta_{\mathrm{crit}} < \hat{U}_{1-\alpha}$, Stochology first enforces the lexicographic survival constraint ($\mathrm{TTS} \ge \mathrm{TTR}$): if the intervention threatens an absorbing ruin barrier, Sosa's meta-aptness mandates withholding the shot; if the intervention is reversible and safe, Manski's (2003, 2013) *minimax regret* rule allocates fractional treatment proportional to the upside/downside span while targeted data collection narrows $\Theta_I$.

Recognizing Manski's identification region is thus the precondition for genuine severity in Mayo's sense: applying a conventional $t$-test to a biased point estimate while ignoring non-random missingness has zero severity against selection bias.

## **IV. The Second Filter: When the Model's Norm Narrates and the Archive Omits**

### **4.1. Tension 3 — Domesticating Formalization versus Reflexive Formalization**

Whereas the first filter governs problem framing and hypothesis testing, the second filter operates in the symbolic instruments that translate phenomena into measures: formal models and documentary archives. Historically, mathematical and classificatory formalization made it possible to expose hidden premises, compare distant regimes, and subject arguments to public verification. Yet this achievement harbors the third pathological inversion: the internal grammar of the formal model begins to legislate over what counts as real.

While Theodor Adorno (1966/2009) critiqued the philosophical tendency of classificatory concepts to dismiss qualitative singularity as "noise," Nicholas Georgescu-Roegen (1971) analyzed its methodological mechanism by contrasting **arithmomorphic concepts** with **dialectical concepts**. Arithmomorphic concepts possess discrete, motionless boundaries like integers ($2 \ne 3$, with no intermediate zone). Dialectical concepts—such as "health," "justice," "clinical severity," or "ecological stability"—are surrounded by a constitutive **dialectical penumbra** ($[a, b]$) within which qualitative transitions unfold. Unlike Adorno's anti-systematic negative dialectics, Georgescu-Roegen's project remained constructively analytical: the error of uncritical formalism lies not in employing mathematics, but in erasing the penumbra by imposing a sharp knife-edge cut where a transition zone exists.

Stochology therefore distinguishes **domesticating formalization**—which adopts Gaussian curves, linear additive models, and lone arithmetic means simply for analytical convenience—from **reflexive formalization**, which uses formal tools to measure the cost of simplification across four dimensions:

1. *Convex harm asymmetry and Jensen's Gap (Jensen 1906):* Let $g(X)$ be a twice-differentiable convex harm or congestion function ($g''(x) > 0$) evaluated over a random demand or environmental stress $X$ with mean $\mu = \mathbb{E}[X]$ and variance $\sigma_X^2$. By Johan Jensen's (1906) inequality, expected harm strictly exceeds the harm evaluated at the average scenario:
   $$\Delta_J = \mathbb{E}\bigl[g(X)\bigr] - g\bigl(\mathbb{E}[X]\bigr) \ge 0, \qquad \Delta_J \approx \frac{1}{2} g''(\mu)\,\sigma_X^2$$
   A human organism exposed to one hour at $27^\circ\mathrm{C}$ and one hour at $47^\circ\mathrm{C}$ experiences a mean temperature of $37^\circ\mathrm{C}$ yet dies of hyperthermia; a hospital ward whose utilization oscillates between $60\%$ and $99\%$ suffers explosive queueing delays that a plan sized for the $80\%$ mean never anticipates. Planning for the lone mean systematically underfunds convex reality by $\Delta_J$;
2. *Heavy-tailed extremes and Pareto moment regimes (Hill 1975):* For variables governed by power-law right tails $\mathbb{P}(X > x) \sim C x^{-\alpha}$ (such as financial losses, wildfire areas, epidemic superspreading, or infrastructure delays), Bruce M. Hill (1975) derived the order-statistic tail-index estimator over the top $k$ extremes $X_{(n)} \ge \dots \ge X_{(n-k)}$:
   $$\hat{\alpha}_k = \left[\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k} \ln\!\left(\frac{X_{(n-i+1)}}{X_{(n-k)}}\right)\right]^{-1}$$
   The tail exponent $\alpha$ partitions reality into three distinct moment regimes: (i) $\alpha > 2$, where variance $\sigma^2 < \infty$ is finite but higher moments and kurtosis can still destabilize Gaussian intervals; (ii) $1 < \alpha \le 2$, where the population mean $\mu < \infty$ remains finite but the **population variance is infinite ($\sigma^2 = \infty$)**, rendering sample standard deviations erratic downward-biased mirages; and (iii) $\alpha \le 1$, where even the population mean ceases to exist ($\mu = \infty$);
3. *Structural model discrepancy (Kennedy and O'Hagan 2001):* Every mechanistic or econometric model $f(x, \theta)$ omits real-world processes. Marc Kennedy and Anthony O'Hagan (2001) demonstrated that calibrating $\theta$ under the fiction of zero structural error forces parameter estimates to absorb omitted physics or social dynamics, producing overconfident out-of-sample extrapolations. Reflexive formalization decomposes reality as $y(x) = f(x, \theta) + \delta(x) + \varepsilon$, modeling structural discrepancy $\delta(x)$ explicitly so that predictive uncertainty widens as $x$ moves outside the historical support;
4. *Measurement scale invariance and CORP calibration decomposition (Stevens 1946; Krantz et al. 1971; Murphy 1973; Gneiting, Balabdaoui, and Raftery 2007; Dimitriadis, Gneiting, and Jordan 2021):* In representational measurement theory (Stevens 1946; Krantz et al. 1971), computing arithmetic means on ordinal scales is ill-defined because strictly increasing monotone transformations $\phi(\cdot)$ can reverse mean rankings while leaving medians, quantiles, and first-order stochastic dominance invariant. In probabilistic forecasting, Allan H. Murphy (1973) and Tilmann Gneiting, Fadoua Balabdaoui, and Adrian E. Raftery (2007) established that forecast adequacy requires both **calibration (reliability)** and **resolution (sharpness)**. Timo Dimitriadis, Tilmann Gneiting, and Alexander I. Jordan (2021) placed this decomposition on a rigorous parameter-free footing via the isotonic Pool-Adjacent-Violators (**CORP**) identity for the Brier score:
   $$\mathrm{BS} = \underbrace{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(p_i - \hat{\pi}_i)^2}_{\mathrm{REL}_{\mathrm{CORP}} \text{ (miscalibration)}} - \underbrace{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\hat{\pi}_i - \bar{y})^2}_{\mathrm{RES}_{\mathrm{CORP}} \text{ (discriminative resolution)}} + \underbrace{\bar{y}(1 - \bar{y})}_{\mathrm{UNC}}$$
   where $\hat{\pi}_i$ are the PAV-recalibrated probabilities. This exact identity provides the mathematical engine for Stochology's first reflexive Goodhart brake (Section 7.2).

### **4.2. Tension 4 — Sample Volume versus Evidential Weight, the Silence of the Archive, and the Epistemic Margin**

When the fragility of simplified models becomes undeniable, institutions frequently turn to the accumulation of massive digital registries (*Big Data*), assuming that millions of rows ($n \to N$) automatically wash away measurement bias. Here the fourth pathological inversion takes hold: archival volume is mistaken for evidential quality, converting selective omission into high-precision error.

In Chapter 6 of *A Treatise on Probability*, John Maynard Keynes (1921) distinguished the **probability** $p$ of an argument from its **weight of evidence** $V(E, H)$:

$$V = \frac{K}{K + I} \in (0, 1)$$

where $K$ measures the balance of relevant knowledge and $I$ the remaining relevant ignorance. Two clinical or judicial assessments may both yield $p = 0.50$, yet one rests on a two-line administrative note ($V \ll 1$) while the other rests on exhaustive diagnostic testing and cross-examination ($V \to 1$). Probability measures the tilt of the scale; evidential weight measures how much of the relevant reality has actually been placed upon the pans.

Nearly a century later, Xiao-Li Meng (2018) proved an exact finite-population identity that explains why ignoring evidential weight and collection design produces the **Big Data paradox**. Let $R_i \in \{0, 1\}$ indicate whether individual $i$ in a population of size $N$ is recorded in an archive of size $n = \sum_{i=1}^N R_i$, with sampling fraction $f = n / N$. The error between the archive sample mean $\bar{Y}_n$ and the true population mean $\bar{Y}_N$ is identically:

$$\bar{Y}_n - \bar{Y}_N = \underbrace{\rho_{R, Y}}_{\text{data defect correlation}} \cdot \underbrace{\sqrt{\frac{1 - f}{f}}}_{\text{dropout odds factor}} \cdot \underbrace{\sigma_Y}_{\text{population standard deviation}}$$

Equating the mean squared error of $\bar{Y}_n$ to that of a simple random sample yields the effective independent sample size $n_{\mathrm{eff}} \approx \frac{f}{1 - f}\frac{1}{\rho_{R,Y}^2}$. Three consequences follow immediately:
1. *Relative coverage $f = n/N$, not raw count $n$, governs the dropout factor:* a registry of one million records in a nation of two hundred million ($f = 0.005$) retains a large dropout multiplier $\sqrt{(1-f)/f} \approx 14.1$;
2. *Selection correlation $\rho_{R,Y}$ does not vanish as $n$ grows under homogeneous collection:* if a digital platform or administrative registry systematically under-records poorer or sicker strata ($\rho_{R,Y} \ne 0$), scaling $n$ upward without altering the inclusion mechanism leaves $\rho_{R,Y}$ untouched while shrinking the nominal standard error $\sigma_Y / \sqrt{n}$ toward zero;
3. *Maximum confidence in the wrong answer:* In Meng's (2018) empirical calibration of the 2016 U.S. presidential election polls, a non-random sample of $n = 2.3 \times 10^6$ respondents with a tiny recording correlation of $\rho_{R,Y} \approx -0.005$ possessed the same effective information content as a genuinely randomized sample of roughly $n_{\mathrm{eff}} \approx 400$ respondents. The larger the unadjusted archive, the closer to zero falls the probability that its nominal confidence interval covers the true parameter $\bar{Y}_N$.

Two further mechanisms compound this contraction of information in the transit from world to archive. First, observations in social, clinical, and financial archives arrive in correlated clusters (hospitals, schools, neighborhoods, trading days). As Cathy Campbell (1977) and Brent Moulton (1986) showed, when cluster sizes $m_g$ vary with coefficient of variation $\mathrm{CV}_m$ and intra-cluster correlation $\rho_{\mathrm{intra}} > 0$, the **design effect** inflates variance according to

$$\mathrm{DEFF} = 1 + \bigl[\bar{m}\bigl(1 + \mathrm{CV}_m^2\bigr) - 1\bigr]\rho_{\mathrm{intra}}, \qquad n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{cluster}} = \frac{n}{\mathrm{DEFF}}$$

Second, archival omission is a socio-material and institutional process. In Donald Rubin's (1976; Little and Rubin 1987/2002) taxonomy, missingness is **Missing Not At Random (MNAR)** whenever the probability of non-recording depends on the unobserved outcome itself. Yet as the sociology of classification and quantification demonstrates (Hacking 1995; Desrosières 1998; Bowker and Star 1999), "vulnerable populations" and archival silences are not pre-existing natural facts: censuses, diagnostic manuals, and welfare registries actively co-produce social categories (*making up people*) while rendering uncategorized suffering invisible—a structural failure that Miranda Fricker (2007) terms **hermeneutical injustice** (when an institution's conceptual schema lacks the very category needed to register a group's harm) paired with **Proctor's agnotology** (when exclusion is preserved by administrative design).

Where Meng's selection defect, Moulton's cluster design effect, and socio-institutional omission operate together, Stochology's **epistemic margin** replaces point extrapolation with four formal audit tools:
- When inclusion probabilities $\pi_i$ approach zero in underserved strata (positivity violations that destabilize Horvitz and Thompson's, 1952, inverse-probability weights), **Kish's (1965) effective sample size** $n_{\mathrm{Kish}} = (\sum w_i)^2 / \sum w_i^2$ exposes weight concentration;
- When multiple incomplete lists exist, **Chao's (1987) capture–recapture lower bound** $\hat{n}_{00} = f_1^2 / (2 f_2)$ estimates the minimum size of the uncounted population from singleton ($f_1$) and doubleton ($f_2$) overlaps;
- When subjects exit follow-up under dependent competing risks, **Peterson's (1976) sharp competing-risks bounds** bracket the joint survival distribution without assuming independent censoring, while **Lee's (2009) trimming bounds** bracket treatment effects under selective sample attrition;
- Most generally, for any bounded outcome $Y \in [y_{\min}, y_{\max}]$ with missingness fraction $p_{\mathrm{miss}}$ and observed mean $\bar{Y}_{\mathrm{obs}}$, **Manski's (2003) worst-case partial-identification bounds** delimit the sharp ambiguity interval:
  $$\bigl[L_M,\, U_M\bigr] = \Bigl[(1 - p_{\mathrm{miss}})\bar{Y}_{\mathrm{obs}} + p_{\mathrm{miss}} y_{\min},\; (1 - p_{\mathrm{miss}})\bar{Y}_{\mathrm{obs}} + p_{\mathrm{miss}} y_{\max}\Bigr]$$

## **V. The Third Filter: When the Truth of the World Tears Under Action and Time**

### **5.1. Tension 5 — Passive Observation versus Performative Intervention**

Suppose an investigator has constrained situational framing biases, subjected hypotheses to severe sequential tests, respected convex tail asymmetries, and declared partial-identification bounds over archival omissions. Even then, the third mediating filter opens as soon as that representation is deployed to govern society: the instant a model or indicator is used to intervene in the world, the social and material field reacts to the intervention and alters the regularities the model was built upon.

The first ground of this rupture is causal and structural, formalized by Judea Pearl (2009; Pearl and Mackenzie 2018) in the three-rung **Ladder of Causation**:
1. *Rung 1 — Association ($\mathbb{P}(Y \mid X)$, passive observation):* recording empirical correlations in an undisturbed regime, which cannot distinguish structural causes from confounding (such as higher mortality in tertiary hospitals that receive the sickest patients);
2. *Rung 2 — Intervention ($\mathbb{P}(Y \mid \mathrm{do}(X = x))$, material action):* actively setting $X$ to $x$, which severs incoming confounding arrows and alters the data-generating distribution—requiring predictors whose structural coefficients remain invariant across distinct environments (**Invariant Causal Prediction**, Peters, Bühlmann, and Meinshausen 2016) and accounting for spillover and interference when scaling from a pilot trial to a population (Hudgens and Halloran 2008);
3. *Rung 3 — Counterfactuals ($\mathbb{P}(Y_x = y' \mid X = x, Y = y)$, retrospective attribution):* evaluating what would have occurred in a singular historical case under an alternative action, where Jin Tian and Judea Pearl (2000) derived sharp partial-identification bounds for the **Probability of Necessity ($\mathrm{PN}$)** and **Probability of Necessity and Sufficiency ($\mathrm{PNS}$)** combining experimental and observational evidence.

In social, economic, and administrative life, climbing from passive observation to intervention encounters a second complication: the agents being modeled interpret the rule and adapt strategically. In macroeconomics, Robert Lucas (1976) showed that historical correlations dissolve when agents adjust expectations to a new policy regime, while Charles Goodhart (1975) formulated the law that any observed statistical regularity tends to collapse once control pressure is placed upon it—a mechanism generalized in the anthropology of audit culture by Marilyn Strathern (1997) ("when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure") and in the sociology of institutional rankings by Wendy Espeland and Michael Sauder (2007).

Conceptual clarity requires distinguishing three mechanisms that are often conflated: **Goodhart's Law**, **sociotechnical performativity**, and **performative prediction**. First, David Manheim and Scott Garrabrant (2018) classify four distinct modes of Goodhart target collapse when an institution optimizes a proxy $M$ in place of a latent value $V$: *regressional collapse* (selecting noise tails where $M - V$ is largest), *extremal collapse* (pushing the state outside the historical domain where $M$ and $V$ co-varied), *causal collapse* (intervening on a downstream symptom $M$ rather than the upstream cause $V$, as analyzed in strategic classification by Milli et al. 2019; Kleinberg and Raghavan 2020; Miller, Milli, and Hardt 2020), and *adversarial collapse* (direct gaming of $M$ by strategic actors).

Second, in Science and Technology Studies (STS), the program of **performativity**—grounded in the reflexive sociology of Barry Barnes (1983), formulated for economic sociology by Michel Callon (1998), and developed historically by Donald MacKenzie (2006)—demonstrates that formal models are not passive *cameras* recording a pre-existing market or bureaucracy, but *engines* that actively format socio-material practices. MacKenzie (2006) distinguishes four levels of performativity: (i) *generic performativity*, where a model is used by practitioners; (ii) *effective performativity*, where its use measurably alters processes; (iii) *Barnesian performativity* (named after Barry Barnes), where practical adoption of a model reshapes institutional arrangements so that reality conforms more closely to the model's postulates (a self-fulfilling prophecy); and (iv) **counterperformativity**, where widespread adoption of the model alters material practices in a way that undermines the model's own empirical premises (as the mechanized use of Black–Scholes portfolio insurance helped precipitate the 1987 crash and created the volatility skew that violated the model's flat-volatility assumption). Whereas Goodhart's Law describes the statistical decoupling of a target proxy under incentive pressure, MacKenzie's counterperformativity describes the material undoing of a model's truth conditions through its own infrastructural enactment.

Third, Juan Perdomo, Tijana Zrnic, Celestine Mendler-Dünner, and Moritz Hardt (2020) formalized **performative prediction**, where deploying a model parameter $\theta$ induces a distribution shift $\mathcal{D}(\theta)$. A parameter $\theta_{\mathrm{PS}}$ achieves **performative stability** when it is a fixed point of repeated retraining, $\theta_{\mathrm{PS}} = \arg\min_{\theta} \mathbb{E}_{Z \sim \mathcal{D}(\theta_{\mathrm{PS}})}[\ell(Z; \theta)]$: the model appears well-calibrated on the distribution its own deployment generated (for example, a credit-scoring rule that denies loans to a neighborhood, induces business failures, and then cites those failures as confirmation of its risk score). **Performative optimality** ($\theta_{\mathrm{PO}} = \arg\min_{\theta} \mathbb{E}_{Z \sim \mathcal{D}(\theta)}[\ell(Z; \theta)]$), demanded by Stochology, evaluates the policy not by the self-confirming stability of $\theta_{\mathrm{PS}}$, but by the substantive social risk of the equilibrium it brings into being.

### **5.2. Tension 6 — Ensemble Averages versus Trajectories in Irreversible Time**

The second rupture of the third filter concerns the arrow of time. As Nicholas Georgescu-Roegen (1971) argued in *The Entropy Law and the Economic Process*, standard formal models routinely adopt the reversible mechanical time of classical kinematics, in which trajectories can be run backward without loss. Living organisms, ecosystems, households, and public infrastructures, however, inhabit **irreversible thermodynamic and historical time**: dissipated reserves, cumulative physiological wear, and crossings of ruin barriers admit no time-reversal symmetry.

Ignoring temporal irreversibility produces the sixth constitutive tension: conflating the **ensemble average** across parallel realizations at a fixed instant with the **time average** along a single historical trajectory. Consider a sequence of river crossings over thin ice where each crossing carries a $90\%$ probability of multiplying wealth by $1.5$ and a $10\%$ probability of drowning (an absorbing barrier where wealth drops permanently to $0$). Across an ensemble of $100$ parallel travelers crossing once on a single afternoon, $90$ survive with $1.5 W_0$, yielding a positive ensemble mean return of $+35\%$. For a single historical traveler—a person, a firm, or a municipal reserve—who must cross the river $100$ days in succession carrying prior accumulations forward, survival probability after $100$ crossings is $0.9^{100} \approx 2.66 \times 10^{-5}$: the time-trajectory growth rate collapses to ruin with near certainty.

To analyze this divergence without historical or mathematical conflation, Stochology distinguishes three levels:

1. *Discrete multiplicative compounding versus Itô's continuous-time stochastic lemma (Itô 1944, 1951):* Already in elementary discrete arithmetic, multiplicative processes obey the geometric–arithmetic mean inequality: losing $50\%$ of a reserve and then gaining $50\%$ yields $(1 - 0.50)(1 + 0.50) = 0.75$, a net loss of $25\%$ despite a zero arithmetic mean return. In continuous time, Kiyosi Itô's (1944, 1951) stochastic calculus establishes the exact differential rule. For a resource $W_t$ governed by geometric Brownian motion $\mathrm{d}W_t / W_t = \mu\,\mathrm{d}t + \sigma\,\mathrm{d}B_t$ with ensemble drift $\mu$ and volatility $\sigma$, Itô's lemma proves that logarithmic wealth evolves as
   $$\mathrm{d}\ln W_t = \left(\mu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2\right)\mathrm{d}t + \sigma\,\mathrm{d}B_t$$
   The term $-\frac{1}{2}\sigma^2$ is the continuous **Itô volatility drain**: variance pulls the long-run time-average growth rate $g = \mu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2$ strictly below the ensemble drift $\mu$;
2. *Kelly–Breiman growth, Samuelson's expected-utility critique, and Baker–McHale epistemic shrinkage (Kelly 1956; Breiman 1961; Merton 1969, 1971; Samuelson 1971, 1979; Baker and McHale 2013; Peters 2019):* For a leveraged exposure fraction $f$ in an excess-return process, the time-average logarithmic growth rate is $g(f) = r + f(\mu - r) - \frac{1}{2}f^2\sigma^2$, which is maximized at the **Kelly fraction** $f_{\mathrm{plug\text{-}in}}^* = (\mu - r)/\sigma^2$ (Kelly 1956; Breiman 1961) and becomes strictly negative whenever $f > 2 f_{\mathrm{plug\text{-}in}}^*$. Precision requires noting two critical caveats. First, Kelly's rule avoids *asymptotic* ruin in idealized infinitely divisible processes, but over finite horizons it still permits severe interim drawdowns; furthermore, when drift $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$ are estimated from a finite effective sample size $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$, Rose Baker and Ian McHale (2013) show that estimation uncertainty requires **epistemic Kelly shrinkage**:
   $$f_{\mathrm{epi}}^* = \frac{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}{n_{\mathrm{eff}} + 1}\,\frac{\hat{\mu} - r}{\hat{\sigma}^2}$$
   Second, as Paul Samuelson (1971, 1979) and Robert C. Merton (1969, 1971) emphasized in their classic debate with Kelly proponents, maximizing the time-average growth rate $g(f)$ of a multiplicative process is mathematically equivalent to maximizing expected logarithmic utility $\mathbb{E}[\ln W]$ within standard expected-utility theory, and does not override all other risk preferences over finite horizons when survival is not at stake. Consequently, when Ole Peters (2019) synthesized this literature under the banner of *ergodicity economics*, its genuine methodological contribution lay not in refuting expected-utility axiomatics, but in foregrounding how unbuffered multiplicative exposure in the presence of absorbing barriers renders ensemble averages a treacherous guide for policy;
3. *Conditions for non-ergodic criticality:* Not every decision problem is non-ergodic. When losses are small, additive, and bounded far away from ruin thresholds, ensemble expectations remain serviceable approximations. Non-ergodic divergence becomes decisive when two structural conditions hold jointly: **(a) sequential multiplicative or state-dependent compounding** and **(b) absorbing ruin barriers** (clinical death, insolvency, ecosystem collapse, infrastructure failure) from which no recovery is possible.

### **5.3. Survivorship Bias, Ecological Resilience, and the Operational Mechanics of the Vital Margin**

Invoking survival barriers requires dispelling two symmetrical fallacies surrounding the concept of "survival":
- *Statistical survivorship bias (from world to archive, Wald 1943):* Analyzing Allied bombers returning from European sorties during the Second World War, Abraham Wald (1943) demonstrated that fuselage zones riddled with bullet holes on returning aircraft (wings and tail) indicated where hits were *survivable*, whereas the scarcity of hits on returning engines and cockpits indicated that aircraft struck there were lost at sea ($\mathbb{P}(\text{lost} \mid \text{engine hit}) \gg \mathbb{P}(\text{lost} \mid \text{wing hit})$). Conditioning an archive on survivors inverts the true vulnerability map;
- *The normative fallacy of survival (from history to value):* In a non-ergodic world with unequal power, the mere fact that an institution or speculator has survived to date is neither proof of epistemic rationality nor a certificate of moral legitimacy, since tail risks can remain latent for years and powerful actors routinely externalize ruin onto unbuffered populations.

Stochology therefore separates (1) **material survival** as a *lexicographic precondition* (the physical floor required for agents and communities to continue existing and deliberating), (2) **epistemic survival** of conjectures under severe testing, and (3) **legitimate institutional survival**, which is bound by the duty not to cannibalize the vital margins of others.

The positive grounding of the **vital margin** draws on Georges Canguilhem's (1943/2009) philosophy of medicine and Crawford S. Holling's (1973) systems ecology, while explicitly distinguishing **operational buffer stocks** from **structural basin width**. In *The Normal and the Pathological*, Canguilhem (1943/2009) rejected the positivist identification of health with conformity to a statistical mean: an organism capable of surviving only at a fixed average temperature or workload is already pathological. Health is *biological normativity*—defined as "a margin of tolerance for the inconsistencies of the environment" (*une marge de tolérance des infidélités du milieu*). In systems ecology, Holling (1973) similarly separated **engineering resilience** (local return speed $\lambda$ near an equilibrium point) from **ecological resilience** (the width of the attraction basin measuring how large a shock the system can absorb before crossing an unstable separatrix into an irreversible degraded regime).

Here a crucial theoretical tension must be resolved: in Section 4.1, Jensen's inequality treated environmental fluctuation as a convex cost ($\Delta_J \ge 0$), whereas Holling (1973) and Crawford Holling and Gary Meffe (1996), in their classic diagnosis of the **"Pathology of Natural Resource Management" (*command-and-control*)**, showed that policies obsessed with suppressing all natural fluctuations (such as extinguishing every minor brushfire or stabilizing river flows artificially) destroy ecological resilience. How can fluctuation be simultaneously a convex cost and a condition of resilience? The synthesis lies in separating **scale and regime**: rigid command-and-control suppression of low-amplitude variability causes hidden stress or combustible fuel to accumulate silently, shrinking the width of the resilience basin until an inevitable shock triggers a catastrophic convex Jensen jump across the boundary. A well-designed vital margin therefore *permits and absorbs low-amplitude natural variability* (which exercises adaptive capacity and preserves basin depth) while *maintaining hard buffers against crossing the absorbing ruin threshold*.

To avoid conflating distinct physical processes under a single metaphor, Stochology separates the **operational stock dimension** of the vital margin from its **dynamical basin dimension** across three distinct mechanisms with distinct empirical signatures:

1. *Heavy-traffic queueing congestion (Kingman 1961; Scarf 1960):* On the operational stock plane, when a service facility (hospital, court, electrical grid) is pushed toward $100\%$ utilization ($\rho = \lambda_{\mathrm{arr}} / \mu_{\mathrm{serv}} \to 1^-$) to eliminate "idle capacity," John F. C. Kingman's (1961) heavy-traffic approximation proves that expected waiting time explodes hyperbolically with arrival and service coefficients of variation ($c_a^2, c_s^2$):
   $$\mathbb{E}[W_q] \approx \left(\frac{\rho}{1 - \rho}\right)\left(\frac{c_a^2 + c_s^2}{2}\right)\tau_{\mathrm{service}}$$
   Preserving operational headroom ($1 - \rho \ge 0.20$) and Herbert Scarf's (1960) $(s, S)$ inventory buffers is the direct stock-level defense against queue strangulation;
2. *Allostatic load and capacity erosion (McEwen 1998):* On the physiological and organizational plane, chronic operation near the capacity ceiling without recovery intervals accumulates **allostatic load** $A_t$ (McEwen 1998), which progressively lowers the upper capacity boundary $K(A_t) = K_0 - \gamma A_t$ and contracts the radius of the Canguilhem–Holling resilience basin;
3. *Dynamical bifurcation and Critical Slowing Down as an epistemic early-warning signal (Scheffer et al. 2009):* On the structural basin plane, because the width of an attraction basin cannot be observed directly ex ante, Marten Scheffer et al. (2009) showed that as a system approaches a fold bifurcation, its local recovery rate $\lambda \to 0^+$ (**Critical Slowing Down**). In time-series data, this manifests as a rise in lag-1 autocorrelation ($\rho_1 = e^{-\lambda \Delta t} \to 1$) and a rise in fluctuation variance ($\sigma_X^2 = \sigma_\varepsilon^2 / (1 - \rho_1^2) \to \infty$). Observed micro-variance thus serves an *epistemic* diagnostic role (warning that the basin is shallowing, subject to finite-sample false-positive caveats), while David Simchi-Levi, William Schmidt, and Yehua Wei's (2014) **breathing-time inequality** enforces the hard physical floor:
   $$\mathrm{TTS}_t \;\text{(Time-to-Survive on local buffer)} \;\ge\; \mathrm{TTR}_t \;\text{(Time-to-Recover)}.$$

## **VI. The Architecture of Judgment Under Turbulence: *Télos*, Moral Luck, Apt Agency, and Institutional Contract**

### **6.1. Tension 7 (First Face) — Antipater's Archer (*Télos* and *Skopós*), Moral Luck, and the Fact-to-Duty Bridge**

Even when an agent respects all six preceding locks, no human precaution eliminates contingency: a scrupulous clinician may lose a patient to an unpredictable complication, a civil-defense evacuation may be followed by a sudden shift in a cyclone's track, and a reckless executive may survive several quarters unharmed by sheer luck. This generates the seventh constitutive tension: how can we evaluate the responsibility of decision-makers under uncertainty without either worshiping fortuitous outcomes or granting impunity to reckless governance?

Hellenistic philosophy confronted this problem through a technical distinction formulated within the Stoa, whose philological transmission requires precision. In Book III of *De Finibus Bonorum et Malorum* (III.22), Cicero presents in Latin the analogy of the archer or spear-thrower: the archer must do everything within his craft to aim straight (which Cicero terms the *ultimum* or *extremum bonorum*, the supreme good internal to right conduct), even though striking the external target (*propositum*) remains exposed to crosswinds after the shaft leaves the bow. While Cicero leaves the Greek author unnamed in that passage, the anthology of **Joannes Stobaeus** (*Eclogae* II.76.3–15 Wachsmuth = Long and Sedley 1987, 64C) explicitly preserves the authorship and Greek terminology: the distinction belongs to **Antipater of Tarsus** (head of the Stoa in the second century BCE, before Panaetius of Rhodes shifted Stoic ethics toward the doctrine of the four *personae* and practical appropriateness, *kathēkon*, in Cicero's *De Officiis* I.107–121). Antipater separated the **τέλος (*télos*, the internal end—doing everything in one's power continuously and undeviatingly to achieve the primary natural things)** from the **σκοπός (*skopós*, the external target set before the agent)**. Hitting the *skopós* is a **preferred indifferent** (**προηγμένον, *proēgmenon***; *seligendum*, not *expetendum*). At the same time, historical honesty requires noting that our fullest narrative source—**Plutarch** (*De communibus notitiis adversus Stoicos* 1070f–1071e = Long and Sedley 1987, 64E)—is a hostile polemicist who attacks Antipater's archer analogy as incoherent, arguing that no archer could value the act of aiming (*télos*) higher than hitting the target (*skopós*) unless aiming is ordered to hitting.

In twentieth-century ethics, **Thomas Nagel (1979)** and **Bernard Williams (1981)** reopened this problem as **moral luck**—distinguishing *constitutive*, *circumstantial*, *causal*, and *resultant* luck—while **Jonathan Baron and John C. Hershey (1988)** demonstrated experimentally how **outcome bias** leads observers to rate the same decision as competent when the realization is favorable and negligent when it fails.

How does Stochology answer both Plutarch's ancient objection to Antipater and the modern challenge of moral luck? First, to answer Plutarch without retreating into a purely inward ethics of good intentions, Stochology draws on **Ernest Sosa's (2007, 2015)** virtue epistemology (*AAA structure*): a performance is **accurate** if it hits the target (even by luck); **adroit** if it exercises genuine methodological competence (incorporating epistemic and vital margins); and **apt** if it succeeds *because* it is adroit. Aiming (*télos*) is therefore not severed from hitting (*skopós*): adroitness is defined by objective reliability across counterfactually close worlds. At the second order, Sosa (2015) defines **fully apt performance** (*meta-aptness*) as the reflective competence to assess the environmental risk of the field and determine whether one is in a position to take an apt shot at all. Extending Sosa's epistemic concept to practical institutional action—a normative extension specific to Stochology—yields the duty to **lower the bow and withhold irreversible intervention** when identification bounds are wide and absorbing barriers are close, while simultaneously weighing the **cost of the unshot arrow (Type II / failure-to-rescue error)** whenever status-quo inaction itself inflicts irreversible harm within the $\mathrm{TTS} \ge \mathrm{TTR}$ window.

Second, Stochology does not claim that *aptness* dissolves resultant moral luck. Here Bernard Williams's (1981) distinction between *blame* and **agent-regret** is decisive. Even when an adroit lorry driver—or a scrupulous clinician or infrastructure planner—took every due precaution and is rightly exempted from blame for negligence (correcting Baron–Hershey outcome bias), an adverse tail realization leaves an irreducible moral residue: *agent-regret*, the recognition that it was *one's own action or policy* that stood in the causal chain of another's loss. In Stochology, agent-regret is not a psychological curiosity to be brushed aside; it grounds the institutional obligation of **no-fault reparative compensation**, solidaristic insurance for those struck by residual tail events, and mandatory recording of the adverse outcome in the public registry.

We are now in a position to construct explicitly the **fact-to-duty bridge** (*is* $\to$ *ought*): why are declaring the epistemic margin and preserving the vital margin strict *moral obligaciones of justice* rather than optional technical heuristics? The argument proceeds in three distinct steps that avoid any naturalistic fallacy:

1. *The epistemic-pragmatic threshold (Fantl and McGrath 2009):* Jeremy Fantl and Matthew McGrath's (2009) principle of **pragmatic encroachment** establishes an *epistemic* bridge: whether a body of evidence $E$ suffices for an agent to know (and warrantably assert) that "policy $a$ is safe" is not invariant to practical stakes; as the severity of potential harm increases, the evidential threshold required for warrant rises. By itself, however, pragmatic encroachment is a thesis about epistemic warrant, not yet a moral duty;
2. *The structural asymmetry of risk imposition and aggregation (Hansson 2013):* Sven Ove Hansson's (2013) ethics of risk supplies the material premise that distinguishes public governance from solitary decision-making: in institutional action, **the agent who selects the risk exposure is systematically distinct from the third parties who bear the physical downside if the gamble fails**. Moreover, even when an individual probability of ruin appears small ($p_i = 10^{-3}$), imposing that risk across an unbuffered population of $N = 10^5$ citizens converts a small individual fraction into a near-certainty of grave harm for roughly a hundred concrete persons;
3. *Execution of Scanlon's contractualist test (Scanlon 1998):* Under Thomas M. Scanlon's (1998) contractualism, an act is wrong if its performance under the circumstances would be disallowed by any principle for the general regulation of behavior that no one could reasonably reject. Consider the **Candidate Principle of Unbuffered Point Optimization** ($P_{\mathrm{naive}}$): *"An institution may size reserves and deploy interventions based on point estimates and high-utilization averages whenever doing so maximizes aggregate expected net efficiency."* Under Scanlon's individualist comparison of generic reasons, consider two affected standpoints:
   - *Standpoint A (the uncounted or unbuffered citizen exposed to an absorbing barrier):* If $P_{\mathrm{naive}}$ is adopted, this citizen faces irreversible ruin (death, bankruptcy, loss of livelihood) without warning whenever a heavy-tailed shock or archival bias materializes, and is deprived of the information needed to exercise consent or self-protection;
   - *Standpoint B (the taxpayer or institutional beneficiary of lean efficiency):* Under the rival **Stochological Principle of Bounded Exposure** ($P_{\mathrm{stoch}}$), this party bears the modest, bounded opportunity cost of maintaining capacity slack ($1 - \rho \ge 0.20$) and conducting partial-identification audits.
   Because the generic burden imposed on Standpoint A under $P_{\mathrm{naive}}$ (unconsented exposure to irreversible ruin and epistemic deception) is orders of magnitude graver than the bounded efficiency cost borne by Standpoint B under $P_{\mathrm{stoch}}$—and because, following Hansson's (2013) equitable risk-exchange criterion, small efficiency gains to many cannot justify imposing unbuffered ruin on a vulnerable subset unless the system serves the reciprocal survival of those very persons—$P_{\mathrm{naive}}$ is reasonably rejectable. Concealing the width of $[L_t, U_t]$ or liquidating the vital margin when another person will cross the minefield violates reciprocal justification.

When the three margins come into practical conflict under scarcity or emergency, this contractualist foundation yields an explicit **Normative Priority Rule**:
- **First Priority (Lexicographic Survival Floor):** Protecting immediate life and preventing the crossing of an absorbing ruin barrier ($\mathrm{TTS}_t \ge \mathrm{TTR}_t$, Roy 1952) takes lexical priority over procedural delay (governing Reflexive Brake 3 in acute emergencies) and over point-efficiency gains;
- **Second Priority (Epistemic Non-Concealment):** Within the survival-admissible set, the duty to declare honest partial-identification bounds $[L_t, U_t]$ without fabrication or agnotological inflation is non-negotiable;
- **Third Priority (Equitable Allocation of Opportunity Cost and Liability):** The trade-off between present relief and future reserve stock (Reflexive Brake 2) and the distribution of classification errors must be publicly justified under constitutional governance with downside liability at the top.

### **6.2. Tension 7 (Second Face) — Group Agency, Cybernetic Veto, Contractual Asymmetry, and the Fairness Trilemma**

In modern society, the agent who sets interest rates, hospital bed ratios, environmental quotas, or judicial risk scores is not a solitary Stoic archer, but a corporate or state institution. Relying on individual moral heroism inside a perversely structured bureaucracy is sociologically naive. Translating apt agency into institutional architecture requires combining **Christian List and Philip Pettit's (2011) theory of group agency**, **Miranda Fricker's (2007, 2020) critique of epistemic injustice**, **Stafford Beer's (1972) organizational cybernetics**, and the **economics and sociology of contracts and classification**:

1. *Constitutionalizing the conjunctive safety brake (List and Pettit 2011; Beer 1972; Fricker 2007):* List and Pettit (2011) proved via the **discursive dilemma** that a committee voting by simple majority on a conclusion ($C \leftrightarrow P_1 \wedge P_2 \wedge P_3$, where $P_1, P_2, P_3$ are the three safeguards) can vote to approve an unsafe deployment even when a majority of specialists judges at least one safeguard violated. Rational group agency therefore requires a **premise-based conjunctive constitution** in which any violated safety premise exercises veto power. In Stafford Beer's (1972) Viable System Model, this requires separating operational management from an independent **System $3^*$ ($S3^*$) sporadic audit channel** and an **algedonic emergency channel** that bypasses hierarchical filtering to trigger automatic suspension when errors breach declared bounds. Crucially, Miranda Fricker (2007) shows that **testimonial injustice**—applying a credibility discount $\delta_{\mathrm{cred}} \in (0, 1)$ to frontline workers, nurses, or marginalized residents—acts as a **fatal cybernetic delay**, multiplying the shifts required to trip the algedonic circuit breaker by $1 / (1 - \delta_{\mathrm{cred}})$ until the recognition time exceeds $\mathrm{TTS}$;
2. *Ending the "free option on volatility" at the top while protecting the base (Holmström 1979; Innes 1990; Taleb 2018):* While Nassim Nicholas Taleb (2018) popularized the demand for symmetry of exposure ("skin in the game"), applying raw downside penalties to frontline operators (on-call clinicians, teachers, maintenance crews) who lack authority over system capacity or leverage is both unjust and inefficient. Bengt Holmström's (1979) **informativeness principle** shows that an agent's compensation should depend on a signal only to the extent that the agent controls its structural variance. Conversely, at the executive top, **Robert D. Innes (1990)** proved that when an entrepreneur or executive with **limited liability** (bounded downside at zero) chooses risk exposure ex ante, the constrained-optimal incentive contract exhibits a convex payoff structure that creates a structural incentive to shift mass into extreme tails when upside bonuses are uncapped. Combined with the corporate-governance critique of asymmetric executive bonuses (Taleb 2018), limited liability without clawback grants top decision-makers a **free option on tail volatility**: private bonuses are cashed immediately in calm years by liquidating "idle" vital reserves, while catastrophic tail losses are socialized onto the public. Stochology therefore mandates **solidaristic limited-liability protection at the operational base** paired with **bitemporal clawback escrows (`TemporalVault`) and patrimonial restitution at the decision-making top**;
3. *Protecting unmeasured care in multitask environments (Holmström and Milgrom 1991; Strathern 1997; Espeland and Sauder 2007):* When professional work comprises both an easily counted proxy task (consultations per hour, arrest quotas, publication counts) and an unmeasured high-value dimension (diagnostic listening, de-escalation, replicable rigor), Bengt Holmström and Paul Milgrom (1991) proved that high-powered incentives on the counted metric rationally drive effort on unmeasured care toward zero—a formal mechanism reinforced sociologically by audit and ranking reactivity (Strathern 1997; Espeland and Sauder 2007);
4. *Public justification under Chouldechova's Fairness Trilemma and the duty of the embedded practitioner (Chouldechova 2017):* When an institution deploys a risk instrument across groups $g \in \{A, B\}$ marked by unequal historical base rates $p_A \ne p_B \in (0, 1)$, Alexandra Chouldechova (2017) proved the exact algebraic identity linking the False Positive Rate ($\mathrm{FPR}_g$, the proportion of negative-outcome individuals subjected to false alarms), the False Negative Rate ($\mathrm{FNR}_g$, the proportion of positive-need or positive-risk individuals missed by the rule), and Positive Predictive Value ($\mathrm{PPV}_g$, calibration parity):
   $$\mathrm{FPR}_g = \frac{p_g}{1 - p_g} \cdot \frac{1 - \mathrm{PPV}_g}{\mathrm{PPV}_g} \cdot \bigl(1 - \mathrm{FNR}_g\bigr)$$
   Whenever $p_A \ne p_B$ and $\mathrm{PPV}_A = \mathrm{PPV}_B$, it is algebraically impossible to equalize both $\mathrm{FPR}$ and $\mathrm{FNR}$ across groups. While Chouldechova's identity is a mathematical theorem, connecting it to Scanlon's contractualist principle of non-rejectability yields a direct **normative consequence**: because no value-neutral technical optimum exists under unequal base rates, presenting an algorithm as "objectively fair" conceals a political choice about which group will bear excess false alarms or excess unmet need. The institution is morally obligated to disclose its chosen error trade-off publicly, and the individual statistician or engineer embedded inside an institution that refuses disclosure has the professional duty to record the disparity in the independent $S3^*$ audit log and refuse to sign off on a false claim of neutrality;
5. *Cognitive ergonomics and natural frequencies against alert fatigue (Gigerenzer and Hoffrage 1995; Wong et al. 2021; Zheng and Meister 2025):* Finally, institutional accountability collapses if frontline human operators are cognitively overwhelmed. Jieyu Zheng and Markus Meister (2025) demonstrated that conscious human deliberation operates under a biological throughput bottleneck of roughly $10\text{ bits/s}$. When commercial clinical algorithms flood wards with low-specificity alarms—as Andrew Wong et al. (2021) documented in showing that the widely deployed Epic Sepsis Model generated an $88\%$ false discovery rate—operators suffer alert fatigue and disable or ignore the channel. Stochology requires filtering automated alerts to respect the $10\text{ bits/s}$ deliberative ceiling and communicating residual uncertainty in **natural frequencies** (Gigerenzer and Hoffrage 1995; e.g., *"out of $1,000$ patients in this ward, $20$ develop sepsis; the filtered alert triggers on $28$ patients, of whom $16$ are true cases and $12$ are false alarms"*).

## **VII. Practical Resonances Across Multiple Methods and Reflexive Self-Application**

### **7.1. Stochology as a Transversal Grammar: Dialogue with Five Methodological Families**

Because it is bound neither to a single software package nor to a single disciplinary canon, Stochology functions as a **transversal grammar** across fields that rarely converse. Whether an investigator works with demographic registries, clinical trials, infrastructure networks, machine-learning classifiers, or historical archives, inquiry traverses the same mediation circuit between world, record, and institution. Table 3 and the five syntheses below summarize how the Nuclear Triad transforms practice across five major methodological families:

1. *Survey sampling, demography, censuses, and administrative Big Data:*
   - *Habitual trap:* Assuming that millions of digital records eliminate sampling error while ignoring non-random exclusion and cluster correlation.
   - *Stochological transformation:* Applying Keynes's (1921) evidential weight $V$ and Meng's (2018) identity $\bar{Y}_n - \bar{Y}_N = \rho_{R,Y}\sqrt{(1-f)/f}\,\sigma_Y$, the analyst discounts group echoing via Campbell's (1977) and Moulton's (1986) $\mathrm{DEFF}$, audits weight concentration via Kish's (1965) $n_{\mathrm{Kish}}$ and uncounted strata via Chao's (1987) $\hat{n}_{00}$, and replaces false point precision with Manski's (2003, 2013) partial-identification bounds;
2. *Experimental, quasi-experimental, clinical, and public-policy evaluation:*
   - *Habitual trap:* Optional stopping at convenient $p$-values, ignoring selective dropout (LaLonde 1986), and extrapolating pilot results to population scale without modeling interference or strategic reaction.
   - *Stochological transformation:* Pre-registering protocols in a bitemporal vault (`TemporalVault`), monitoring sequential evidence via anytime-valid *e*-processes (Shafer 2021; Grünwald, de Heide, and Koolen 2024; Ramdas et al. 2023) and *e*-BH control (Wang and Ramdas 2022), applying the Mayo–Manski severe test to Lee's (2009) attrition bounds, and auditing spillover (Hudgens and Halloran 2008) and performative feedback (Lucas 1976; Perdomo et al. 2020);
3. *Operations research, infrastructure planning, ecology, and extreme-risk management:*
   - *Habitual trap:* Sizing hospitals, power grids, supply chains, or ecosystems for the historical mean and liquidating capacity headroom as "idle cost."

4. *Predictive modeling, machine learning, and algorithmic governance:*
   - *Habitual trap:* Deploying black-box classifiers evaluated solely by static in-sample accuracy, inducing self-fulfilling prophecies, concealing disparate error burdens, and flooding operators with noisy alerts.

5. *Qualitative, historiographical, clinical, legal, and ethnographic methods:*
   - *Habitual trap:* Treating methodological rigor under uncertainty as a monopoly of quantitative calculation, or conversely constructing elastic narratives immune to refutation.
   - *Stochological transformation:* Qualitative and interpretive methods stand as co-equal guardians of the mediation circuit across all three transits:

### **7.2. Reflexive Self-Application: Goodhart Brakes, Empirical Falsifiers, and Meta-Theoretical Limits**

An essay that diagnoses how theories insulate themselves and how institutional indicators degenerate under control pressure would fail its own standard if it presented Stochology as immune to perversion. Two reflexive questions must therefore be answered explicitly: what happens if an organization turns the three safeguards of Stochology itself into isolated bureaucratic targets under Goodhart's Law? And what observable phenomena would empirically falsify the core claims of this program?

First, each axis of the Nuclear Triad degenerates if maximized as a raw one-sided quantity detached from the other two, and each possesses an internal mathematical and institutional brake already embedded in the definition of $(S_{\mathrm{epi}}, S_{\mathrm{vital}}, S_{\mathrm{gov}})$:

1. *Degeneration of the Epistemic Margin — Skeptical Inflation and the Alibi of Indecision:* If an agency rewards analysts solely for nominal interval coverage ($\mathbf{1}\{S_t \in [L_t, U_t]\}$), analysts can game the target by widening $[L_t, U_t]$ to the trivial support $[y_{\min}, y_{\max}]$ (predicting that demand will lie "between zero and infinity"). Such an interval is never empirically falsified, yet conveys zero information and shields officials from action. **Reflexive Brake 1:** Under the Murphy (1973), Gneiting, Balabdaoui, and Raftery (2007), and Dimitriadis, Gneiting, and Jordan (2021) CORP decomposition, calibration ($\mathrm{REL}_{\mathrm{CORP}} \to 0$) is inseparable from **discriminative resolution** ($\mathrm{RES}_{\mathrm{CORP}} > 0$), and $S_{\mathrm{epi}}$ penalizes uninformative width via $1 - ((U - L)/(y_{\max} - y_{\min}))^2$. Lazy skeptical inflation drives $\mathrm{RES}_{\mathrm{CORP}} = 0$ and collapses $S_{\mathrm{epi}} \to 0$;
2. *Degeneration of the Vital Margin — Regressive Hoarding and Bureaucratic Self-Preservation:* If reserve accumulation is turned into an unconditional target blind to present deprivation, a ministry or corporation may hoard liquidity or withhold urgent relief from starving households under the pretext of "protecting the future buffer." **Reflexive Brake 2:** In Scarf's (1960) inventory theory and Samuelson's (1971, 1979) intertemporal welfare analysis, every precautionary buffer $R$ is bounded above by **marginal social opportunity cost** $c_{\mathrm{urgent}}$. Modeling net social welfare as $W(R) = \kappa \ln(1 + R) - c_{\mathrm{urgent}} R$, welfare peaks at the finite optimum $R^* = \max(0, \kappa / c_{\mathrm{urgent}} - 1)$ and declines strictly under regressive hoarding ($R \gg R^*$);
3. *Degeneration of Institutional Governance — Procedural Theater and Paralysis in Emergency:* If pre-registration, collegiate deliberation, and documentary auditing become a slow ritual ($\tau_{\mathrm{bureaucratic}} > \mathrm{TTS}$), an institution facing an acute flood, sepsis shock, or grid collapse will miss the critical instant of intervention (*kairós*) while completing paperwork. **Reflexive Brake 3:** Governance is lexicographically subordinated to the physical survival clock ($\mathrm{TTS} \ge \mathrm{TTR}$, Simchi-Levi, Schmidt, and Wei 2014). Whenever $\tau_{\mathrm{bureaucratic}} > \mathrm{TTS}$, Sosa's (2015) meta-aptness mandates triggering a pre-committed emergency fast-path ($\tau_{\mathrm{fast}} \le \mathrm{TTS}$) that releases vital buffers immediately, deferring forensic auditing until survival is secured.

Second, honoring Karl Popper's (1934/2002) demarcation criterion and Deborah Mayo's (2018) severity principle requires distinguishing **mathematical identities** (such as Meng's decomposition or Jensen's inequality, which are deductively proved theorems and therefore cannot serve as empirical falsifiers) from the **contingent empirical claims** of the Stochological program. The substantive empirical theses of this essay would be refuted if any of the following three observable regularities were demonstrated out of sample:

- **Empirical Falsifier 1 (on the plane of the archive and policy transport):** The claim that partial-identification bounds and effective-sample-size adjustments ($S_{\mathrm{epi}}$) are necessary for reliable policy design would be empirically refuted if, across multi-site observational registries and policy evaluations with documented non-random missingness ($\rho_{R,Y} \ne 0$) and cluster correlation ($\rho_{\mathrm{intra}} > 0$), sizing interventions by unadjusted point means consistently achieved out-of-sample coverage and decision regret equal to or better than Manski–Lee and Moulton-adjusted bounds;
- **Empirical Falsifier 2 (on the plane of operations and irreversible time):** The claim that vital slack and epistemic Kelly shrinkage ($S_{\mathrm{vital}}$) are necessary for long-run survival under heavy-tailed shocks would be empirically refuted if longitudinal cohorts of service infrastructures or leveraged reserves operating at lean utilization ($\rho > 0.92$) and unshrunk ensemble-mean exposure under heavy tails ($\hat{\alpha} \le 2$) exhibited equal or lower rates of insolvency, queue collapse, and cumulative social harm over multi-year horizons than systems maintaining Kingman/Scarf headroom and $\mathrm{TTS} \ge \mathrm{TTR}$ buffers;
- **Empirical Falsifier 3 (on the plane of institutional governance):** The claim that symmetric governance ($S_{\mathrm{gov}}$) is required to prevent Collapse Proposition 3 would be empirically refuted if complex multitask institutions governed solely by high-powered single-metric rankings and limited-liability bonus contracts without clawbacks or independent $S3^*$ algedonic channels sustained long-run causal outcome quality without inducing multitask care erosion, performative gaming, or delayed crisis recognition.

Distinct from these three empirical falsifiers are two **constitutive and meta-theoretical commitments**:
- *Constitutive Methodological-Normative Commitment (Public Memory of Failed Conjectures):* Stochology's requirement to separate hypothesis formulation ($t_1$) from confirmatory testing ($t_2$) and to maintain an unedited public registry of failed predictions (`TemporalVault`) is not an empirical hypothesis about nature; it is a **constitutive ethical-methodological norm** that defines intellectual rectitude and prevents degenerating *ad hoc* problemshifts;
- *Positional Reflexivity, Boundary of Failure, and Openness of the Seven-Tension Topology:* Following Pierre Bourdieu's (2001) call for *participant objectivation*, Stochology must also acknowledge its own social and operational limits. Sociologically, if formal tools such as partial-identification bounds or algorithmic audits are monopolized by technocratic elites as badges of scholastic authority detached from the testimony of affected citizens, Stochology itself risks becoming an instrument of bureaucratic legitimacy. Operationally, Stochology encounters a concrete **boundary of failure** in regimes of *radical scarcity and total archival erasure*: when missingness $p_{\mathrm{miss}} \to 1$ renders $[L_M, U_M]$ nearly as wide as $[y_{\min}, y_{\max}]$ while a community's current reserves already lie below subsistence, formal bounds cannot manufacture resources out of nothing, and unbridled conservatism would collapse into fatal inaction unless overridden by Reflexive Brakes 2 and 3. Finally, the heuristic topology of three filters and seven tensions makes no claim to metaphysical closure: it remains open to revision whenever historical or empirical inquiry identifies a structural failure of mediation between world, archive, and institution that escapes all seven locks, or shows that two locks are empirically redundant.

## **VIII. Conclusion: The Seven Tensions and the Integrity of Aim**

Throughout this programmatic essay, we have articulated **Stochology** as an integrative framework and transversal grammar open to specialists across the humanities, law, social sciences, medicine, ecology, engineering, and statistical sciences. Starting from the classical Greek distinction between exact mechanical measurement (*métron*) and conjectural aiming through opacity (*stókhos*, *stokhastikē tékhnē*), we argued that responsible judgment when action must precede the outcome requires analyzing, as a single coupled loop, the passages across the **material-historical world**, the **archive and formal model**, and **institutional deliberation**.

Across the **three mediating filters and seven constitutive tensions**, we traced how each achievement of rationalization turns into a pathology when severed from its limits, and how the three safeguards of the Nuclear Triad—the **epistemic margin** ($S_{\mathrm{epi}}$), the **vital margin** ($S_{\mathrm{vital}}$), and **fully apt agency under institutional governance** ($S_{\mathrm{gov}}$)—cohere under the Liebig–Leontief minimum $M_{\mathrm{Leontief}} = \min(S_{\mathrm{epi}}, S_{\mathrm{vital}}, S_{\mathrm{gov}})$. Where the epistemic margin is suppressed, physical buffers are sized blindly around a biased point mirage (Collapse Proposition 1); where the vital margin is eliminated in the name of lean efficiency, complete diagnostic lucidity watches helplessly as heavy-tailed shocks breach the absorbing ruin barrier (Collapse Proposition 2); where symmetric governance, algedonic listening, and downside clawbacks are absent, epistemic doubt is weaponized as an agnotological alibi while common reserves are pillaged under moral hazard and metric gaming (Collapse Proposition 3). To declare without fabrication the width of what the archive leaves unknown, to preserve the material and temporal slack that living systems require to endure an unfaithful environment, and to answer in institutional solidarity for the impact of the arrow once released: such is the task of Stochology and the substantive meaning of the ethics of aim.

## **Glossary of Classical and Technical Terms**

- **Ἀκρίβεια (*akríbeia*):** Exactness or precision grounded in fixed mechanical measurement, number, and weighing (*métron*, *arithmós*, *stathmós*), contrasted by Plato (*Philebus* 55e–56c) with the conjectural rigor of *stokhasmós*.
- **Καιρός (*kairós*):** The fleeting, critical instant of acute crisis or opportunity in clinical medicine, navigation, and strategy when decisive action cannot be postponed beyond the physical survival window ($\mathrm{TTS}$).
- **Καθῆκον (*kathēkon*):** In Stoic ethics (developed especially by Panaetius of Rhodes; Cicero, *De Officiis* I.107–121), an "appropriate action" or incumbency suited to one's roles (*personae*) and circumstances.
- **Μῆτις (*mētis*):** Plastic, vigilant, and adaptive practical intelligence capable of navigating shifting and ambiguous terrains (Detienne and Vernant 1974).
- **Non-Identical (*das Nichtidentische*):** In Theodor W. Adorno's (1966/2009) *Negative Dialectics*, the singular, historical, and material excess of an object that resists exhaustion by any classificatory concept or statistical summary.
- **Προηγμένα (*proēgmena*):** In Stoic ethics, "preferred indifferents" (*producta* / *seligenda* in Cicero, *De Finibus* III.22)—external outcomes such as health or hitting the target (*skopós*) that a rational agent selects without equating them with moral virtue (*télos*).
- **Σημεῖα (*sēmeîa*):** Observable clinical symptoms, clues, and diagnostic traces theorized in the Hippocratic *Prognostic* and *On the Art* and in Galenic medicine (alongside conclusive signs, *tekmēria*).
- **Σκοπός (*skopós*):** The watched external target or mark (connected to *skopeîn*, "to look at," rendered in Latin as *scopus* or *propositum*), distinguished in Antipater of Tarsus's Stoic ethics from the internal end (*télos*).
- **Στόχος (*stókhos*), στοχάζεσθαι (*stokházesthai*), στοχαστικὴ τέχνη (*stokhastikē tékhnē*):** *Stókhos* originally denoted the narrow upright stake set up as an aiming mark, and by extension conjecture or aim (Latin *coniectura*); from it derive the verb *stokházesthai* (to aim at a mark, to conjecture), the noun *stokhastēs* (conjecturer), and the Galenic category *stokhastikē tékhnē* (conjectural art), which Jacob Bernoulli (1713/2006) revived as *ars conjectandi sive stochastice*.
- **Τέλος (*télos*):** In Antipater of Tarsus's archer analogy (Stobaeus, *Eclogae* II.76.3–15), the internal consummate end of doing everything within one's craft to aim rightly, as distinct from the external outcome of striking the *skopós*.
- **Τόξον (*tóxon*) and τοξότης (*toxótēs*):** The bow (*tóxon*) and the archer (*toxótēs*), an etymological family distinct from *stókhos* that supplies the classical figure of the archer aiming under crosswinds.
