# **The Ethics of Aim: Conjecture, Construction, and Rupture in the Knowledge of the Real**

*(Foundations of a Stochology)*

## **Abstract**

This programmatic essay presents the theoretical and methodological foundations of **Stochology**, an integrative framework and transversal disciplinary program that investigates the validity, formal limits, and institutional responsibility of human judgment under structural uncertainty. Knowing and deliberating when action must precede the outcome requires analyzing a single coupled circuit across three planes: the material-historical world, the documentary archive and formal model, and institutional deliberation. Combining an immanent critique of methodological inversions with abductive triangulation across statistical, physical-ecological, and ethical-sociological lineages, the essay maps three mediating filters and seven constitutive tensions where analytical instruments generate specific pathologies when treated as self-sufficient. From this traversal emerge three safeguards that are non-substitutable at their critical survival thresholds—the **epistemic margin** ($S_{\mathrmmathrm{epi}}$), the **vital margin** ($S_{\mathrm{vital}}$), and **fully apt agency under institutional governance** ($S_{\mathrm{gov}}$)—whose joint threshold viability obeys a Liebig–Leontief minimum,

$$M_{\mathrm{Leontief}} = \min\bigl(S_{\mathrm{epi}},\, S_{\mathrm{vital}},\, S_{\mathrm{gov}}\bigr),$$

articulated through three structural collapse propositions, tested in a reproducible sequential simulation, and subjected to internal Goodhart brakes and pre-registered empirical falsifiers.

**Keywords:** Stochology; ethics of aim; immanent critique; partial identification; non-ergodicity; performativity; institutional responsibility; Liebig–Leontief complementarity.

*Note on Provenance and Notation:* Mathematical expressions use standard LaTeX/KaTeX notation. ## **I. Introduction: The Genealogy of the Shot and the Program of Stochology**

Technical, linguistic, and institutional mediators carve up reality long before it reaches human deliberation. Economic indicators, forensic reports, medical records, demographic censuses, historical chronicles, and digital databases never offer events in the raw. As the history and sociology of quantification have shown (Porter 1995; Desrosières 1998; Bowker and Star 1999), such instruments deliver a world already formatted by administrative conventions of equivalence (*conventions d'équivalence*) and classification infrastructures designed to make certain ways of seeing, comparing, and governing possible. Knowing and deciding are therefore never equivalent to the serene contemplation of a motionless landscape. They unfold as a permanent friction between the administrative effort to order experience into concepts and the material resistance of historical and biological life. Whenever an agent must act before the course of events is fully revealed—as happens to the clinician facing an acute syndrome, the magistrate weighing incomplete evidence, the planner managing critical infrastructure, or the historian confronting a mutilated archive—the decisive question ceases to be the raw volume of accumulated records. The central problem becomes the cognitive and moral integrity of judgment while the outcome of action remains open.

The history of Western thought preserved the trace of this problem in the etymology of the word *stochastic*. When Jacob Bernoulli (1713/2006), in Part IV, Chapter 2 of his posthumous *Ars Conjectandi*, sought a name for the art of measuring the probability of uncertain things, he defined it as *ars conjectandi sive stochastice*—the art of conjecturing, or stochastics. In doing so, Bernoulli drew upon a Greek semantic family bound to the practical experience of aiming at a mark through opacity. In classical Greek, the noun **στόχος (*stókhos*)** primarily denoted an aim, shot, conjectural guess, or target-mark—a cognitive projection that Latin rendered as *coniectura* (with part of the lexical tradition also associating *stókhos* concretely with an upright aiming mark or pillar, though standard lexica treat "aim, mark, conjecture" as the primary attested senses). From *stókhos* derive the verb **στοχάζεσθαι (*stokházesthai*)**—to aim at a mark, to endeavor to hit or divine—and the agent noun **στοχαστής (*stokhastēs*)**, the conjecturer or interpreter of signs. Philological precision requires distinguishing this root both from the noun for the archer (**τοξότης, *toxótēs***), which derives from a separate root meaning the bow (**τόξον, *tóxon***), and from the noun **σκοπός (*skopós*)**, the watched target or lookout (connected to *skopeîn*, "to behold," borrowed into Latin as *scopus* alongside the native Latin equivalents ***meta*** and ***propositum***, whereas native Latin *signum* corresponds instead to Greek **σημεῖον, *sēmeîon***, "sign"). What bridges the lexical field of *stókhos* directly to the classical figure of the archer (*toxótēs*) is the compound **εὐστοχία (*eustokhía*)** and its verb **εὐστοχεῖν (*eustokheîn*)**: "good aim" or skill in hitting the mark across an uncertain medium. Whereas *skopós* names the external object under watch, *stókhos* and *eustokhía* name the disciplined orientation of judgment across opacity—which is why this program takes the name *Stochology* while engaging the Stoic ethical distinction between *télos* and *skopós* (Section 6.1).

Centuries before the mathematical calculus of probabilities, Plato granted *stokházesthai* a central place in the classification of human knowledge. In the *Philebus* (55e–56c), examining the structure of the arts and crafts (**τέχναι, *tékhnai***), Plato divides them not by the ontological turbulence of their matter, but by an epistemic criterion: the degree of exactness (**ἀκρίβεια, *akríbeia***) afforded by measurement, number, and weighing (*métron*, *arithmós*, *stathmós*). On one side stand the arts supported by the rule, the lathe, the compass, the plumb line, and the scale, whose paradigm is carpentry and building construction. On the other side stand the arts that cannot rely on a mechanical gauge and must operate through practiced conjecture and aim (**στοχασμός, *stokhasmós***). The first example invoked by Plato (56a) is not a game of chance, but music, which attunes consonance "not by measure, but by the conjectural aim of practiced study and exercise" (*ou métrōi allà melétēs stokhasmōi*, where **μελέτη, *melétē*** denotes disciplined practice or exercise); it is followed by clinical medicine, agriculture, maritime navigation, and military command.

Whether such conjectural practices truly deserved the status of a rational art (*tékhnē*) rather than mere knack or routine (*empeiría*, as Plato himself pressed against rhetoric in the *Gorgias* 463a–465a) became one of the defining controversies of ancient epistemology and medicine. In the fifth century BCE, treatises of the *Corpus Hippocraticum* approached this terrain from three angles: *On Ancient Medicine* (*De prisca medicina*, ch. 9; Hippocrates 1923) maintained that the physician, confronting the individual constitution of each sick body, holds no exact arithmetic measure of diet beyond the "sensation of the body" (*toû sṓmatos tḕn aísthēsin*) and must aim (*stokhásasthai*) without fixed weights; the opening sentence of the *Aphorisms* (*Aphorismi* I.1; Hippocrates 1923) anchored clinical action in the fleeting critical instant (**καιρός, *kairós***: *ho bíos brakhýs, hē dè tékhnē makrē, ho dè kairòs oxýs*—"life is short, the art long, the critical instant acute"); and the semiotic treatises *Prognostic* (*Prognosticon*) and *On the Art* (*De arte*) codified how the clinician reads observable symptoms and diagnostic signs (**σημεῖα, *sēmeîa***; **τεκμήρια, *tekmēria***). In the Hellenistic and Roman periods, debates among medical and philosophical schools—systematized in the Aristotelian commentator tradition by Alexander of Aphrodisias (*In Aristotelis Topicorum libros octo commentaria*) and in medicine by Galen of Pergamon (1985, 2000; *De sectis*; *Ars medica*)—crystallized the technical category of **στοχαστικὴ τέχνη (*stokhastikē tékhnē*)**: a "conjectural art" in which method is fully rational and accountable to criticism even though material success remains exposed to external contingencies beyond the practitioner's mastery. Alongside this scholastic medical tradition, modern classical scholarship led by Marcel Detienne and Jean-Pierre Vernant (1974) reconstructed the broader archaic and classical figure of **μῆτις (*mētis*)**—the plastic, vigilant intelligence of the pilot and clinician who navigate shifting currents at the acute *kairós*.

The scope of this genealogy must be stated without equivocation. As historians of science and philosophy have emphasized (Sambursky 1956; Hacking 1975), Plato, the Hippocratic authors, Alexander of Aphrodisias, and Galen were theorizing qualitative diagnostic prudence rather than a quantitative calculus of relative frequencies, which emerged only in the seventeenth century. Evoking *stokhastikē tékhnē*, *eustokhía*, *sēmeîa*, and *mētis* here is neither an anachronistic projection of modern statistics onto Antiquity nor an appeal to classical authority; etymology licenses no mathematical theorem. Rather, this heuristic genealogy recalls that the problem of judging methodically without possessing certainty precedes modern mathematization and was not dissolved by it. When modernity narrowed the art of aim (*stókhos*) into the formal calculus of stochastic processes, it gained extraordinary analytical power, yet frequently bracketed the epistemological and ethical question that originally animated the conjectural arts: what does it mean to aim justly when the map is incomplete and another person will bear the impact of the arrow?

### **1.1. Definition of Stochology, the Three-Plane Circuit, and Theoretical Demarcation**

To address this problem in the age of formal models and bureaucratic institutions, **Stochology** (from *stókhos*, aim or conjecture, and *lógos*, reasoned account or critical study) is formulated here as an integrative framework and transversal disciplinary program that investigates the conditions of validity, formal limits, and institutional responsibility of judgment under structural uncertainty.

What justifies organizing this inquiry as a distinct integrative framework rather than leaving its parts dispersed across statistics, decision theory, and moral philosophy? A transversal program is defined not by a monopoly over a single technical tool, but by the delimitation of a unit of analysis that specialized fields habitually sever into non-communicating fragments. Whereas statistical methodology examines the mapping from phenomena to data, decision theory analyzes the mapping from probability distributions to rational choice, and sociology or law studies the impact of rules on society, Stochology takes as its indivisible unit of analysis the complete circuit of mediation across three planes:

1. *The plane of the material and historical world:* the terrain where events unfold in irreversible time, marked by extreme shocks, interdependencies among individuals, unequal social visibility, and human agents who react strategically when observed, classified, or governed;
2. *The plane of representation, model, and archive:* the documentary and symbolic space—whether a judicial file, a historical archive, a sample survey, or an algorithmic model—in which the world is compressed into classifications, tables, and equations subject to structural discrepancy and selective omissions;
3. *The plane of deliberation and institutions:* the practical sphere in which individuals, committees, or organizations act upon those imperfect representations, allocating scarce reserves, imposing norms, and distributing across social groups the benefits of success and the burdens of error.

The central thesis of Stochology is that grave failures of judgment under uncertainty rarely originate inside a single isolated plane; they originate in the unguarded passages from one plane to another. Three direct transits drive institutional action. First, in the passage from the world to the archive, vulnerable populations and structural dependencies are lost through selective non-recording and cluster echoing. Second, in the passage from the archive to the decision, simplifying point summaries (such as the lone arithmetic mean) conceal convex tail hazards and overwhelm human deliberative capacity. Third, in the passage from the decision back to the world, the intervention itself alters strategic behavior and consumes finite physical and temporal buffers. Watching over these three direct transits requires three corresponding reflexive locks: requiring the model to declare its causal and structural premises before fitting numbers; requiring the institution to seal its hypotheses, failed attempts, and error-liability rules before consulting the archive; and requiring the temporal scale of evidence and auditing to respect the physical survival clock of the system under governance.

Situating Stochology with precision requires demarcating how it connects and restructures three major twentieth- and twenty-first-century traditions on uncertainty:

- *Ambiguity, partial identification, and the separation of description from prescription:* In 1921, Frank Knight (1921) and John Maynard Keynes (1921) independently warned against reducing all epistemic doubt to a single numerical probability, distinguishing measurable *risk* from unquantifiable *uncertainty* and separating the balance of probability from the *weight of argument* ($V$). Four decades later, Daniel Ellsberg's (1961) two-urn thought experiments showed that reasonable decision-makers distinguish known proportions (risk) from unknown proportions (ambiguity) in ways that systematically violate Leonard J. Savage's (1954) *Sure-Thing Principle*—a system of subjective expected utility (SEU) that Savage himself restricted to logically closed "small worlds" (a logical completeness assumption distinct from physical *ergodicity*, examined in Section 5.2). To operate when a unique prior is untenable, Peter Walley (1991) developed imprecise probabilities, Peter J. Huber (1964, 1981) founded robust statistics, and Charles Manski (2003, 2013) established *partial identification* in econometrics. Crucially, Stochology enforces a strict boundary that applied work often blurs: partial identification is a *descriptive* epistemic discipline (determining the sharp identification region $\Theta_I$ compatible with the observed data and maintained assumptions), whereas choosing an action given $\Theta_I$ requires an explicit *prescriptive* decision rule.
- *Taxonomy of decision rules and lexicographic survival constraints:* In decision theory and engineering, distinct prescriptive rules under ambiguity are frequently conflated under the generic label of "robustness." When Yakov Ben-Haim (2006) proposed *Info-Gap* theory—maximizing local immunity to error around a nominal reference model—Moshe Sniedovich (2007, 2012) proved that if the reference model is misspecified, local robustness creates a "No-Man's Land" of policies that collapse under global deviations. Replacing local robustness requires distinguishing four non-equivalent global criteria:
  1. Savage's (1954) *Subjective Expected Utility* (SEU), which averages over $\Theta_I$ with a single subjective prior;
  2. Itzhak Gilboa and David Schmeidler's (1989) *Maxmin Expected Utility* (MEU), axiomatized via uncertainty aversion, certainty-independence, and weak order, which maximizes minimum expected utility $\min_{P \in \mathcal{P}} \mathbb{E}_P[u(a, \theta)]$ over a closed convex credal set of priors $\mathcal{P}$;
  3. Abraham Wald's (1945) *Minimax Risk* (or *Maximin Utility*, $\max_a \min_{\theta \in \Theta_I} u(a, \theta)$), which evaluates the worst *point state* $\theta \in \Theta_I$ (coinciding with MEU when $\mathcal{P}$ contains all distributions supported on $\Theta_I$, but differing whenever $\mathcal{P}$ restricts prior shapes or state-space dependencies); and
  4. Savage's (1951) and Manski's (2003, 2013) *Minimax Regret*, which minimizes maximum opportunity loss $\max_{\theta \in \Theta_I}[\max_{a'} u(a', \theta) - u(a, \theta)]$ relative to the ex-post optimal act.

  Neither Wald's maximin nor Manski's minimax regret guarantees a physical survival floor by itself: if every action leads to ruin in the worst state, Wald's criterion merely selects the least bad ruin, while in a binary treatment choice where the identified interval $[L_M, U_M]$ straddles the per-unit treatment cost $C$ ($L_M < C < U_M$), maximin dictates universal abstention whereas minimax regret recommends fractional treatment $a^* = (U_M - C)/(U_M - L_M)$ `[Identity]`. Stochology resolves this by subordinating optimization to a *lexicographic safety-first constraint* (Roy 1952; Simchi-Levi, Schmidt, and Wei 2014): actions that violate the physical survival condition ($\text{Time-to-Survive} \ge \text{Time-to-Recover}$, or ruin probability above a hard floor $\epsilon$) are filtered out first; within the survival-admissible set, residual ambiguity is adjudicated by minimax regret for reversible allocations and by worst-case bounds when irreversible thresholds are at stake (detailed with comparative statics in Section 4.2, Table 3).
- *The integrative contribution of Stochology:* Each of these traditions solved a decisive local problem: Manski addressed archival missingness; Wald, Savage, and Gilboa–Schmeidler formalized choice under ambiguity; Roy, Kelly, and Simchi-Levi addressed survival over time; Callon, MacKenzie, and Goodhart analyzed performative reactivity; Sosa, Scanlon, and Hansson theorized epistemic virtue and the ethics of risk. Applied in isolation, however, each safeguard remains vulnerable to failure on the adjacent planes. Computing an unimpeachable partial-identification interval on the plane of the archive does not prevent ruin if operations run at $96\text{--}100\%$ `[Assumed]` capacity on the temporal plane, or if limited-liability contracts on the institutional plane reward executives for liquidating safety buffers. The specific object of Stochology is the structural interdependence of these three planes.

### **1.2. The Nuclear Triad, Liebig–Leontief Complementarity, and the Three Collapse Propositions**

To keep the mediation circuit intact across world, archive, and institution, Stochology maintains that responsible judgment under structural uncertainty requires the simultaneous presence of three safeguards:

1. **The epistemic margin (on the plane of knowledge and the archive):** the methodological and ethical duty to refuse false point exactness. Whenever archives suffer from selective non-recording, clustering, or structural model discrepancy, the analyst must declare the sharp partial-identification interval $[L_t, U_t]$ compatible with the evidence (Manski 2003; Imbens and Manski 2004; Lee 2009), the structural discrepancy between simulator and reality (Kennedy and O'Hagan 2001), the discounted effective sample size (Kish 1965; Moulton 1986; Meng 2018), and the antidogmatic reserve required to learn from refutation (Lindley 1985);
2. **The vital margin (on the material and temporal plane):** the operational duty to preserve physical, biological, financial, and temporal slack—across both *operational buffer stocks* and *structural attraction-basin width*—against absorbing barriers and hysteretic tipping points. It requires maintaining capacity headroom ($1 - \rho_t$) against heavy-traffic queue explosions (Kingman 1961; Scarf 1960), shrinking multiplicative exposure under parameter uncertainty so that volatility drain does not trigger bankruptcy (Itô 1951; Kelly 1956; Baker and McHale 2013), ensuring that local Time-to-Survive exceeds Time-to-Recover ($\mathrm{TTS}_t \ge \mathrm{TTR}_t$, Simchi-Levi, Schmidt, and Wei 2014), and preserving biological normativity and ecological basin depth (Canguilhem 1943/2009; Holling 1973; Scheffer et al. 2009);
3. **Fully apt agency under institutional governance (on the ethical and political plane):** the constitutional duty to bind the act of judging to symmetry of risk and accountability. It requires translating the reflective competence to withhold an unsafe shot (Sosa 2015) into collective constitutional vetoes and independent audit channels (Beer 1972; List and Pettit 2011), eliminating testimonial credibility discounts against frontline alarms (Fricker 2007, 2010), protecting deliberative attention from alert flooding (Gigerenzer and Hoffrage 1995; Zheng and Meister 2025), and structuring contracts with downside clawbacks at the decision-making top, limited-liability protection at the vulnerable base, and public disclosure of error trade-offs (Jensen and Meckling 1976; Holmström 1979; Holmström and Milgrom 1991; Innes 1990; Chouldechova 2017).

*Upfront Normative License and Division of Normative Registers:* Because Sections III through V employ prescriptive language ("must," "duty," "obligation"), the normative architecture of the essay must be stated at the outset (and constructed in full in Section 6.1). Taken in isolation for a solitary individual whose actions affect no one else, the epistemic margin is an *epistemic norm of truth-tracking* (Clifford 1877; Adler 2002; Fantl and McGrath 2009) and the vital margin is a *prudential norm of self-preservation* (Roy 1952; Kelly 1956). In institutional governance, however, where decision-makers impose unbuffered tail risks and classification errors onto third parties who did not consent to bear them (Hansson 2013), an explicitly imported moral premise—Thomas M. Scanlon's (1998) contractualist principle of *non-rejectability* and *due care*—transforms all three safeguards into **strict moral obligations of interpersonal justice**: an affected citizen can reasonably reject any institutional principle that conceals archival ignorance or liquidates physical survival buffers merely to capture modest efficiency gains for others.

Why do these three safeguards aggregate at their critical boundary via a **Liebig–Leontief minimum** rather than an additive sum or a smooth Cobb–Douglas product? Two theoretical clarifications are required:
1. *Commensurability on the scale of threshold adequacy:* Raw interval coverage, physical queue headroom, and constitutional veto rules do not share a common physical unit. They become commensurable only when mapped onto normalized indices $S_i \in [0, 1]$ measuring *functional adequacy relative to each plane's critical failure threshold* $\tau \in (0, 1)$ (set to $\tau = 0.50$ `[Assumed]` in the reference benchmark).
2. *Serial non-compensability at the critical threshold ($S_i < \tau$) versus above-threshold trade-offs ($S_i \ge \tau$):* Well above the critical threshold ($S_{\mathrm{epi}}, S_{\mathrm{vital}}, S_{\mathrm{gov}} \gg \tau$), partial trade-offs exist at the margin—for instance, allocating slightly more precautionary stock versus immediate relief, governed by Reflexive Brake 2 (Section 7.2). At or below the critical boundary ($S_i < \tau$), however, the three planes form a **serial causal chain of viability**: an uncalibrated, downward-biased archive ($S_{\mathrm{epi}} \to 0$) causes the institution to miscalculate the baseline around which physical buffers are sized; zero physical slack ($S_{\mathrm{vital}} \to 0$) causes immediate ruin under a heavy-tailed shock even when the shock was accurately predicted; and a captured governance regime ($S_{\mathrm{gov}} \to 0$) actively liquidates the buffer and weaponizes epistemic uncertainty as an alibi for inaction. Like Justus von Liebig's Law of the Minimum in ecology and Wassily Leontief's fixed-proportions technology in economics, threshold viability is governed by the bottleneck safeguard:

$$M_{\mathrm{Leontief}}\bigl(S_{\mathrm{epi}},\, S_{\mathrm{vital}},\, S_{\mathrm{gov}}\bigr) = \min\bigl(S_{\mathrm{epi}},\, S_{\mathrm{vital}},\, S_{\mathrm{gov}}\bigr) \in [0, 1] \qquad \text{\texttt{[Assumed aggregator]}}$$

To test this architecture transparently without hiding simulation assumptions behind rhetorical claims of deductive necessity, the companion pure-Rust verification suite implements a sequential agent–world–institution reference environment (`CommunityReserveEnv`, deterministic `SplitMix64` stream with pre-registered `DEFAULT_SEED = 2026`, horizon $T = 24$ shifts, initial reserve $R_0 = 45.0$, demand support $[y_{\min}, y_{\max}] = [5.0, 65.0]$, and survival threshold $\tau = 0.50$ `[Assumed]`). In this reference environment, each safeguard index $S_i \in [0, 1]$ is defined by an explicit **`[Assumed]` operational specification** over trajectory observables:
- **Epistemic Margin Index ($S_{\mathrm{epi}}$, `[Assumed]` functional form):** empirical coverage of true latent community demand $S_t$ by the declared interval $[L_t, U_t]$, penalized quadratically by normalized interval width so that lazy $[y_{\min}, y_{\max}]$ inflation yields zero score (Reflexive Brake 1):
  $$S_{\mathrm{epi}} = \left(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} \mathbf{1}\bigl\{S_t \in [L_t, U_t]\bigr\}\right) \cdot \left[1 - \left(\frac{\overline{U - L}}{y_{\max} - y_{\min}}\right)^2\right]$$
- **Vital Margin Index ($S_{\mathrm{vital}}$, `[Assumed]` functional form uniting operational stock and dynamical basin stability):** mean product of four normalized survival factors—(i) operational Kingman headroom relative to the heavy-traffic knee $\rho_{\mathrm{crit}} = 0.88$ `[Assumed]` (where queueing delay $\rho/(1-\rho)$ exceeds $7.3\times$, whereas precautionary design targets $\rho \le 0.80$, i.e., $1 - \rho \ge 0.20$ `[Assumed]`), (ii) epistemic Kelly leverage safety relative to the overbetting ceiling $1.60$ `[Assumed]`, (iii) minimum reserve buffer commitment $B_{\min} = 10.0$ `[Assumed]`, and (iv) **structural attraction-basin stability** $S_{\mathrm{basin}, t} = \operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}\!\bigl(\frac{1 - \hat{\rho}_{1,t}}{1 - \rho_{1,\mathrm{crit}}}\bigr)$ tracking Scheffer's lag-1 autocorrelation $\hat{\rho}_{1,t} = e^{-\lambda_t \Delta t}$ against the Critical Slowing Down bifurcation threshold $\rho_{1,\mathrm{crit}} = 0.85$ `[Assumed]`:
  $$S_{\mathrm{vital}} = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} \underbrace{\operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}\!\left(\frac{1 - \rho_{\mathrm{base}, t}}{1 - \rho_{\mathrm{crit}}}\right) \cdot \operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}(1.60 - f_t) \cdot \operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}\!\left(\frac{B_t}{B_{\min}}\right)}_{S_{\mathrm{stock}, t}\text{ (operational stock slack)}} \cdot \underbrace{\operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}\!\left(\frac{1 - \hat{\rho}_{1, t}}{1 - \rho_{1, \mathrm{crit}}}\right)}_{S_{\mathrm{basin}, t}\text{ (basin resilience)}}$$
- **Institutional Governance Index ($S_{\mathrm{gov}}$, `[Assumed]` functional form):** conjunction of active constitutional circuit-breaker authority ($S3^*$), undiscounted reception of frontline algedonic signals ($1 - \delta_{\mathrm{cred}, t}$), and restraint of single-metric proxy pressure ($1 - \beta_{\mathrm{proxy}, t}$):
  $$S_{\mathrm{gov}} = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} \mathbf{1}\bigl\{\text{brake}_t\bigr\} \cdot \bigl(1 - \delta_{\mathrm{cred}, t}\bigr) \cdot \bigl(1 - \beta_{\mathrm{proxy}, t}\bigr)$$

Crucially, we must distinguish two epistemic levels in the **Three Collapse Propositions** below: first, the **qualitative structural mechanism** (which describes how unguarded dynamics on the suppressed plane subvert the remaining two safeguards), and second, its **parametric realization** in `CommunityReserveEnv` (`[Simulated]`, `DEFAULT_SEED = 2026`, reported in Table 1 alongside multi-seed sensitivity and negative-control checks):

- **Collapse Proposition 1 ($\neg\text{Epistemic Margin}: S_{\mathrm{epi}} < \tau \le \min(S_{\mathrm{vital}}, S_{\mathrm{gov}}) \implies \text{Blind Undersizing}$):**
  - *Structural mechanism:* When an institution enforces constitutional governance and commits to physical headroom rules ($S_{\mathrm{vital}}, S_{\mathrm{gov}} \ge \tau$), but suppresses the epistemic margin ($S_{\mathrm{epi}} < \tau$) by sizing capacity around a naive point mean $\bar{O}_t$ from an archive with non-random exclusion of vulnerable strata ($\rho_{R,Y} \ne 0$) and unadjusted intra-cluster correlation ($\mathrm{DEFF} > 1$), nominal standard errors shrink as $O(n^{-1/2})$ while selection bias $\bar{Y}_n - \bar{Y}_N = \rho_{R,Y}\sqrt{(1-f)/f}\,\sigma_Y$ `[Identity]` remains invariant to sample size. The vital buffer is therefore anchored to a downward-biased baseline, causing realized utilization $\rho_t = S_t / C_t$ and convex queue harm to spike during surges despite benign organizational intentions.
  - *Reference simulation (`[Simulated]`, `DEFAULT_SEED = 2026`, Table 1):* Under $60\%$ `[Assumed]` non-recording in the two high-need clusters ($c \in \{4, 5\}$), the epistemic-ablated policy (`NoEpistemicMargin`) mistakes raw $n = 46.5$ `[Simulated]` for $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$, suffers $0.0\%$ `[Simulated]` interval coverage ($< 25\%$ benchmark; $S_{\mathrm{epi}} = 0.000$), and more than doubles mean social harm from $2.23$ to $4.58$ `[Simulated]` (with peak crisis harm jumping from $2.46$ to $6.04$ `[Simulated]`).
- **Collapse Proposition 2 ($\neg\text{Vital Margin}: S_{\mathrm{vital}} < \tau \le \min(S_{\mathrm{epi}}, S_{\mathrm{gov}}) \implies \text{Impotent Lucidity Before Ruin}$):**
  - *Structural mechanism:* When technical staff compute valid Manski–Lee partial-identification intervals ($S_{\mathrm{epi}} \ge \tau$) and constitutional auditing is active ($S_{\mathrm{gov}} \ge \tau$), but "lean efficiency" eliminates physical and temporal slack ($S_{\mathrm{vital}} < \tau$), operating near unit utilization ($\rho_{\mathrm{base}} \to 1^-$) with minimal reserve buffers ($\mathrm{TTS}_t < \mathrm{TTR}_t$) and unshrunk leverage, a heavy-tailed surge ($\alpha < 2$)—even one explicitly contained inside the analysts' announced interval $[L_t, U_t]$—triggers hyperbolic Kingman congestion $\rho_t/(1-\rho_t)$ and Critical Slowing Down ($\hat{\rho}_{1,t} \to 1$), exhausting the reserve at the absorbing ruin barrier before governance can restore capacity.
  - *Reference simulation (`[Simulated]`, `DEFAULT_SEED = 2026`, Table 1):* Operating at $\rho_{\mathrm{base}} = 0.96$, buffer $B_t = 0.02 R_t$, and leverage $f_t = 1.55$ `[Assumed]`, the vital-ablated policy (`NoVitalMargin`) achieves $100.0\%$ `[Simulated]` epistemic coverage ($S_{\mathrm{epi}} = 0.879 > 0.85$), yet collapses to $S_{\mathrm{vital}} = 0.000$, suffers explosive queue harm ($99.80$ `[Simulated]`), and hits permanent ruin at shift $\tau_{\mathrm{ruin}} = 3$ (`terminal_reserve = 0.00` `[Simulated]`).
- **Collapse Proposition 3 ($\neg\text{Institutional Governance}: S_{\mathrm{gov}} < \tau \le \min(S_{\mathrm{epi}}, S_{\mathrm{vital}}) \implies \text{Cynical Capture and Pillaging of the Reserve}$):**
  - *Structural mechanism and four-stage sociological process:* When an organization possesses competent analysts ($S_{\mathrm{epi}} \ge \tau$) and nominal engineering buffers ($S_{\mathrm{vital}} \ge \tau$), but lacks symmetric institutional governance ($S_{\mathrm{gov}} < \tau$), the first two safeguards are subverted through a four-stage organizational sequence involving three distinct actors (executive leadership under short-term market or political ranking pressure, staff analysts/engineers, and frontline operators):
    1. *Commensuration and high-powered proxy imposition:* Leadership imposes a single auditable throughput metric (Strathern 1997; Espeland and Sauder 2007) backed by high-powered bonus incentives ($\beta_{\mathrm{proxy}} \to 1$, Holmström and Milgrom 1991) and limited-liability executive payoffs without downside clawbacks (Jensen and Meckling 1976; Innes 1990).
    2. *Agnotological weaponization of epistemic bounds:* Rather than using wide partial-identification bounds $[L_t, U_t]$ to trigger precautionary buffering, executives deploy the width of $[L_t, U_t]$ externally as a structural **agnotological alibi** (Proctor and Schiebinger 2008)—arguing that "scientific uncertainty remains too broad to justify costly protective intervention"—while disabling the internal conjunctive $S3^*$ veto (Beer 1972; List and Pettit 2011).

## **II. The Method: Immanent Critique, Abductive Triangulation, and the Heuristic Topology**

### **2.1. Immanent Critique, Reification, and the Operational Incidence of the Non-Identical**

Why do scientific and administrative institutions fall repeatedly into these three collapse modes? Stochology advances the hypothesis that systematic failures of judgment under uncertainty arise not merely from technical incompetence or individual malice, but from the internal dynamic of rationalization whenever a methodological instrument forgets the conditions that bound its validity. To analyze this dynamic, our method combines three distinct operations that must be named with transparency rather than conflated under a single label:
1. **Immanent critique** of four recurring methodological inversions (tracking how each analytical instrument defeats its own declared aim when absolutized);
2. **Abductive triangulation** across three convergent disciplinary lineages (Section 2.2); and
3. **Explicit normative reconstruction** via contractualist risk ethics (Section 6.1).

The first operation draws on the tradition of critical theory—specifically Georg Lukács's (1923/1971) analysis of **reification (*Verdinglichung*)**, Max Horkheimer's (1937/2002) demarcation between **traditional theory** (which treats the division of scientific labor and institutional categories as natural facts) and **critical theory** (which reflects on their historical production and social function), Max Horkheimer and Theodor W. Adorno's (1944/2002) diagnosis in *Dialectic of Enlightenment* of how instrumental formalization inverts into domination, and Adorno's (1966/1973) epistemology of the **Non-Identical (*das Nichtidentische*)** in *Negative Dialectics*.

In the history of inquiry and governance, reason repeatedly constructs a disciplining instrument to cure a real defect of unreflective practice; yet when that instrument is reified as self-sufficient, it generates a higher-order blindness by its own standards:
- To overcome arbitrary guesswork, inquiry demands *structured theory*; yet theory, insulated by auxiliary assumptions and institutional interests, begins to select confirming evidence and patch over anomalies;
- To overcome verbal vagueness, thought turns to *mathematical formalization*; yet formal models, seduced by analytical tractability, amputate qualitative transition zones, convex asymmetries, and heavy tails in favor of a docile average;
- To overcome the fragility of small samples, states and corporations build *monumental archives and digital registries*; yet archival volume fosters an illusion of omniscience while driving nominal error bands toward zero around populations whose most vulnerable members were systematically omitted;
- To become practically effective, knowledge translates itself into *rules of intervention and performance targets*; yet imposing a static indicator upon a reactive social and material world induces strategic gaming, erodes causal mechanisms, and consumes finite buffers in irreversible time.

Clarifying the philosophical status of this movement is essential to avoid a performative contradiction. Whereas Hegelian dialectics treats *determinate negation* (*Aufhebung*) as a synthetic engine culminating in a closed system, Adorno's (1966/1973) *Negative Dialectics* insists that conceptual identification is constitutive of thought and that the **Non-Identical**—the singular, historical, and material excess of the object—resists exhaustion by any concept, parameter, or closed table of categories. For Adorno, respecting the Non-Identical is not an anti-intellectual refusal of construction; it requires assembling **constellations** of concepts that expose where each isolated concept falls short of its object. If Stochology claimed that declaring a partial-identification interval $[L, U]$ "captures" the Non-Identical, or presented its three filters and seven tensions as a closed transcendental deduction, it would commit the very reification Lukács (1923/1971) and Adorno (1966/1973) diagnosed.

At the same time, if the Non-Identical were merely quarantined inside a separate "ontological register" with zero incidence on formal practice, it would be reduced to an inert Kantian *thing-in-itself* (*Ding an sich*). In Stochology, the Non-Identical is not captured by formalism, but **its resistance leaves three operational traces inside the formal apparatus itself**: first, in the width of Manski's partial-identification interval $[L_M, U_M]$ (which marks what the archive failed to record); second, in Marc Kennedy and Anthony O'Hagan's (2001) structural discrepancy function $\delta(x) = y(x) - f(x, \theta)$ (which forces predictive uncertainty to widen as action moves outside historical support); and third, in Bengt Holmström and Paul Milgrom's (1991) protection of unmeasured qualitative care against high-powered proxy metrics. The seven-tension topology summarized in Table 2 is therefore an open, revisable **institutional constellation** mapped onto the seven chronological junctures of the action loop—framing a question (Tension 1), testing a hypothesis over time (Tension 2), formalizing a summary measure (Tension 3), aggregating an archive (Tension 4), intervening in a reactive social field (Tension 5), compounding exposure across irreversible time (Tension 6), and assigning moral and contractual liability for the outcome (Tension 7). Note that while Tensions 1 through 6 each occupy a single subsection, Tension 7 is examined across two subsections (Sections 6.1 and 6.2) to separate the individual ethics of aim and moral luck (*First Face*) from collective corporate and constitutional design (*Second Face*).

### **2.2. Abductive Triangulation Across Three Convergent Lineages**

Across these seven junctures, Stochology braids together three distinct intellectual lineages whose convergence provides abductive support for the Nuclear Triad:

1. *The epistemological and statistical lineage (partial identification, severe testing, and model discrepancy):* articulating Adorno's (1966/1973) critique of identificatory closure with Deborah Mayo's (2018) severe-testing error statistics, Charles Manski's (2003, 2013) partial identification, Marc Kennedy and Anthony O'Hagan's (2001) Bayesian model discrepancy, and Xiao-Li Meng's (2018) analysis of archival selection defects, it establishes that the gap between representation and reality must remain visible as an *epistemic margin*;
2. *The material, thermodynamic, and ecological-sociotechnical lineage (irreversible time, resilience, and performativity):* uniting Nicholas Georgescu-Roegen's (1971) critique of mechanistic reversibility, Georges Canguilhem's (1943/2009) biological normativity, Crawford S. Holling's (1973) ecological resilience, John F. C. Kingman's (1961) heavy-traffic queueing theory, and the sociology of quantification and performativity (Porter 1995; Callon 1998; Desrosières 1998; Bowker and Star 1999; MacKenzie 2006), it establishes that every intervention acts upon a reactive, entropic medium, demanding a *vital margin*;
3. *The ethical, contractual, and political-institutional lineage (apt agency, risk imposition, and organizational constitution):* uniting Antipater of Tarsus's Stoic archer (Stobaeus, *Eclogae* II.76.9–15; Cicero, *De Finibus* III.22), Bernard Williams's (1981) and Thomas Nagel's (1979) analyses of moral luck, Ernest Sosa's (2007, 2015) virtue epistemology, Thomas M. ## **III. The First Filter: When Interest Conceals and Theory Insulates Itself**

### **3.1. Tension 1 — Situational Interest versus Intellectual Rectitude**

The first mediation occurs before any data are gathered or any equation is written. It resides in the social, economic, and institutional position occupied by the questioner: interest selects what deserves to be seen and conceals what is convenient to ignore. No researcher, magistrate, physician, public administrator, or corporate board deliberates outside society; all operate embedded in structures of funding, career incentives, bureaucratic prestige, and state or corporate power. When project approval, budget renewal, a firm's market valuation, or an administration's political survival depends on a given conclusion, a gravitational field inclines attention. Clues favoring the desired result are received with benevolence, while contrary signs are subjected to asymmetric skepticism or left outside the frame of inquiry.

The most pervasive form of this distortion rarely requires explicit data fabrication. Altering a primary document is a crude offense; far safer and more routine is shaping the prior architecture of inquiry. An institution adopts a narrow definition of the problem that excludes collateral externalities; slices a time series at a convenient base year; designs a safety study with a sample size too small to detect real harm; or runs twenty exploratory models behind closed doors and publishes only the single specification that crossed a nominal threshold.

This pathology must be analyzed across two distinct registers that should not be conflated: the macro-institutional register of **structural agnotology** (Proctor and Schiebinger 2008) and the micro-organizational register of **epistemic vices** and bureaucratic ritual (Frankfurt 2005; Cassam 2019). As Robert Proctor demonstrated in coining *agnotology*, ignorance is frequently not a passive vacuum awaiting scientific light, but an actively manufactured political-economic product—exemplified by tobacco, asbestos, and fossil-fuel campaigns that funded deliberately underpowered or narrowly framed studies to sustain the regulatory alibi that "the evidence remains inconclusive." Distinct from this organized macro-political manufacture of doubt is the dispositional and procedural pathology that Quassim Cassam (2019) terms **epistemic insouciance**—a routinized bureaucratic indifference to truth that generalizes Harry Frankfurt's (2005) diagnosis in *On Bullshit*. Whereas the liar and the industrial agnotologist track the truth strategically in order to conceal or delay its regulatory impact, the producer of bureaucratic *bullshit* severs commitment to factual reality altogether, caring only that the technical report satisfies formal ritual and projects institutional authority.

To prevent inquiry from being corrupted at its origin by structural agnotology, selective reporting, or framing asymmetry, Stochology establishes three methodological locks:

1. *Antidogmatic reserve (Cromwell's Rule):* In 1650, Oliver Cromwell addressed to the General Assembly of the Kirk of Scotland the plea: *"I beseech you, in the bowels of Christ, think it possible you may be mistaken."* Dennis Lindley (1985) formalized this warning as **Cromwell's Rule**: in probabilistic updating, assigning a prior probability of strictly $p = 0$ or $p = 1$ to any contingent empirical proposition destroys the capacity to learn from experience, since Bayes's rule leaves a zero or unit prior invariant regardless of how much contrary evidence accumulates. Intellectual rectitude requires clamping prior beliefs to the interior interval $[\epsilon, 1 - \epsilon]$ with $\epsilon > 0$ (`\epsilon = 10^{-4}` `[Assumed]` in the reference suite);
2. *Bitemporal pre-registration and the separation of invariant principle from contingent parameter:* Because institutional memory readily reinterprets the past to justify the present, the only reliable defense against post-hoc target painting (*Texas sharpshooter* bias) is bitemporal separation: sealing the hypothesis, the loss function, and the evaluation protocol at time $t_1$ before observing the outcome realization at time $t_2$. This temporal discipline rests on a distinction between *invariant methodological principles*—such as sealing rules before observing outcomes and disclosing all tested specifications—and *contingent domain parameters* (such as choosing a significance level $\alpha = 0.05$ or $\alpha = 0.01$ `[Assumed]`), which remain adjustable to context so long as they are fixed ex ante;
3. *Multiverse transparency, e-BH discovery control, and strong objectivity against Type III framing error:* When an analyst searches across $K$ plausible data-cleaning and modeling choices, reporting only the most favorable path inflates false positives dramatically. Sara Steegen et al. (2016) counter this via the **specification curve (*multiverse analysis*)**, evaluating stability across the entire combinatorics of defensible specifications, while Ruodu Wang and Aaditya Ramdas (2022) provide the **e-BH procedure**, which controls the false discovery rate under arbitrary dependence among tests. Yet even a reproducible calculation fails if it commits what Allyn Kimball (1957), Howard Raiffa (1968), and Ian Mitroff and Tom Featheringham (1974) termed **Type III Error**: giving a mathematically precise answer to the wrong framing of the problem. Two standard decision parameterizations must be distinguished here:
   - In the canonical operational **cost–loss model** (Murphy 1973; Richardson 2000), where preventive action incurs a certain protection cost $C > 0$ and eliminates an avoidable loss $L > C$, expected expense is minimized by acting whenever event probability exceeds $p_{\mathrm{CL}}^* = C / L$ `[Identity]`;
   - In the clinical and classification **expected-regret model** (Pauker and Kassirer 1975), where $C_{\mathrm{FP}} > 0$ denotes the net cost of a false alarm (acting when no event occurs) and $L_{\mathrm{FN}} > 0$ denotes the net loss of a missed event (failing to act when the event occurs), expected regret is minimized by acting whenever $p_{\mathrm{PK}}^* = C_{\mathrm{FP}} / (C_{\mathrm{FP}} + L_{\mathrm{FN}})$ `[Identity]` (implemented in `CostLossFraming`, where $C_{\mathrm{FP}} = 100$, institutional miss loss $L_{\mathrm{inst}} = 200 \implies p_{\mathrm{inst}}^* = 0.33$, and affected-citizen miss loss $L_{\mathrm{affected}} = 2{,}000 \implies p_{\mathrm{affected}}^* = 0.05$ `[Assumed]`).

   Under *either* parameterization, when an institution evaluates the threshold using only its own reputational or budgetary loss ($L_{\mathrm{inst}} \ll L_{\mathrm{affected}}$), it inflates the action threshold ($p_{\mathrm{inst}}^* \gg p_{\mathrm{affected}}^*$) and systematically under-protects exposed citizens. Overcoming Type III Error therefore requires integrating the **strong objectivity** of Helen Longino (1990) and Sandra Harding (1991) directly into the method: abandoning the bureaucratic "view from nowhere" by auditing problem definitions, statistical power, and loss parameters from the standpoint of the populations who bear the downside of error.

### **3.2. Tension 2 — Accommodating Confirmation versus Fruitful Refutation and the Mayo–Manski Bridge**

Even when immediate situational interests are constrained, a second tension arises from the logic of empirical testing itself: when observations contradict an established theory or policy model, the path of least resistance is to introduce *ad hoc* adjustments that rescue the model from refutation.

Since David Hume showed that no finite sequence of past observations deductively entails future regularities, Karl Popper (1934/2002) located scientific demarcation in *falsifiability*—the willingness to state in advance which observable outcomes would refute a conjecture. Yet Pierre Duhem (1906/1954) and Willard Van Orman Quine (1951) exposed the structural vulnerability of naive falsificationism (**the Duhem–Quine thesis**): because a core hypothesis $H$ generates testable predictions only when conjoined with a bundle of auxiliary assumptions $A_1 \wedge A_2 \wedge \dots \wedge A_m$ (instrument calibration, survey comprehension, absence of external confounders), an empirical failure $\neg O$ entails only $\neg(H \wedge A_1 \wedge \dots \wedge A_m)$ `[Identity]`, leaving the analyst free to deflect blame onto an auxiliary clause. Imre Lakatos (1970) resolved this ambiguity dynamically by evaluating *research programs* over time: a modification is **progressive** when the revised auxiliary assumptions predict novel, independently testable facts (as Le Verrier's postulation of Neptune did in 1846), and **degenerating** when exceptions are tailored solely to absorb counterexamples post hoc, shrinking the empirical content of the theory.

In contemporary data-intensive science, the temptation toward accommodating adjustment is amplified by optional stopping and post-hoc threshold selection. Deborah Mayo (2018) formulated the error-statistical antidote in the **severity principle (*severe testing*)**: data $x_0$ that agree with a claim $H$ count as genuine evidence for $H$ if and only if the test procedure had a high probability of detecting the falsity of $H$ had $H$ been false. Examining drinking water with the naked eye in a clear glass yields an outcome ("clear water") that agrees with the hypothesis of purity, yet has zero severity against a colorless toxin. Likewise, peeking at a sequential trial day after day and stopping as soon as a conventional $p$-value dips below $0.05$ destroys error control. Alongside Mayo's classical error-statistical school, a distinct game-theoretic paradigm has recently matured to guarantee anytime-valid sequential inference under optional stopping: Glenn Shafer's (2021) *testing by betting*, Peter Grünwald, Rianne de Heide, and Wouter Koolen's (2024) *safe testing* via growth-rate optimal (*GROW*) **e-values** (single-stage variables $E \ge 0$ satisfying $\mathbb{E}_{H_0}[E] \le 1$), and Aaditya Ramdas et al.'s (2023) **e-processes**—sequential nonnegative supermartingales $(E_t)_{t \ge 1}$ satisfying $\mathbb{E}_{H_0}[E_\tau] \le 1$ `[Identity]` at any arbitrary data-dependent stopping time $\tau$ via Ville's inequality.

At this point arises a central methodological question that Stochology addresses: how can Deborah Mayo's (2018) demand for severe, refutable testing be coordinated with Charles Manski's (2003, 2013) partial identification, which demonstrates that observational and attrition-ridden data rarely identify a single point parameter, yielding instead an interval $\Theta_I = [\theta_L, \theta_U]$?

Rather than claiming a new probability theorem, the **Mayo–Manski bridge** is an operational synthesis that enforces a strict division of labor between **descriptive identification**, **post-data error-statistical severity**, and **prescriptive choice**:

1. *Manski's descriptive moment (delimiting the sharp identification region $\Theta_I$):* In evaluating a clinical treatment or social policy (as in Robert LaLonde's (1986) classic comparison of experimental National Supported Work data against observational CPS controls, where in `nsw_experimental.csv` vs. `cps_observational.csv` the experimental ATE is $+\$1{,}794$ whereas the unadjusted observational difference is $-\$8{,}498$ `[Measured]`), counterfactual outcomes are unobserved and a fraction $p_{\mathrm{miss}}$ of outcomes is missing or selectively censored. Manski (2003) shows that producing a single point estimate $\hat{\theta}$ requires imposing untestable assumptions (such as missing-at-random). Partial identification refuses unverifiable extrapolation and computes the sharp **identification region** $\Theta_I = [\theta_L, \theta_U]$ compatible with the empirical distribution and bounded support $[y_{\min}, y_{\max}]$ (or monotone trimming under selective attrition, Lee 2009);
2. *The Imbens–Manski confidence interval and Mayo's post-data severity on the boundary of $\Theta_I$:* Let $\theta_{\mathrm{crit}}$ denote the minimum substantively worthwhile effect (or safety threshold) required to justify an intervention. To account simultaneously for identification ambiguity ($\hat{\Delta} = \hat{\theta}_U - \hat{\theta}_L \ge 0$) and finite-sample sampling variability ($\hat{\sigma}_L, \hat{\sigma}_U$ over variance-adjusted sample size $n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}$), Guido Imbens and Charles Manski (2004) constructed the two-sided $(1-\alpha)$ confidence interval for the partially identified parameter $\theta \in [\theta_L, \theta_U]$:
   $$\mathrm{CI}_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{IM}} = \left[\hat{L}_{1-\alpha},\; \hat{U}_{1-\alpha}\right] = \left[\hat{\theta}_L - c_\alpha\frac{\hat{\sigma}_L}{\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}}},\; \hat{\theta}_U + c_\alpha\frac{\hat{\sigma}_U}{\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}}}\right] \qquad \text{\texttt{[Identity]}}$$
   Crucially, the critical factor $c_\alpha \in [z_{1-\alpha},\, z_{1-\alpha/2}]$ is *not* a fixed normal quantile; it solves the adaptive coverage equation
   $$\Phi\!\left(c_\alpha + \sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}}\,\frac{\hat{\theta}_U - \hat{\theta}_L}{\max(\hat{\sigma}_L, \hat{\sigma}_U)}\right) - \Phi(-c_\alpha) = 1 - \alpha \qquad \text{\texttt{[Identity]}},$$
   interpolating smoothly between the two-sided quantile $z_{1-\alpha/2}$ when $\hat{\theta}_U = \hat{\theta}_L$ and the one-sided quantile $z_{1-\alpha}$ when the identification width $\hat{\theta}_U - \hat{\theta}_L$ dominates sampling noise. Rather than conflating Mayo's post-data severity with the pre-data confidence level $1 - \alpha$, the Mayo–Manski bridge evaluates the composite null hypothesis $H_0: \theta_L \le \theta_{\mathrm{crit}}$ against the realized lower-bound statistic $T(x_0) = \hat{\theta}_L(x_0)$ via the **post-data severity function** (Mayo 2018):
   $$\mathrm{SEV}\bigl(\theta_L > \theta_{\mathrm{crit}};\, x_0\bigr) = \mathbb{P}\!\left(T(X) \le T(x_0) \;\middle|\; \theta_L = \theta_{\mathrm{crit}}\right) = \Phi\!\left(\frac{\hat{\theta}_L(x_0) - \theta_{\mathrm{crit}}}{\hat{\sigma}_L / \sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}}}}\right);$$
3. *Separating the diagnostic outcome from the prescriptive choice rule:* Combining $\mathrm{CI}_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{IM}}$ and $\mathrm{SEV}(\cdot)$ yields three distinct epistemic states:
   - **Severe worst-case adequacy ($\hat{L}_{1-\alpha} > \theta_{\mathrm{crit}}$ and $\mathrm{SEV}(\theta_L > \theta_{\mathrm{crit}}; x_0) \ge 1 - \alpha$):** Even under the most adverse completion of missing records in the archive, the effect exceeds $\theta_{\mathrm{crit}}$ with high post-data severity;
   - **Severe refutation ($\hat{U}_{1-\alpha} \le \theta_{\mathrm{crit}}$ and $\mathrm{SEV}(\theta_U \le \theta_{\mathrm{crit}}; x_0) \ge 1 - \alpha$):** Even under the most optimistic completion of the archive, the policy fails to reach $\theta_{\mathrm{crit}}$;
   - **Underdetermined ambiguity ($\hat{L}_{1-\alpha} \le \theta_{\mathrm{crit}} < \hat{U}_{1-\alpha}$) or non-informative bounds ($\Theta_I = [y_{\min}, y_{\max}]$):** Here, descriptive identification alone does not select an action. Claiming that one must always decide solely by $\hat{L}_{1-\alpha}$ is not a theorem of Manski—it is Wald's (1945) prescriptive *maximin* rule, which can induce paralysis when $\Theta_I$ is wide. As detailed in Section 4.2 (Table 3), when $\hat{L}_{1-\alpha} \le \theta_{\mathrm{crit}} < \hat{U}_{1-\alpha}$, Stochology first enforces the lexicographic survival constraint ($\mathrm{TTS} \ge \mathrm{TTR}$): if the intervention threatens an absorbing ruin barrier, Sosa's meta-aptness mandates withholding the shot; if the intervention is reversible and survival-safe, Manski's (2003, 2013) *minimax regret* rule allocates fractional treatment proportional to the upside/downside span while targeted data collection narrows $\Theta_I$.

Recognizing Manski's identification region is thus the precondition for genuine severity in Mayo's sense: applying a conventional $t$-test to a biased point estimate while ignoring non-random missingness yields $\mathrm{SEV} \to 0$ against selection bias.

## **IV. The Second Filter: When the Model's Norm Narrates and the Archive Omits**

### **4.1. Tension 3 — Domesticating Formalization versus Reflexive Formalization**

Whereas the first filter governs problem framing and hypothesis testing, the second filter operates in the symbolic instruments that translate phenomena into measures: formal models and documentary archives. Historically, mathematical and classificatory formalization made it possible to expose hidden premises, compare distant regimes, and subject arguments to public verification. Yet this achievement harbors the third pathological inversion: the internal grammar of the formal model begins to legislate over what counts as real.

While Theodor Adorno (1966/1973) critiqued the tendency of identificatory concepts to dismiss qualitative singularity as "noise" and called for conceptual constellations to expose that remainder, Nicholas Georgescu-Roegen (1971) analyzed the parallel methodological mechanism inside mathematical economics by contrasting **arithmomorphic concepts** with **dialectical concepts**. Arithmomorphic concepts possess discrete, motionless boundaries like integers ($2 \ne 3$, with no intermediate zone). Dialectical concepts—such as "health," "justice," "clinical severity," or "ecological stability"—are surrounded by a constitutive **dialectical penumbra** ($[a, b]$) within which qualitative transitions unfold (for example, in `nyc_flights_2013.csv`, $8.4\%$ `[Measured]` of flight delays fall inside the $10\text{--}20$ minute penumbra around the FAA's sharp $15$-minute on-time cutoff). The error of uncritical formalism lies not in employing mathematics, but in erasing the penumbra by imposing a knife-edge cut where a transition zone exists.

Stochology therefore distinguishes **domesticating formalization**—which adopts Gaussian curves, linear additive models, and lone arithmetic means simply for analytical convenience—from **reflexive formalization**, which uses formal tools to measure the cost of simplification across four dimensions:

1. *Convex harm asymmetry and Jensen's Gap (Jensen 1906):* Let $g(X)$ be a twice-differentiable convex harm or congestion function ($g''(x) > 0$) evaluated over a random demand or environmental stress $X$ with mean $\mu = \mathbb{E}[X]$ and variance $\sigma_X^2$. By Johan Jensen's (1906) inequality, expected harm strictly exceeds the harm evaluated at the average scenario:
   $$\Delta_J = \mathbb{E}\bigl[g(X)\bigr] - g\bigl(\mathbb{E}[X]\bigr) \ge 0, \qquad \Delta_J \approx \frac{1}{2} g''(\mu)\,\sigma_X^2 \qquad \text{\texttt{[Identity]}}$$
   Exposed to one hour in a $27^\circ\mathrm{C}$ environment and one hour in a $47^\circ\mathrm{C}$ environment `[Assumed]`, a human organism faces an arithmetic mean ambient temperature of $37^\circ\mathrm{C}$ yet dies of hyperthermia; a hospital ward whose utilization oscillates between $60\%$ and $99\%$ `[Assumed]` suffers explosive queueing delays that a plan sized for the $80\%$ mean never anticipates. In the Danish fire insurance archive (`danish_fire.csv`, $n = 2{,}167$), quadratic loss exhibits a mean of $3.39$, a median of $1.78$, a $95\text{th}$ percentile of $8.41$, and a Jensen convexity gap of $\Delta_J = 72.35$ `[Measured]`. Planning for the lone mean systematically underfunds convex reality by $\Delta_J$;
2. *Heavy-tailed extremes, Pareto moment regimes, and threshold selection (Hill 1975):* For variables governed by power-law right tails $\mathbb{P}(X > x) \sim C x^{-\alpha}$ (such as financial losses, wildfire areas, epidemic superspreading, or infrastructure delays), Bruce M. Hill (1975) derived the order-statistic tail-index estimator over the top $k$ extremes $X_{(n)} \ge \dots \ge X_{(n-k)}$:
   $$\hat{\alpha}_k = \left[\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k} \ln\!\left(\frac{X_{(n-i+1)}}{X_{(n-k)}}\right)\right]^{-1} \qquad \text{\texttt{[Identity]}}$$
   The tail exponent $\alpha$ partitions reality into three distinct moment regimes: (i) $\alpha > 2$, where variance $\sigma^2 < \infty$ is finite but higher moments and kurtosis can still destabilize Gaussian intervals; (ii) $1 < \alpha \le 2$, where the population mean $\mu < \infty$ remains finite but the **population variance is infinite ($\sigma^2 = \infty$)** (as in `danish_fire.csv` at $k = 120$, where $\hat{\alpha}_{120} = 1.448 \in (1, 2]$ `[Measured]`), rendering sample standard deviations erratic downward-biased mirages; and (iii) $\alpha \le 1$, where even the population mean ceases to exist ($\mu = \infty$). In practice, applying $\hat{\alpha}_k$ requires confronting the **threshold-selection bias–variance trade-off**: choosing $k$ too small inflates estimation variance, whereas choosing $k$ too large contaminates the tail with central-body observations, requiring stability audits across a plateau of $k$ values (Hill plots) before feeding $\hat{\alpha}_k$ into reserve sizing;
3. *Structural model discrepancy (Kennedy and O'Hagan 2001):* Every mechanistic or econometric model $f(x, \theta)$ omits real-world processes. Marc Kennedy and Anthony O'Hagan (2001) demonstrated that calibrating $\theta$ under the fiction of zero structural error forces parameter estimates to absorb omitted physics or social dynamics, producing overconfident out-of-sample extrapolations. Reflexive formalization decomposes reality as $y(x) = f(x, \theta) + \delta(x) + \varepsilon$, modeling structural discrepancy $\delta(x)$ explicitly via a Gaussian-process prior so that predictive uncertainty widens automatically as $x$ moves outside historical support (in Chapter 6's anchored discrepancy benchmark, predictive standard deviation widens from $\sigma_{\mathrm{in}} = 0.048$ in-sample to $\sigma_{\mathrm{out}} = 0.650$ out-of-sample `[Simulated]`);
4. *Measurement scale invariance and CORP calibration decompositions for Brier and CRPS (Stevens 1946; Krantz et al. 1971; Murphy 1973; Gneiting, Balabdaoui, and Raftery 2007; Dimitriadis, Gneiting, and Jordan 2021):* In representational measurement theory (Stevens 1946; Krantz et al. 1971), computing arithmetic means on ordinal scales is ill-defined because strictly increasing monotone transformations $\phi(\cdot)$ can reverse mean rankings while leaving medians, quantiles, and first-order stochastic dominance invariant. In probabilistic forecasting, Allan H. Murphy (1973) and Tilmann Gneiting, Fadoua Balabdaoui, and Adrian E. Raftery (2007) established that forecast adequacy requires **maximizing sharpness (resolution) subject to calibration (reliability)**. Timo Dimitriadis, Tilmann Gneiting, and Alexander I. Jordan (2021) placed this decomposition on a parameter-free footing via the isotonic Pool-Adjacent-Violators (**CORP**) identity for binary Brier scores:
   $$\mathrm{BS} = \underbrace{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(p_i - \hat{\pi}_i)^2}_{\mathrm{REL}_{\mathrm{CORP}} \text{ (miscalibration)}} - \underbrace{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\hat{\pi}_i - \bar{y})^2}_{\mathrm{RES}_{\mathrm{CORP}} \text{ (discriminative resolution)}} + \underbrace{\bar{y}(1 - \bar{y})}_{\mathrm{UNC}} \qquad \text{\texttt{[Identity]}}$$
   where $\hat{\pi}_i$ are the PAV-recalibrated probabilities (verified on `atlantic_storms.csv`, $n = 1{,}200$, where $\mathrm{BS} = 0.1456 = 0.0080 - 0.0724 + 0.2100$ to machine precision $1.4 \times 10^{-17}$ `[Measured]`). For continuous heavy-tailed variables (such as flood stages, electricity load, or financial drawdowns), the same isotonic CORP decomposition extends directly to the **Continuous Ranked Probability Score** ($\mathrm{CRPS}(F, y) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} [F(z) - \mathbf{1}\{y \le z\}]^2\,\mathrm{d}z = \mathrm{REL}_{\mathrm{CRPS}} - \mathrm{RES}_{\mathrm{CRPS}} + \mathrm{UNC}_{\mathrm{CRPS}}$, Gneiting and Raftery 2007; Dimitriadis, Gneiting, and Jordan 2021). This exact identity provides the mathematical engine for Stochology's first reflexive Goodhart brake (Section 7.2).

### **4.2. Tension 4 — Sample Volume versus Evidential Weight, the Silence of the Archive, and the Epistemic Margin**

When the fragility of simplified models becomes undeniable, institutions frequently turn to the accumulation of massive digital registries (*Big Data*), assuming that millions of rows ($n \to N$) automatically wash away measurement bias. Here the fourth pathological inversion takes hold: archival volume is mistaken for evidential quality, converting selective omission into high-precision error.

In Chapter 6 of *A Treatise on Probability*, John Maynard Keynes (1921) distinguished the **probability** $p$ of an argument from its **weight of evidence** $V(E, H)$, which can be represented operationally by the normalized knowledge ratio:

$$V = \frac{K}{K + I} \in (0, 1) \qquad \text{\texttt{[Assumed functional representation]}}$$

where $K$ measures the balance of relevant knowledge and $I$ the remaining relevant ignorance. Two clinical or judicial assessments may both yield $p = 0.50$, yet one rests on a two-line administrative note ($V \ll 1$) while the other rests on exhaustive diagnostic testing and cross-examination ($V \to 1$). Probability measures the tilt of the scale; evidential weight measures how much of the relevant reality has actually been placed upon the pans.

Nearly a century later, Xiao-Li Meng (2018) proved an exact finite-population identity that explains why ignoring evidential weight and collection design produces the **Big Data paradox**. Let $R_i \in \{0, 1\}$ indicate whether individual $i$ in a population of size $N$ is recorded in an archive of size $n = \sum_{i=1}^N R_i$, with sampling fraction $f = n / N$. Since $\mathrm{Cov}(R, Y) = f(\bar{Y}_n - \bar{Y}_N)$ and $\sigma_R = \sqrt{f(1 - f)}$, the error between the archive sample mean $\bar{Y}_n$ and the true population mean $\bar{Y}_N$ is identically:

$$\bar{Y}_n - \bar{Y}_N = \underbrace{\rho_{R, Y}}_{\text{data defect correlation}} \cdot \underbrace{\sqrt{\frac{1 - f}{f}}}_{\text{dropout odds factor}} \cdot \underbrace{\sigma_Y}_{\text{population standard deviation}} \qquad \text{\texttt{[Identity]}}$$

Equating the mean squared error of $\bar{Y}_n$ to that of a simple random sample yields the MSE-equivalent effective sample size $n_{\mathrm{Meng}} \approx \frac{f}{1 - f}\frac{1}{\rho_{R,Y}^2}$ `[Identity]`. Three consequences follow immediately:
1. *Relative coverage $f = n/N$, not raw count $n$, governs the dropout factor:* a registry of one million records in a nation of two hundred million ($f = 0.005$ `[Assumed]`) retains a large dropout multiplier $\sqrt{(1-f)/f} \approx 14.1$ `[Identity]`;
2. *Selection correlation $\rho_{R,Y}$ does not vanish as $n$ grows under homogeneous collection:* if a digital platform or administrative registry systematically under-records poorer or sicker strata ($\rho_{R,Y} \ne 0$), scaling $n$ upward without altering the inclusion mechanism leaves $\rho_{R,Y}$ untouched while shrinking the nominal standard error $\sigma_Y / \sqrt{n}$ toward zero;
3. *Maximum confidence in the wrong answer:* In Meng's (2018) empirical calibration of the 2016 U.S. presidential election polls, a non-random sample of $n = 2.3 \times 10^6$ respondents with a tiny recording correlation of $\rho_{R,Y} \approx -0.005$ possessed the same effective information content as a genuinely randomized sample of roughly $n_{\mathrm{Meng}} \approx 400$ respondents `[Literature]`. The larger the unadjusted archive, the closer to zero falls the probability that its nominal confidence interval covers the true parameter $\bar{Y}_N$.

Two further mechanisms compound this contraction of information in the transit from world to archive:

First, observations in social, clinical, and financial archives arrive in correlated clusters (hospitals, schools, neighborhoods, trading days). In regression and survey sampling with equal cluster sizes $\bar{m}$ and intra-cluster correlation $\rho_{\mathrm{intra}} > 0$, Brent Moulton (1986) and Cathy Campbell (1977) highlighted the variance inflation factor $1 + (\bar{m} - 1)\rho_{\mathrm{intra}}$, which Leslie Kish (1965) generalized to unequal cluster sizes $m_g$ with coefficient of variation $\mathrm{CV}_m$:

$$\mathrm{DEFF} = 1 + \bigl[\bar{m}\bigl(1 + \mathrm{CV}_m^2\bigr) - 1\bigr]\rho_{\mathrm{intra}}, \qquad n_{\mathrm{cluster}} = \frac{n}{\mathrm{DEFF}} \qquad \text{\texttt{[Identity]}}$$

(In `cps_observational.csv`, $n = 1{,}500$ clustered observations with $\mathrm{DEFF} = 2.02$ reduce to $n_{\mathrm{cluster}} = 742.5$, while in `bank_calls.csv`, $\mathrm{DEFF} = 3.21$ reduces $n = 1{,}200$ to $n_{\mathrm{cluster}} = 373.8$ `[Measured]`.)

Second, archival omission is a socio-material and institutional process that requires deploying the analytical concepts of Science and Technology Studies (STS) rather than treating "missing data" as a purely technical accident. In Donald Rubin's (1976; Little and Rubin 1987/2002) statistical taxonomy, missingness is **Missing Not At Random (MNAR)** whenever the probability of non-recording depends on the unobserved outcome itself. Sociologically, however, three concrete institutional mechanisms produce MNAR silences:
- *Conventions of equivalence and mechanical objectivity (Desrosières 1998; Porter 1995):* Before any phenomenon can be counted, state and corporate bureaucracies must establish **conventions of equivalence** (Desrosières 1998) that strip away local context to treat heterogeneous lives as commensurable units, relying on rigid rules of **mechanical objectivity** (Porter 1995) to shield vulnerable officials from political distrust;
- *Classification infrastructure, residual categories, and biographical torque (Bowker and Star 1999; Hacking 1995):* Every classification system (such as the International Classification of Diseases or a municipal welfare registry) privileges certain institutional workflows while relegating atypical or marginalized trajectories to mute **residual categories** ("other / not elsewhere classified"). When individuals whose lived reality does not fit the administrative grid are forced to contort their biographies to obtain care or legal recognition, they experience what Geoffrey Bowker and Susan Leigh Star (1999) term **torque**, while the categories themselves reshape social identities through Ian Hacking's (1995) **looping effects** (*making up people*);
- *Hermeneutical injustice and structural agnotology (Fricker 2007; Proctor and Schiebinger 2008):* When an archive lacks the conceptual category needed to register a group's harm, it inflicts **hermeneutical injustice** (Fricker 2007); when authorities preserve that blind spot to avoid regulatory or fiscal liability—or revise historical macroeconomic series across release vintages (`rtdsm_routput_vintages.csv` exhibits a mean revision of $1.14$ percentage points and a $10.5\%$ sign-flip rate across $Q = 190$ quarters `[Measured]`)—omission operates as structural agnotology.

Where selection defects, cluster correlations, and socio-institutional omissions operate together, Stochology's **epistemic margin** replaces point extrapolation with four formal audit tools and an explicit unification of effective sample sizes:
- *Unifying the three notions of effective sample size:* Statistical rigor requires distinguishing (a) **Kish's (1965) weight-concentration sample size** $n_{\mathrm{Kish}} = (\sum w_i)^2 / \sum w_i^2$ `[Identity]`, which measures sampling variance inflation when inverse-probability weights (Horvitz and Thompson 1952) concentrate on underserved strata; (b) **cluster-adjusted sample size** $n_{\mathrm{cluster}} = n / \mathrm{DEFF}$ `[Identity]`, which measures sampling variance inflation from intra-cluster correlation; and (c) **Meng's (2018) selection-defect sample size** $n_{\mathrm{Meng}} \approx \frac{f}{1-f}\rho_{R,Y}^{-2}$ `[Identity]`, which measures non-random *bias* rather than sampling variance. When selection bias is already bracketed inside the partial-identification location interval $[L_M, U_M]$, the sampling-variance denominator in the Imbens–Manski interval uses $n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{var}} = \min(n_{\mathrm{cluster}}, n_{\mathrm{Kish}})$; when an agent requires a single scalar conservative discount for finite-sample shrinkage (as in `CommunityReserveEnv`), Stochology adopts the conservative floor $n_{\mathrm{eff}} = \min(n_{\mathrm{cluster}}, n_{\mathrm{Kish}}, n_{\mathrm{Meng}})$ `[Assumed analytical bridge]`;
- *Unseen population lower bounds (Chao 1984, 1987):* When incomplete registries or capture–recapture lists record $f_1$ singletons (individuals seen once) and $f_2$ doubletons (individuals seen twice), **Anne Chao's (1984, 1987) nonparametric lower bound** $\hat{N}_{\mathrm{unseen}} = f_1^2 / (2 f_2)$ `[Identity]` estimates the minimum size of the uncounted population under heterogeneous catchability without assuming equal inclusion probabilities;
- *Dependent censoring and attrition bounds (Peterson 1976; Lee 2009):* When subjects exit follow-up under dependent competing risks, **Peterson's (1976) sharp competing-risks bounds** bracket the joint survival distribution without assuming independent censoring, while **Lee's (2009) trimming bounds** bracket treatment effects under selective sample attrition;
- *Worst-case partial identification (Manski 2003):* Most generally, for any bounded outcome $Y \in [y_{\min}, y_{\max}]$ with missingness fraction $p_{\mathrm{miss}}$ and observed mean $\bar{Y}_{\mathrm{obs}}$, **Manski's (2003) sharp worst-case bounds** delimit the ambiguity interval:
  $$\bigl[L_M,\, U_M\bigr] = \Bigl[(1 - p_{\mathrm{miss}})\bar{Y}_{\mathrm{obs}} + p_{\mathrm{miss}} y_{\min},\; (1 - p_{\mathrm{miss}})\bar{Y}_{\mathrm{obs}} + p_{\mathrm{miss}} y_{\max}\Bigr] \qquad \text{\texttt{[Identity]}}$$

How does an institution pass from this descriptive interval $[L_M, U_M]$ to a concrete action without conflating identification with prescription—and how does it address standard decision-theoretic objections to lexicographic rules? In classical utility theory, unconstrained lexicographic preferences violate Archimedean continuity and cannot be represented by a single real-valued utility function. In engineering, clinical triage, and institutional risk management, however, **discontinuity at an absorbing ruin barrier is a physical feature of reality, not a mathematical defect**: no finite marginal efficiency gain compensates for crossing an irreversible threshold of death or systemic insolvency. Moreover, coupling the hard survival floor with minimax regret alters the unconstrained comparative statics whenever the survival constraint is active: if the unconstrained minimax-regret fractional allocation $a_{\mathrm{MMR}}^* = (U_M - C)/(U_M - L_M)$ `[Identity]` would consume reserves beyond the survival ceiling $a_{\max}(\mathrm{TTS} \ge \mathrm{TTR})$, the constrained optimal allocation is clipped to $a_{\mathrm{safe}}^* = \min\bigl(a_{\mathrm{MMR}}^*,\, a_{\max}(\mathrm{TTS} \ge \mathrm{TTR})\bigr)$, shifting comparative statics from the upside boundary $U_M$ to the physical recovery rate $1/\mathrm{TTR}$. ## **V. The Third Filter: When the Truth of the World Tears Under Action and Time**

### **5.1. Tension 5 — Passive Observation versus Performative Intervention**

Suppose an investigator has constrained situational framing biases, subjected hypotheses to severe sequential tests, respected convex tail asymmetries, and declared partial-identification bounds over archival omissions. Even then, the third mediating filter opens as soon as that representation is deployed to govern society: the instant a model or indicator is used to intervene in the world, the social and material field reacts to the intervention and alters the regularities the model was built upon.

The first ground of this rupture is causal and structural, formalized by Judea Pearl (2009; Pearl and Mackenzie 2018) in the three-rung **Ladder of Causation**:
1. *Rung 1 — Association ($\mathbb{P}(Y \mid X)$, passive observation):* recording empirical correlations in an undisturbed regime, which cannot distinguish structural causes from confounding (such as higher mortality in tertiary hospitals that receive the sickest patients);
2. *Rung 2 — Intervention ($\mathbb{P}(Y \mid \mathrm{do}(X = x))$, material action):* actively setting $X$ to $x$, which severs incoming confounding arrows and alters the data-generating distribution—requiring predictors whose structural coefficients remain invariant across distinct environments (**Invariant Causal Prediction**, Peters, Bühlmann, and Meinshausen 2016; verified in `atlantic_storms.csv`, where the physical barometric-pressure slope range across three storm eras is $0.030$ versus $0.354$ for the non-invariant latitude proxy `[Measured]`) and accounting for spillover and interference when scaling from a pilot trial to a population (Hudgens and Halloran 2008);
3. *Rung 3 — Counterfactuals ($\mathbb{P}(Y_x = y' \mid X = x, Y = y)$, retrospective attribution):* evaluating what would have occurred in a singular historical case under an alternative action, where Jin Tian and Judea Pearl (2000) derived sharp partial-identification bounds for the **Probability of Necessity ($\mathrm{PN}$)** and **Probability of Necessity and Sufficiency ($\mathrm{PNS}$)** `[Identity]` combining experimental and observational evidence.

In social, economic, and administrative life, climbing from passive observation to intervention encounters a second complication: the agents being modeled interpret the rule and adapt strategically. In macroeconomics, Robert Lucas (1976) showed that historical correlations dissolve when agents adjust expectations to a new policy regime, while Charles Goodhart (1975) formulated the law that any observed statistical regularity tends to collapse once control pressure is placed upon it—a mechanism generalized in the anthropology of audit culture by Marilyn Strathern (1997) ("when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure").

Going beyond the proverb requires engaging the distinct sociological mechanisms analyzed by Wendy Espeland and Michael Sauder (2007), David Manheim and Scott Garrabrant (2018), Donald MacKenzie (2006), and Juan Perdomo et al. (2020):

First, in the sociology of institutional rankings, Espeland and Sauder (2007) demonstrate that **reactivity** operates through two distinct mechanisms prior to overt cheating: **commensuration** (reducing qualitative institutional diversity to a single ordinal rank, which transforms cognitive attention and redistributes internal resources toward ranked items) and **self-fulfilling prophecy** (where external audiences allocate funding and prestige according to the rank, confirming the ranking's initial tiering). Complementing this sociological analysis, Manheim and Garrabrant (2018) classify four formal modes of Goodhart target collapse when an institution optimizes a proxy $M$ in place of a latent value $V$: *regressional collapse* (selecting noise tails where $M - V$ is largest), *extremal collapse* (pushing the state outside the historical domain where $M$ and $V$ co-varied), *causal collapse* (intervening on a downstream symptom $M$ rather than the upstream cause $V$, as analyzed in strategic classification by Milli et al. 2019; Kleinberg and Raghavan 2020; Miller, Milli, and Hardt 2020), and *adversarial collapse* (direct gaming of $M$ by strategic actors).

Second, in Science and Technology Studies, the program of **performativity**—grounded in the reflexive sociology of Barry Barnes (1983), formulated for economic sociology by Michel Callon (1998), and developed historically by Donald MacKenzie (2006)—demonstrates that formal models are not passive *cameras* recording a pre-existing market or bureaucracy, but *engines* that actively format socio-material practices. MacKenzie (2006) distinguishes four levels of performativity: (i) *generic performativity*, where a model is used by practitioners; (ii) *effective performativity*, where its use measurably alters processes; (iii) *Barnesian performativity* (named after Barry Barnes), where practical adoption of a model reshapes institutional arrangements so that reality conforms more closely to the model's postulates; and (iv) **counterperformativity**, where widespread adoption of the model alters material practices in a way that undermines the model's own empirical premises (as, in MacKenzie's 2006 historical reconstruction, the automated hedging of Black–Scholes portfolio insurance interacted with market illiquidity during the October 1987 crash, after which options markets developed the persistent volatility skew that violated the flat-volatility premise of the classic Black–Scholes formula `[Literature]`). Whereas Goodhart's Law describes the statistical decoupling of a target proxy under incentive pressure, MacKenzie's counterperformativity describes the material undoing of a model's truth conditions through its own infrastructural enactment.

Third, Juan Perdomo, Tijana Zrnic, Celestine Mendler-Dünner, and Moritz Hardt (2020) formalized **performative prediction**, where deploying a model parameter $\theta$ induces a distribution shift $\mathcal{D}(\theta)$. A parameter $\theta_{\mathrm{PS}}$ achieves **performative stability** when it is a fixed point of repeated retraining, $\theta_{\mathrm{PS}} = \arg\min_{\theta} \mathbb{E}_{Z \sim \mathcal{D}(\theta_{\mathrm{PS}})}[\ell(Z; \theta)]$ `[Identity]`: the model appears well-calibrated on the distribution its own deployment generated (for example, a credit-scoring rule that denies loans to a neighborhood, induces business failures, and then cites those failures as confirmation of its risk score). **Performative optimality** ($\theta_{\mathrm{PO}} = \arg\min_{\theta} \mathbb{E}_{Z \sim \mathcal{D}(\theta)}[\ell(Z; \theta)]$ `[Identity]`) minimizes expected loss accounting explicitly for how $\theta$ shifts $\mathcal{D}(\theta)$. Because in Perdomo et al.'s (2020) formal definition $\ell(Z; \theta)$ is still the designer's chosen loss function, Stochology adds an explicit **normative requirement**: the loss function $\ell$ evaluated in $\theta_{\mathrm{PO}}$ must include the externalized social harm borne by affected citizens rather than narrow institutional default loss alone.

### **5.2. Tension 6 — Ensemble Averages versus Trajectories in Irreversible Time**

The second rupture of the third filter concerns the arrow of time. As Nicholas Georgescu-Roegen (1971) argued in *The Entropy Law and the Economic Process*, standard formal models routinely adopt the reversible mechanical time of classical kinematics, in which trajectories can be run backward without loss. Living organisms, ecosystems, households, and public infrastructures, however, inhabit **irreversible thermodynamic and historical time**: dissipated reserves, cumulative physiological wear, and crossings of ruin barriers admit no time-reversal symmetry.

Ignoring temporal irreversibility produces the sixth constitutive tension: conflating the **ensemble average** across parallel realizations at a fixed instant with the **time average** along a single historical trajectory. Consider a sequence of river crossings over thin ice where each crossing carries a $90\%$ probability of multiplying wealth by $1.5$ and a $10\%$ probability of drowning (an absorbing barrier where wealth drops permanently to $0$) `[Assumed]`. Across an ensemble of $100$ parallel travelers crossing once on a single afternoon, $90$ survive with $1.5 W_0$, yielding a positive ensemble mean return of $+35\%$ `[Identity]`. For a single historical traveler—a person, a firm, or a municipal reserve—who must cross the river $100$ days in succession carrying prior accumulations forward, survival probability after $100$ crossings is $0.9^{100} \approx 2.66 \times 10^{-5}$ `[Identity]`: the time-trajectory growth rate collapses to ruin with near certainty.

To analyze this divergence without historical or mathematical conflation, Stochology distinguishes three levels:

1. *Discrete multiplicative compounding versus Itô's continuous-time stochastic lemma (Itô 1944, 1951):* Already in elementary discrete arithmetic, multiplicative processes obey the geometric–arithmetic mean inequality: losing $50\%$ of a reserve and then gaining $50\%$ yields $(1 - 0.50)(1 + 0.50) = 0.75$ `[Identity]`, a net loss of $25\%$ despite a zero arithmetic mean return. In continuous time, Kiyosi Itô's (1944, 1951) stochastic calculus establishes the exact differential rule. For a resource $W_t$ governed by geometric Brownian motion $\mathrm{d}W_t / W_t = \mu\,\mathrm{d}t + \sigma\,\mathrm{d}B_t$ with ensemble drift $\mu$ and volatility $\sigma$, Itô's lemma proves that logarithmic wealth evolves as
   $$\mathrm{d}\ln W_t = \left(\mu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2\right)\mathrm{d}t + \sigma\,\mathrm{d}B_t \qquad \text{\texttt{[Identity]}}$$
   The term $-\frac{1}{2}\sigma^2$ is the continuous **Itô volatility drain**: variance pulls the long-run time-average growth rate $g = \mu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2$ strictly below the ensemble drift $\mu$ (in `djia_1885_2023.csv`, across $n = 35{,}000$ daily returns, ensemble drift is $\hat{\mu} = 0.000265$ whereas time-average logarithmic growth is $g = 0.000201$ `[Measured]`);
2. *Kelly–Breiman growth, Samuelson's expected-utility critique, and Baker–McHale epistemic shrinkage (Kelly 1956; Breiman 1961; Merton 1969, 1971; Samuelson 1971, 1979; Baker and McHale 2013; Peters 2019):* For a leveraged exposure fraction $f$ in an excess-return process with risk-free rate $r$, the time-average logarithmic growth rate is
   $$g(f) = r + f(\mu - r) - \frac{1}{2}f^2\sigma^2 \qquad \text{\texttt{[Identity]}}$$
   Its **excess growth rate over the risk-free rate**, $g(f) - r = f(\mu - r) - \frac{1}{2}f^2\sigma^2$, is maximized at the **Kelly fraction** $f_{\mathrm{plug\text{-}in}}^* = (\mu - r)/\sigma^2$ (Kelly 1956; Breiman 1961). At double the Kelly fraction ($f = 2 f_{\mathrm{plug\text{-}in}}^*$), excess growth vanishes so that $g(2 f_{\mathrm{plug\text{-}in}}^*) = r$ `[Identity]`; for $f > 2 f_{\mathrm{plug\text{-}in}}^*$, the portfolio underperforms the risk-free rate ($g(f) < r$), and total growth $g(f)$ becomes **strictly negative ($g(f) < 0$)** whenever $f$ exceeds the zero-crossing threshold $f_0 = f_{\mathrm{plug\text{-}in}}^* + \sqrt{(f_{\mathrm{plug\text{-}in}}^*)^2 + 2r/\sigma^2}$ `[Identity]` (which reduces to $2 f_{\mathrm{plug\text{-}in}}^*$ when $r = 0$). Precision requires noting two critical caveats. First, Kelly's rule avoids *asymptotic* ruin in idealized continuous processes, but over finite horizons it still permits severe interim drawdowns; furthermore, when drift $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$ are estimated from a finite variance-adjusted effective sample size $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$, Rose Baker and Ian McHale (2013) show under Gaussian estimation uncertainty that the optimal fraction requires **epistemic Kelly shrinkage**:
   $$f_{\mathrm{epi}}^* = \frac{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}{n_{\mathrm{eff}} + 1}\,\frac{\hat{\mu} - r}{\hat{\sigma}^2} \qquad \text{\texttt{[Identity under Gaussian estimation variance]}}$$
   Second, as Paul Samuelson (1971, 1979) and Robert C. Merton (1969, 1971) emphasized in their classic debate with Kelly proponents, maximizing the time-average growth rate $g(f)$ of a multiplicative process is mathematically equivalent to maximizing expected logarithmic utility $\mathbb{E}[\ln W]$ within standard expected-utility theory, and does not override all other risk preferences over finite horizons when survival is not at stake. Consequently, when Ole Peters (2019) synthesized this literature under the banner of *ergodicity economics*, its genuine methodological contribution lay not in refuting expected-utility axiomatics, but in foregrounding how unbuffered multiplicative exposure in the presence of absorbing barriers renders ensemble averages a treacherous guide for policy;
3. *Conditions for non-ergodic criticality and the distinction between absorbing ruin and ecological hysteresis:* Not every decision problem is non-ergodic. When losses are small, additive, and bounded far away from ruin thresholds, ensemble expectations remain serviceable approximations. Non-ergodic divergence becomes decisive when **(a) sequential multiplicative or state-dependent compounding** interacts with **(b) critical boundary crossings**. Here physical precision requires distinguishing two boundary regimes:
   - **Strictly absorbing ruin barriers** (clinical death, legal bankruptcy/insolvency, species extinction), from which recovery is physically or legally impossible ($\mathrm{TTR} = \infty$); and
   - **Hysteretic regime shifts** in ecological and socio-ecological systems (Holling 1973; Scheffer et al. 2009), where crossing an unstable fold bifurcation flips a lake, fishery, or climate subsystem into a degraded alternative stable state. Unlike clinical death, a hysteretic regime shift is not strictly absorbing—restoration remains physically possible, but only by driving environmental pressure far below the original tipping threshold at a drastically higher social and material recovery cost ($\mathrm{TTR}_{\mathrm{hysteretic}} \gg \mathrm{TTS}$).

### **5.3. Survivorship Bias, Ecological Resilience, and the Operational Mechanics of the Vital Margin**

Invoking survival barriers requires dispelling two symmetrical fallacies surrounding the concept of "survival":
- *Statistical survivorship bias (from world to archive, Wald 1943):* Analyzing Allied bombers returning from European sorties during the Second World War, Abraham Wald (1943) demonstrated that fuselage zones riddled with bullet holes on returning aircraft (wings and tail) indicated where hits were *survivable*, whereas the scarcity of hits on returning engines and cockpits indicated that aircraft struck there were lost at sea ($\mathbb{P}(\text{lost} \mid \text{engine hit}) \gg \mathbb{P}(\text{lost} \mid \text{wing hit})$, reconstructed in Chapter 7 as $37.5\%$ engine lethality vs. $7.2\%$ wing lethality `[Simulated]`). Conditioning an archive on survivors inverts the true vulnerability map;
- *The normative fallacy of survival (from history to value):* In a non-ergodic world with unequal power, the mere fact that an institution or speculator has survived to date is neither proof of epistemic rationality nor a certificate of moral legitimacy, since tail risks can remain latent for years and powerful actors routinely externalize ruin onto unbuffered populations.

Stochology therefore separates (1) **material survival** as a *lexicographic precondition* (the physical floor required for agents and communities to continue existing and deliberating), (2) **epistemic survival** of conjectures under severe testing, and (3) **legitimate institutional survival**, which is bound by the duty not to cannibalize the vital margins of others.

The positive grounding of the **vital margin** draws on Georges Canguilhem's (1943/2009) philosophy of medicine and Crawford S. Holling's (1973) systems ecology, while preserving the full depth of both concepts. In *The Normal and the Pathological*, Canguilhem (1943/2009) rejected the positivist identification of health with conformity to a statistical mean: an organism capable of surviving only at a fixed average temperature or workload is already pathological. Crucially, Canguilhem's **biological normativity** is not merely a static buffer stock within a fixed regime; it is the *generative capacity to institute new physiological and practical norms* when the environment proves unfaithful, supported operationally by "a margin of tolerance for the inconsistencies of the environment" (*une marge de tolérance des infidélités du milieu*). In systems ecology, Holling (1973) similarly separated **engineering resilience** (local return speed $\lambda > 0$ near an equilibrium point) from **ecological resilience** (the width $D_t = x_{\mathrm{high}}^* - x_{\mathrm{tip}}^*$ of the attraction basin measuring how large a shock the system can absorb before crossing an unstable separatrix into a hysteretic degraded regime).

Here a crucial theoretical tension must be resolved: in Section 4.1, Jensen's inequality treated environmental fluctuation as a convex cost ($\Delta_J \ge 0$), whereas Holling (1973) and Crawford Holling and Gary Meffe (1996), in their classic diagnosis of the **"Pathology of Natural Resource Management" (*command-and-control*)**, showed that policies obsessed with suppressing all natural fluctuations (such as extinguishing every minor brushfire or stabilizing river flows artificially) destroy ecological resilience. How can fluctuation be simultaneously a convex cost and a condition of resilience? The synthesis lies in separating **scale and regime**: rigid command-and-control suppression of low-amplitude variability causes hidden stress or combustible fuel to accumulate silently, shrinking the basin radius $D_t$ until an inevitable shock triggers a catastrophic convex jump across the separatrix. A well-designed vital margin therefore *permits and absorbs low-amplitude natural variability* (which exercises adaptive normativity and preserves basin width $D_t$) while *maintaining hard buffers against crossing the tipping or ruin boundary*.

To operationalize both dimensions without collapsing basin resilience into a static queue heuristic, Stochology links the **operational stock dimension** ($S_{\mathrm{stock}}$) and the **dynamical basin dimension** ($S_{\mathrm{basin}}$) across three coupled mechanisms:

1. *Heavy-traffic queueing congestion (Kingman 1961; Scarf 1960):* On the operational stock plane, when a service facility (hospital, court, electrical grid) is pushed toward $100\%$ utilization ($\rho = \lambda_{\mathrm{arr}} / \mu_{\mathrm{serv}} \to 1^-$) to eliminate "idle capacity," John F. C. Kingman's (1961) heavy-traffic approximation proves that expected waiting time explodes hyperbolically with arrival and service coefficients of variation ($c_a^2, c_s^2$):
   $$\mathbb{E}[W_q] \approx \left(\frac{\rho}{1 - \rho}\right)\left(\frac{c_a^2 + c_s^2}{2}\right)\tau_{\mathrm{service}} \qquad \text{\texttt{[Identity]}}$$
   (In `vic_elec.csv`, pushing utilization from $\rho = 0.78$ to $\rho = 0.97$ `[Assumed]` inflates the Kingman delay multiplier from $3.55\times$ to $32.33\times$ `[Measured]`.) Preserving operational headroom and Herbert Scarf's (1960) $(s, S)$ inventory buffers is the direct stock-level defense against queue strangulation;
2. *Allostatic load, basin contraction, and the heavy-tail basin-breach probability (McEwen 1998; Hill 1975; Holling 1973):* On the physiological and ecological plane, chronic operation near the ceiling accumulates **allostatic load** $A_t$ (McEwen 1998), lowering carrying capacity $K(A_t) = K_0 - \gamma A_t$ and contracting the attraction-basin radius $D_t = x_{\mathrm{high}}^*(A_t) - x_{\mathrm{tip}}^*(A_t)$ (in Chapter 7's bistable May–Holling model, allostatic wear of $\Delta K = 2.5$ shrinks the basin radius from $D_0 = 6.23$ to $D_{\mathrm{worn}} = 4.35$ `[Simulated]`). Connecting Section 4.1's heavy-tailed shocks $X_t \sim \mathrm{Pareto}(x_m, \alpha)$ directly to Holling's basin radius $D_t > x_m$ yields the exact **single-shock basin-breach probability**:
   $$\mathbb{P}\bigl(X_t > D_t\bigr) = \left(\frac{x_m}{D_t}\right)^{\alpha} \qquad \text{\texttt{[Identity]}}$$
   This equation formalizes the exact synergy between Tension 3 and Tension 6: a heavier tail (smaller $\alpha \le 2$) and an allostatically narrowed basin (smaller $D_t$) interact as a power law to accelerate regime collapse;
3. *Dynamical bifurcation, Critical Slowing Down, and the $\mathrm{TTS}_t \ge \mathrm{TTR}_t$ bridge (Scheffer et al. 2009; Boettiger and Hastings 2012; Simchi-Levi, Schmidt, and Wei 2014):* Because the basin radius $D_t$ cannot always be observed directly ex ante, Marten Scheffer et al. (2009) showed that as a system approaches a fold bifurcation, its local engineering recovery rate $\lambda_t \to 0^+$ (**Critical Slowing Down**), causing lag-1 autocorrelation $\rho_{1, t} = e^{-\lambda_t \Delta t} \to 1$ and stationary fluctuation variance $\sigma_X^2 = \sigma_\varepsilon^2 / (2\lambda_t) \to \infty$ `[Identity]`. As Carl Boettiger and Alan Hastings (2012) demonstrated, however, rising variance or autocorrelation in finite samples can suffer from substantial false-positive and false-negative rates (especially under shifting noise regimes or post-hoc selection of collapsing series). Stochology therefore refuses to rely on Critical Slowing Down in isolation; instead, it couples the basin indicator $S_{\mathrm{basin}, t} = \operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}\!\bigl(\frac{1 - \hat{\rho}_{1,t}}{1 - \rho_{1,\mathrm{crit}}}\bigr)$ with David Simchi-Levi, William Schmidt, and Yehua Wei's (2014) physical **breathing-time inequality**, linking operational recovery time $\mathrm{TTR}_t$ directly to the inverse recovery rate $1/\lambda_t$:
   $$\mathrm{TTS}_t = \frac{B_t}{d_{\mathrm{crisis}, t}} \;\ge\; \mathrm{TTR}_t \propto \frac{1}{\lambda_t} = \frac{\Delta t}{-\ln \rho_{1, t}} \qquad \text{\texttt{[Identity / Analytical Bridge]}}$$
   As the basin shallows ($\lambda_t \to 0^+, \rho_{1,t} \to 1$), recovery time $\mathrm{TTR}_t \to \infty$ diverges, triggering the lexicographic safety brake before the separatrix is crossed.

## **VI. The Architecture of Judgment Under Turbulence: *Télos*, Moral Luck, Apt Agency, and Institutional Contract**

### **6.1. Tension 7 (First Face) — Antipater's Archer (*Télos* and *Skopós*), Moral Luck, and the Normative Bridge**

Even when an agent respects all six preceding locks, no human precaution eliminates contingency: a scrupulous clinician may lose a patient to an unpredictable complication, a civil-defense evacuation may be followed by a sudden shift in a cyclone's track, and a reckless executive may survive several quarters unharmed by sheer luck. This generates the seventh constitutive tension: how can we evaluate the responsibility of decision-makers under uncertainty without either worshiping fortuitous outcomes or granting impunity to reckless governance?

Hellenistic philosophy confronted this problem through a technical distinction formulated within the Stoa, whose philological transmission requires precision. In Book III of *De Finibus Bonorum et Malorum* (III.22; Cicero 1914), Cicero presents in Latin the analogy of the archer (*toxótēs*) or spear-thrower: the archer must do everything within his craft to aim straight (which Cicero terms the *ultimum* or *extremum bonorum*, the supreme good internal to right conduct—the quality that Greek links back to the *stókhos* root as **εὐστοχία, *eustokhía***, "good aim"), even though striking the external target (*propositum* or *scopus*) remains exposed to crosswinds after the shaft leaves the bow. While Cicero leaves the Greek scholarch unnamed in that passage, the anthology of **Joannes Stobaeus** (*Eclogae* II.76.9–15 Wachsmuth = Long and Sedley 1987, 64C; Stobaeus 1884) explicitly preserves the authorship and Greek terminology: the distinction belongs to **Antipater of Tarsus** (head of the Stoa in the second century BCE, before Panaetius of Rhodes shifted Stoic ethics toward the four *personae* and practical appropriateness, **καθῆκον, *kathēkon***, in Cicero's *De Officiis* I.107–121; Cicero 1913). Antipater separated the **τέλος (*télos*, the internal end—doing everything in one's power continuously and undeviatingly to achieve the primary natural things)** from the **σκοπός (*skopós*, the external target set before the agent)**. Hitting the *skopós* is a **preferred indifferent** (**προηγμένον, *proēgmenon***; *seligendum*, not *expetendum*). At the same time, historical honesty requires noting that our fullest narrative witness—**Plutarch** (*De communibus notitiis adversus Stoicos* 1070f–1071e = Long and Sedley 1987, 64E; Plutarch 1976)—is a hostile polemicist attacking the Stoic archer analogy at the school level, arguing that no archer could value the act of aiming (*télos*) higher than hitting the target (*skopós*) unless aiming is ordered to hitting.

In twentieth-century ethics, **Thomas Nagel (1979)** and **Bernard Williams (1981)** reopened this problem as **moral luck**—distinguishing *constitutive*, *circumstantial*, *causal*, and *resultant* luck—while **Jonathan Baron and John C. Hershey (1988)** demonstrated experimentally how **outcome bias** leads observers to rate the very same decision as competent when the realization is favorable and negligent when it fails.

How does Stochology answer both Plutarch's ancient objection to the Stoic archer and the modern challenge of moral luck? First, to answer Plutarch without retreating into a purely inward ethics of good intentions, Stochology draws on **Ernest Sosa's (2007, 2015)** virtue epistemology (*AAA structure*): a performance is **accurate** if it hits the target (even by luck); **adroit** if it exercises genuine methodological competence (incorporating epistemic and vital margins); and **apt** if it succeeds *because* it is adroit. Aiming (*télos*) is therefore not severed from hitting (*skopós*): adroitness is defined by objective reliability across counterfactually close worlds. At the second order, Sosa (2015) defines **fully apt performance** (*meta-aptness*) as the reflective competence to assess the environmental risk of the field and determine whether one is in a position to take an apt shot at all. Extending Sosa's epistemic concept to practical institutional action—an explicit normative bridge constructed below—yields the duty to **lower the bow and withhold irreversible intervention** when identification bounds are wide and absorbing barriers are close, while simultaneously weighing the **cost of the unshot arrow (Type II / failure-to-rescue error)** whenever status-quo inaction itself inflicts irreversible harm within the $\mathrm{TTS} \ge \mathrm{TTR}$ window.

Second, Stochology does not claim that *aptness* dissolves resultant moral luck. Here Bernard Williams's (1981) distinction between *blame* and **agent-regret** is decisive. Even when an adroit lorry driver—or a scrupulous clinician or infrastructure planner—took every due precaution and is rightly exempted from blame for negligence (correcting Baron–Hershey outcome bias), an adverse tail realization leaves an irreducible moral residue: *agent-regret*, the recognition that it was *one's own action or policy* that stood in the causal chain of another's loss. While Williams himself remained skeptical of reducing ethical life to a formal "morality system," Stochology translates the institutional analogue of agent-regret into a concrete public design requirement: **no-fault reparative compensation**, solidaristic insurance for citizens struck by residual tail events, and mandatory recording of the adverse outcome in the public registry.

We are now in a position to state transparently the **normative bridge** that licenses the prescriptive duties of this essay. Rather than claiming to deduce moral duties from bare statistical facts alone, Stochology derives **ought from ought applied to institutional facts**—combining descriptive facts about archival omission and non-ergodic ruin with explicitly imported normative premises across three steps:

1. *The ethics of belief and pragmatic encroachment (Clifford 1877/1879; Adler 2002; Fantl and McGrath 2009):* In epistemology, William Kingdon Clifford (1877/1879) and Jonathan Adler (2002) established that belief and public assertion are governed by an evidentialist norm of proportioning assent to evidence, while Jeremy Fantl and Matthew McGrath's (2009) principle of **pragmatic encroachment** shows that whether a body of evidence $E$ suffices to warrant asserting "policy $a$ is safe" is not invariant to practical stakes: as the severity of potential harm increases, the evidential threshold required for warrant rises. By itself, however, this first step establishes an *epistemic* norm of warrant, not yet an interpersonal moral duty;
2. *The structural asymmetry of institutional risk imposition (Hansson 2013):* For a solitary decision-maker who exposes only their own wealth or body to risk, preserving a vital margin is a *prudential* requirement, not a duty of justice. Sven Ove Hansson's (2013) ethics of risk supplies the empirical-institutional premise that transforms prudence into interpersonal morality: in bureaucratic and corporate governance, **the decision-maker who selects the risk exposure is systematically distinct from the third parties who bear the physical downside if the gamble fails**. Moreover, even when an ex-ante individual ruin probability appears small ($p_i = 10^{-3}$ `[Assumed]`), imposing that exposure across an unbuffered population of $N = 10^5$ `[Assumed]` citizens yields an expected toll of $\mathbb{E}[\text{ruin}] = N p_i = 100$ `[Identity]` ruined lives—stripping the institution of the excuse that resultant catastrophe was an unforeseeable fluke;
3. *Scanlonian contractualism and reconciliation of the individualist restriction (Scanlon 1998; Hansson 2013):* Under Thomas M. Scanlon's (1998) contractualism, an act is wrong if its performance under the circumstances would be disallowed by any principle for the general regulation of behavior that no affected person could reasonably reject. Crucially, Scanlon's **individualist restriction** forbids justifying a principle by summing aggregate conveniences across a group to outweigh a grave burden imposed on a single person. To respect Scanlon's individualist restriction strictly without illicitly aggregating "a hundred people" into a collective super-claimant, the contractualist comparison must be conducted **person by person** using Scanlon's (1998, ch. 5) requirement of **due care** and Hansson's (2013, ch. 4) **reciprocal risk-acceptance criterion**. Consider the **Candidate Principle of Unbuffered Point Optimization** ($P_{\mathrm{naive}}$): *"An institution may size reserves and deploy interventions based on point estimates and high-utilization averages whenever doing so maximizes expected net efficiency."* Compare the generic reasons of two individual standpoints:
   - *Standpoint A (any single uncounted or unbuffered citizen exposed to an absorbing barrier):* Under $P_{\mathrm{naive}}$, this individual citizen is exposed without warning or consent to preventable irreversible ruin (death, insolvency, loss of livelihood) because the institution declined to bound archival missingness or maintain survival slack;
   - *Standpoint B (any single taxpayer or institutional beneficiary of lean efficiency):* Under the rival **Stochological Principle of Bounded Exposure** ($P_{\mathrm{stoch}}$), this individual bears a bounded, reversible opportunity cost to fund capacity headroom ($1 - \rho \ge 0.20$ `[Assumed]`) and partial-identification audits.

   Under the explicitly imported normative premise that **an individual's generic objection to unconsented, preventable exposure to irreversible ruin is weightier than another individual's generic objection to a bounded, reversible efficiency cost**—unless the risk exposure is integral to a reciprocal system of protection that safeguards that very individual (Hansson 2013)—any single citizen in Standpoint A can reasonably reject $P_{\mathrm{naive}}$. Concealing the width of $[L_t, U_t]$ or liquidating the vital margin when another person will bear the impact of the arrow violates reciprocal justification.

When the three margins come into practical conflict under scarcity or emergency, Stochology proposes an explicit **Normative Priority Rule** (formulated as a revisable constitutional working order):
- **First Priority (Lexicographic Survival Floor):** Protecting immediate human life and preventing the crossing of an absorbing ruin barrier ($\mathrm{TTS}_t \ge \mathrm{TTR}_t$, Roy 1952)—including preventing life-threatening classification errors in acute clinical or emergency triage—takes lexical priority over procedural delay (governing Reflexive Brake 3) and over point-efficiency gains;
- **Second Priority (Epistemic Non-Concealment):** Within the survival-admissible set, the duty to declare honest partial-identification bounds $[L_t, U_t]$ without fabrication or agnotological inflation is non-negotiable, since concealing uncertainty deprives affected citizens of the capacity to evaluate or contest the third-level allocation;
- **Third Priority (Equitable Allocation of Opportunity Cost and Liability):** The trade-off between present relief and future reserve stock (Reflexive Brake 2) and the distribution of non-lethal classification errors must be publicly justified under constitutional governance with downside liability at the top.

### **6.2. Tension 7 (Second Face) — Group Agency, Cybernetic Veto, Contractual Asymmetry, and the Fairness Trilemma**

In modern society, the agent who sets interest rates, hospital bed ratios, environmental quotas, or judicial risk scores is not a solitary Stoic archer, but a corporate or state institution. Relying on individual moral heroism inside a perversely structured bureaucracy is sociologically naive. Translating apt agency into institutional architecture requires combining **Christian List and Philip Pettit's (2011) theory of group agency**, **Miranda Fricker's (2007, 2010, 2020) critique of epistemic injustice and institutional epistemic vices**, **Stafford Beer's (1972) organizational cybernetics**, and the **economics and sociology of contracts and classification**:

1. *Constitutionalizing the conjunctive safety brake and eliminating testimonial injustice (List and Pettit 2011; Beer 1972; Fricker 2007, 2010, 2020):* List and Pettit (2011) proved via the **discursive dilemma** that a committee voting by simple majority on a conclusion ($C \leftrightarrow P_1 \wedge P_2 \wedge P_3$, where $P_1, P_2, P_3$ are the three safeguards) can vote to approve an unsafe deployment even when a majority of specialists judges at least one safeguard violated `[Identity]`. Rational group agency therefore requires a **premise-based conjunctive constitution** in which any violated safety premise exercises veto power. In Stafford Beer's (1972) Viable System Model, this requires separating operational management from an independent **System $3^*$ ($S3^*$) sporadic audit channel** and an **algedonic emergency channel** that bypasses hierarchical filtering to trigger automatic suspension when errors breach declared bounds. Here Miranda Fricker's (2007, 2010, 2020) work operates on two distinct levels that must both be preserved:
   - First, on the **intrinsic deontological plane**, **testimonial injustice** (discounting a speaker's credibility due to identity prejudice, Fricker 2007) dishonors frontline workers, nurses, or marginalized residents in their fundamental capacity as knowers—an intrinsic epistemic and moral wrong even before its downstream consequences materialize;
   - Second, on the **cybernetic-institutional plane** (Fricker 2010, 2020), when an organization exhibits *inferential inertia* or applies a credibility discount $\delta_{\mathrm{cred}} \in [0, 1)$ (whether rooted in identity prejudice or hierarchical status devaluation) to frontline algedonic distress signals, it attenuates the effective per-shift log-evidence increment by $(1 - \delta_{\mathrm{cred}})$, multiplying the shifts required to reach an emergency circuit-breaker threshold by $1 / (1 - \delta_{\mathrm{cred}})$ `[Assumed model mechanism]` (in Chapter 8's benchmark, $\delta_{\mathrm{cred}} = 0.55$ `[Assumed]` stretches recognition from $12$ shifts to $26$ shifts, breaching $\mathrm{TTS} = 8$ `[Simulated]`);
2. *Ending asymmetric risk-shifting at the top while protecting the operational base (Jensen and Meckling 1976; Holmström 1979; Stiglitz and Weiss 1981; Innes 1990; Taleb 2018):* While Nassim Nicholas Taleb (2018) popularized the demand for symmetry of exposure ("skin in the game"), applying raw downside penalties to frontline operators (on-call clinicians, teachers, maintenance crews) who lack authority over system capacity or leverage is both unjust and inefficient. Bengt Holmström's (1979) **informativeness principle** proves that an additional performance signal should enter an agent's incentive contract if and only if it is **statistically informative about the agent's hidden action (effort)** conditional on already contracted signals (that is, the existing signals are not sufficient statistics for the pair) `[Identity]`. Because frontline operators do not control macro-level reserve sizing or exogenous tail surges, penalizing them for systemic overload injects uninsurable noise without effort informativeness. Conversely, at the executive top, two distinct contract-theoretic mechanisms interact: **Robert D. Innes (1990)** proved that when an agent subject to **limited liability** (downside bounded at zero) chooses hidden **effort ex ante**, the constrained-optimal monotonic contract takes a *"live-or-die"* (debt/option-like) form; and as **Michael C. Jensen and William H. Meckling (1976)** and **Joseph E. Stiglitz and Andrew Weiss (1981)** demonstrated for **asset substitution and risk-shifting**, an option-like limited-liability payoff over project variance creates a direct incentive to shift probability mass into extreme tails whenever upside bonuses are uncapped. In Chapter 8's variance-decomposition audit, $88.9\%$ `[Simulated]` of tail deficit variance originates in executive structural sizing versus $11.1\%$ `[Simulated]` in operational noise. Stochology therefore mandates **solidaristic limited-liability protection at the operational base** paired with **bitemporal clawback escrows (`TemporalVault`) and patrimonial restitution at the decision-making top**;
3. *Protecting unmeasured care in multitask environments (Holmström and Milgrom 1991; Strathern 1997; Espeland and Sauder 2007):* When professional work comprises both an easily counted proxy task (consultations per hour, arrest quotas, publication counts) and an unmeasured high-value dimension (diagnostic listening, de-escalation, replicable rigor), Bengt Holmström and Paul Milgrom (1991) proved that high-powered incentives on the counted metric rationally drive effort on unmeasured care toward zero—a formal mechanism reinforced sociologically by audit and ranking reactivity (Strathern 1997; Espeland and Sauder 2007);
4. *Public justification under Chouldechova's Fairness Trilemma and the duty of the embedded practitioner (Chouldechova 2017):* When an institution deploys a risk instrument across groups $g \in \{A, B\}$ marked by unequal historical base rates $p_A \ne p_B \in (0, 1)$, Alexandra Chouldechova (2017) proved via Bayes's rule the exact algebraic identity linking the False Positive Rate ($\mathrm{FPR}_g$, the proportion of negative-outcome individuals subjected to false alarms), the False Negative Rate ($\mathrm{FNR}_g$, the proportion of positive-need or positive-risk individuals missed by the rule), and Positive Predictive Value ($\mathrm{PPV}_g$, calibration parity):
   $$\mathrm{FPR}_g = \frac{p_g}{1 - p_g} \cdot \frac{1 - \mathrm{PPV}_g}{\mathrm{PPV}_g} \cdot \bigl(1 - \mathrm{FNR}_g\bigr) \qquad \text{\texttt{[Identity]}}$$
   Whenever $p_A \ne p_B$ and $\mathrm{PPV}_A = \mathrm{PPV}_B$, it is algebraically impossible to equalize both $\mathrm{FPR}$ and $\mathrm{FNR}$ across groups (in `toronto_arrests.csv`, where group base rates are $p_A = 0.322$ and $p_B = 0.179$, enforcing equal $\mathrm{PPV} = 0.60$ and equal $\mathrm{FNR} = 0.25$ forces a $2.18\times$ false-positive disparity ratio $\mathrm{FPR}_A / \mathrm{FPR}_B$ `[Measured]`). Connecting Chouldechova's mathematical theorem to Scanlon's contractualist principle of non-rejectability yields a direct **normative consequence**: because no value-neutral technical optimum exists under unequal base rates, presenting an algorithm as "objectively fair" conceals a political choice about which group will bear excess false alarms or excess unmet need. The institution is morally obligated to disclose its chosen error trade-off publicly, and the individual statistician or engineer embedded inside an institution that refuses disclosure has the professional duty to record the disparity in the independent $S3^*$ audit log and refuse to sign off on a false claim of neutrality;
5. *Cognitive ergonomics and natural frequencies against alert fatigue (Gigerenzer and Hoffrage 1995; Wong et al. 2021; Zheng and Meister 2025):* Finally, institutional accountability collapses if frontline human operators are cognitively overwhelmed. Jieyu Zheng and Markus Meister (2025) demonstrated that conscious human deliberation operates under a biological throughput bottleneck of roughly $10\text{ bits/s}$ `[Literature]`. When commercial clinical algorithms flood wards with low-specificity alarms—as Andrew Wong et al. (2021) documented in showing that the widely deployed Epic Sepsis Model generated an $88\%$ false discovery rate `[Literature]`—operators suffer alert fatigue and disable or ignore the channel. Stochology requires filtering automated alerts to respect the $10\text{ bits/s}$ deliberative ceiling and communicating residual uncertainty in **natural frequencies** (Gigerenzer and Hoffrage 1995; e.g., in Chapter 8's filtered clinical benchmark on $N = 1{,}000$ patients with $2\%$ sepsis prevalence: *"out of $1{,}000$ patients, $20$ develop sepsis; the filtered alert triggers on $31$ patients, of whom $16$ are true cases and $15$ are false alarms, raising $\mathrm{PPV}$ from $6.1\%$ to $52.2\%$"[Simulated]*).

### **7.2. Reflexive Self-Application: Goodhart Brakes, Operationalized Falsifiers, and Meta-Theoretical Limits**

An essay that diagnoses how theories insulate themselves and how institutional indicators degenerate under control pressure would fail its own standard if it presented Stochology as immune to perversion. Two reflexive questions must therefore be answered explicitly: what happens if an organization turns the three safeguards of Stochology itself into isolated bureaucratic targets under Goodhart's Law? And what pre-registered observable outcomes would empirically falsify the core claims of this program?

First, each axis of the Nuclear Triad degenerates if maximized as a raw one-sided quantity detached from the other two, and each possesses an internal mathematical and institutional brake embedded in $(S_{\mathrm{epi}}, S_{\mathrm{vital}}, S_{\mathrm{gov}})$:

1. *Degeneration of the Epistemic Margin — Skeptical Inflation and the Alibi of Indecision:* If an agency rewards analysts solely for nominal interval coverage ($\mathbf{1}\{S_t \in [L_t, U_t]\}$), analysts can game the target by widening $[L_t, U_t]$ to the trivial support $[y_{\min}, y_{\max}]$. Such an interval is never empirically falsified, yet conveys zero information and shields officials from action. **Reflexive Brake 1:** Under the Murphy (1973), Gneiting, Balabdaoui, and Raftery (2007), and Dimitriadis, Gneiting, and Jordan (2021) CORP decomposition, calibration ($\mathrm{REL}_{\mathrm{CORP}} \to 0$) is inseparable from **discriminative resolution** ($\mathrm{RES}_{\mathrm{CORP}} > 0$), and $S_{\mathrm{epi}}$ penalizes uninformative width via $1 - ((U - L)/(y_{\max} - y_{\min}))^2$. Lazy skeptical inflation drives $\mathrm{RES}_{\mathrm{CORP}} = 0.000$ (versus $0.250$ for a sharp calibrated forecast in Chapter 9's audit `[Simulated]`) and collapses $S_{\mathrm{epi}} \to 0$;
2. *Degeneration of the Vital Margin — Regressive Hoarding and Bureaucratic Self-Preservation:* If reserve accumulation is turned into an unconditional target blind to present deprivation, a ministry or corporation may hoard liquidity or withhold urgent relief from vulnerable households under the pretext of "protecting the future buffer." **Reflexive Brake 2:** In Scarf's (1960) inventory theory and Samuelson's (1971, 1979) intertemporal welfare analysis, every precautionary buffer $R$ is bounded above by **marginal social opportunity cost** $c_{\mathrm{urgent}}$. Modeling net social welfare as $W(R) = \kappa \ln(1 + R) - c_{\mathrm{urgent}} R$ `[Assumed]`, welfare peaks at the finite optimum $R^* = \max(0, \kappa / c_{\mathrm{urgent}} - 1)$ `[Identity]` and turns strictly negative under regressive hoarding ($W(R^*) = +2.55$ at $R^* = 3.0$ vs. $W(R_{\mathrm{hoard}}) = -11.97$ at $R_{\mathrm{hoard}} = 25.0$ for $\kappa = 4.0, c_{\mathrm{urgent}} = 1.0$ `[Simulated]`);
3. *Degeneration of Institutional Governance — Procedural Theater and Paralysis in Emergency:* If pre-registration, collegiate deliberation, and documentary auditing become a slow ritual ($\tau_{\mathrm{bureaucratic}} > \mathrm{TTS}$), an institution facing an acute flood, sepsis shock, or grid collapse will miss the critical instant of intervention (*kairós*) while completing paperwork. **Reflexive Brake 3:** Governance is lexicographically subordinated to the physical survival clock ($\mathrm{TTS} \ge \mathrm{TTR}$, Simchi-Levi, Schmidt, and Wei 2014). Whenever $\tau_{\mathrm{bureaucratic}} > \mathrm{TTS}$ (e.g., $\tau_{\mathrm{bureaucratic}} = 12.0 > \mathrm{TTS} = 4.0$ `[Assumed]`), Sosa's (2015) meta-aptness mandates triggering a pre-committed emergency fast-path ($\tau_{\mathrm{fast}} = 1.5 \le \mathrm{TTS} = 4.0$ `[Simulated]`) that releases vital buffers immediately, deferring forensic auditing until survival is secured.

Second, honoring Karl Popper's (1934/2002) demarcation criterion, Imre Lakatos's (1970) standard of progressive problemshifts, and Deborah Mayo's (2018) severity principle requires classifying the propositions of Stochology into **four distinct epistemic categories**:
1. **Deductive Mathematical Identities (`[Identity]`):** Theorems such as Meng's decomposition, Jensen's inequality, Hill's estimator, Itô's lemma, the CORP Brier/CRPS decomposition, Manski's worst-case bounds, and Chouldechova's fairness trilemma hold by mathematical proof; they cannot be "empirically falsified," though their domain preconditions (e.g., $\rho_{R,Y} \ne 0$, $g'' > 0$, $p_A \ne p_B$) are empirically testable.
2. **Model-Relative Analytical and Simulated Consequences (`[Assumed]` + `[Simulated]`):** The Three Collapse Propositions in `CommunityReserveEnv` (Section 1.2, Table 1) establish that *given* the structural dynamics of selective archival missingness, Kingman/Pareto congestion, and multitask proxy erosion, suppressing any single safeguard drives the system into a specific failure mode across all $16$ tested random seeds (`seeds = 2020..2035`), whereas removing those world defects in a benign small-world negative control allows even the naive policy to survive.
3. **Contingent Empirical Hypotheses Subject to Pre-Registered Refutation (Empirical Falsifiers 1–4):** To avoid vague comparative clauses ("consistently equal or better"), Stochology specifies four quantitative, pre-registered empirical falsifiers with explicit sample sizes, horizons, and post-data severity thresholds ($\mathrm{SEV} \ge 0.95$ at significance level $\alpha = 0.05$ and statistical power $1 - \beta \ge 0.90$ `[Assumed protocol]`):
   - **Empirical Falsifier 1 (Refuting the necessity of $S_{\mathrm{epi}}$ in observational policy transport):** Across a pre-registered benchmark of $K \ge 10$ multi-site observational registries paired with experimental ground truth (extending the LaLonde `nsw_experimental.csv` / `cps_observational.csv` design) where documented data-defect correlation satisfies $|\rho_{R,Y}| \ge 0.02$ and cluster design effect satisfies $\mathrm{DEFF} \ge 1.5$, the claim that partial-identification and $\mathrm{DEFF}$ adjustments are required for reliable transport is refuted if unadjusted OLS/point-mean intervals achieve out-of-sample coverage $\ge 90\%$ of the experimental ATE and lower mean minimax decision regret than Imbens–Manski / Moulton-adjusted bounds in at least $8$ of $10$ sites ($\mathrm{SEV} \ge 0.95$);
   - **Empirical Falsifier 2 (Refuting the necessity of $S_{\mathrm{vital}}$ in heavy-traffic and heavy-tailed operations):** Across a longitudinal cohort of $M \ge 20$ service infrastructures or leveraged funds operating over a horizon of $T \ge 5$ years under heavy-tailed demand or return shocks ($\hat{\alpha}_k \le 2.0$) and high baseline utilization ($\rho_{\mathrm{base}} \ge 0.92$) without $\mathrm{TTS} \ge \mathrm{TTR}$ reserve buffers or epistemic Kelly shrinkage, the claim that vital slack is necessary for survival is refuted if the unbuffered cohort exhibits a 5-year insolvency or queue-collapse rate ($\mathbb{E}[W_q] > 10\tau_{\mathrm{service}}$) statistically non-inferior (within margin $\Delta \le 2\%$ at $\mathrm{SEV} \ge 0.95$) to matched systems maintaining $\rho_{\mathrm{base}} \le 0.80$ and $\mathrm{TTS} \ge \mathrm{TTR}$;
   - **Empirical Falsifier 3 (Refuting the necessity of $S_{\mathrm{gov}}$ in multitask institutions):** Across a panel of $J \ge 15$ multitask public or corporate organizations introducing high-powered single-metric rankings ($\beta_{\mathrm{proxy}} \ge 0.70$) under limited-liability executive bonuses without downside clawbacks or independent $S3^*$ algedonic channels over $T \ge 12$ quarters, the claim of Collapse Proposition 3 is refuted if audited causal outcome quality on unmeasured care dimensions shows zero statistically significant degradation ($\Delta_{\mathrm{care}} \ge -0.10\sigma$ with $\mathrm{SEV} \ge 0.95$) and alert-to-action latency remains within $\mathrm{TTS}$;
   - **Empirical Falsifier 4 (Refuting the non-redundancy or sufficiency of the Seven-Tension Topology):** The heuristic claim that the seven tensions represent non-redundant failure modes of the mediation circuit is refuted if, across a multi-domain institutional failure registry ($N \ge 100$ audited failures), either (a) principal-component or factor analysis shows that two or more tensions collapse into a single collinear factor with shared variance $R^2 \ge 0.85$ (proving redundancy), or (b) more than $10\%$ of documented mediation failures originate in a structural transit that escapes all seven locks (requiring topological expansion).
4. **Constitutive Methodological-Normative Commitments and Boundary of Failure:**
   - *Constitutive Commitment (`TemporalVault`):* Separating hypothesis formulation ($t_1$) from confirmatory evaluation ($t_2$) and maintaining an unedited public registry of failed predictions is a **constitutive ethical-methodological norm** of intellectual rectitude, evaluated functionally by whether it reduces hindsight outcome bias (Baron and Hershey 1988);
   - *Positional Reflexivity and Boundary of Failure (Bourdieu 2001):* Following Pierre Bourdieu's (2001) call for *participant objectivation*, Stochology acknowledges its own socio-material limits. Sociologically, if formal tools such as partial-identification bounds or algorithmic audits are monopolized by technocratic elites detached from the testimony of affected citizens, Stochology itself risks becoming an instrument of bureaucratic legitimacy. Operationally, Stochology encounters a concrete **boundary of failure** in regimes of *radical scarcity and total archival erasure*: when missingness $p_{\mathrm{miss}} \to 1$ renders $[L_M, U_M]$ nearly as wide as $[y_{\min}, y_{\max}]$ while a community's current reserves already lie below subsistence, formal bounds cannot manufacture resources out of nothing, and Reflexive Brakes 2 and 3 must override conservative abstention in favor of immediate survival relief.

## **VIII. Conclusion: The Seven Tensions and the Integrity of Aim**

Throughout this programmatic essay, we have articulated **Stochology** as an integrative framework and transversal grammar open to specialists across the humanities, law, social sciences, medicine, ecology, engineering, and statistical sciences. Starting from the classical Greek distinction between exact mechanical measurement (*métron*) and conjectural aiming through opacity (*stókhos*, *eustokhía*, *stokhastikē tékhnē*), we argued that responsible judgment when action must precede the outcome requires analyzing, as a single coupled loop, the passages across the **material-historical world**, the **archive and formal model**, and **institutional deliberation**.

Across the **three mediating filters and seven constitutive tensions**, we traced how each achievement of rationalization turns into a pathology when severed from its limits, and how the three safeguards of the Nuclear Triad—the **epistemic margin** ($S_{\mathrm{epi}}$), the **vital margin** ($S_{\mathrm{vital}}$), and **fully apt agency under institutional governance** ($S_{\mathrm{gov}}$)—cohere at their critical threshold under the Liebig–Leontief minimum $M_{\mathrm{Leontief}} = \min(S_{\mathrm{epi}}, S_{\mathrm{vital}}, S_{\mathrm{gov}})$. Where the epistemic margin is suppressed, physical buffers are sized blindly around a downward-biased point mirage (Collapse Proposition 1); where the vital margin is eliminated in the name of lean efficiency, complete diagnostic lucidity watches helplessly as heavy-tailed shocks breach the absorbing or hysteretic boundary (Collapse Proposition 2); where symmetric governance, algedonic listening, and downside clawbacks are absent, epistemic doubt is weaponized as an agnotological alibi while common reserves are pillaged under moral hazard and metric gaming (Collapse Proposition 3). To declare without fabrication the width of what the archive leaves unknown, to preserve the material and temporal slack that living systems require to institute norms in an unfaithful environment, and to answer in institutional solidarity for the impact of the arrow once released: such is the task of Stochology and the substantive meaning of the ethics of aim.

## **Glossary of Classical and Technical Terms**

### **Part A — Classical Greek, Latin, and Philosophical Terms**

- **Ἀκρίβεια (*akríbeia*):** Exactness or precision grounded in fixed mechanical measurement, number, and weighing (*métron*, *arithmós*, *stathmós*), contrasted by Plato (*Philebus* 55e–56c) with the conjectural rigor of *stokhasmós*.
- **Εὐστοχία (*eustokhía*) and εὐστοχεῖν (*eustokheîn*):** "Good aim" or skill in hitting the mark across an uncertain medium; the lexical bridge uniting the root **στόχος (*stókhos*)** with the classical figure of the archer (**τοξότης, *toxótēs***).
- **Καιρός (*kairós*):** The fleeting, critical instant of acute crisis or opportunity in clinical medicine (*Aphorismi* I.1: *ho dè kairòs oxýs*), navigation, and strategy when decisive action cannot be postponed beyond the physical survival window ($\mathrm{TTS}$).
- **Καθῆκον (*kathēkon*):** In Stoic ethics (developed especially by Panaetius of Rhodes; Cicero, *De Officiis* I.107–121), an "appropriate action" or incumbency suited to one's roles (*personae*) and circumstances.
- **Μελέτη (*melétē*):** Disciplined practice, study, or exercise, by whose conjectural aim (*melétēs stokhasmōi*, Plato, *Philebus* 56a) the conjectural arts attune judgment without a mechanical gauge.
- **Μῆτις (*mētis*):** Plastic, vigilant, and adaptive practical intelligence capable of navigating shifting and ambiguous terrains (Detienne and Vernant 1974).
- **Non-Identical (*das Nichtidentische*) and Reification (*Verdinglichung*):** In Georg Lukács (1923/1971) and Theodor W. Adorno (1966/1973), *reification* treats historically produced abstractions as natural things, whereas the *Non-Identical* names the singular, material excess of the object that resists exhaustion by any concept or statistical summary—leaving operational traces in partial-identification width $[L_M, U_M]$, model discrepancy $\delta(x)$, and unmeasured care.
- **Προηγμένα (*proēgmena*):** In Stoic ethics, "preferred indifferents" (*producta* / *seligenda* in Cicero, *De Finibus* III.22)—external outcomes such as health or hitting the target (*skopós*) that a rational agent selects without equating them with moral virtue (*télos*).
- **Σημεῖα (*sēmeîa*):** Observable clinical symptoms, clues, and diagnostic traces (rendered in Latin as *signa*) theorized in the Hippocratic *Prognostic* and *On the Art* and in Galenic medicine (alongside conclusive signs, *tekmēria*).
- **Σκοπός (*skopós*):** The watched external target or mark (connected to *skopeîn*, "to look at," rendered in Latin as *scopus*, *meta*, or *propositum*), distinguished in Antipater of Tarsus's Stoic ethics from the internal end (*télos*).
- **Στόχος (*stókhos*), στοχάζεσθαι (*stokházesthai*), στοχαστικὴ τέχνη (*stokhastikē tékhnē*):** *Stókhos* primarily denotes an aim, shot, conjecture, or aiming mark (Latin *coniectura*); from it derive the verb *stokházesthai* (to aim at a mark, to conjecture), the noun *stokhastēs* (conjecturer), and the category *stokhastikē tékhnē* (conjectural art, systematized by Alexander of Aphrodisias and Galen), which Jacob Bernoulli (1713/2006) revived as *ars conjectandi sive stochastice*.
- **Τέλος (*télos*):** In Antipater of Tarsus's archer analogy (Stobaeus, *Eclogae* II.76.9–15 = Long and Sedley 1987, 64C), the internal consummate end of doing everything within one's craft to aim rightly, as distinct from the external outcome of striking the *skopós*.
- **Τόξον (*tóxon*) and τοξότης (*toxótēs*):** The bow (*tóxon*) and the archer (*toxótēs*), an etymological family distinct from *stókhos* that supplies the classical figure of the archer aiming under crosswinds.

### **Part B — Core Technical and Methodological Terms**

- **CORP and CRPS Decomposition:** The Consistent, Optimally Binned, Reproducible Pool-Adjacent-Violators isotonic decomposition of the Brier score ($\mathrm{BS} = \mathrm{REL}_{\mathrm{CORP}} - \mathrm{RES}_{\mathrm{CORP}} + \mathrm{UNC}$) and Continuous Ranked Probability Score ($\mathrm{CRPS}$) into miscalibration ($\mathrm{REL}$) and discriminative resolution ($\mathrm{RES}$) (Gneiting and Raftery 2007; Dimitriadis, Gneiting, and Jordan 2021).
- **Design Effect ($\mathrm{DEFF}$) and Effective Sample Size ($n_{\mathrm{eff}}$):** Variance inflation from intra-cluster correlation, $\mathrm{DEFF} = 1 + [\bar{m}(1 + \mathrm{CV}_m^2) - 1]\rho_{\mathrm{intra}}$ (Kish 1965; Campbell 1977; Moulton 1986), distinguished from Kish's weight-concentration sample size $n_{\mathrm{Kish}} = (\sum w_i)^2/\sum w_i^2$ and Meng's (2018) selection-bias MSE sample size $n_{\mathrm{Meng}} \approx \frac{f}{1-f}\rho_{R,Y}^{-2}$.
- **e-value and e-process:** A single-stage *e*-value is a nonnegative random variable $E \ge 0$ with $\mathbb{E}_{H_0}[E] \le 1$; a sequential *e*-process $(E_t)_{t \ge 1}$ is a nonnegative supermartingale satisfying $\mathbb{E}_{H_0}[E_\tau] \le 1$ at any arbitrary stopping time $\tau$ (Shafer 2021; Grünwald, de Heide, and Koolen 2024; Ramdas et al. 2023).
- **Imbens–Manski Confidence Interval ($\mathrm{CI}_{1-\alpha}^{\mathrm{IM}}$):** A two-sided confidence interval $[\hat{\theta}_L - c_\alpha\hat{\sigma}_L/\sqrt{n},\, \hat{\theta}_U + c_\alpha\hat{\sigma}_U/\sqrt{n}]$ for a partially identified parameter $\theta \in [\theta_L, \theta_U]$, where $c_\alpha \in [z_{1-\alpha}, z_{1-\alpha/2}]$ adapts to the ratio of identification width to sampling error (Imbens and Manski 2004).
- **Kelly Fraction and Epistemic Shrinkage ($f_{\mathrm{plug\text{-}in}}^*, f_{\mathrm{epi}}^*$):** The exposure fraction $f_{\mathrm{plug\text{-}in}}^* = (\mu - r)/\sigma^2$ maximizing excess logarithmic growth $g(f) - r$ (Kelly 1956), shrunk by $n_{\mathrm{eff}}/(n_{\mathrm{eff}} + 1)$ under finite-sample parameter uncertainty (Baker and McHale 2013).
- **Liebig–Leontief Minimum ($M_{\mathrm{Leontief}}$):** The bottleneck aggregation rule $M_{\mathrm{Leontief}} = \min(S_{\mathrm{epi}}, S_{\mathrm{vital}}, S_{\mathrm{gov}}) \in [0, 1]$ capturing serial non-compensability of the three safeguards at their critical survival threshold $\tau$.
- **Post-Data Severity ($\mathrm{SEV}$):** In Deborah Mayo's (2018) error statistics, the post-data probability $\mathrm{SEV}(\theta > \theta_{\mathrm{crit}}; x_0) = \mathbb{P}(T(X) \le T(x_0) \mid \theta = \theta_{\mathrm{crit}})$ that a test would have produced a less favorable result had the claim been false.
- **Time-to-Survive ($\mathrm{TTS}$) and Time-to-Recover ($\mathrm{TTR}$):** The physical breathing-time inequality $\mathrm{TTS}_t = B_t / d_{\mathrm{crisis}, t} \ge \mathrm{TTR}_t \propto 1/\lambda_t$ (Simchi-Levi, Schmidt, and Wei 2014), linked to the inverse recovery rate $1/\lambda_t$ of Critical Slowing Down (Scheffer et al. 2009).
